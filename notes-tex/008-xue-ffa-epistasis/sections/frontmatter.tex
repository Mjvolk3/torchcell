\renewcommand{\abstractname}{Abstract\cb{$\leq$150 words}}
\abstract{Strain engineering proceeds one edit at a time, keeping what improves and
discarding what does not. The procedure assumes that an improving combination can be
reached through improving intermediates. We test that assumption on a complete
three-factor design: ten transcription factors deleted in every single, double and triple
combination in a free-fatty-acid-producing \org{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} strain, with all
720 deletion orders measured rather than inferred. Interaction is the rule. Of 119 testable
triples, 86 deviate from the multiplicative null at a 5\% false discovery rate, and the
median deviation moves from $+0.40$ at two deletions to $-0.72$ at three. Fifty-one triples
exceed the base strain, yet only two sit at the end of a route whose every intermediate
also improves, and the shallowest route falls a median 31\% below the starting titer before
recovering. None of the seven interaction networks tested is enriched among the interacting
triples.}

\keywords{metabolic engineering, epistasis, gene interactions, strain design, combinatorial
