\section{Round eleven: the decoder takes the rules' functional form\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:round11}

\pfstep{What the round asked} Ten rounds left three readings: composition is what buys
the private readout, no public screen lifts the target or is learned at a held-out
compound, and every dose arm over-predicts the isobole interiors. Round eleven tested five
new parts of the model, each switchable and each defaulting to the round-ten arm, so every
cell of the round is an ablation of one part. \emph{Displacement}: the compound token
moves every gene embedding along a learned direction, gated per gene, which is the
environment's version of the encoder's deletion operator, against the round-ten design in
which the compound tokens attend to the genes. \emph{Cell-only}: the head reads the
perturbed cell state and nothing else, so a compound's whole effect has to pass through
the gene embeddings. \emph{Hill form}: a second readout of the same state gives each
inhibitor a dose-response curve, an IC50 and a slope, and the host's fitness is that curve
at the medium's dose. \emph{Composition}: the single-inhibitor readings of a mixture
combine by Bliss independence, by Loewe additivity solved on the curves, or by the highest
single agent, the three model-free rules of the figure now computed inside the decoder.
\emph{Pair term}: a learned correction on every pair of a mixture's compounds, zero at
the start, which is the environment's analog of a genetic interaction. The \rElevennArms{}
arms ran as \rElevennJobs{} one-GPU jobs on Delta; \rElevennScored{} of their
\rElevennConfigs{} configs are scored, the rest rerunning after Lustre write errors.

\pfstep{The control and the noise} Every arm is read against the round-ten Bliss arm,
re-run in this round on the same folds, seeds and inputs. Its growth AUROC is
\rElevencontrolAuroc{} and its compound-cold Vanacloig score \rElevencontrolVan{}. A
single seed scatters around its fold's three-seed ensemble by \rElevenseedSdVan{} on the
Vanacloig score and \rElevenseedSdAuroc{} on the AUROC; the \hydnCombinations{}
combinations are the same wells on every fold, so the fold-to-fold spread of a host score,
\rElevencontrolFoldSd{} for the control, is the floor a host difference has to clear.
Everything here is one fold partition.

\tcfig{\figRElevenHost}{\textbf{Round eleven on the wet-lab wells.} One row per arm, the
bar the mean over folds of each fold's three-seed ensemble, the open circles the folds.
Hatched arms never saw a titration or a combination well: their only host records are

combinations that grew. The displacement arm with the dose gain is the only arm that
falls far below the control.}{fig:r11host}


\pfstep{The reading} The Hill form composed by Bliss and trained on the public anchors
alone orders the combinations at a growth AUROC of \rElevenzsAuroc{} (folds
\rElevenzsAurocMin{} to \rElevenzsAurocMax{}) against Loewe's \hydloeweAuroc{}, and ranks
all of them at a worst-scenario Spearman of \rElevenzsWorst{}; it is the first arm of the
experiment above the Loewe rule, on one partition and three seeds, with single seeds from
\rElevenzsSeedMin{} to \rElevenzsSeedMax{}. Fitted on the titration, the decoder's rules
order as the model-free rules do: Loewe \rElevenloeweAuroc{}, Bliss \rElevenhillAuroc{},
the highest single agent \rElevenhsaAuroc{}. The Hill fine-tune is the balanced arm
(\rElevenftAuroc{} growth, \rElevenftGrown{} on the combinations that grew, against
Bliss's \hydblissSpearman{}). Displacing the gene embeddings leaves the public score
(\rElevenoperatorVanMin{} to \rElevenoperatorVanMax{}) and the host unchanged, and with the
dose gain it reads the titration at \rElevendisplaceDoffTit{} while composition collapses
to \rElevendisplaceDoffAuroc{}. No arm reaches nested ridge's \hydladderRidge{} on the
public side, no dose channel beats none on a held-out public dose
(\rElevendoseOff{} without one), and the gene-cold split reads zero
(\rElevengeneMin{} to \rElevengeneMax{}). \Cref{fig:r11model} wires the leading arm.
