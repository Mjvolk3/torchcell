\notesec{H1: an essentiality label averaged into a measured fitness}
The SGD essentiality entry is a statement about the gene, not a measurement of a
strain. Costanzo and Kuzmin measured essential genes through temperature-sensitive or
decreased-abundance alleles that grow, so the same gene name carries a measured
allele fitness and a converted 0, and the deduplicator's key, experiment type plus
gene set, cannot tell them apart. Of the 1{,}140 singles with an essentiality entry,
907 also carry measurements; their stored fitness has median 0.679 where the measured
mean has median 0.843, and the four bands in Figure~\ref{fig:hazards}a are the values
of $k$. Those singles enter 141{,}099 closure doubles and 78{,}707 triples, and
Section~\ref{sec:strength} measured what they cost. The 233 essentiality-only singles
touch no closure double or triple, so they cost the identity nothing, but they teach a
fitness model that 233 genes have a fitness of exactly 0 with no colony behind it.

\notesec{H2: two fitness values in one record}
A SynthLethDB single-gene record lists its gene twice, so its sorted gene set differs
from the Costanzo single's and the two are not merged; the trainer then finds two
fitness values in one record, 0.0 and about 1.0. Three singles do this (YPL212C,
YHL047C, YKL117W), and they killed the first S3 run at epoch 0 before the decode was
changed to mask such rows. The 691 closure doubles with a SynthLethDB entry merge
cleanly, a measured double-mutant fitness averaged with a 0, which is H1 again at
order two.

\notesec{H3: cross-screen singles in a within-screen identity}
Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} is exact on a source row because $f_a$, $f_b$ and $f_{ab}$ were
normalized together. The build's single is a mean over every source that measured the
gene, and its double is a mean over every screen of the pair. The single-screen
stratum of Table~\ref{tab:strength} isolates this: a double whose fitness and score
are one screen's, against singles that are not, reaches $r = 0.339$.

\notesec{H4: the merge replaces the p-value}
Section~\ref{sec:confidence} measured this. The count in Table~\ref{tab:hazards} is
697{,}825 closure doubles and 12{,}914 triples whose stored p-value is a $t$-test
with one to four degrees of freedom over duplicate scores, and the 1{,}754 doubles and
406 triples that every source called significant and the build does not.

\notesec{H5: the source substituted 1.0 for a missing fitness}
The released Kuzmin 2020 table has no query fitness on 99.6 percent of its digenic
rows and the reported scores were computed with 1.0 in its place; Costanzo 2016 does
the same on 2.1 percent of its rows. The build stores the score and stores whatever
single-mutant fitness it has for the gene from other sources, so for these pairs no
fitness in the build can reproduce the score, and a model asked to learn both is
asked to learn an inconsistency the source created.
