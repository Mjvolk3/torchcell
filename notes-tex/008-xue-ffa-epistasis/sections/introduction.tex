\section{Introduction\secstatus{todo}}

A strain is improved by editing it, measuring it, keeping the edits that helped, and
editing again. Every round of that loop is a decision made on the strain in hand, and a
round that produced nothing better than its parent is a round whose output is discarded.
The procedure is the design-build-test-learn cycle in its most common form, and its cost
scales with the number of rounds rather than with the number of edits eventually carried.

The procedure is efficient exactly when the effects of edits compose. If a deletion that
raises titer on its own keeps raising titer in the presence of other deletions, then a
greedy walk reaches a good combination, and the number of strains that must be built grows
linearly with the number of edits rather than exponentially. The assumption is rarely
stated because it is rarely testable: a campaign that builds a few hundred strains out of a
combinatorial space of millions cannot see the combinations it did not build, and cannot
tell a combination that is genuinely bad from one it never reached.

Epistasis is the name for the failure of that assumption. In its quantitative form it is
the deviation of a combination's phenotype from what the phenotypes of its parts predict,
and it is measured against a stated null. Systematic genetic interaction mapping in
\org{Saccharomyces cerevisiae} established both the scale of the phenomenon on
growth rate and the estimator now used for it: a digenic interaction score defined as the
departure of a double mutant's fitness from the product of the two single-mutant
fitnesses, and a trigenic score that removes the digenic contributions before asking what
the third deletion adds. Those maps were built on fitness in rich medium. Whether their
conclusions transfer to a production phenotype is not obvious, because fitness and titer
are under different selection, are measured on different instruments, and need not share a
null.

What has been missing for a production phenotype is a design in which every combination and
every construction order is present. Without one, a claim that a combination was
unreachable is indistinguishable from a claim that nobody happened to build it. The
free-fatty-acid dataset re-analyzed here supplies exactly that: ten transcription factors
deleted in every single, double and triple combination in one producing background, which
makes all 720 orders of deleting three genes available as measurements rather than as
inferences. The design permits a question that a partial screen cannot ask. Not how many
combinations interact, which any screen can estimate, but how many good combinations a
stepwise campaign could have found.
