\section{The plan: every gene-level readout trained together\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:proposal}

Every gene-level readout of the store trains in one model, and the question it answers
is an engineering one: does the model predict the transcriptome of a deletion it has not
seen, and of a double deletion from its two measured singles? Table~\ref{tab:plan} gives
the sets. An earlier version of this section proposed two separate training batches,
deletions and then isolates with segregants; the owner superseded it on 2026-10-19.

\notesec{Training}
Twelve datasets train together: every dataset of the store except Sameith 2015 Sm,
whose 82 profiles are Kemmeren's own arrays (82 of 82 identical), Sameith 2015 Dm, which
is split B, and Ohya 2005, which is the probe. Vaishnav 2022 stays in as the promoter
signal: one sort-seq level per promoter insert, read off the strain-level summary and
the promoter encoder of Sec.~\ref{sec:input-token}. Expression and protein readouts come
off the perturbed graph: a gene's value is read from that gene's own perturbed node state,
and the CLS state and the perturbed-gene summary $z_S$ are the strain-level sites
(Sec.~\ref{sec:output-sites}). The low-agreement datasets train too, and their measured
agreement is what the weighting decision (O5 in Table~\ref{tab:decisions}) and the arm
without them read: Nadal-Ribelles agrees with itself across batches at a median $r$ of
0.043 against 0.022 for different genotypes and with Kemmeren at 0.002; two Jackson 2020
constructions of one genotype agree at 0.129 against 0.095; Hu 2007 released no
replicate and agrees with Kemmeren at 0.107 per genotype, on an X score that is not a log
ratio; Zelezniak 2018 agrees with Messner at 0.049 per genotype over 715 proteins though
its own ceiling is 0.916; Muenzner 2024 turnover agrees with abundance at $-$0.006 per
isolate.

\notesec{Split A, Kemmeren singles by gene}
A tenth of Kemmeren's deletion genes, 148, is held out of every deletion dataset, so a
held-out gene is never seen as a perturbation. The bound is Kemmeren's own-culture
ceiling, 0.729 over 114 mutants with two cultures (Sec.~\ref{sec:within}); the 019
reference, by strain rather than by gene, is $0.180 \pm 0.006$ per gene over four runs.

\notesec{Split B, the Sameith doubles}
All 72 Sameith 2015 double deletions are held out and scored against their measured
profiles and against the additive null of their two Kemmeren singles, the paper's own
expected value. Sameith's Methods defer to Kemmeren's, so the doubles were profiled by
the same procedure; no double has a replicate, and the Kemmeren ceiling of 0.729 is the
bound for the same arrays.

\notesec{Split C, the Muenzner proteome}
A fifth of the 795 Muenzner 2024 proteome isolates, 159, is held out of every readout of
those isolates (Caudal 2024 shares 759 of them). No Muenzner replicate ceiling is
measured; the reference is how far another readout of the same isolate agrees, Caudal
mRNA against Muenzner protein at a median per-isolate $r$ of 0.208, against 0.031 for
Kemmeren mRNA against Messner protein on deletions.

\notesec{The morphology probe}
Ohya 2005 CalMorph enters no training. Its 4{,}678 genotypes are run through the frozen
model and a ridge reads the 116 moving features from the latent. A ridge on the measured
inputs already reaches a mean $r$ of 0.238 from Kemmeren expression (1{,}437 shared
genotypes) and 0.179 from Messner protein (4{,}279) over all 281 features, against
row-permutation nulls near zero, and Ohya's own ceiling is 0.611 over 122 wild-type
replicates (Table~\ref{tab:morph}). Sec.~\ref{sec:output-protocol} gives the bars for the
probe.

\notesec{The genotypes the model has not yet read}
Isolates and segregants need a gene-level genotype the \texttt{Perturbation} processor
does not build: marking every isolate perturbation sets 5{,}575 of 6{,}607 nodes, and a
segregant sets none. Sec.~\ref{sec:input} gives the change-type marks that replace it,
with the definite rule for segregants (a gene inherits the non-reference parent only
inside a block called at posterior 0.9 or above). The posterior cut removes little on the
pooled panels (a median of 0\% of markers for Boocock pooled, 1.7\% for N'Guessan) and a
quarter to a third on Boocock's de novo cells, whose single cells carry medians of 2{,}000
to 4{,}000 UMIs; whether those cells train is decision P1. The measured genotype signal
across segregant panels is small: nearest-genotype deviation $r$ 0.113 against 0.070
for random pairs (Sec.~\ref{sec:within}).
