### The extrapolation rule

An adapter's generation time is modeled as its dev-LMDB bytes divided by the throughput of
the family its record count puts it in. The two families are the two code paths, not a
taxonomy: `inprocess_max_records: 25000` in
`torchcell/knowledge_graphs/conf/kg_uncapped.yaml` is the boundary, so a dataset at or
below it is rendered in-process and one above it goes through the chunk pool. Each family's
throughput is the **median** of the per-adapter throughputs job 3297 measured for that
family:

| family | boundary | throughput of dev-LMDB bytes | spread over the KG 3.0 adapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| pool | more than 25,000 records | 11.5 MB/s | 4.4 to 56.3 MB/s, n = 17 |
| in-process | 25,000 records or fewer | 2.6 MB/s | 0.6 to 12.5 MB/s, n = 33 |

Dev-LMDB bytes, not records, because the per-record rate is not a rate: it spans 195 us
(`DmiCostanzo2016Adapter`) to 404 ms (`DmMicroarraySameith2015Adapter`), a factor of 2,000,
and the spread is entirely byte volume per record. The single-pass chunk is itself a byte
budget (`single_pass_chunk_budget_mb: 128`) with a 256-record floor, so bytes are what the
pool actually schedules.

**Back-applied to its own calibration build the rule gives 19,223 s against 11,102 s
measured, an overstatement of 1.73x**, because the median understates the largest pooled
adapters, which run fastest. That bias is reported rather than tuned away, and the
projection for the new datasets carries it as headroom.

### The slurm request, and why

`database/slurm/scripts/gilahyper_live_rebuild-slurm_docker.slurm` is edited in this branch
to carry what the last three builds actually ran with:

| directive | was | now | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--cpus-per-task` | 24 | **64** | `SLURM_CPUS_PER_TASK` is what sets the adapter worker count (`get_num_workers` in `torchcell/knowledge_graphs/create_scerevisiae_kg_small.py`), and `MaxMemPerCPU=4100` makes a 256 G request a 64-CPU floor (`ceil(262144 / 4100) = 64`). With 24 in the header the worker count was decided by slurm's enforcement rather than chosen: job 2032 requested 24 and was allocated 48. Jobs 3198, 3297 and 3547 all passed `--cpus-per-task=64` on the command line. |
| `--time` | 4-00:00:00 | **12:00:00** | 4 days dates from the 28 h to 66 h era. Measured now: 3 h 11 m (3198) and 3 h 36 m (3297); projected 4 h 15 m with a 6 h 30 m upper bound. GilaHyper's `main` is FIFO on one node, so an honest limit is what lets the GPU packs backfill. Jobs 3198, 3297 and 3547 all passed `--time=12:00:00`. |
| `--mem` | 256G | **256G, unchanged** | see below |

**Memory does not need to grow with the dataset count, and this is the load-bearing claim.**
The pool recycles when the container's cgroup memory passes `pool_memory_fraction: 0.8` of
its limit, so the measured 235.4 GiB peak is the recycler riding a 256 GiB ceiling, not a
requirement: job 3297's 22 recycles fired between 0.80 and 0.88 of the limit. What a larger
request would have to cover is the per-worker working set, which is a 128 MiB resolved-record
chunk times the `process_workers + 2` chunks the submission window keeps in flight, and that
is set by the config, not by how many datasets follow. The one way a new dataset could raise
it is by carrying wider records than anything already built, and none does: the widest new
store is `PutidaPrecise321Lim2022Dataset` at 292 kB per record on disk, against
`CaudalPanTranscriptome2024Dataset` at 977 kB and the three Kemmeren and Sameith microarray
stores at 620 kB, all of which KG 3.0 generated inside 256 G.

Going to 320 G would cost a GPU pack. The node is 128 CPUs and 513,000 MB
(`scontrol show node gilahyper`); 64 CPUs plus 256 G leaves exactly the 64 CPUs and
216,000 MB the four 16-CPU / 54,000 M packs want, which is what is coresident with job 3547
right now. 320 G forces an 80-CPU floor and leaves room for three.

Worker split, derived from the code rather than logged: `num_workers = 64`,
`io_workers = int(0.02 * 64) = 1`, so `process_workers = 63`.

### Chunking: no change, and the measurement that says so

The question was whether the bacterial stores with many small records want a different chunk
size from the yeast ones. They do not, because the chunk is already sized in bytes.
`single_pass_chunk_budget_mb: 128` with a `SINGLE_PASS_MIN_CHUNK` of 256 records means a
store of many small records automatically gets a large record-count chunk, and the measured
chunk lines bear it out: `DmfCostanzo2016Adapter` at 21,502 resolved bytes per record opened
on 6,427-record chunks, `MicroarrayKemmeren2014Adapter` at 1,164,966 bytes per record on
256-record chunks (the floor).

Borchert 2024 is 4.15 kB per record on disk. At the measured median resolved-to-LMDB byte
ratio of 5.55 that is about 23 kB resolved, giving roughly 5,800-record chunks, well under
`chunk_size: 1e5`, and 1.37 M records over 63 process workers is about four waves. That is
the same regime the Kuzmin and Bloom stores run in (3.7 kB per record on disk, about
21.5 kB resolved, 6,100 to 6,400-record chunks), and those measured 11 to 12 MB/s. Price
2018, Choe 2025 and Mutalik 2020 sit in the same band. The knob that would matter is the
256-record floor, and it binds only for wide-record stores, all of which are yeast and
already built; the new stores that fall under 256 records
(`PutidaPrecise321Lim2022Dataset` at 180, `ProteomeLim2025Dataset` at 3) are below
`inprocess_max_records` and never reach the pool.

So `kg_uncapped.yaml` stays as it is: `chunk_size: 1e5`, `chunks_per_worker: 64`,
`pool_memory_fraction: 0.8`, `single_pass_chunk_budget_mb: 128`,
`inprocess_max_records: 25000`, `inprocess_max_mb: 256`, `io_to_total_worker_ratio: 0.02`,
`single_pass: true`, `fast_writer: true`, `completion_order: true`.

### Submit-time checklist, in order

1. **Every dev store fresh.** `PYTHONPATH=<worktree> python -m torchcell.database.build_dataset_lmdb --list-stale --include-private` must print nothing. **Measured today it prints 99 of the 109 mapped classes**, with `GenePerturbationType` naming the drift in 97 of them: the additive union member that landed in `e1acbb28b` and `9db9b2fcb` after `v1.7.2`. `[[torchcell.datamodels.bacterial-perturbation-ontology]]` records the same thing as 113 stale stores across the whole dev tree, mapped and unmapped. Job 3547 is clean only because it builds from `v1.7.2`, where they were fresh. **A KG 4.0 built from any tag later than `v1.7.2` therefore needs the 99 stores rebuilt first**, via
   ```bash
   CLASSES=/scratch/projects/torchcell/database/slurm/stale_classes.txt
   PYTHONPATH=$TORCHCELL_SRC python -m torchcell.database.build_dataset_lmdb \
       --list-stale --include-private > "$CLASSES"
   sbatch --array=1-$(wc -l < "$CLASSES")%4 --export=ALL,CLASSES_FILE="$CLASSES" \
       database/slurm/scripts/gilahyper_build_dataset_lmdbs_array.slurm
   ```
   Sizing for that array is measured: 16 CPUs and 64 G per task, 12 h limit, and the two
   Costanzo interaction stores are the long poles at 4 h 41 min and 4 h 20 min
   (2026-10-05); every other store took minutes. The 2026-10-08 array (job 3410, 101 tasks
   at `%4`) ran 16:27 to 17:41, about 1 h 15 min wall.
2. **The `v*` tag and a dedicated worktree at that tag.** The script refuses a build commit
   that carries no package tag, and refuses a `TORCHCELL_SRC` whose `HEAD` is not that
   commit, because the stamp records the tag and an untagged build leaves every published
   package incompatible with the store. Set `TORCHCELL_SRC` to the worktree checked out at
   the tag, not the primary checkout.
3. **`--dependency=afterok` on the dev-store array**, so the rebuild cannot start against a
   half-built store: `sbatch --dependency=afterok:<array job id> ... gilahyper_live_rebuild-slurm_docker.slurm`.
4. **The compound-identity curator pass, #726, is still blocked.** PubChem is throttling
   (HTTP 200 once at 04:05:13, then 429 on the next four probes), 276 bacterial gap labels
   remain, and 0 of them resolves to a row in `compound_identity_table.json` today, so
   `_TABLE_SHA256` on main is unchanged. **Owner decision:** build KG 4.0 with those typed
   InChIKey gaps, or wait on PubChem. The gaps are honest records, not errors, but a later
   curator pass that resolves a label changes that record's content, which is the
   full-rebuild case, not an incremental admission.
5. **Admission preflight at 0 blockers.** Not applicable to a full rebuild, which is what
   the admission check blocks *into*; the equivalent gate here is the script's own preflight,
   which must report `109 mapped datasets, every dev store fresh`, `df` above
   `MIN_FREE_GB`, no existing `tc-neo4j-build` or `tc-neo4j-gen` container, an empty
   `$NEXT_ROOT/data`, and a clean `cmp` of the repo and build-tree
   `torchcell_schema_config.yaml`. Read `[[torchcell.knowledge_graphs.incremental-admission]]`
   before the next *incremental* admission, not before this build.
6. **Rollback path.** The swap is two renames, so the reverse is two renames: stop
   `tc-neo4j-readonly`, `mv /db/database/data` aside, `mv` the
   `data.superseded.<ts>` back to `data`, relaunch with the arguments
   `scripts/migrate_storage_tiers.sh` uses. Nothing is deleted by the build.
7. **`DB(kg)` snapshot commit, then retag.** The build's `record` stage writes
   `database/releases/<release id>.json` and `.closures.json` into the checkout; those are
   committed with a `DB` prefix so semantic-release cuts the paired package version, and
   `releases retag` is the only repair if a build ran from an untagged commit. KG releases
   and package releases are pairs: KG 3.0 is paired with `v1.6.2`.
8. **Supported-queries validation.** `bash scripts/run-supported-queries.sh` (the
   pre-commit wrapper over `python -m torchcell.knowledge_graphs.supported_queries check`)
   checks every registered query against the newest committed release snapshot and blocks on
   a `supported` query drifting. A bacterial-wave ontology revision will drift queries, and
   `TORCHCELL_QUERY_DRIFT_ACK=1` is the deliberate acknowledgment after the report is read.
9. **Compatibility page.** #822 is closed: PR #821 landed the regeneration for 1.7.1 and
   1.7.2, but the fix was the page, not the release job, so the `query-drift` job in
   `.github/workflows/docs.yaml` still goes red on the version commit semantic-release
   pushes. Run `python scripts/kg_compat_page.py` after the release lands and commit the
   result, or main stays red and every open PR inherits it.
