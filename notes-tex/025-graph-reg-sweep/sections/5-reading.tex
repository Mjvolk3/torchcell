\section{Reading, and what is still missing\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:reading}

\notesec{On the hypothesis}
Stated as proposed: the graphs help, but only when the model is optimized through the
divergence; imposing them as a mask does not help. The mask half holds on both readings
and all three seeds: the mask is indistinguishable from no penalty (0.421 against 0.425
at epoch 29, 0.446 against 0.448 at the best epoch), and it trains without the collapse
the earlier cosine-schedule runs showed. The prior half holds on the fixed-epoch reading
from $\lambda = 10^{-3}$ up, with the gain growing to 0.021 to 0.023 at $\lambda = 1$ and
holding through $\lambda = 100$, and holds weakly on the best-epoch reading (0.003 to
0.007 at $\lambda = 1$, positive on every seed). The word ``help'' needs its reading
attached: a best-checkpoint deployment gains at most 0.006 from the prior; a fixed-budget
run gains 0.02, because the prior removes the decline after epoch 16 rather than raising
the peak. The random-graph control at $\lambda = 10^{-1}$ and 1 (Section~\ref{sec:control})
establishes that the help comes from the biology of the graphs: a prior of the same
weight toward degree-matched rewirings gives 0.003 to 0.004 over no penalty at epoch 29
where the biological graphs give 0.020 to 0.022, and at the best epoch it falls 0.011 to
0.012 below no penalty on every seed. The alternative, that a strong prior toward any
graph of the same degree sequence helps as much, is rejected at both $\lambda$; what
stays open is whether the biological gain at the peak (0.004 to 0.006 over no penalty)
is worth a prior that costs 0.011 when its target is wrong.

\notesec{What the mock-up predicted and what was measured}
The figure design (\file{notes/assets/drawio/FigS-graph-regularization-sweep.gen.py})
drew six panels. Panel a, contraction with $\lambda$: measured, and it saturates at
$10^{-2}$. Panel b, accuracy peaking between the extremes: measured, and there is no
interior peak; the maximum is at the top of the ladder. Panel c, the penalty carrying the
gradient before the point loss can: measured, and the penalty dominates the gradient at
every probe epoch for $\lambda \geq 10^{-2}$, not only early. Panels d and e, discovery
of edges outside a head's prior and overruling of cataloged edges by the digenic
interaction, need the layer-1 attention of the saved checkpoints, which are on Delta
\texttt{/scratch} and have not been read; the edge-recovery precision logged at $k =
32$ (0.21 for every prior arm from $10^{-3}$ up, 0.006 with no penalty) is the nearest
logged quantity and is not the discovery statistic. Panel f, the random-graph control:
measured first at the wrong $\lambda$, then in round 1b at the two $\lambda$ where the
gain is largest, where it separates.

\notesec{The panel titles, checked}
Each title of Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} states a finding, which is a place for bias to
enter, so each is set against the number it rests on. (a) ``Prior gains at epoch 29,
little at the peak'': $+0.022$ at $\lambda = 1$ on the fixed reading against $+0.004$ on
the max reading (Table~\ref{tab:paired}). (b) ``Held-out loss falls with $\lambda$ from
0.001 up'': 0.859, 0.839, 0.826, 0.825 at $10^{-3}$ to 1 against 0.867 with no penalty;
the two smallest weights are above no penalty (0.880, 0.878), which the title's ``from
0.001 up'' carries (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). (c) ``Any $\lambda$ tested moves attention
onto its target'': 0.782 at $10^{-5}$ against 0.006 at 0. (d) ``Divergence floors near
300 from $\lambda = 0.01$'': 300, 297, 298, 298, 297, 298 from $10^{-2}$ to 100. (e)
``Penalty dominates the gradient from
$\lambda = 0.01$'': ratio 15 to 24 at $10^{-2}$ by epoch 20 against 1.1 to 1.5 at
$10^{-3}$, and $1.0 \times 10^{4}$ and $9.6 \times 10^{4}$ at 10 and 100. (f) ``Biology beats random graphs by $\approx$0.017 at $\lambda \geq 0.1$'':
biological minus random on seeds 1 to 3 is $+0.017$ at epoch 29 at both $10^{-1}$
($t = 14.5$, $p = 0.005$) and 1 ($t = 8.8$, $p = 0.013$), $+0.018$ and $+0.015$ at the
max ($p = 0.03$ and $0.014$), and $+0.019$ and $+0.012$ at minimum validation loss
($p = 0.06$ and $0.26$); at $10^{-3}$ it is $+0.012$ at epoch 29 ($p = 0.11$) and within
noise on the other readings. (g)
``Strong prior removes the decline after epoch 16'': seed-mean 0.441 to 0.425 with no
penalty against 0.435 to 0.447 at $\lambda = 1$ and 0.441 to 0.445 at $10^{-1}$. (h)
``Strong priors fit training like no penalty'': 0.625 and 0.624 against 0.624. (i)
``Held-out loss rises less under a strong prior'': from the epoch-13 minimum, 0.801 to
0.867 with no penalty against 0.803 to 0.825 at $\lambda = 1$.

  Section~\ref{sec:training} about where the penalty's gradient lands.
\end{enumerate}
