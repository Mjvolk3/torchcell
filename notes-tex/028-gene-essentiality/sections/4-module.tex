\section{The metabolic module: the solve helps, so why not the layer?\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:module}

One linear program per deletion on yeast-GEM ranks essential genes at 0.65 on its own and
lifts every model in this document by 0.05 when its four growth ratios are appended
(Sections~\ref{sec:comparators} and~\ref{sec:backbone}). The metabolic module is built on
the same genome-scale model: the same stoichiometry, the same bounds, the same
gene-protein-reaction rules closing the deleted gene's reactions. The question of this
section is why routing the prediction through it does not do what the solve does. The
rounds answer it in steps: the module neither helps nor hurts on the backbone (rounds 2
and 4); it adds nothing on top of the solve (round 6); its growth column is not growth
but a free coordinate, because the layer never reaches the polytope the solve optimizes
over (the probe, round 7); handed the solve's own point on that polytope, the anchored
layer's constraint set discards it (round 10) while the GEM's own box keeps it, and the
gated module cell then reaches the level of the solve appended as an input (round 13);
a layer that solves the program instead of projecting toward it is infeasible on the
anchored box, whose capacity ceilings exclude growth (round 15), and on the GEM's box it
is feasible, ranks above FBA before training, and gated reaches the same level (round
15b); with the ceilings calibrated to admit growth, the growth column alone ranks the
held-out genes at 0.84 on one seed and the program's infeasible region absorbs the other
two (round 15c). The gradient reaches the alternating layer throughout, so its null is
not a gradient-flow failure; the layer that solves has the linear program's own
gradient, the dual of the binding bounds. The section closes with what the comparison
says a layer has to compute to help.

The pooled backbone reads its logit from an MLP on the perturbed context
(Figure~\ref{fig:readout-pooled}). A flux arm builds the enzyme-constrained thermodynamic
flux layer on the same context and exposes one physically named quantity, the flux through
the growth reaction \texttt{r\_2111}. Three readouts put that quantity to different uses,
and they differ in what the backbone's gradient has to pass through.

\begin{description}
  \item[biomass (forced)] $\ell = a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b$. The growth column is the
    only path from the context to the logit, so the module carries the whole prediction or
    the cell scores chance (Figure~\ref{fig:readout-biomass}). This is FBA's own reading of
    essentiality with a learned threshold, and it is the wave-9 design.
  \item[context + growth] $\ell = \mathrm{MLP}(h) + a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b$. Both paths
    from the first step; the per-module gradient norms say how much of each update the
    module carries when it is not forced to carry all of it.
  \item[gated] $\ell = \mathrm{MLP}(h) + g\,(a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b)$ with $g_0 = 0$
    (Figure~\ref{fig:readout-gated}). At initialization the cell is the pooled model
    exactly; the gate's trajectory, logged every epoch, says whether the optimizer chose
    the flux path, and the \texttt{flux\_layer} gradient line says whether gradient reached
    the layer, which at $g = 0$ it cannot.
\end{description}

\subsection{What the layer's flux violates\secstatus{todo}}

Every flux arm in this document builds the anchored layer: the predicted $k_{cat}$ tier
(97 measured, 782 homolog, 2,831 predicted, 18 default catalytic units), the
union-external reaction energies (3,779 of 4,131 reactions with a $\Delta_r G'$), the
enzyme-capacity ceiling from the abundance head in the box, and the second law, the
protein budget and the Gibbs dissipation as penalty terms at the constraint scale. The
layer logs every one of those residuals each epoch, and Table~\ref{tab:violations}
reads them at the selected epoch across the module arms. With the balance exact, the
flux is outside its bounds on 58 to 63 percent of reactions, runs against its reaction
energy on 44 to 51 percent, uses 0.2 to 7 times the protein pool, and dissipates 5,000
to 12,000 J/gDW/h against the 3,700 limit, with the penalty on or off; with the bounds
exact (round 7) the second-law violation falls to a third and the dissipation rises to
100,000. No arm's flux satisfies the constraints the layer was built to hold, so on
this endpoint the $k_{cat}$ table and the reaction energies are inputs to a
projection that does not honor them, which is the precise sense in which the module
is not the solve.

The Gibbs dissipation is reported here as a diagnostic and nothing else. The Niebel
limit is an empirical plateau of one organism's physiology, not a constraint of the
network, and a model that stays under it because it was penalized for exceeding it has
been told the answer. The anchored arm's registry entry carries the dissipation hinge,
so the round-2 and round-4 cells at constraint scale 0.1 trained against it; every
cell of the layer that solves ran at constraint scale 0, where the hinge has weight
zero, and from round 18 the module cells run on the \texttt{nodiss} entry of the same
arm, where the term is absent by construction and the rate is logged only.

\subsection{What holds by construction, and what does not\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:by-construction}

Three constructions of the module appear in this section, and they differ in which
constraints their flux satisfies exactly, which it satisfies through a penalty, and
which it satisfies only when the backbone finds a way to. The alternating layer
(Figure~\ref{fig:module-projection}) writes a proposal in the null space of $S$, so mass
balance holds at the start, and alternates the balance projection with the box clip
$K$ times, so whichever of the two runs last holds exactly and the other is a residual;
the second law, the protein pool and the capacity ceilings are hinges at the constraint
scale, and Table~\ref{tab:violations} measures what the hinges leave. The layer that
solves (Figure~\ref{fig:module-lp}) returns the growth optimum of the linear program
under the layer's bounds, so mass balance, the box and every capacity ceiling hold
exactly at once, with no penalty in the loss; what it does not guarantee is that a
program exists, and rounds 15 to 15d measure what happens when it does not. The elastic
layer that solves (Figure~\ref{fig:module-elastic}) keeps all of that and adds two
things by construction: the protein pool, by projecting the abundance head's output onto
the budget before the ceilings are formed, and feasibility, by letting the one forced
flux in the model, the ATP maintenance reaction's lower bound of 0.7 mmol/gDW/h, go
unmet at a price. Its zero flux with the maintenance fully unmet satisfies every
constraint, so the program solves for every genotype at every ceiling, and a genotype
that cannot meet its maintenance is not absent from the gradient but priced into it.
Under every construction the second law enters only through the anchored reaction
energies and the dissipation only as a logged rate. What ``the enzyme-capacity box
contains no growing flux'' means, stated exactly with the elastic program in hand: at
the ceilings as parameterized the maintenance flux the model forces cannot be carried,
the shortfall is 0.54 of 0.70 with zero growth (\texttt{capacity\_calibration.py
--lp-slack-penalty 1}, 32 held-out genotypes), the hard program's feasible set is
therefore empty, and the solver returns nothing; from a ceiling scale of 10 the
shortfall is zero and growth is 0.01, and from 30 the box admits the growth the rounds
train on.

\subsection{The growth column is a signed free coordinate\secstatus{todo}}

Four rounds put the layer's growth column level with the pooled head and blind to its
own $k_{cat}$ table while one linear program per deletion lifts every model by 0.05. The
probe in Table~\ref{tab:growth-probe} reads the column itself. It rebuilds a forced cell
from its result file, deletes each of the 982 labeled genes through the same batches
training used, and reads $v_{\text{growth}}$ at initialization and under the trained
weights, scoring the negative column against the label (a deletion that stops growth is
essential) and the column against FBA's ratio.

\input{tables/growth_probe}

At initialization the column carries a weak signal in the right direction: 0.55 on the
held-out genes and a Spearman of $+0.11$ with the solve, nothing like FBA's 0.65, and no
deletion reaches zero growth. Training inverts it. Under the round-4 forced cell's weights
the negative column reads 0.23 on the held-out genes, which is that cell's 0.77 with the
sign of growth reversed: the affine learned a positive slope, an essential deletion gets
the \emph{higher} growth, and the column is anticorrelated with the solve ($-0.25$). The
round-2 forced cell does the same ($0.24$, $-0.26$). The median growth under the trained
weights is $-1.4$ and $-2.4$ mmol/gDW/h against a wild-type FVA range of 0.48 to 0.54 on
YPD: for most deletions the column is negative, which growth cannot be.

The column can do this because the arm's alternations end on the balance projection, so
$Sv = 0$ holds to machine precision and the box does not: 58 percent of reactions sit
outside their bounds at the selected epoch (0.64 on the round-2 cell, 0.59 on the round-6
cell), the growth reaction's floor of zero among them. $v_{\text{growth}}$ is then a
signed, unbounded coordinate the backbone writes through the abundance head, and the
readout uses it as a free latent. That is the mechanism behind the shuffled-$k_{cat}$
parity and behind the layer matching the head: the quantity the forced readout reads is
not growth.

\subsection{Why the ceilings cannot carry the ranking\secstatus{todo}}

The gradient the layer that solves hands the backbone is the dual of the bounds that
bind at the optimum, so what the backbone can learn through the solve is bounded by
which bounds those are and whether they differ between genotypes.
\texttt{lp\_dual\_overlap.py} reads them off the elastic cell at initialization on 64
held-out genotypes at three ceiling scales (\texttt{results/lp\_dual\_overlap.json}).

sets is 1.0: the same six reactions bind whichever gene is deleted, with a union of 96

\subsection{Why the solve helps and the layer does not\secstatus{todo}}

What FBA contributes to the prediction is one number per deletion: the maximum of growth
over the polytope $\{Sv = 0,\ \ell \le v \le u\}$ with the deleted gene's reactions
closed. That number ranks the held-out genes at 0.65 alone, its four-medium version
lifts a logistic regression, a random forest and the transformer by 0.05 each, and the
solve has more to give than the number: eight statistics of the gene's reactions under
the wild-type solve reach 0.80 with no sequence, and the whole deletion flux vector
0.77. The value is in the optimum over the constraint set and in the flux that attains
it.

The module holds the same constraint set and computes something else. Its flux is $K$
alternating projections of a proposal the backbone writes, and the rounds measure, in
order, that the projections stop short of the polytope (58 percent of reactions out of
bounds when the balance is exact, a balance residual of 0.85 to 2.0 when the bounds
are), that the growth column of a flux off the polytope is a free coordinate the readout
learns to use with either sign (the probe: 0.23 on the held-out genes under trained
weights, Spearman $-0.25$ with the solve), that the learned parts of the layer are not
what the column carries (the shuffled-$k_{cat}$ control matches the real table), and
that the layer's constraint set rejects the solve when the solve is supplied as the
proposal (round 10: the reference violates the anchored box on a third of its reactions
and the returned flux sits half the reference's norm away, sign reversed). The gradient
is not the cause; it reaches the layer on every arm that lets it.

The comparison also bounds what a feasible layer could add. A layer that reached the
polytope would return a feasible flux, not the optimal one, unless the backbone learned
to write proposals whose projection is the optimum; the solve finds the optimum by
construction. And a growth column is one number, where the solve's flux vector scores
0.11 to 0.13 more AUROC on the ladder than its growth ratio does. A module that helped on this
endpoint would therefore have to solve rather than project, with the backbone setting
the bounds it solves under (the capacity ceilings from the abundance head are that
interface already), and read more of the resulting flux than its growth column. The
first of those is a linear program per genotype per forward pass with the gradient taken
through its dual, about 50 milliseconds each. Rounds 13, 15 and 15b test both routes on
both boxes. On the GEM's own box the solve survives the projections (round 13) and the
program that solves is feasible and ranks at 0.73 before training (round 15b), and in
both cases the gated module cell reaches the affine's level, 0.816 and 0.824, three of
three seeds above the round-4 gated cell, with the growth column entering through a
positive gate. On the anchored box the projections reverse the solve (round 10) and the
program has no feasible point (round 15), because the enzyme-capacity ceilings as
parameterized exclude growth; with those ceilings scaled to admit growth the program
solves, the ceilings bind, and on one of three seeds the growth column alone ranks the
