\section{What pooling buys chemically\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:chemistry}

Pooling is worth the trouble only if the partner panels put chemistry near the application
panel. Table~\ref{tab:chemspace} measures that for every Vanacloig compound: its most similar
compound in each partner, under Tanimoto on count ECFP4. The fingerprint metric is the one
reported because cosine between dense embeddings is high for any two molecules and says
nothing about closeness.

Wildenhain supplies essentially all of the gain. Exact overlap with the panel rises from two
compounds against either Hillenmeyer arm to ten against Wildenhain and twelve against the
union, and the median nearest neighbor rises from 0.33 and 0.32 to 0.60. Hoepfner contributes
nothing chemically: two exact matches, a median nearest neighbor of 0.29, and no non-exact
neighbor above 0.5.

\notesec{The gain is uneven, and it fails where the application needs it}
Sorting the hydrolysate panel by its nearest neighbor in the union of the other four, ferulic
acid becomes an exact match, vanillin reaches 0.62 against it, syringaldehyde 0.60 against
syringic acid, and coumaric acid 0.55 against caffeic acid. The phenolics are genuinely
covered. Isobutanol improves from 0.14 against sorbitol, which was its best neighbor when only
Hillenmeyer was available, to 0.47, but its neighbor is an amino alcohol rather than an
alcohol. Furfural sits at 0.30 against benzaldehyde and ethanol at 0.31. At two and five heavy
atoms the small alcohols are nearly empty under a substructure fingerprint, so this is a limit
of the representation as much as of the panel.

Pooling therefore buys phenolic coverage and does not buy alcohol coverage, and isobutanol is
the application target.
