\section{Open\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:open}

The held-out number is close to saturated: the round-12 ensemble's interval, 0.893
[0.84, 0.94], spans what 195 genes with 31 essential can resolve, and every lever on the
transformer side has been moved (width, depth, the preprocessor, the budget, the class
weight, six encodings). What remains is attribution and the module.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Which columns of the chromosome-pathway indicator carry the round-9 gain. The
    encoding holds five continuous gene properties, two of them expression, beside the
    chromosome and pathway indicators; the registry's \texttt{normalized\_chrom}
    (properties and chromosome, no pathways) drops the pathways, so the pair of cells
    separates the annotation from the properties. Untested.
  \item Whether the solve's other columns add to the transformer what they add to the
    ladder: round 11 puts the eight reaction statistics and the deletion flux vector on
    the affine of the ProtT5 cell, and the round-9 backbone takes them next if they pass.
  \item Round 13: the pFBA warm start on the stoichiometry-only layer. If its growth
    column reads at the solve's level at initialization and keeps it, the projection
    route carries the solve and the anchored constraint set was the loss; if not, the
    alternations are.
  \item A layer that solves. The one module construction the rounds have not ruled out is
    a linear program per genotype inside the forward pass, with the backbone's capacity
    ceilings as its bounds and the gradient through the LP's dual prices, reading the
    optimal flux rather than a growth column. One solve costs about 50 milliseconds, so a
    200-epoch cell at batch 32 is about two hours of CPU per seed. Untested.
  \item The module's own hyperparameters have not been moved on this endpoint: the
    $k_{cat}$ tier (measured against predicted), the thermodynamic source, the number of
    alternations. Given the probe and round 10, none of them reaches the polytope.
\end{itemize}
