\section{The question\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:question}

Five chemogenomic datasets are served and together hold 7.48 million records. Training one
model on all of them is attractive for an obvious reason and blocked by a less obvious one.
The attraction is scale and chemical breadth: no single dataset has both a genome-wide gene
panel and a large compound panel. The block is that a model takes one input format, and the
five datasets disagree about what a strain is and about what a dose is.

Three axes carry the disagreement. Ploidy runs from haploid single deletions through
homozygous diploids to heterozygous diploids where one wild-type copy survives. Compound
panels run from 41 to 5{,}170. Dose runs from an unquantified target inhibition, through a
molar range spanning nine orders of magnitude, to a single fixed concentration applied to
every compound.

The application target is Vanacloig-Pedros 2022 (\texttt{10.1128/spectrum.02017-22}), the
only served screen that doses the industrial hydrolysate panel at a matched target
inhibition and the only one in which isobutanol appears as a stress. The others enter as
partners: Hillenmeyer 2008 (\texttt{10.1126/science.1150021}) and Hoepfner 2014
(\texttt{10.1016/j.micres.2013.11.004}) for record count and gene coverage, Wildenhain 2015
(\texttt{10.1016/j.cels.2015.12.003}) for compound diversity.

This document measures what a shared representation can absorb and what it cannot, and ends
with the representation itself in Section~\ref{sec:representation}.
