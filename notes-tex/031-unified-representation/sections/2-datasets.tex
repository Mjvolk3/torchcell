Three observations set up the rest of the document. Hoepfner is the largest store and the
only one holding both ploidy arms, 1{,}759{,}255 heterozygous records and 1{,}365{,}064
homozygous, over a shared gene universe. Wildenhain has eighteen times the compound panel of
the next largest and a fifth of the gene panel. Vanacloig is the smallest and is the
application target, and it is the only dataset whose dose is not a number.

One correction worth recording, because it was wrong in an earlier reading of the raw files.
The Hoepfner score matrices are genes by screens, not screens by genes: the roughly 5{,}900
columns per file are 2{,}956 experiments run in duplicate, and the 6{,}681 rows are the
genes.

\notesec{The physical and medium fields are dataset identity}
The served record carries the medium, the temperature, the aerobicity, the duration, the pH
when it was set, and the solvent. Table~\ref{tab:physical} reads every one of them from the
records of all five datasets. The result is that none of them varies inside a dataset in a
way a model could learn from. Temperature is 30~C wherever it is stated and is stated for 1
to 2 percent of Hillenmeyer records, the few exceptions being its heat and cold arms.
Aerobicity separates Vanacloig, which is anaerobic, from everything else. Duration is stored
in hours by two datasets and in generations by three, with no dataset varying it except
Hillenmeyer's generation arms. pH is stated only by Vanacloig, at 5.0, and by 2 percent of
Hillenmeyer homozygous records. Across the five, no stated pH or duration in hours appears in
more than one dataset.

The consequence for the representation is direct. A temperature channel, an aerobicity
channel or a pH channel would take a constant value inside each dataset and a different
constant in each, which makes it the dataset identity written a second way. The representation
in Section~\ref{sec:representation} therefore gives the physical fields no channel of their own
and lets the dataset token carry them. The fields stay in the record, and the day a served
dataset varies temperature within itself the channel is a one-line addition.

The same table shows the output disagreement. The response is a log ratio in Vanacloig and
Hillenmeyer HET, a z score in Hillenmeyer HOM and Wildenhain, and a sensitivity score in
Hoepfner; four datasets read it from a pooled barcode competition and Wildenhain from liquid
optical density. Section~\ref{sec:model} takes this up as the decoder question.
