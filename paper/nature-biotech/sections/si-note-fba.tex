\suppnote{note:fba}{The Yeast9 flux-balance baseline for trigenic interactions\secstatus{todo}}

The Yeast9 value in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d is a mechanistic baseline with no fitted parameter:
each deletion set of the trigenic screens is imposed on the yeast-GEM~9.0.2 metabolic
reconstruction, growth is computed by flux-balance analysis (FBA), and the interaction of
Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}a is formed from the predicted growth rates exactly as it is from the
measured colony sizes (\suppfig{fig:fba}a). The run behind the printed value was made
once, on 2025-09-15, and every number here is recomputed from its frozen output files and
the screens' raw tables; the rerun on the screen's own medium (2026-09-10) is reported at
the end of the note.

\notesec{Model and medium} The model is yeast-GEM 9.0.2 as distributed
(\texttt{yeast-GEM.xml}: 4,131 reactions, 2,806 metabolites, 1,161 genes, 271 exchange
reactions), loaded through \texttt{torchcell.metabolism.yeast\_GEM.YeastGEM}. The medium is
the model's default, \texttt{model.medium}, which the run never modified: 16 exchange
reactions open for uptake, glucose at 1~mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$ as the sole carbon source,
ammonium as the sole nitrogen source, oxygen and the inorganic ions unbounded, and no amino
acid, vitamin, or nucleobase (\supptab{tab:fba-medium}). The objective is the growth
pseudoreaction \texttt{r\_2111}, non-growth-associated maintenance is fixed at
0.7~mmol\,ATP\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$ (\texttt{r\_4046}), and the wild-type optimum is
$\mu_{\mathrm{WT}}=0.0858$~h$^{-1}$, which reloading the same SBML file reproduces to
$5\times10^{-8}$. Each deletion set was solved as one linear program with GLPK (cobrapy's
default solver, through optlang) under a 60~s limit, in 128 worker processes; the
4,036 singles, 651,181 doubles, and 332,313 triples took 10~min in all, every solve returned
\texttt{optimal}, and none hit the limit. The cobrapy version at run time was not recorded.

\notesec{Deletion simulation} A deletion set $p$ sets the state $s_g=0$ for each of its genes
and leaves every other gene at 1. cobrapy's \texttt{Gene.knock\_out} then evaluates each
reaction's gene-reaction rule, a Boolean expression in which isozymes are joined by OR and
complex subunits by AND, and sets the flux bounds of every reaction whose rule evaluates
false to zero; single, double, and triple deletions differ only in how many genes are set to
zero before the rules are evaluated. A gene absent from the model changes no rule, so its
deletion leaves growth at the wild-type value. The deletion sets are the 332,313 triples of
the dataset build, the trigenic records of Kuzmin~2018 (91,050) and Kuzmin~2020 (241,746;
483 in both), and all of their sub-collections (651,181 doubles, 4,036 singles). Yeast9
covers 736 of the 4,036 genes, 18,565 of the doubles, and 7,419 triples (2.2\%) in full;
220,392 triples (66.3\%) contain no model gene (\suppfig{fig:fba}e).

\notesec{From growth to interaction} Fitness is the growth ratio $f=\mu/\mu_{\mathrm{WT}}$,
with a non-optimal or timed-out solve counted as $f=0$. Digenic and trigenic interactions
are then the definitions of Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}a, $\varepsilon_{ij}=f_{ij}-f_if_j$ and
$\tau_{ijk}=f_{ijk}-f_if_jf_k-\varepsilon_{ij}f_k-\varepsilon_{ik}f_j-\varepsilon_{jk}f_i$,
computed from the predicted fitness of the triple and of its singles and doubles. The
predicted $\tau_{ijk}$ of each triple is compared by Pearson correlation with the measured
$\tau_{ijk}$ of the same gene set, taken from the screens' raw tables as the mean adjusted
score over the gene set's records (the dataset's deduplication rule); no split, threshold, or
fit enters.

\notesec{Result} Predicted against measured $\tau_{ijk}$ gives $r=0.0019$ ($n=332{,}313$,
$P=0.28$; Spearman $\rho=0.003$; \suppfig{fig:fba}b), and predicted against measured
triple-mutant fitness $r=0.151$ ($n=332{,}313$; 0.254 within the 111,921 triples that
contain a model gene; \suppfig{fig:fba}c). The value printed in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d, 0.0006,
was transcribed from the pipeline's output at the time and does not reproduce; whether it
came from a different subset or a different run is not recorded, and the scripted value is
the one to carry. The pipeline's own matched file cannot be used for either number: its
\texttt{experimental} column is the true label set in the wrong row order (the sorted stored
and raw values coincide for 99.98\% of triples, row by row they agree for 1.2\%), which left
the interaction correlation unchanged, since the prediction is nearly constant, but reported
the fitness correlation as $-0.005$. The interaction correlation is near zero because the
prediction is nearly constant: 97.6\% of the predicted $\tau_{ijk}$ lie within $10^{-6}$ of
zero and 99.93\% within $10^{-3}$, leaving 231 triples with a nonzero prediction, almost all
$+1$ (122), $+2$ (62), or $-1$ (19). The constancy has two measured sources. First, the
deletions rarely change the optimum: 97.7\% of single, 97.0\% of double, and 93.1\% of triple
deletions grow at the wild-type rate to within $10^{-3}$, and the rest fall almost entirely
into no growth (1.6\%, 2.2\%, 4.9\%) or one of a few discrete bands (predicted triple-mutant
fitness takes 1, 0, and values near 0.15, 0.37, and 0.98--0.99; \suppfig{fig:fba}c,d), so
$f_{ijk}$ almost always equals $f_if_jf_k$ exactly. Second, two-thirds of the triples contain
no model gene and so have $\tau_{ijk}=0$ by construction. The nonzero predictions are mostly
artifacts: 164 of the 231 arise in triples with only one gene in the model, where
$\tau_{ijk}$ must be zero, and tracing them shows 253 of 632,616 checkable doubles and 52 of
285,606 checkable triples whose growth contradicts their own single-deletion result (246 of
the doubles contain \texttt{YGR144W}, whose single deletion grows at the wild-type rate while
these doubles return zero growth with status \texttt{optimal}), together with 173 triples
that grow faster than the least of their three doubles, which FBA cannot produce. The cause
of these solver failures was not determined. Within the 7,419 fully covered triples, 48 have
a nonzero prediction, and the measured landscape there is as sparse as elsewhere: by the
screens' convention ($\lvert\tau\rvert>0.08$, $P<0.05$) 48 are negative and 33 positive
(1.1\%), against 1,404 and 407 among the partly covered and 3,742 and 1,240 among the
uncovered triples (2.1\% overall; \suppfig{fig:fba}f).

\notesec{Rerun on the screen's medium} The FBA above ran on the model's default ammonium
minimal medium, whereas the screen's final triple-mutant selection plates were, from the
Kuzmin~2018 Supplementary Materials, ``SD$_{\text{MSG}}$ - His/Arg/Lys/Ura +
canavanine/thialysine/G418/clonNAT (0.17\% yeast nitrogen base without amino acids and
ammonium sulfate, 0.1\% monosodium glutamic acid, 0.2\% amino acid supplement, 2\% agar, 2\%
glucose, 50 $\mu$g/mL of each analog \ldots)'', with ``The incubation temperature for all the
selection steps was 26$^{\circ}$C'' (the 2020 screen's medium was not checked). The same
pipeline was therefore rerun on that medium (\supptab{tab:fba-screen-medium};
\suppfig{fig:fba}g), with the same deletion sets, solver, and 60~s limit, and with two
controls: the model's default medium again, which reproduces the frozen wild-type growth
to $10^{-9}$, and a synthetic minimal medium with ammonium nitrogen and glucose opened to
3.3~mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$, which separates the change of carbon rate from the change
of nitrogen source and supplements. The screen medium is the ontology's record of the
selection recipe mapped onto 42 exchange reactions: glucose at 3.3, the YNB vitamins, and
the 17 amino acids and adenine of the supplement minus the His/Arg/Lys/Ura dropouts, each
supplement at 0.165~mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$ (the rich-medium rate of Suthers et
al.\ 2020), agar and the four selection agents excluded because a constraint-based model
cannot represent them. Monosodium glutamate, the nitrogen source, is bounded at the same
0.165 rather than opened: opened, the optimum is 9.8~h$^{-1}$ on 299~mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$\,h$^{-1}$
of glutamate, which makes glutamate the carbon source; at 0.165, which is also the
medium's MSG-to-glucose molar ratio scaled to the glucose uptake, the wild type grows at
$\mu_{\mathrm{WT}}=0.497$~h$^{-1}$ on glucose. Every one of the 987,530 solves returned
\texttt{optimal}, in 12~min on 96 processes.

The medium moves the growth rates without moving the interaction correlation. On the
screen medium 98.5\% of single, 98.0\% of double, and 94.3\% of triple deletions grow at
the wild-type rate (against 97.7, 97.0, and 93.1\% on the default medium), and no growth
falls from 4.9\% to 3.5\% of triples. Predicted against measured $\tau_{ijk}$ gives
$r=-0.0007$ ($n=332{,}313$, $P=0.69$; Spearman $\rho=0.026$), predicted against measured
triple-mutant fitness $r=0.173$ (0.289 within the 111,921 triples that contain a model
gene, from 0.151 and 0.254 on the default medium). The nonzero predictions change character
rather than number: 30 triples have $\lvert\tau_{ijk}\rvert>10^{-3}$, all 30 among the
7,419 fully covered triples, with values between $-0.39$ and $0.04$, and the internal
checks that fail on the default medium (doubles contradicting their own single-deletion
result, triples growing faster than their slowest double) pass with zero violations. On
the default medium the rerun reproduces the wild-type growth and the growth bands of the
frozen run but not its solver failures: 157 inconsistent doubles (146 containing
\texttt{YGR144W}) against 253, 127 triples above their slowest double against 173, and
182 nonzero $\tau_{ijk}$ against 231, so the $\pm 1$ and $+2$ values of
\suppfig{fig:fba}b are solver artifacts of that medium at glucose 1 and are not
reproducible solve to solve. The ammonium control at glucose 3.3 sits between the two
(93.3\% of triples at wild-type growth, 27 nonzero $\tau_{ijk}$, all in fully covered
triples, no violations, $r=-0.0005$), so most of the change in growth bands comes from the
carbon rate and the remainder from the supplements.

\notesec{Caveats} Two discrepancies are visible in the code rather than in the data. The
knowledge-graph records of these experiments carry the environment YEPD at 30$^{\circ}$C,
the filter of the query that built the dataset (\texttt{queries/001\_small\_build.cql}),
which does not match the selection medium quoted above and should be corrected at the
source. And the row misalignment of the matched file
(\texttt{match\_fba\_to\_experiments.py} pairs \texttt{dataset[i]} with
\texttt{label\_df.iloc[i]}) affects any other analysis built on that file. The prediction's
constancy, not the medium, sets the ceiling: with 93 to 94\% of triple deletions at
wild-type growth on every medium tried and 66\% of triples outside the model's gene set,
the medium correction removed the solver artifacts and raised the fitness correlation by
0.02 while leaving the interaction correlation at zero.
