\section{The random-graph control: biology or any target\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:control}

The control arm keeps everything of the biological arm at the same $\lambda$ except the
graphs: each of the nine is rewired before the model sees it, by double-edge swaps that
keep every gene's degree, five swaps per edge, seeded by the run seed
(\texttt{cgt\_s0\_r\_kl\_rand\_031}, \texttt{torchcell.graph.rewire}). The prior pulls
the heads toward the rewired graphs and the edge diagnostics are scored against them.
The arm ran first at $10^{-3}$, the 010 operating point, with three seeds, and in round
1b at the two $\lambda$ where the biological prior's gain is largest, $10^{-1}$ and 1,
with five seeds each (Delta jobs 22461957 to 22461967). The paired comparisons below use
seeds 1 to 3, the seeds the biological arms have.

\notesec{The heads learn the random graphs, less completely}
Edge recall at degree against the rewired graphs reaches 0.814 at $10^{-3}$ and 0.822 at
$10^{-1}$ and 1, with divergence 980, 902 and 904 (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}c, d;
Table~\ref{tab:diag}), against 0.906 to 0.915 and 337 to 298 for the biological graphs at
the same $\lambda$. A random graph of the same degree sequence is a target the attention
can and does move toward, but it does not get as close in 30 epochs at any $\lambda$,
and raising $\lambda$ a hundredfold closes none of the gap. Whether that is because the
biological graphs are more compatible with the point loss, or because the rewired graphs
are less clustered and so harder to represent with a low-rank attention, is not separated
by anything logged.

\notesec{At $\lambda = 10^{-3}$ the accuracy question is underpowered}
On the fixed-epoch reading the three random-graph seeds give 0.417, 0.433 and 0.410,
against 0.429, 0.438 and 0.430 for the biological graphs on the same seeds and 0.424,
0.425 and 0.425 with no penalty. The biological graphs are above the random ones on every
seed, by 0.012, 0.005 and 0.020 (mean $+0.012$, paired $t = 2.8$, $p = 0.11$); the random
ones straddle no penalty, with paired differences of $-0.008$, $+0.008$ and $-0.015$
(mean $-0.005$, $t = -0.7$, $p = 0.56$). At the max-over-epochs reading the three arms
are 0.451, 0.447 and 0.448 (biological minus random $p = 0.44$), and at minimum
validation loss 0.444, 0.444 and 0.440 ($p = 0.86$). The effect being tested at this
$\lambda$ is the 0.005 to 0.013 that the biological prior adds over no penalty at epoch
29, and three seeds of a control whose own seed spread is 0.012 cannot resolve it.

\notesec{At $\lambda = 10^{-1}$ and 1 the biology separates from the conditioning}
The five random-graph seeds give $0.430 \pm 0.006$ at $10^{-1}$ and $0.427 \pm 0.004$
at $\lambda = 1$ on the fixed-epoch reading, $0.437 \pm 0.003$ and $0.435 \pm 0.004$ at
the max over epochs, and $0.434 \pm 0.004$ and $0.430 \pm 0.006$ at minimum validation
loss (Table~\ref{tab:arms}). Paired on seeds 1 to 3, the biological graphs are above the
random ones on every seed under every reading (Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}f). At $10^{-1}$
the differences are $+0.019$, $+0.017$ and $+0.015$ at epoch 29 (mean $+0.017$,
$t = 14.5$, $p = 0.005$), $+0.025$, $+0.017$ and $+0.014$ at the max ($+0.018$,
$t = 5.6$, $p = 0.03$) and $+0.029$, $+0.014$ and $+0.014$ at minimum loss ($+0.019$,
$t = 3.9$, $p = 0.06$). At $\lambda = 1$ they are $+0.014$, $+0.018$ and $+0.020$ at
epoch 29 ($+0.017$, $t = 8.8$, $p = 0.013$), $+0.018$, $+0.015$ and $+0.012$ at the max
($+0.015$, $t = 8.3$, $p = 0.014$) and $+0.027$, $+0.006$ and $+0.003$ at minimum loss
($+0.012$, $t = 1.6$, $p = 0.26$). Against no penalty the random arms are not the
biological arms: at epoch 29 they sit at or just above it ($+0.003$ at $10^{-1}$,
$p = 0.52$; $+0.004$ at 1, up on every seed, $p = 0.16$), where the biological prior is
$+0.020$ and $+0.022$ above; at the max over epochs they are below it on every seed, by
$-0.012$ at $10^{-1}$ ($t = -6.1$, $p = 0.026$) and $-0.011$ at 1 ($t = -12.2$,
$p = 0.007$), with a minimum validation point loss of 0.813 and 0.816 against 0.801
(Table~\ref{tab:diag}). A strong prior toward degree-matched random graphs therefore
removes little of the late decline and lowers the peak; the fixed-epoch gain of the
biological prior is not reproduced by conditioning on any graph of the same degree
sequence. The gain over random at the peak reading, 0.015 to 0.018, is larger than the
biological arm's own gain over no penalty there (0.004 to 0.006), because most of it is
the random arm falling below no penalty rather than the biological arm rising above it.
What the biological graphs buy a best-checkpoint deployment remains at most 0.006; what
they buy a fixed-budget run is 0.02, and that 0.02 is theirs.
