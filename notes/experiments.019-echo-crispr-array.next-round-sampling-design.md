## 2026.07.26 - Which noise limits us, and how to spend the next round

Generated by `experiments/019-echo-crispr-array/scripts/next_round_design.py` (reads the
run-3 results; re-run it to regenerate every number below).

### The one number that matters: between-plate SD = 0.140

Decomposing a strain's normalised fitness into plate and colony terms, measured on run 3:

| term | value | shrinks with |
|---|---|---|
| `sigma_plate` -- between-plate, per strain | **0.140** | **more PLATES** |
| `sigma_colony` -- within-plate, across colonies | 0.167 | more colonies *and* plates |
| `sigma_strain` -- biological, across strains in a class | 0.081 | nothing (it is the signal's context) |

For a mean over `P` plates with `c` colonies each,

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar{y}) \;=\; \frac{\sigma_{\text{plate}}^{2}}{P} \;+\; \frac{\sigma_{\text{colony}}^{2}}{P\,c}$$

At the current `c = 27`, the plate term is **94.9 %** of the variance. Colonies only ever
divide the smaller term; plates divide both. **`SE ~ sigma_plate / sqrt(P)` -- plates are the
only real lever.**

| | c=8 | c=12 | c=20 | c=29 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P=2** | 0.107 | 0.105 | 0.102 | 0.101 |
| **P=3** | 0.088 | 0.085 | 0.084 | 0.083 |
| **P=4** | 0.076 | 0.074 | 0.072 | 0.072 |
| **P=6** | 0.062 | 0.060 | 0.059 | 0.058 |
| **P=8** | 0.054 | 0.052 | 0.051 | 0.051 |
| **P=12** | 0.044 | 0.043 | 0.042 | 0.041 |

**Consequence for the next round: adding the 14 doubles is nearly free.** Going from 12 to 26
strains drops replicates from ~27 to ~12 per plate, which moves the SE from 0.083 to 0.085 --
0.002. Well-replication is already saturated; spend the wells on more *strains*, and spend
effort on more *plates*.

### Capacity

`384 - 24 WT - 6 blanks - 40 cushion = 314` -> **12 replicates x 26 strains**. Keep WT on every
plate (it is the per-plate normaliser), keep the blanks (they are what caught the P3
registration slip), and keep the cushion for failed transfers and M/N/C invalidation -- run 3
lost 20-30 % of wells per strain that way (`n_used` 20-30 of 29).

### Two different claims need two different amounts of data

**(a) The ladder claim -- already well powered.** The central argument (fitness improves as you
climb WT -> single -> double -> triple) is an *ordered-trend* hypothesis, and the right test is
**Jonckheere-Terpstra**: one test over the whole chain, **no multiple-testing correction**, and
more powerful than a Bonferroni chain of pairwise t-tests. See
[[experiments.010-kuzmin-tmi.scripts.inference_dataset_3_jonckheere_terpstra_thresholds]] --
that is exactly the reframing that unlocked the candidate pool when pairwise thresholds were
starving it.

Critically, the JT observations are **strains within a class**, not colonies: colonies inside a
plate are pseudo-replicates (the plate term is 95 % of variance), so counting them as `n` would
badly overstate power. With 12 singles / 14 doubles, power at alpha = 0.05:

| per-step gap | P=3 | P=4 | P=6 | P=8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 66 % | 72 % | 77 % | 80 % |
| **0.10** | **99 %** | 99 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| 0.15 | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |

So **P = 3-4 plates already gives ~99 % power for a per-step gap of 0.10**. Beyond ~6 plates the
gain is small, because biological strain-to-strain spread (0.081) has overtaken measurement
error (0.060 at P=6) -- you stop being measurement-limited and become biology-limited.

**(b) The per-strain claim -- this is what is expensive.** Saying *"this particular double beats
its constituent singles"* needs the difference to clear ~`2.8 x SE`:

| plates | SE | min resolvable strain-vs-strain gap |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.085 | **0.24** |
| 4 | 0.074 | 0.21 |
| 6 | 0.060 | 0.17 |
| 8 | 0.052 | **0.15** |

Given observed single-mutant fitness spans 0.67-0.92, a 0.24 threshold only catches large
effects. **If the goal is to rank individual candidates for the next rung of the ladder, that is
where extra plates buy something real.**

### Design recommendation

- **26 strains x 12 replicates**, WT on every plate, 6 blanks, ~40 wells cushion.
- **Re-randomise the layout independently per plate** (as run 3 did) so plate/position bias
  averages out rather than aliasing onto a strain.
- **P = 4 plates** to defend the ladder claim comfortably; **P = 8** if the round must also rank
  individual doubles (0.15 resolution).
- Report the ladder with **JT**, not a corrected pairwise chain.
- The bootstrap SE at small `P` is biased low by `sqrt((P-1)/P)` (18 % at P = 3, 13 % at P = 4)
  -- see [[experiments.010-kuzmin-tmi.12_panel_crispr_fitness_assay]]; use the classical
  `s/sqrt(P)` or a corrected bootstrap when quoting it.

### The open lever: reduce `sigma_plate` itself

Everything above takes `sigma_plate = 0.140` as given. It is the dominant term, so anything that
shrinks it (same-batch agar, same incubator shelf/position, same imaging session, tighter
timing) is worth more than any amount of extra replication -- halving it would do what
quadrupling the plate count does.

## 2026.07.26 - Feasibility check: is there upward headroom at all?

The design above answers *how precisely* the next round can measure a ladder. It does not
answer *whether an upward ladder exists*. That headroom audit is now in
[[experiments.019-echo-crispr-array.scripts.ladder_feasibility]]
(`experiments/019-echo-crispr-array/scripts/ladder_feasibility.py`).

Verdict: **the upward version of the claim is not reachable with a deletion panel scored on
growth.** Zero of our 12 singles are above WT; only 2.7% of all 7,738 genome-wide deletion
strains are significantly above WT, and the genome-wide ceiling is 1.1118. Stacking
deletions makes it worse, not better -- the fraction above WT falls 23.0% -> 13.8% -> 6.9%
from singles to doubles to triples (Kuzmin 2018). Fourteen randomly chosen doubles yield
0.051 expected significant "rungs" (`P(zero) = 95%`).

So the power numbers above should be read against a **descending** ordered trend
(WT > single > double) or against an **epistasis** ordering, not against fitness gain.
