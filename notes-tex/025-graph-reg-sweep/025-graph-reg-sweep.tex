\title{\vspace{-1.5em}Soft Prior or Hard Mask\\
  {\large How the nine gene-gene graphs enter the cell graph transformer:
   the graph-regularization sweep on the 025 triples, read at three seeds per arm}}
\author{Michael Volk}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\thispagestyle{empty}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Nine gene-gene graphs enter layer 1 of the cell graph transformer by one of two
mechanisms: a soft prior, a divergence penalty of weight $\lambda$ that pulls each
head's attention toward its graph's normalized adjacency, or a hard mask that restricts
each head's attention to its graph's edges. The sweep runs one protocol (the 376{,}732
triples of experiment 010 on their pinned random split, a learnable gene table, AdamW at
a constant $2.5 \times 10^{-4}$, 30 epochs, four A40s per run) at $\lambda \in \{0,
10^{-5}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-3}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-1}, 1, 10, 100\}$, under the mask, and under
the prior toward degree-matched random rewirings of the same graphs, three
seeds per arm. Read at epoch 29, the mask sits with no penalty, $0.421 \pm 0.003$ against
$0.425 \pm 0.000$ held-out Pearson, and the prior rises with $\lambda$ to $0.447 \pm
0.001$ at $\lambda = 1$, above no penalty on every seed by 0.021 to 0.023, and holds
there for two more decades ($0.444 \pm 0.008$ at 10, $0.443 \pm 0.004$ at 100, above no
penalty on every seed) with no turnover in reach. Read at each run's best epoch, all
eleven arms lie within 0.010 of one
another (0.444 to 0.454): the prior moves the peak by at most 0.006 and moves when it
occurs, from epoch 15 or 16 without a penalty to epochs 23 to 26 at $\lambda = 1$, so most
of what it buys at the fixed reading is resistance to the decline the unpenalized model
shows after epoch 16. The attention contracts onto the graph at every $\lambda$ tested,
with edge recall at degree 0.78 at $10^{-5}$ and 0.915 from $10^{-2}$ up against 0.006
with no penalty, and saturates two decades below the $\lambda$ at which accuracy is
highest, so the contraction itself is not what carries the accuracy. The random-graph
control, a prior of the same weight toward degree-matched rewirings of the nine graphs,
sits at the no-penalty level at epoch 29 at every $\lambda$ tested ($0.420 \pm 0.012$ at
$10^{-3}$, three seeds; $0.430 \pm 0.006$ at $10^{-1}$ and $0.427 \pm 0.004$ at 1, five
seeds each, against 0.425), where the biological graphs give 0.432, 0.445 and 0.447;
paired by seed the biological graphs are above the random ones on every seed at
$10^{-1}$ and 1, by 0.017 at epoch 29 ($p = 0.005$ and $0.013$) and 0.015 to 0.018 at
the best epoch ($p = 0.03$ and $0.014$), and at the best epoch the random arms fall below
no penalty ($-0.011$ to $-0.012$, $p = 0.026$ and $0.007$). The hypothesis under test,
that the graphs help only when the model is optimized through the divergence and not when
they are imposed as a constraint, holds on the fixed-epoch reading, and the alternative
that any graph of the same degree sequence would do the same is rejected at the two
$\lambda$ where the gain is largest. The follow-up rounds (Section~\ref{sec:round2})
measure the rest: a two- or three-hop target or mask does not beat one hop; a symmetric
target or a directed mask moves neither mechanism toward the other; the prior on layers
3 to 4 equals the prior on layer 1 while every mask placement stays at no penalty; at 60
epochs the unpenalized model keeps declining (0.391 to 0.405 at epoch 59) and the prior
at $\lambda = 1$ holds (0.433 to 0.437); with the composite sequence embedding the model
does not train at all without the prior and trains slowly with it; and the width-360 arms
did not run, out of GPU memory on the A40.
\end{abstract}
