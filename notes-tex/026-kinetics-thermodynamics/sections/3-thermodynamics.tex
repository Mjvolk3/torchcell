\section{Thermodynamics recomputed from eQuilibrator\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:thermo}

\subsection{Why the shipped table is a dead end\secstatus{todo}}

yeast-GEM 9.0.2 ships \texttt{model\_metDeltaG.csv} and \texttt{model\_rxnDeltaG.csv}, and
they are usable: 2{,}389 of 2{,}806 metabolites and 3{,}210 of 4{,}131 reactions carry a
value. They also close two doors. They are point values with no covariance, so the
correlated error term $\Delta_r G^{\circ,\mathrm{error}} = Qm$ that a thermodynamic flux
formulation calls for cannot be formed from them at all. And they exist only for this
model, so a table built on them transfers to no other organism.

Component contribution answers both at once: it decomposes a compound into groups, fits
group energies against the measured reaction corpus, and returns a formation energy with
the covariance implied by that fit, from a structure alone.

The shipped files also use \emph{two} missing-value conventions: most gaps are the sentinel
$10^7$, but a further 51 metabolites and 120 reactions are the literal string \texttt{NaN}.
Filtering only the sentinel leaves the NaNs to propagate through every sum, producing NaN
gradients from what looks like a coverage of 87 percent.

\subsection{What the pipeline actually does, stage by stage\secstatus{todo}}

Each metabolite is resolved by accession, MetaNetX first, then ChEBI, then KEGG, then BiGG,
with an InChI structure lookup as a fallback (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-pipeline}a).
A metabolite becomes a formation energy in four stages, each of which loses some. An
identifier has to resolve to an entry in eQuilibrator's compound cache; that entry has to
carry a group decomposition; the fit returns an untransformed $\Delta_f G^\circ$; and a
Legendre transform to (pH, ionic strength, temperature, pMg) turns it into the
$\Delta_f G'^\circ$ the layer consumes. Reaction energies are then
$\sum_i S_{ij} \Delta_f G'^\circ_i$, so a reaction survives only if \emph{every}
participant does, which is what makes reaction coverage the binding number and a
metabolite-level figure an overstatement.

\subsection{The largest coverage gap was the proton, and it was our bug\secstatus{todo}}

The recomputation covered 1{,}755 reactions, 42.5 percent, against the shipped table's 84.9,
and that shortfall was read as the price of an organism-independent method. Ranking
metabolites by how many otherwise-priceable reactions each blocks says otherwise: the top
twelve were all \textbf{H$^+$}, in twelve compartments, together blocking 1{,}477 reaction
slots.

\texttt{standard\_dg\_formation} returns nothing for the proton, and that is correct rather
than a failure. A bare proton has no group decomposition, and its chemical potential is
what the Legendre transform to a fixed pH is defined \emph{against}: the proton is the
reference of the transform, not a compound to be priced by it.

\textbf{The convention is that the transformed proton contributes zero, and it was verified
rather than assumed.} On four proton-carrying, mass-balanced reactions, alcohol, lactate and
malate dehydrogenase and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, the sum
$\sum_i S_{ij}\Delta_f G'^\circ_i$ with the proton term set to zero reproduces
eQuilibrator's own \texttt{standard\_dg\_prime} to \textbf{0.0000\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$}. The
whole-table check still passes afterwards: over 40 single-compartment balanced reactions the
two routes agree to $6.8\times10^{-13}$\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.

\textbf{Reaction coverage goes from 1{,}755 to 2{,}741, a gain of 986 reactions and 23.9
percentage points, from one line.} The union with the shipped table does not move at all,
staying at 3{,}716: every one of those 986 reactions was already covered by the GEM. What
changed is how much of the coverage two independent methods confirm, which is what makes a
disagreement between them detectable.

\textbf{One caveat, and it is a wrong number rather than a missing one.} Fixing the proton
at zero is exact within a single compartment, which is what the validation tested, and not
for a proton moved \emph{between} compartments at different pH, where the translocation term
is now zero by construction. \textbf{183 reactions
move a proton across a membrane and 170 of them are now priced}, each missing that term.
Before the fix they were unpriced; now they carry a value that is confidently wrong. That
term is supplied separately, and
Section~\ref{sec:transport-term} is where it is computed and checked.

and riboflavin, chemicals the shipped table simply lacks. The 967 it loses are the real

\subsection{Where the shipped values came from, and why they disagree\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:shipped-provenance}

Yeast9 almost certainly used a group-contribution method too, and the disagreement is still
expected, for three reasons read out of the upstream artifacts rather than inferred from the
values. \textbf{The shipped column is in kcal per mole and is labeled kilojoules; its
metabolite half was flattened across compartments; and its estimator is a different
group-contribution method fitted to a different corpus.} The first two are bookkeeping
errors in yeast-GEM. The third is the genuine method difference and is the smaller
effect.

Two names recur below. \textbf{yETFL} is the expression-and-thermodynamics model of yeast
(Oftadeh et al.\ 2021), which supplies almost every shipped value. \textbf{matTFA} is the
MATLAB thermodynamic-flux toolbox that produced yETFL's thermodynamic fields, and its
conventions are what the shipped columns inherit.

yeast-GEM's own \texttt{code/modelCuration/v8\_7\_0.m} (PR \#330) assigns both columns.

\subsubsection*{The shipped column is kcal per mole\secstatus{todo}}

yETFL's model carries the field \texttt{thermo\_units = 'kcal/mol'}, and
\texttt{v8\_7\_0.m} copies its values across with a \texttt{round(x,2)} and no conversion.
The yeast9 paper reports the resulting columns in kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.

This is measured, not read off a label. Regressing our transformed formation energies, which
are in kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, on yETFL's over the 1{,}927 metabolites both price gives a slope
through the origin of \textbf{4.187} at Pearson $r = 0.987$, against the thermochemical
4.184\,kJ\,kcal$^{-1}$. Individual compounds settle it beyond argument: yETFL prices
cytosolic water at $-37.21$ and ATP at $-564.41$ against Alberty's tabulated $-157.6$ and
$-2292$\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$. Read as kcal those are $-155.7$ and $-2361$, within 1 and 3
percent; read as kJ they would be wrong by a factor of four. The 69\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ still
separating yETFL's ATP from Alberty's is a single-compound estimation difference, and it is
the size of difference the single-compartment cloud in
Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-vs-shipped}b is made of. The reaction column inherits the unit,
being yETFL's reaction field verbatim: on the 3{,}177 reactions in both, the largest
difference is $5.0\times10^{-3}$, which is exactly what \texttt{round(x,2)} leaves. The 33
reactions that are \emph{not} yETFL's are the dG-predictor block, which is in kilojoules, so
the shipped reaction column mixes two unit systems.

\subsubsection*{The metabolite column was flattened across compartments\secstatus{todo}}

matTFA transforms at each compartment's own pH and ionic strength, so yETFL holds a
different value per compartment, while \texttt{v8\_7\_0.m} matches on the
compartment-stripped name, so every copy receives whichever compartment survived the
de-duplication.

Half of the shipped metabolite column is therefore the value for a different compartment:
\textbf{1{,}162 of 2{,}324} entries match their own compartment's yETFL value and the rest do
not, with a 75th-percentile error of \textbf{86.7} and a mean of \textbf{74.5}\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.
Those are large because the transform's pH dependence scales with a compound's proton count:
ATP carries about twelve protons at pH 7, so moving it two pH units moves its transformed
formation energy by more than 130\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.

\subsection{The uncertainty, which is the point\secstatus{todo}}

Of 1{,}673 estimable reactions, 888 have a standard error of exactly zero and the remaining
785 carry a median of 2.30 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ and a 95th percentile of 6.01
(Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-uncertainty}).

Estimability is a separate question from uncertainty and is easy to conflate. Component
contribution returns directions with finite variance and directions with infinite variance,
the second being what the training corpus never determined. Almost every compound has a
nonzero infinite component, since an absolute formation energy is defined only up to
element conservation, and those directions cancel in a mass-balanced reaction, so the test
applies to a reaction and never to a compound in isolation. A reaction whose infinite
component does not cancel is masked rather than given a finite error bar.

\subsection{Agreement with the shipped table\secstatus{todo}}

Drawn as published, the disagreement is a cross (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-vs-shipped}a).
Correcting the unit and rebuilding our table at yETFL's own conditions separates the two
arms.

\textbf{The transport arm closes.} On the 752 transport reactions the shipped column prices
above 0.1, the median absolute difference falls from 17.4 to \textbf{5.6}\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$
and Pearson $r$ rises from 0.30 to \textbf{0.75}, and over all 1{,}023 it is 0.57. That arm
was two builds holding different compartment conditions rather than a disagreement about
thermodynamics.

\textbf{The single-compartment cloud does not close, and it should not.} Its median
absolute difference moves from 16.25 to \textbf{17.60}\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ and Pearson $r$ from
0.537 to 0.513. That residual is the genuine estimator difference, and its size is
unsurprising: yETFL's value for glucose-6-phosphate is about 39\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ from
Alberty's, and errors of that size on individual compounds cancel within a reaction only
when the compounds share groups. Only the recomputation carries an uncertainty that shows
this without the archaeology.

\textbf{No number in that table traces to a measurement in the library, and the literature

sources for any of them are two modeling papers that tabulate them as parameters rather than

reactions (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-diag}b). \textbf{A shift of that size is what the Legendre
transform must produce, and it is not evidence against the method}, by the proton-count
dependence that flattens the shipped column above. 

\subsection{Why the table is built per metabolite, and what panel c checks\secstatus{todo}}

The table stores per-metabolite formation energies and forms reaction energies as
$\sum_i S_{ij}\Delta_f G'^\circ_i$, rather than asking eQuilibrator for each reaction
directly. The reason is the flux layer, and it is a real one: in its anchored mode the
reaction potential is
$\Delta_r G_j = \Delta_r G^{\circ\prime}_j + RT\sum_i S_{ij}\ln c_i + \Delta_r G^t_j +
(Qm)_j$, with $\ln c$ a learned per-metabolite vector squashed into the physiological
window, and in its free mode it is $\sum_i S_{ij}\mu_i$ with $\mu$ unconstrained. Both need
a quantity indexed by metabolite. A reaction-level $\Delta_r G^{\circ\prime}$ cannot be
decomposed back into one, and the square-root covariance factor $\Sigma$ that gives $Q$ is
per metabolite as well.

\textbf{Nothing about the transform itself is reimplemented.} The transform is
\texttt{Compound.transform}, eQuilibrator's own, and the formation energy is
\texttt{ComponentContribution.standard\_dg\_formation}. What is ours is the assembly, and each part of it can be wrong in a way that produces a
reasonable-looking table, so panel c checks it against eQuilibrator's own reaction-level
call (Figure~\ref{fig:thermo-diag}c).

As an absolute configuration check, four textbook reactions price at pH 7.2, ionic strength
0.25 M and 303.15 K as:
ATP hydrolysis $-28.93 \pm 0.30$, phosphoglucose isomerase $+2.66 \pm 0.38$, fumarase
$-3.43 \pm 0.28$ and triosephosphate isomerase $+5.61 \pm 0.55$ kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.

\subsection{The transport term, computed rather than recovered\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:transport-term}

Because the same chemical appears on both sides,
$\sum_i S_{ij}\Delta_f G'^\circ_i = 0$ over a transport reaction priced at one pH, so its
entire standard energy is its transport term and a metabolite-column sum asserts that moving
a proton up a two-unit pH gradient is free. Thermo-Flux gives that term as

\begin{equation}
  \Delta_r G^t_j = -N_H\,RT\ln(10)\,\Delta\mathrm{pH} - Fq\,\Delta\Phi ,
  \label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}

with $N_H$ the protons moved across the membrane and $q$ the charge moved.
\texttt{torchcell/metabolism/transport.py} evaluates it per reaction from the stoichiometry,
the compartment of each metabolite and its charge.

\textbf{The paper does not fix which side each difference is taken from, so the module fixes
it and derives the signs.} A species moves out of the compartment it appears in as a
reactant and in to the one it appears in as a product; the proton potential
$\mu^\circ - RT\ln(10)\,\mathrm{pH}_X$ and the electrical work
$+qF(\Phi_\mathrm{in} - \Phi_\mathrm{out})$ then give
$\Delta\mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{pH}_\mathrm{in} - \mathrm{pH}_\mathrm{out}$ and
$\Delta\Phi = \Phi_\mathrm{out} - \Phi_\mathrm{in}$. This is the sign matTFA implements, and
choosing it the other way would invert every proton pump in the model without producing any
other symptom.

Identifying which species move is the part that needs care. A chemical is matched by name,
because yeast-GEM gives the same chemical a different \texttt{s\_XXXX} identifier in every
compartment, and copies in the same compartment on both sides are canceled before the
remainder is paired across compartments. Mitochondrial ATP
synthase is the worked case: yeast-GEM writes it as three cytosolic protons consumed against
two matrix protons produced, so one proton is chemistry and $N_H = 2$ translocate.

$N_H$ counts the explicit H$^+$ species yeast-GEM carries per compartment and nothing else.
Thermo-Flux's $N_H$ also absorbs the protons released or bound when a reactant's average
protonation differs between two compartments at different pH, which needs a species
distribution for every transported compound. That part is not computed here and is the
next term to add.

\textbf{Against yeast-GEM's own curated column the computed terms track but do not
reproduce it.} Of 1{,}468 reactions crossing a compartment boundary, 326 receive a nonzero
term, 183 move a proton and 482 move charge. On the 287 reactions carrying a nonzero value
in both, with the shipped column converted to kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, the sign agrees on 84.7
percent, Pearson $r = 0.804$, and the median absolute difference is 7.04\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$
against a median magnitude of 12.0. Rebuilding under yETFL's own compartment conditions
rather than the pipeline's tightens it slightly, to $r = 0.813$ and a median difference of
6.08 over 299 reactions, which says the residual is mostly method rather than conditions.

\textbf{Where they disagree is informative.} 613 reactions carry a nonzero shipped value and
a zero computed one, which is the protonation term left out above. Eleven
go the other way, computed nonzero against a shipped zero, and those are reactions yETFL
declined to price at all rather than priced as zero.

\texttt{transport\_delta\_g\_source} takes \texttt{off}, the default and what every arm here
ran; \texttt{shipped}, which recovers the term from the GEM's reaction column where the
metabolite-sum energy is structurally zero, converted out of kcal at parse time by
\texttt{\_read\_delta\_g\_csv}; and \texttt{computed}, which reads this module's table and is
the only route that carries to a model shipping no thermodynamics of its own.
