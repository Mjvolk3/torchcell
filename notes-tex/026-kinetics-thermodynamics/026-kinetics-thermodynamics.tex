\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The enzyme-constrained flux layer needs three parameter tables and had almost none of
them. Measured $k_{cat}$ covered 148 of 3{,}728 catalytic units, 4.0 percent, with the
other 96 percent running on a single flat default of 10.3 s$^{-1}$; $K_M$ had no table at
all; and the thermodynamics came from a shipped file of point values with no covariance,
usable only for this organism. All three are now built, and every value carries the
\texttt{sha256} of the weights or the model version that produced it.

Six kinetic predictors were mirrored into a standard location with per-file hashes,
retrieval commands and git commits, and run over the enzyme-substrate pairs of yeast-GEM
9.0.2, with protein sequences taken from the genome object rather than from the table the
model bundles. Validating each against its own authors' published examples paid for itself:
\textbf{two of these models exponentiate base 2 and three base 10}, and reading either
wrong yields turnover numbers that still look credible. They agree on the median $k_{cat}$
and disagree per pair, at a rank correlation of \textbf{0.015 to 0.509} on identical pairs.
Since almost none of these pairs has a measured value, that spread, and not any published
accuracy figure, is the honest uncertainty on a single predicted turnover number.

Formation energies were recomputed from eQuilibrator's component contribution, which
returns a covariance and applies to any organism because it works from structure. Summing
the resulting table reproduces eQuilibrator's own reaction call to
$6.8\times10^{-13}$ kJ\,mol$^{-1}$. \textbf{The file it is compared against turns out to be
in kilocalories and labeled kilojoules}: yeast-GEM's curation script copies yETFL's values
across with no conversion, and regressing our table on yETFL's over 1{,}927 shared
metabolites gives a slope of 4.187 at $r = 0.987$. Correcting that and matching the
compartment conditions closes the transport arm of the disagreement, median 17.4 to
5.6 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, and leaves the single-compartment cloud where it was, 16.3 to 17.6.
Only the second is a disagreement about thermodynamics.

\textbf{The largest single coverage change in this work was a bug of ours.} The proton is
the reference of the Legendre transform rather than a compound to be priced, and
eQuilibrator correctly declines to give it a formation energy; left as a missing value it
removed every reaction that moves a proton. Pricing it at zero, verified against
eQuilibrator's own reaction call to 0.0000 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, takes reaction coverage from
42.5 to \textbf{66.4 percent} and leaves the union with the shipped table unchanged at
90.0. The translocation term those 170 proton-moving reactions then omit is computed
separately and checked against yeast-GEM's own column. Writing structures for the lipids no
database indexes prices 347 of the 665 metabolites the recomputation could not reach,
taking it to 86.3 percent of reactions and past the shipped column, on one method with one
uncertainty.
\end{abstract}
