\label{sec:summary}

\begin{description}
  \item[The best method is two models averaged, and the best single model is a tie.]
    Under the fold protocol on the released 195 genes the equal-weight average of graph
    convolution on GO with the transformer reading the solve scores 0.954 with the
    cached solve head and 0.953 with the layer-free solve, the maximum of eleven post
    hoc averages. Alone, the transformer with the layer-free solve scores 0.920 and
    graph convolution on GO 0.917, with overlapping intervals, against Flux Cone
    Learning's 0.742 and FBA's 0.640 on the same genes
    (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}\textbf{a}). On ten other held-out fifths of the labeled
    genes, with the average fixed in advance, the average beats the better single model
    on nine (mean $+0.023$) and graph convolution on GO beats the transformer on nine
    (Section~\ref{sec:round75}).
  \item[The module form that helps is the one with no layer.] On one backbone the cell
    with no metabolism reads 0.860, FBA's growth ratios on the logit 0.890, and the
    deletion's solved flux vector as $\log(1 + |v|)$ on the logit 0.908 on six seeds
    (sd 0.008, minimum 0.896). Every form that keeps a linear program in the training
    loop reads lower and less reliably: the cached solve with a flux head 0.882 with one
    seed at 0.772, learned ceilings with a flux head 0.845 with two seeds unfit, the
    gated growth column 0.867, and the forced growth column 0.699 on twelve seeds. The
    layer-free form costs 30 minutes per cell with three cells on a card against 83 to
    100 for the forms that solve in the loop
    (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}\textbf{b} and \textbf{c}). On the ten other held-out
    fifths the vector's gain over no metabolism holds on ten of ten ($+0.047$) and its
    gain over the four growth ratios alone does not separate from zero ($+0.011$, sd
    0.023, seven of ten; Section~\ref{sec:round75}).
  \item[The layer-free form scales, and it covers only the genes with a reaction.] Over
    the 13.5 million records of the solid-growth build the transform is 469,474
    programs, solved once in 71 minutes on 20 cores and read as a gather in training
    (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}\textbf{e}). Off the metabolic genes every deletion reads
    one constant row, and the transformer with the layer-free solve scores 0.570 on
    the 4,674 genome-wide genes where graph convolution on ProtT5 scores 0.755 and the
    raw degree in the physical graph 0.740 (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}\textbf{d}). A
    model over the whole database therefore needs both inputs: the solve store for the
    genes with a GEM reaction, and the gene graphs as a message-passing input for the
    rest.
  \item[The two families miss different genes, and the annotations say which.] On the
    released 195 the essential genes the transformer ranks low are the ones FBA calls
    viable: the 19 of 31 with a knockout growth ratio of 1.0 have a mean misrank of
    0.134 against 0.025 for the rest (effect 0.91, $q$ 0.003), and under graph
    convolution on GO the same feature has no effect (0.51). No biological-process term
    reaches $q < 0.1$ among the 31 essential genes under either model. The nonessential
    genes both models rank high sit in steroid biosynthesis (effect 0.85 for the
    transformer and 0.96 for the graph model) and, for the transformer, in nucleoside
    triphosphate metabolism (0.88) and monosaccharide biosynthesis (0.83). On the
    genome-wide set the graph model ranks essential genes low when their degree in the
    physical graph is under the class median (effect 0.70) and when they act in
    cytoskeleton organization (0.79) or a microtubule-based process (0.81), and ranks
    nonessential genes high in ncRNA metabolism (0.90), rRNA processing (0.92), RNA
    modification (0.92) and cytoplasmic translation (0.85)
    (Figure~\ref{fig:summary}\textbf{f} and \textbf{g},
    Table~\ref{tab:misclass-enrichment}). Hypothesis, untested: the graph model scores
    a gene by its neighborhood, and the nonessential members of the ribosome biogenesis
    and translation machinery sit in neighborhoods made of essential genes.
\end{description}

\subsection{Round 75: the finalists on ten repeated outer splits\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:round75}

The released held-out set has 31 essential genes, the finalists' intervals on it overlap,
and the average's 0.954 is the maximum of eleven averages chosen on those genes. Round 75
(jobs 2547 to 2567; Table~\ref{tab:resplit}) reads the same models on ten other held-out
sets, each a class-stratified fifth of the released labeled genes with validation a
stratified fifth of the rest (\texttt{build\_essentiality\_splits.py --resplit}), three
seeds per model per split. The models, the equal-weight average and the statistics were
committed before any cell finished (\texttt{resplit\_readout.py}).

\input{tables/resplit_readout}

The average is the best method and the margin is its own, not a selection. It reads 0.896
as the mean of ten splits against 0.869 for graph convolution on GO and 0.848 for the
transformer with the layer-free solve; it is above the transformer on ten of ten
($+0.048$, sd 0.017), above the graph model on nine ($+0.026$, sd 0.021), and above the
better of the two on nine ($+0.023$, sd 0.017). The two single models are not the tie the
released genes showed: graph convolution on GO is above the transformer on nine of ten
($-0.022$, sd 0.024 for the transformer minus the graph model).

Every model reads lower on these fifths than on the released held-out genes, the
transformer's cells 0.843 against 0.908 and the graph model's 0.868 against 0.905 as seed
means, so the
released held-out set ranks more easily than a random fifth of the labeled genes. What
makes it so is not measured.

The module's contribution splits in two on the same backbone. The solve as an input is
worth $+0.047$ over no metabolism, on ten of ten splits (sd 0.034). Within that, the
deletion's flux vector over FBA's four growth ratios alone is $+0.011$ (sd 0.023, seven
of ten), where the released split read $+0.018$ (0.908 against 0.890): the sign agrees
and the difference does not separate from zero at ten splits of three seeds. So what the
ten splits support is that the solve helps and that the ratios carry most of it.

\subsection{What is left to run\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:left-to-run}

Four experiments would change a conclusion above, in the order of what they decide per
hour of compute. The first is run (Section~\ref{sec:round75}); the second and third are
submitted as rounds 77 and 76 and unread.

\begin{description}
  \item[Repeated outer splits for the three finalists.] The released held-out set has 31
    essential genes, the finalists' intervals overlap, and 0.954 is the maximum of
    eleven averages chosen on those genes. Ten stratified re-splits of the 1,121 labeled
    genes at the released proportions, with the layer-free cell, graph convolution on GO
    and their equal-weight average fixed in advance, three seeds each, give a paired
    difference per split. The read: the average minus the better single model, its sign
    on ten splits and its mean. About six card-hours.
  \item[A rule-aware input for the essential genes the solve calls viable.] The
    solve's vector cannot mark the 19 essential genes with a growth ratio of 1.0, four
    of them covered by an alternative gene set on every reaction and fifteen holding a
    reaction of their own that the program does not need
    (Section~\ref{sec:hard-genes}). Hypothesis, untested: the number of alternative gene
    sets in the gene-protein-reaction rule of each reaction a gene catalyzes, and
    whether every one of its reactions has an alternative, as affine columns beside the
    vector, lowers their misrank. The read: the mean misrank of those 19 genes against
    0.134, and the six-seed AUROC against 0.908.
  \item[The gene graph as an input to the transformer that reads the solve.] Degree
    and clustering in the physical graph as affine columns is the cheapest form, and a
    graph-convolved ProtT5 embedding on the node the next. The read: the genome-wide
    AUROC against 0.570 and 0.755, with the released 195 held at 0.908.
  \item[The store on a second phenotype.] The transformer trained over the solid-growth
    build with the store as an input is the direct test of panel \textbf{e}, and its
    number is not yet read.
\end{description}

The construction in which the binding ceilings differ per genotype
(Section~\ref{sec:open}) stays a design question and is not on this list.
