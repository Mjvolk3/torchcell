\title{\vspace{-1.5em}A Positive Trigenic Panel\\
  {\large What the last panel taught, whether the model can pick positives at
   all, and a 20-strain build that tests it}}
\author{Michael Volk}
\date{\today}

\maketitle
\thispagestyle{empty}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The recommendation is the 20-strain panel of Section~\ref{sec:panel20}, on five
genes at the pyruvate node, and the reasons for preferring it to the 10-gene
design worked through first are themselves the substance of this document.
Sections~\ref{sec:lessons} to~\ref{sec:strong} build that first panel and
Section~\ref{sec:screens} withdraws it. Read the last two sections for what to
build and Section~\ref{sec:panel20}'s diagnostic for how far a predicted $\tau$
may be trusted, which is less far than the earlier sections claim.

The previous 010 panel was selected from predictions that were invalid, by an
objective with no diversity term, on genes some of which had no training
evidence. The first replacement is drawn from \texttt{inference\_1}, the one
run that survived both index defects, and it is designed against each of those
three failures rather than around them.

Positive interactions are worth picking, which was not previously established.
Kuzmin 2018 defines a one-sided negative call, so the earlier retrieval
measurement covered negatives only. Under the symmetric Kuzmin 2020 call,
$\tau > +0.08$ with $p < 0.05$, the ensemble of three checkpoints reaches
precision $0.100$ at $K = 100$ against a base rate of $0.526$ percent, an
enrichment of $19.0$ times, where the additive null reaches $0.030$. Average
precision is $0.0352$ against the null's $0.0110$. At $\tau > +0.20$ the ratio
widens to $0.111$ against $0.0109$. Over the whole test split a regression of
actual on predicted has slope $0.877$, and of the 18 test records predicted above
$+0.20$ the mean actual $\tau$ is $+0.312$. Two thirds of those exceed
$\tau > +0.08$ in magnitude, but only 2 of the 18 also clear $p < 0.05$, so the
enrichment is real and the phrase ``real call'' has to be reserved for the
conjunction Kuzmin defines.

Confidence no longer tracks absence of evidence. On the previous panel predicted
interaction rose with the number of genes having no trigenic training record.
Here the correlation runs the other way, Spearman $+0.267$ between prediction and
the least-supported gene's record count.

The design is two gene-disjoint complete five-gene blocks: 10 genes, 20 triples,
20 doubles, and every gene in exactly 6 of the 20. Balance is a property of the
shape rather than a penalty term, and a complete block is the cheapest closed
design because $\tau$ needs all three doubles and all three singles of a triple
measured beside it.

One finding constrains what can be claimed. Across 6{,}934{,}927 complete
five-gene blocks, the model's positive confidence sits in a single clique: the
best block away from it has a median predicted $\tau$ of $0.029$, under half the
training-label standard deviation of $0.063$, and within the leading block the
six triples containing \texttt{YER059W} score $+0.590$ to $+0.662$ while the four
without it score $+0.069$ to $+0.127$. The panel is therefore built as a
contrast rather than as 20 equivalent wagers, and the contrast is what makes it
informative either way.

A second panel is specified against an industrial question rather than a
model-diagnostic one: flux-rechanneling deletions cost growth, and a third
deletion that restores it is a positive trigenic interaction worth building.
Within 20 constructed strains at most 6 interactions can be closed, on 5 genes
with 9 of the 10 doubles built, and that bound is tight. Taking the engineering
axis to be the roster genes yeast-GEM assigns to the four central-carbon areas a
15{,}000-article strain-design survey names, the chosen panel is the pyruvate
node, \gene{PDC1} with \gene{LAT1}, crossed against three regulators. All five
singles and all nine doubles are already published, so the measured chassis double
at $0.8593$ makes the predicted recoveries readable directly: $+0.246$ for
\gene{CUP9}, $+0.174$ for \gene{CAD1}, $+0.030$ for \gene{TOS8}. That ordering is
robust to any shrinkage of the $\tau$ scale, and it is a weak test of the model,
because the published lower rungs alone carry $82$ percent of the separation. Two
findings run against the panel. The model's strong predictions avoid the
engineering axis: two-axis triples are $1.11$ percent of the space, hold $0.23$
percent of the consensus tier at $+0.16$, and none at all above $+0.20$. And the
median objective cannot separate three designs that tie at the optimum, so the
panel's weakest member is $+0.020$ where an equally optimal panel reaches
$+0.081$.

Scoring all twenty panel strains with the same three checkpoints tests the model
before a strain is built, and the six triples reproduce the GPU run. The fourteen
known rungs go badly. A single gene's interaction is zero by definition, yet the
five singles average $+0.0714$, above the label standard deviation, and
\gene{LAT1} alone scores $+0.2441$. On the nine doubles the prediction exceeds the
measured $\varepsilon$ in all nine, a sign test at $p = 0.004$, and no prediction
is negative although six of the measured values are. Both orders are zero-shot,
since the 010 build is 376{,}732 records and every one is 3-gene, so none of this
measures the triples. It does make one null concrete: \gene{LAT1} alone outscores
five of the six triples the panel is built to test, so ``the model reports a
per-gene score summed over the perturbation set'' has to be stated as a hypothesis
and tested by the arm contrast.

A last check changes the panel's premise. The generator never removed
combinations the screen had already measured, and 4{,}877 of the 41{,}877{,}232
triples are in the 010 build. Ranking on prediction concentrates them: the top
100 is $429\times$ enriched over that base rate. The panel's highest-scoring
triple is one of them, in the \emph{training} split, with a measured $\tau$ of
$+0.2280$ against a worst-checkpoint prediction of $+0.2286$, so that entry is
recall and becomes a positive control rather than a test. The panel is then 15
known rungs and 5 unknown triples. All 15 triples of the top 500 that carry a
measured $\tau$ are over-predicted, measured being $0.23$ of predicted on average.
Those measured triples are worth more as a probe than the assumption they broke,
since they are the only ones in this space carrying both a prediction and a
measurement. Labeled by split, train reaches Pearson $+0.417$ over 3{,}947, which
matches the checkpoints' own validation and says the scoring path is sound, while
the 930 held out reach $+0.182$. Range restriction does not explain that gap, the
spread inside the space being $0.0499$ against $0.0633$. The tail cannot be
validated at all: the top 10{,}000 of the ranking holds 6 held-out measured
triples. Where they accumulate, at the top 100{,}000, 2 calls of 26 are real
positives against a $0.65$ percent base rate, an enrichment of $11.9\times$
against train's $21.5\times$ on 11 of 119. So the ordering generalizes at about
half the rate it recalls, on two events, and the magnitudes do not generalize at
all: the calibration slope is $0.35$ pooled and $0.24$ held out, and across a
predicted range spanning $-0.8$ to $+1.0$ the mean measured $\tau$ moves from
$-0.02$ to $+0.03$.

That last number contradicts the $0.877$ above, and the contradiction resolves
into the document's main caution. Both are correct on their own population. The
$0.877$ is a whole-split slope carried by the bulk near zero, and inside the head
of its own test split the model does not inflate at all, the slope running $1.59$
at the top 100. The head of \texttt{inference\_4} is somewhere else entirely: its
top ensemble mean is $+2.33$ where the largest value these checkpoints ever emit
on labeled data is $+0.63$. Calibration was established on a population that does
not contain the one a panel is drawn from. Use the ordering, distrust the
numbers.
\end{abstract}
