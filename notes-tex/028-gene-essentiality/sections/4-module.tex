\section{The metabolic module: three ways to read essentiality off it\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:module}

The pooled backbone reads its logit from an MLP on the perturbed context
(Figure~\ref{fig:readout-pooled}). A flux arm builds the enzyme-constrained thermodynamic
flux layer on the same context and exposes one physically named quantity, the flux through
the growth reaction \texttt{r\_2111}. Three readouts put that quantity to different uses,
and they differ in what the backbone's gradient has to pass through.

\begin{description}
  \item[biomass (forced)] $\ell = a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b$. The growth column is the
    only path from the context to the logit, so the module carries the whole prediction or
    the cell scores chance (Figure~\ref{fig:readout-biomass}). This is FBA's own reading of
    essentiality with a learned threshold, and it is the wave-9 design.
  \item[context + growth] $\ell = \mathrm{MLP}(h) + a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b$. Both paths
    from the first step; the per-module gradient norms say how much of each update the
    module carries when it is not forced to carry all of it.
  \item[gated] $\ell = \mathrm{MLP}(h) + g\,(a\,v_{\text{growth}} + b)$ with $g_0 = 0$
    (Figure~\ref{fig:readout-gated}). At initialization the cell is the pooled model
    exactly; the gate's trajectory, logged every epoch, says whether the optimizer chose
    the flux path, and the \texttt{flux\_layer} gradient line says whether gradient reached
    the layer, which at $g = 0$ it cannot.
\end{description}

\subsection{Round 2: the module on the stable backbone, with and without the penalties\secstatus{todo}}

Round 2 (jobs 2182 to 2208) puts the three readouts on the width-12 cell of
Section~\ref{sec:backbone}, on the released-label split, at three seeds each: the forced
and gated readouts at constraint scale 0.1 and 0, the context readout at both scales, the
forced readout at scale 0 with the $k_{cat}$ vector permuted across catalytic units (same
architecture, no unit-to-value correspondence), and the stoichiometry-only layer
(\texttt{flux\_off}: no thermodynamics, no capacity) with the forced and context readouts.
Every flux arm carries the wave-9 flux configuration otherwise: the predicted $k_{cat}$
tier, the union-external thermodynamic source, twenty warmup epochs, the dataset medium,
the mixture concentration space. The paired comparator is the round-1 pooled cell on the
same split at the same batch, weight decay and budget (Table~\ref{tab:w13},
Figure~\ref{fig:w13}).

The reads were fixed before the numbers: \emph{not hurt} is the gated arm against the
pooled cell paired by seed, with the gate's trajectory beside it; \emph{help} is the forced
arm above the pooled cell on three of three seeds and above its shuffled-$k_{cat}$ control;
the \emph{collapse's cause} is the forced arm at scale 0.1 against scale 0, since on the
betaxanthin targets the penalty scale was the dose that starved the layer's input.

come from the correspondence between units and constants. The forced arm's pooled
bootstrap, 0.773 [0.68, 0.86], is above Flux Cone Learning's 0.742 and FBA's 0.640 to

0.80 line.

The collapse on this endpoint is not the penalty's. Seed 2701 falls at both scales, to
0.585 at 0.1 and 0.653 at 0, and the two runs are the same run: the gradient norms start
where the other seeds' do, decay within ten epochs to a hundredth of them (preprocessor
0.002, transformer 0.003 at epoch 80, against 1.0 and 0.5 on seed 2700) and stay there,
with the training loss flat at 0.46 against 0.34, which is a stalled seed rather than a
starved layer. The
stoichiometry-only layer collapses on all three seeds (0.524, 0.631, 0.661), so the
capacity bound and the thermodynamic term are what keep the growth column informative
rather than what starve it, the opposite of the betaxanthin reading. Adding the context
head beside the growth affine without a gate costs 0.06 on the seed mean (0.666 and
0.667 against 0.725) and puts one seed at 0.56; the same sum behind the zero-initialized
gate loses nothing.

\subsection{The growth column is a signed free coordinate\secstatus{todo}}

Four rounds put the layer's growth column level with the pooled head and blind to its
own $k_{cat}$ table while one linear program per deletion lifts every model by 0.05. The
probe in Table~\ref{tab:growth-probe} reads the column itself. It rebuilds a forced cell
from its result file, deletes each of the 982 labeled genes through the same batches
training used, and reads $v_{\text{growth}}$ at initialization and under the trained
weights, scoring the negative column against the label (a deletion that stops growth is
essential) and the column against FBA's ratio.

\input{tables/growth_probe}

At initialization the column carries a weak signal in the right direction: 0.55 on the
held-out genes and a Spearman of $+0.11$ with the solve, nothing like FBA's 0.65, and no
deletion reaches zero growth. Training inverts it. Under the round-4 forced cell's weights
the negative column reads 0.23 on the held-out genes, which is that cell's 0.77 with the
sign of growth reversed: the affine learned a positive slope, an essential deletion gets
the \emph{higher} growth, and the column is anticorrelated with the solve ($-0.25$). The
round-2 forced cell does the same ($0.24$, $-0.26$). The median growth under the trained
weights is $-1.4$ and $-2.4$ mmol/gDW/h against a wild-type FVA range of 0.48 to 0.54 on
YPD: for most deletions the column is negative, which growth cannot be.

The column can do this because the arm's alternations end on the balance projection, so
$Sv = 0$ holds to machine precision and the box does not: 58 percent of reactions sit
outside their bounds at the selected epoch (0.64 on the round-2 cell, 0.59 on the round-6
cell), the growth reaction's floor of zero among them. $v_{\text{growth}}$ is then a
signed, unbounded coordinate the backbone writes through the abundance head, and the
readout uses it as a free latent. That is the mechanism behind the shuffled-$k_{cat}$
parity and behind the layer matching the head: the quantity the forced readout reads is
not growth.

\subsection{Where the gradient goes\secstatus{todo}}

Every cell logs a gradient norm per module each epoch, and read across the module arms
(Figure~\ref{fig:gradflow}) they say which of the three failure modes each arm is in. On
the forced arms the whole gradient passes through the layer and the layer passes it on:
the flux layer carries 10 to 40 percent of the summed module norm and the preprocessor's
norm climbs to 1 to 3, the same level as under the pooled head, so the backbone is
trained through the layer and the growth column is a trained, free coordinate that ranks
no better than the head. On the gated arms the layer carries 0.3 to 3 percent, the head
carries the rest, and the gate drifts to $-0.1$ under constraint scale 0 and to $+0.2$
with the FBA affine beside it, so the backbone is trained through the MLP and the flux
path is a small correction. On the warm-started forced arm with $\alpha$ frozen the
backbone receives no gradient at all: the preprocessor's norm sits at 0.02 to 0.1 for the
whole run, two orders below every other arm, because the flux is 99 percent a fixed
reference and only the two parameters of the growth affine train; the cell is the affine
on the projected reference, and the projection reversed its sign. The warm-started gated
arms leave the flux path at 0.3 percent of the gradient and push the gate to $-0.8$,
which is the head learning to subtract a sign-reversed growth column. None of the arms is
gradient-starved except the one that was meant to be a fixed function, so the module's
null on this endpoint is not a gradient-flow problem; the gradient reaches the layer, and
what the layer computes is not growth.
