\section{The next rounds: what finishes the figure, what decides the mechanism\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:round2}

The first five subsections are the design of the follow-up Delta rounds, written
before submission so the figures they feed are fixed in advance, as the first round's
mock-up was (Figure~\ref{fig:mockup}); nothing in them is measured. The rounds ran
between 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-10 and the last subsection reads them. Every arm is the same protocol as round 1,
the control config with the named keys changed, three seeds, on Delta
\texttt{gpuA40x4}, so every new column is read against the columns already in hand.
Table~\ref{tab:round2} is generated from
\file{experiments/025-solid-growth/scripts/graph_reg_round2_plan.py}, which is also
where the launcher will read the arm list from once a round is approved. The rounds are
ordered by the two questions the study asks. Round 1b completes
Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} and answers them where the effect is: is the prior better than
the mask (the top of the ladder), and do the graphs help (the random control at the
$\lambda$ that separates). Round 2 asks the question that follows at once from ``do the
graphs help'': how far along the graph should a head reach, two hops and three, for both
mechanisms on identical targets, with direction beside it. Round 3 holds what is
optional or orthogonal: where the graph block sits, the training budget, the
representation, the width. The random-graph arm of
round 1 ran the full 30-epoch protocol on all three seeds (30 logged epochs each), so
its comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:sweep}f is at matched budget.

\notesec{What round 1 left open, and the arm that closes each}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Biology or conditioning.} The random-graph control ran at $\lambda =
  10^{-3}$, where the biological prior adds 0.005 to 0.013 and three seeds do not
  resolve it (random against no penalty $-0.005 \pm 0.012$, $p = 0.56$). At $\lambda =
  1$ the biological prior adds 0.021 to 0.023 on every seed. Three
  seeds of the rewired graphs at $\lambda = 1$ decide whether that is the graphs or the
  penalty; three more at $10^{-1}$ cover the loss-selected optimum.
\item \emph{The top of the ladder.} Accuracy at epoch 29 rises to $\lambda = 1$ with no
  turnover inside the ladder, while contraction and divergence are flat from $10^{-2}$.
  $\lambda = 10$ and $100$ find the turnover, or show there is none in reach.
\item \emph{Direction.} The prior's target is directed, $A[\text{source}, \text{target}]$
  row-normalized; the mask builder symmetrizes it. So the
  mask-versus-prior gap confounds soft-versus-hard with directed-versus-undirected on
  the regulatory and TFLink heads. Two arms separate them: the prior at $\lambda = 1$
  toward symmetrized targets, and a directed mask (the transpose write removed, which
  leaves the seven symmetric graphs unchanged). If direction matters, the symmetric prior
  falls toward the mask and the directed mask rises toward the prior. A two-head variant,
  one head on $A$ and one on $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ for each directed graph, is the follow-up
  if the directed mask helps, not part of this round.
\item \emph{Reach.} A head under either mechanism sees one hop. The $k$-hop support is
  the indicator of $A \cup A^{2} \cup \cdots \cup A^{k}$; the mask restricts attention
  to it (self-loops kept) and the prior's target is its row normalization with the
  diagonal of the one-hop target (the cell graph gives every gene a self-loop, so every
  round-1 target already held a self entry in every row), so the two mechanisms are
  compared on identical two- and three-hop targets and differ from round 1 in reach
  alone. Table~\ref{tab:khop} is the density of that support, measured on
  the graphs the trainer builds. It settles one design choice: with edges made
  undirected, two hops of the physical, co-expression or experimental graph already
  reach 60 to 70 percent of all ordered gene pairs and three hops 75 to 94 percent, so a
  symmetric two-hop mask is close to no mask on those heads. The reach arms therefore
  use the \emph{directed} $k$-hop support for both mechanisms, which is what the prior
  has always used, and the directed one-hop mask is their one-hop anchor. Under the
  stored direction the regulatory and TFLink graphs stay sparse at three hops (1 and 3
  percent of pairs); the seven undirected graphs are symmetric by construction, so on
  those heads the two- and three-hop arms measure how a nearly uniform target behaves,
  which is a finding about the graph rather than a reason to skip the arm.
\item \emph{Placement (optional).} The encoder has eight layers and the graphs enter
  layer 1 only, an $[S, M, S, S, S, S, S, S]$ pattern. Round 3 widens the block and
  shifts it:
  layers 1 to 2 $[S, M, M, S, \ldots]$, layers 3 to 4 $[S, S, S, M, M, S, S, S]$, and
  layers 1 to 4 $[S, M, M, M, M, S, S, S]$, each for the mask and for the prior at
  $\lambda = 1$. The shifted block asks whether the graphs belong where genes are first
  mixed or after two free layers have formed a representation. The mask costs nothing in
  memory; each regularized layer of the prior materializes its attention, about 6 GB per
  layer, which is why the prior stops at four layers.
\item \emph{Budget.} No penalty peaks at epoch 16 and declines; $\lambda = 1$ peaks at
  23 to 26 and was still at its high at epoch 29. Sixty epochs of no penalty, the mask
  and $\lambda = 1$ show whether the strong prior keeps rising or only declines later,
  and whether the fixed-budget gap grows or closes. These need the 48 h clock.
\item \emph{Completion.} Seed 2 of $\lambda = 10^{-5}$ never reached W\&B (Delta job
  22055161); one run fills it. The third random-graph seed at $10^{-3}$ finishes on its
  own.
\item \emph{Representation.} Every arm here uses the learnable gene table, the
  representation that memorizes fastest, and the prior's gain is mostly resistance to
  the late decline. The composite sequence embedding (\texttt{embfit\_035} without its
  fitness head) resisted that decline on its own: on this split with the fitness head it
  reads $+0.016$ over the table at epoch 29 and nothing at the peak. Composite with and
  without the prior at $\lambda = 1$, three seeds each, says whether the graph result
  survives the representation the model will actually use.
\item \emph{Width.} The hidden width of 180 is inherited from 010 and was never swept
  on this task. Doubling it to 360 (heads of 40) takes the transformer from 3.1 M to
  about 12.5 M parameters and the model from 4.8 M to about 16.6 M; memory is set by the
  $9 \times 6{,}608^{2}$ attention of the regularized layer and does not move, the epoch
  should take 1.5 to 2.5 times as long, which is the 48 h clock. Table and composite,
  each with and without the prior, so width is read against the two representations and
  the prior at once; the composite preprocessor is re-matched to the table's parameter
  count at the new width.
\end{enumerate}

\notesec{Four code changes, shipped with the plan}
The $k$-hop support is one function shared by both mechanisms, \texttt{khop\_reach}: the boolean reachability
within $k$ steps, a dense $6{,}608^{2}$ matrix per graph computed once at construction
by repeated boolean products; the mask uses it as its support with every self-loop
kept, and the prior row-normalizes it as its target with the one-hop diagonal in place
of the reach's own (an even walk on an undirected graph returns home, which says
nothing about the graph). The first version cleared the diagonal instead; the
real-size smoke caught it, since that emptied 6,147 of the 6,607 regulatory rows that
the one-hop target constrains to self, and it was fixed before any $k$-hop job ran. The symmetric prior is one flag in the
adjacency normalizer: write $A[j, i]$ beside $A[i, j]$ before row normalization, so the
directed graphs get the treatment the mask gives them today. The directed mask is the
removal of one line, the transpose write in the mask builder, behind a flag that defaults
to today's behavior so no existing config changes meaning. The width arms need the
composite preprocessor re-matched to the table's parameter count at 360. Multi-layer
placement needs no code: the mask takes a list of layers and the prior's
\texttt{graph\_reg\_layer} already accepts a list. The flags default to the behavior
every earlier run had, and \file{tests/torchcell/models/test_cgt_graph_entry.py} pins
both the defaults and the new targets on a hand-built directed four-gene graph: a
directed one-hop prior, a symmetrized one-hop mask with self-loops, the two- and
three-hop reach, the stored self-loops surviving into the $k$-hop and symmetric
targets, and the identity of the mask's support and the prior's support off the
diagonal. Every arm's config and overrides in Table~\ref{tab:round2}
compose under Hydra to the intended keys (checked for all 26 distinct combinations),
and the plan script writes the Delta submitter,
\file{experiments/025-solid-growth/scripts/delta_submit_round2.sh}, one \texttt{sbatch}
per run in six chains with round 1b first in every chain. A chain is a stagger, not a
queue: Slurm's \texttt{after:<id>+30} releases a job 30 minutes after the previous one
starts, so that six 554 GB stage-ins never begin on one Lustre path at once, and a
later round waits on the scheduler like any other job once its predecessor is running.

\notesec{What is not in the rounds, and why}
The mask on all eight layers is left out: the placement question is where a graph
block belongs, not whether the model can survive having no free layer. The 010 checkpoints and the cosine-schedule runs stay out; their schedule and
normalizer differ, and the constant-rate protocol now has its own anchors. Seeds beyond
three are not added: at three seeds the epoch-29 reading already separates the arms that
differ by 0.01 or more, and the readings that do not separate (max over epochs, the
loss-selected epoch) are limited by epoch-to-epoch noise of 0.005 to 0.010 within a run,
which more seeds do not remove.

\notesec{What the rounds measured}
Sixty-five of the eighty runs finished; the fifteen that did not are the width-360
arms and the prior on layers 1 to 4, below. Every number is the epoch-29 reading
unless stated, mean $\pm$ sd over three seeds, with the paired difference against no
penalty on the same seeds (Tables~\ref{tab:arms}, \ref{tab:paired}, \ref{tab:runs}).

\emph{Reach.} Longer reach does not help either mechanism. The prior at $\lambda = 1$
reads $0.438 \pm 0.006$ toward the two-hop target and $0.429 \pm 0.012$ toward the
three-hop target against $0.447 \pm 0.001$ toward one hop; the paired gain over no
penalty shrinks from $+0.022$ to $+0.013$ ($p = 0.05$) and $+0.004$ (mixed by seed),
and at the best epoch both $k$-hop arms sit at 0.442, below no penalty's 0.448 on five
seeds of six. The directed mask reads $0.416 \pm 0.012$, $0.419 \pm 0.006$ and
$0.417 \pm 0.003$ at one, two and three hops, all at or below no penalty, as the
symmetric one-hop mask was. On the seven undirected graphs two hops already reach 60 to
70 percent of all pairs (Table~\ref{tab:khop}), so these arms mostly measure a target
close to uniform, and the prior toward it loses about what the uniform part of the
target adds.

\emph{Direction.} Neither mechanism moves toward the other. The prior toward
symmetrized targets reads $0.439 \pm 0.010$ ($+0.022$, $+0.019$, $+0.002$ over no
penalty by seed), 0.008 below the directed prior in the mean with one seed carrying the
drop; the directed mask reads $0.416 \pm 0.012$ ($-0.002$, $-0.001$, $-0.023$), not
above the symmetric mask's 0.421. The soft-versus-hard gap is therefore not a
directed-versus-undirected gap on the regulatory and TFLink heads.

\emph{Placement.} The prior at $\lambda = 1$ on layers 3 to 4 reads $0.445 \pm 0.007$
($+0.021$, $p = 0.039$), the same as on layer 1, and on layers 1 to 2 $0.437 \pm 0.009$
($+0.012$, $p = 0.15$). The mask on layers 1 to 2, 3 to 4 and 1 to 4 reads 0.419,
0.415 and 0.426, at no penalty like the single-layer mask. Where the graphs enter does
not change which mechanism helps; the three-seed spread (0.007 to 0.009) does not
separate layer 1 from layers 3 to 4.

\emph{Budget.} At 60 epochs the unpenalized model keeps declining and the prior holds
(\file{experiments/025-solid-growth/results/graph_reg_sweep_history.csv}; the tables
read these runs at epoch 29 and their max over 60 epochs). At epoch 59 no penalty is at
0.391, 0.404 and 0.405 by seed, the mask at 0.395, 0.411 and 0.387, the prior at
$\lambda = 1$ at 0.433, 0.437 and 0.433, so the fixed-budget gap grows from 0.02 at
epoch 29 to 0.03 at epoch 59; the no-penalty and mask maxima stay at epochs 14 to 18,
the prior's at 20, 23 and 46. These runs also give a replicate reading: at epoch 29 the
60-epoch runs, the same config and seed as the 30-epoch runs with only
\texttt{max\_epochs} changed and no schedule, read 0.413 to 0.419 with no penalty
against 0.425 on all three 30-epoch seeds, 0.410 to 0.428 under the mask against 0.418
to 0.423, and 0.438 to 0.447 at $\lambda = 1$ against 0.446 to 0.448. A same-seed
replicate therefore moves by up to 0.011 at epoch 29, the size of every difference
among the arms below $\lambda = 10^{-2}$ and of the mask's distance from no penalty, and
smaller than the 0.02 the strong prior adds. Hypothesis (untested): the replicate
spread is the non-determinism of the four-GPU run (unordered reductions and
non-deterministic kernels), since nothing else in the two configs differs.

\emph{Representation.} With the composite sequence embedding (\texttt{emb\_040}) and no
penalty the model did not train: validation Pearson stays within 0.02 of zero at every
epoch on all three seeds (training Pearson 0.003 at epoch 29), and 5 to 11 epochs per
seed log it as NaN, an undefined Pearson from constant predictions
(Table~\ref{tab:runs} reads these at the finite epochs). With the prior at $\lambda = 1$
the same model trains, slowly: 0.158 to 0.178 at epoch 0 and $0.421 \pm 0.007$ at epoch
29, still rising at the last epoch (max at epochs 28 and 29), against $0.447 \pm 0.001$
for the gene table. The earlier reading that the composite embedding gave $+0.016$ over
the table was made with the joint fitness head (\texttt{embfit\_035}); without it the
composite model needs the prior to leave the origin at all. Hypothesis (untested): the
penalty's gradient, $10^{3}$ times the point loss's at these weights, gives the
attention a structure the point loss cannot find from the composite input on its own;
the per-parameter-group probe of Section~\ref{sec:reading} would show where it lands.

\emph{Width, and the prior on four layers.} All twelve width-360 runs (Delta jobs
22462030 to 22462041) and the three runs of the prior on layers 1 to 4 (22462012 to
22462014) failed at the first training step with CUDA out of memory on the 44 GB A40,
every rank, so they have no reading. The plan's memory estimate for width was wrong: the
attention of the regularized layer is not the whole budget once the hidden width doubles,
and four regularized layers exceed the card at width 180. Running these arms means a
smaller per-GPU batch with gradient accumulation, which changes the protocol, or a
larger card; Delta's A100 nodes are 40 GB and do not help.
