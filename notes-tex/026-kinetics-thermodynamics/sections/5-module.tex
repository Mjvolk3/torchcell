\section{Where these tables enter the model\secstatus{todo}}

Figure~\ref{fig:cgt-module} places the flux module inside the cell graph transformer. The
module is neither of the two instrument classes the architecture previously had: a Type I
instrument maps a representation to a representation and a Type II instrument maps a
representation to a measured output, while the flux module maps a representation to a
\emph{flux vector}, a latent physical state from which phenotypes are then read.

Three of its inputs are parameter tables rather than learned weights. The $k_{cat}$ table
bounds the box the flux is parameterized inside; the formation energies set the reaction
potential whose sign the second-law penalty acts on; the $K_M$ table is built and not yet
enabled, for the reason Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives.

\subsection{Why these two tables, in two equations\secstatus{todo}}

\paragraph{$k_{cat}$ is what lets a genotype change a flux.} The genotype reaches the flux
through a predicted enzyme level, not through a gate. Write $h_g$ for the perturbed cell's
embedding of GEM gene $g$, $\gamma_g \in \{0,1\}$ for whether that gene is present,
$E^0_g$ for a prior abundance in mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$, $w$ for a learned readout vector and $r$
for the range, in natural-log units, the head may move a gene away from its prior:
\begin{equation}
  E_g \;=\; \gamma_g\,E^0_g\,\exp\!\big(r\tanh(w^{\!\top}h_g)\big),
  \qquad r = 3 .
  \label{eq:abundance}
\end{equation}
The $\tanh$ bounds the deviation, so a gene stays within a factor of $e^{3}$, about 20, of
its prior in either direction and no gene can be driven to zero by the head alone. A
deletion is the one thing that can: it sets $\gamma_g = 0$ exactly. The prior is
equal-molar, $E^0_g = \tfrac{1}{2}P_{\mathrm{avail}}/\sum_g MW_g$, because no measured
proteome is passed in; $MW_g$ is the gene product's molecular weight in kDa and
$P_{\mathrm{avail}}$ the protein pool defined below.

A catalytic unit is a complex, and a complex is limited by its scarcest subunit. Over the
subunit set $C_u$ of unit $u$, with $\beta = 8$:
\begin{equation}
  E_u \;=\; \Big(\sum_{g\in C_u} E_g^{-\beta}\Big)^{-1/\beta},
  \label{eq:complex}
\end{equation}
a power mean of exponent $-\beta$, which approaches $\min_{g\in C_u} E_g$ as $\beta$ grows.
\textbf{Deleting one subunit therefore takes the whole complex down, not a share of it},
which is what a heteromeric enzyme does and what a sum over subunits would get wrong.

The capacity bound is then the product of a turnover number and an amount of enzyme, summed
over the units that catalyze the reaction. With $\rho(u)$ the reaction unit $u$ serves:
\begin{equation}
  |\nu_j| \;\le\; \sum_{u\,:\,\rho(u)=j} k_{cat,u}\,E_u .
  \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
Isozymes add, since either enzyme can carry the flux on its own. A reaction with no gene
annotation is left uncapped, because an unannotated reaction is one nobody assigned a gene
to rather than one no gene catalyzes.

The pool is finite, so the abundances are priced against a budget:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_g MW_g E_g \;\le\; P_{\mathrm{avail}},
  \qquad
  C_{\mathrm{bud}} \;=\; \Big[\,\sum_g MW_g E_g\big/P_{\mathrm{avail}} - 1\,\Big]_{+},
  \qquad P_{\mathrm{avail}} = 0.35\ \mathrm{g\,gDW^{-1}},
  \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
which is GECKO's constraint~\cite{chenReconstructionSimulationAnalysis2024}. It is a
\emph{penalty} rather than a projection: nothing
renormalizes $E_g$, so a trained model can overspend the pool and pay for it. That makes
$C_{\mathrm{bud}}$ a reported quantity as well as a loss term, and it is what couples the
genes to each other. A deletion frees mass that the remaining genes can take.

\textbf{Only the product $k_{cat,u}E_u$ reaches the bound, and that fixes what the head can
be said to predict.} Equation~\eqref{eq:capacity} never sees $E_u$ alone, so the head is
identifiable only up to that product: a $k_{cat}$ that is low by a factor of ten and an
abundance that is high by ten give the same ceiling and the same loss. Read that way the
head is predicting an enzyme \emph{capacity}, and the $k_{cat}$ table sets the units it is
expressed in rather than pinning its value. It is also why the across-predictor spread of
Section~\ref{sec:accuracy} is not simply absorbed: it moves $k_{cat,u}$ per unit, so it
changes which reactions are tight relative to each other, and that is a shape the head has
to undo gene by gene rather than with one scale factor.

\textbf{$K_M$ appears nowhere in Equations~\eqref{eq:abundance} to \eqref{eq:budget}, and
that is the code rather than a simplification for the page.} The capacity is
$k_{cat,u}E_u$ with nothing multiplying it, and a saturation factor would need
intracellular concentrations that exist for 6.8 percent of the metabolites.

\textbf{Two branches exist and the one described here is not the default.} The abundance
head is off unless an arm turns it on; with it off the layer falls back to a scalar
availability $c_j \in [0,1]$ folded from the same $\gamma_g$ and a nominal constant
$\bar E = 10^{-3}$, giving $|\nu_j| \le c_j \sum_u k_{cat,u}\bar E$, and the budget is
computed from the enzyme demand \emph{implied} by a flux,
$e_g = \sum_{u \ni g} |\nu_{\rho(u)}| / k_{cat,u}$, rather than from a predicted abundance.
The measured consequence is in the flux-layer note: median capacity is
19.1\,mmol\,gDW$^{-1}$h$^{-1}$ under the constant, against 0.18 and 0.11 under the two
abundance arms, and \texttt{feas\_capacity\_active\_frac} is 0.0 for all three at
initialization. \textbf{The bound is not yet observed to bind}, under either branch.

A measured proteome would replace $E^0_g$ rather than the head, and one exists in the same
project: Zelezniak's 726 proteins across 97 kinase deletions. That is the change that would
turn the prior from an equal-molar placeholder into a quantity.

Without Equation~\eqref{eq:capacity} the only limits on $\nu_j$ are yeast-GEM's own bounds,
and those carry no capacity at all: \textbf{4{,}129 of 4{,}131 reactions sit at $\pm1000$ or
$0$}, which encodes direction and on-off and nothing else. A gene deletion would then have
no route to change the \emph{magnitude} of any flux. 

\paragraph{$\Delta_r G$ enters as a term in the loss, not as a constraint.} The idea, before
any implementation detail, is two lines. A reaction should run downhill,
\begin{equation}
  \nu_j\,\Delta_r G_j \;\le\; 0,
  \label{eq:downhill}
\end{equation}
and where it does not, we charge for it:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
  \;+\; \lambda_{\mathrm{th}} \sum_j \big[\,\nu_j\,\Delta_r G_j\,\big]_{+}.
  \label{eq:simple}
\end{equation}
The bracket is zero whenever Equation~\eqref{eq:downhill} holds and positive when it does
not: no solver and no binary variable, and the tabulated formation energies are what make
$\Delta_r G_j$ a measured quantity rather than a free parameter.

\textbf{This is a training-time penalty, and at inference nothing enforces it.} The flux
still comes from the box, so a trained model can emit a thermodynamically infeasible flux;
what training buys is a model that mostly does not. The layer therefore reports
\texttt{feas\_thermo\_violation\_frac}, the fraction of reactions with $\nu_j\Delta_r G_j >
0$, so the question is answered by measurement at evaluation rather than by assertion. 

\medskip
\noindent The form actually implemented adds a tolerance and a normalization:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
  \;+\; \lambda_{\mathrm{th}}\,
  \underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
    \frac{\big[\,\nu_j\,\Delta_r G_j + \epsilon\,\big]_{+}}
         {|\nu_j|\,|\Delta_r G_j| + \epsilon}}_{C_{\mathrm{th}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta_r G_j = \Delta_r G'^{\circ}_j + RT\!\!\sum_{i \neq \mathrm{H}^+}\!\! S_{ij}\ln c_i,
  \label{eq:secondlaw}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{J}$ runs over reactions with a known $\Delta_r G$, excluding exchange,
biomass and water transport, and $\epsilon$ is a tolerance the strict inequality of
Equation~\eqref{eq:downhill} needs once it is made smooth. The tabulated formation
energies supply $\Delta_r G'^{\circ}_j$, which is the one place this document's thermodynamic
table reaches the model.

\textbf{The normalization is load-bearing, not cosmetic.} The raw hinge
$[\nu_j \Delta_r G_j]_+$ carries units and, measured at initialization on a real batch, is
about \textbf{72 against a data loss of about 1}. An unnormalized term of that size does not
regularize the model, it replaces it. Dividing by $|\nu_j||\Delta_r G_j|$ caps each
reaction's contribution at 1 and turns $C_{\mathrm{th}}$ into the fraction of the available
driving force running uphill, which is also the quantity worth reporting.

Why it earns its place: because $\Delta_r G$ is a difference of potentials it sums to zero
around any cycle, so no cycle can run downhill at every step, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:secondlaw} therefore penalizes internal loops. Capacity does not do this.
Requiring growth at 90 percent of optimum narrows 2{,}316 reactions, 56.1 percent of the
network, and leaves \textbf{427 reactions still free to take any value in $\pm1000$}, all of
them internal loops. Only the thermodynamic term removes those, and it does so with no
binary variable, which is what a loopless flux balance program needs one for.
