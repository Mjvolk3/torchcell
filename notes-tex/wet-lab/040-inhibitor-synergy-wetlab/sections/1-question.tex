\section{The question, and what the 2021 runs measured\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:question}

A chemogenomic screen measures a compound's effect on a pool of deletion strains, and
the models of experiments 031 to 038 predict that effect from the compound's structure.
The host those models never see is the one a fermentation runs in: an engineered strain
with no deletion, growing in a medium that holds several inhibitors at once. The
question is whether a model fit on public chemogenomic screens can say anything about
that host, and the measurement that can answer it already exists.

\term{bAID} is the strain of every run below. Lian 2019 built it by integrating
PmeI-digested pAID6 into BY4742 and selecting for G418 resistance, and its
Supplementary Table 9 gives the genotype as
\file{BY4742-Delta::KanMX-[dLbCpf1-VP]-[Csy4]-[dSpCas9-RD1152]-[SaCas9]}; the 2021
report calls the same strain \sourcetext{BY4742-iAID6}.
\external{Lian 2019 (MinerU OCR of the publisher PDF, library key
\texttt{lianMultifunctionalGenomewideCRISPR2019}); the sha256-pinned quotes are in
\file{torchcell/datasets/private\_torchcell/volk2021\_sources.py}}
It carries the CRISPR-AID machinery and no deletion, so a model asked about it is asked
about the \term{empty genotype}: the wild-type genome under a perturbed environment.

\notesec{The six inhibitors} Furfural, acetic acid, 5-(hydroxymethyl)furfural (5-HMF),
formic acid, levulinic acid and lactic acid, the degradation products a bioenergy
sorghum hydrolysate carries into a fermentation. Short forms FF, AA, HMF, FA, LVA and
LA appear in the figure panels and nowhere in the prose.

\notesec{The five runs} All on a Bioscreen C, 200 wells per run, optical density over
time, aerobic YPD, biological replicates randomized across well positions
(Table~\ref{tab:runs}). The single-agent titrations (ex21) ran 72 h, the 63 combinations
(ex23) 85 h, and the three two-way dose grids (ex26 to ex28) 96 h. The doses of ex23
were chosen from ex21 by the report's own rule, \sourcetext{the first concentration that
showed an increase in doubling time while also showing low doubling time variance}.
\external{2021 preliminary exam report, section 2.2.1 (MinerU 2.7.6 OCR, 200 dpi, of the
submitted PDF; sha256 recorded in \file{results/wetlab\_provenance.json})}

\notesec{The four claims, as registered} Each was allowed to fail, and the order was
fixed: the model-free references first, because a reference rule that already explains
the combinations leaves a trained model nothing to add.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item Growth under combinations departs from independence. Reference rules from the ex21
  single-agent curves: Bliss independence, Loewe additivity, highest single agent;
  scored on the 63 ex23 combinations (grew within 85 h over 63; fitness over the 25 that
  grew) and the three isobole grids (front against the Loewe line). Hypothesis:
  synergistic relative to Bliss (Bliss gives 0.60 for the four mildest singles together;
  nothing with four grew).
\item Chemogenomic profile similarity anticipates which pairs deviate. Measured
  Vanacloig profiles where they exist, compound-cold ridge-FCFP4 profiles for formic and
  lactic acid (and, as the fairness check, for the shared compounds from their held-out
  folds). Tested on 15 pairs (permutation) and the three isoboles; HMF x acetic acid is
  shown but excluded (single run, growth islands; 039).
\item Deletion profiles compose under a mixture by a rule measurable on public data:
  Hillenmeyer HET's 26 two-compound environments against their single-compound partners
  (sum, mean, max, Bliss-style product); the base rate of emergent and masked genes.
\item Post-analysis only, not a validated prediction: composed profiles nominate the
  genes a screen under each combination would recover (core, compound-specific,
  conflict). The check is GO enrichment of the nominated sets, and whether it overlaps
  the terms the measured single-compound profiles carry. No in-house screen exists to
  test them (the thesis archive holds only the library-sequencing correspondence for
  that screen).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Measured.} All four claims on CPU, from the committed scripts of
\file{experiments/040-inhibitor-synergy-wetlab/scripts/}: claim 1 holds, claim 2 fails as
registered, claim 3 holds on public data, claim 4 is a post-analysis whose nominations
are hypotheses. Every score is reported under both growth calls.

\textbf{Not measured.} Any trained model on this task. The mixture trainer is being
written, no run exists, and no number below comes from one. No screen of the bAID
deletion pool under any inhibitor combination exists, so claim 3's composition rule is
carried to these compounds as a labeled hypothesis and claim 4's nominations are
untested.
\end{keybox}

\notesec{Vocabulary} \term{Fitness} is the ratio of the uninhibited doubling time to the
well's doubling time, so the uninhibited control is 1 and a well that never grew is 0.
The \term{served call} derives that doubling time from the raw optical-density curve and
is primary; the \term{software call} takes the Bioscreen software's generation time and
is carried as a sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:data}). A \term{combination} is a set of
inhibitors each at one dose, and the 63 combinations of ex23 are every non-empty subset
of the six that the design covers. A \term{profile} is a compound's vector of per-gene
deletion responses, $\log_2$ of treatment over control, negative when the deletion is
sick. \term{Compound-cold} means a compound whose profile was held out of the fit
entirely, which is what a prediction for a never-screened inhibitor has to be. A
\term{reference rule} predicts a combination's fitness from its single agents and
contains no fitted parameter beyond the single-agent curves.
