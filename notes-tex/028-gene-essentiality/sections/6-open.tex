\section{Open\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:open}

The held-out number is close to saturated: the round-12 ensemble's interval, 0.893
[0.84, 0.94], spans what 195 genes with 31 essential can resolve, and every lever on the
transformer side has been moved (width, depth, the preprocessor, the budget, the class
weight, six encodings). What remains is attribution and the module.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The pathway multi-hot carries the round-9 gain (rounds 14 and 22, and the split
    on the cheap models); which pathways, and whether a pathway-level flux statistic from
    the solve would substitute for the annotation, is not measured.
  \item The round-16 cell (the round-11 affine on the round-9 backbone) and the round-9
    cell are one cell to the resolution of 195 genes (rounds 16 and 17); a larger
    held-out set, such as the genome-wide labels scored on genes the released split
    never touched, is what would separate them.
  \item The MLP, the graph convolution and the transformer on the same three inputs are
    one number as fold ensembles (0.886, 0.888, 0.893), and graph convolution on GO
    alone is 0.917 above them with overlapping intervals. On the 4,674 genome-wide
    genes the released split never touched (round 30) the architectures separate:
    the graph convolution transfers at 0.75 and the GO margin does not survive
    (0.70), while the MLP and the transformer's recipe read 0.51 and 0.57. Open on
    that set: whether a model trained on the metabolic genes transfers because it
    reads essentiality or because it reads what the graph encodes about SGD-annotated
    against deletion-collection genes (label origin coincides with class there, and
    for a viable deletion the two are close to one definition); the structure-only
    inputs of the ladder (all-ones, degree) on that set say whether the graph's
    neighborhoods alone carry the 0.75, and a genome-wide set whose nonessential
    labels come from the same source as the essential ones would separate origin from
    class.
  \item The GO multi-hot is the ladder's strongest encoding and the cheap models take
    it (graph convolution 0.887), and the transformer does not (0.833 beside ProtT5,
    a cost of 0.04 beside the pathway block, round 26), and a cell 350 times wider
    reads it the same (round 28), so capacity is not the reason. What separates 173
    sparse pathway columns from 2,362 dense GO columns in this backbone is not
    measured; a per-block projection, the GO vector entering as its own token, and
    the pooled readout itself are the untested constructions, and the answer decides
    whether annotation vectors scale as inputs to this backbone.
  \item The forced growth column is closed by the dual-overlap probe
    (Section~\ref{sec:dual-overlap}): the ceilings that bind are the same for every
    genotype, so no step size, head or reach trains it (rounds 18 to 20). A construction
    that changes which bounds bind per genotype, such as ceilings the deleted gene's
    neighbors own, or an objective other than biomass, since the layer that solves
    accepts any objective column, is untested.
  \item The head over the solved flux is the module's number, and the form that scales
    (round 27: the program solved once per genotype, nothing learned in the layer, a
    zero-initialized head) reads 0.90 to 0.92 on eight runs of nine and misses the
    fitting transition on one, where the learned-ceiling form misses it on three cells
    in seventeen. Open on it: its fold read, lost to the launcher's memory ceiling and
    in flight at 120 GB; what in the forward makes the transition a chance event of
    the run rather than of the seed (round 29: the same seed and program fit at epoch
    70 in one run and never in another, the two diverging from the first epoch, and
    the scalable form repeats it with the solve cached and nothing learned in the
    layer), and whether pinning the sampler and the card's kernels makes the seed
    reproducible;
    the cache's hit rate and where the wall time goes when the solve is cached, since
    six cached cells on one card ran no faster per cell than three uncached; and the
    flux entering the cell representation as a token rather than the logit as a head,
    which is the multi-phenotype form.
  \item The two model families, annotation through the graph and the solve through
    the transformer, miss different genes, and their post hoc average reads 0.949 under
    the fold protocol. A model trained to hold both, or a learned weighting, is untested,
    and the average's number carries the selection of the best of six.
  \item The layer that solves has the wrong shape for a training loop: the program runs
    on the CPU inside the forward pass, the GPU waits on it (0 to 13 percent
    utilization sampled during round 19, against a 1.6 hour cell where the alternating
    layer takes 19 minutes), and the bytes moved are not the cost, the synchronization
    is. Three constructions would remove it, in order of how much they change: a
    per-genotype basis warm start of the simplex through HiGHS's own interface, which
    keeps the exact solve and the exact duals; a batched first-order solve on the GPU;
    a smooth interior-point solve at a fixed barrier, which changes what the layer
    computes and belongs to its own rounds.
  \item The module's own hyperparameters have not been moved on this endpoint: the
    $k_{cat}$ tier (measured against predicted) and the thermodynamic source. Given
    round 15, the $k_{cat}$ tier is the one that sets whether the capacity box holds a
    growing flux at all.
\end{itemize}
