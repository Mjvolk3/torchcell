\section{The decision\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:decision}

Manuscript section R5 covers drug exposure: a compound and its dose as a perturbation the
model reads alongside the genotype. The case study is a hydrolysate. Sorghum hydrolysate
carries six inhibitors that a fermentation host meets together, and the private Bioscreen
runs of 2021 measured all \hydnInhibitors{} of them on one strain, bAID in aerobic YPD:
\hydnExTwoOneWells{} titration wells, \hydnExTwoThreeWells{} wells over
\hydnCombinations{} combinations, and \hydnIsoboleWells{} wells over \hydnIsoboles{}
isobole grids, \hydnWells{} wells in all. The models that would predict them were trained
on the pooled chemogenomic table: \hydnScreens{} public screens, \hydnPoolCells{} cells,
\hydnPoolCompounds{} compounds over \hydnPoolGenes{} genes. Nothing here is approved. The
sheets are mockups of what the result files support, and the symbol after each panel
letter says only whether the values behind it are on file. \colorkey{}

\pfstep{What the figure would claim} A model trained on public chemogenomic screens can
predict how an industrial host behaves under a mixture of inhibitors it has never seen,
and it can do so well enough to rank the combinations a process should avoid. The figure
is built to make that claim testable rather than to assert it: the bottom row puts the
model beside the two model-free references on the same wells, so a reader sees what the
model adds over arithmetic on the single-agent curves, including when the answer is
nothing.

\pfstep{The four claims, and where each one stands} First, that growth under a
combination departs from independence. On file: over the \hydnCombinations{} combinations
the Loewe rule reaches a growth AUROC of \hydloeweAuroc{} and an accuracy of
\hydloeweAccuracy{}, Bliss from the observed singles \hydblissAuroc{} and
\hydblissAccuracy{}, and the highest single agent \hydhsaAuroc{}; over the
\hydnGrewServed{} combinations that grew, Bliss ranks fitness at Spearman
\hydblissSpearman{} with a mean signed error of \hydblissSignedError{} and Loewe at
\hydloeweSpearman{} with \hydloeweSignedError{}. Of \hydnPairs{} pairs,
\hydnPairsSynergy{} are synergistic relative to Bliss, \hydnPairsAdditive{} additive and
\hydnPairsAntagonism{} antagonistic. Second, that chemogenomic profile similarity
anticipates which pairs deviate. This FAILS as pre-registered: with measured profiles in
the matrix the association is \hydsvdBestSpearman{} at a permutation p of
\hydsvdBestP{} over \hydsvdBestPairs{} pairs, and the all-predicted matrix carries the
opposite sign, \hydsvdPredRawSpearman{} at p \hydsvdPredRawP{} on raw profiles, which the
fingerprint explains rather than the biology. Third, that deletion profiles compose under
a mixture by a rule measurable on public data. On file: over \hydhetPairs{} Hillenmeyer
HET pairs the mean of the singles is the best fixed rule in \hydhetMeanWins{} of them,
median R-squared \hydhetrTwomean{} against \hydhetrTwosum{} for the sum and
\hydhetrTwomax{} for the highest single, and the free linear fit puts weight
\hydhetLinA{} on one single and \hydhetLinB{} on the other. Fourth, post-analysis only:
composed profiles nominate the genes a screen under each combination would recover,
\hydnomCoreBest{} core genes from the best-available matrix and \hydnomCorePredicted{}
from the all-predicted one. No screen exists to test them.

\pfstep{The bars a model has to clear} Growth: AUROC \hydloeweAuroc{}, the Loewe rule
from the ex21 Hill fits. Fitness over the combinations that grew: Spearman
\hydblissSpearman{}, Bliss from the observed ex23 singles. Both are model-free, both come
from the same wells the model would be scored on, and both are already in a committed
result file. On the public side the bar is the nested ridge on fingerprint counts at a
median centered Spearman of \hydladderRidge{} over \hydladderCompounds{} compound-cold
folds, which the next best arm of the ladder trails by a hundredth
(\hydladderSecond{}).

\pfstep{Where the full record is} The sheets draw the figure; the measurements behind
them are written up elsewhere, and the source table under each figure points to the file.
\file{notes-tex/wet-lab/040-inhibitor-synergy-wetlab} is the case-study document: the
2021 runs, the growth calls, the four claims scored, and the confounds each caption
carries. \file{notes-tex/metabolism/031-betaxanthin-module} and
\file{notes-tex/metabolism/032-amino-acid-module} hold the campaign whose panel
conventions this document copies. The chemogenomic side is in three experiment records
rather than documents: \file{experiments/033-env-chemgen-pooled} is the pooled table and
its axes, \file{experiments/035-env-chemgen-vanacloig-cgt} the first compound-cold
campaign and the learning curve, and
\file{experiments/038-env-chemgen-vanacloig-cgt-corrected} the ladder and the factorized
rounds on the corrected store. The 035 and 038 branches are not landed, so the panel
script reads their result files by absolute path and records every path it read in
\file{experiments/040-inhibitor-synergy-wetlab/results/figure_gate_manifest.json}.

\pfstep{What a decision here settles} Whether the figure is the four-by-three of
\cref{fig:main} or the three-by-three the experiment note proposes, which of the twelve
panels earns main-text space against supplementary space, and whether the bottom row
ships with the model beside the rules or with the rules alone. Nothing in this document
is a publication-ready panel; the panels are drawn at Nature print width so that the
argument, not the artwork, is what gets judged.
