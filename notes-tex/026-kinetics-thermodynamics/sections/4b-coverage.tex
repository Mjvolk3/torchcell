\section{Pushing coverage across the whole model\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:coverage}

Every number in this section is produced by a script in
\texttt{experiments/026-metabolism-flux/}, named in the source note of each figure and
table; \texttt{improve\_parameter\_}\allowbreak\texttt{coverage.py} and
\texttt{external\_pka\_}\allowbreak\texttt{benchmark.py} need the eQuilibrator env rather
than the torchcell env.

\subsection{The two tables cover different reactions\secstatus{todo}}

The shipped formation energies and the eQuilibrator~\cite{beberEQuilibrator30Database2022}
recomputation were treated as rivals, and they are not. Taking either alone discards what
the other has.

\textbf{The union is worth 211 reactions over the shipped table and costs nothing but the
merge.} What the recomputation contributes is the reactions the shipped table never had, and
that contribution is unchanged by the proton fix of Section~\ref{sec:thermo}. Coverage and
\emph{independent confirmation} of coverage are different things, and only the second one
moved. That merge is built here and is not what \texttt{load\_thermo\_table} reads: it
reads \texttt{model\_metDeltaG.csv} alone, now multiplied by 4.184 at parse time since that
file was found to be in kilocalories.

\textbf{Reading the recomputation as 66.4 against the GEM's 84.9 is the wrong comparison,
and the table is arranged to make the right one visible.} Three things separate the rows
besides the counts.

\emph{Covariance.} The recomputation returns a square-root factor of the formation-energy
error covariance, of rank 590 over 2{,}806 metabolites, so a reaction energy comes with an
uncertainty that is correlated with every other reaction's in the way component
contribution says it should be. The shipped file is a column of numbers with no uncertainty
of any kind, so a coverage number computed from it is a count of values, not of constrained
reactions. Only 1{,}673 of the recomputation's 2{,}741 priced reactions have a
\emph{finite} uncertainty after the infinite-uncertainty component cancels, which is the
stricter figure and the one a sampler would use.

\emph{Transferability.} The shipped table was assembled by matching
yETFL~\cite{oftadehGenomescaleMetabolicModel2021} values on the metabolite name string, so
it exists for this model and no other; the recomputation works from structure and prices all
five betaxanthin intermediates (Section~\ref{sec:thermo}).

\emph{Transport terms.} \textbf{Neither table carries one, and the module now computes it},
nonzero on 326 of the 1{,}468 reactions that cross a membrane and agreeing in sign with the
GEM's own column on 84.7 percent of the 287 nonzero in both
(Section~\ref{sec:transport-term}, \texttt{results/transport\_terms.json}).
\textbf{This is the one row where the shipped table has a partial answer the recomputation
cannot reconstruct}, since yETFL's term carries a protonation contribution this module does
not.

\subsection{Composition matching cannot buy coverage\secstatus{todo}}

455 unpriced metabolites have a heavy-atom composition that the compound cache can already
decompose, which invites pricing them from a same-composition surrogate. The test is to
price every cache compound of the same composition and measure how far apart they land.

\textbf{They land far apart.} Across the 441 metabolites that gain a value, the median
spread within a composition is \textbf{52.6\,kJ/mol} and the 90th percentile is
342\,kJ/mol. Only \textbf{39} fall below 5\,kJ/mol. At 303\,K, $RT\ln 10$ is 5.8\,kJ/mol,
so the median surrogate is uncertain by roughly nine orders of magnitude in the equilibrium
constant, which is not a constraint on a sign. Wiring all 441 in would report coverage of
93.49 percent, three and a half points above the union, on values that cannot orient a
reaction. The 39 tight ones are kept and the rest are refused: \textbf{a resolution rate
and a usable rate are different quantities}, and only the second constrains a model.

\subsection{What is missing is lipids, and mostly cannot carry flux\secstatus{todo}}

Of the 332 metabolites that match no accession, 231 are phosphatidylinositol, cardiolipin or
triacylglycerol species (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}c), the rest other acyl-chain classes plus
tRNA- and protein-linked pseudo-metabolites. These are combinatorial species: yeast-GEM enumerates acyl-chain compositions that no
database assigns an identifier to, so the gap is a naming problem in the model rather than a
hole in eQuilibrator, and a name that states the acyl composition is one a structure can be
written from (Section~\ref{sec:lipids}).

That matters because of what those reactions do. \textbf{Only 2{,}845 of the 4{,}131
reactions have nonzero flux-variability width under the wild-type medium}, so the remaining
1{,}286 cannot carry flux at all and pricing them changes nothing the layer can express.
Counted over the reactions that can carry flux, the shipped table covers 88.47 percent
against its 84.85 over all 4{,}131, and the recommended union \textbf{94.38} against 90.29.
That right-hand pair is what bounds the flux layer.

\subsection{Writing the missing lipid structures\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:lipids}

The 409 metabolites with a definite formula and no structure in any namespace are lipids
whose acyl composition is written into the name, so the structure is derivable from the
name even though no database indexes it.
\texttt{lipid\_structure\_writer.py} parses each name into a headgroup template and a list
of acyl chains, assembles the SMILES in RDKit, and prices the result two ways.

\textbf{168 of the 206 distinct names parse, covering 347 of the 409 metabolite rows, and
all 168 reproduce yeast-GEM's own elemental formula exactly.} The 38 that do not split
into 17 SLIME backbone pseudo-metabolites, whose formula is a difference formula rather
than a molecule, and 21 names with no acyl composition to read: glycans, hemes,
cytochromes, protein-linked lysines and a GPI anchor. Every double bond is placed at
$\Delta$-9 and cis, a convention yeast-GEM's \texttt{16:1} does not state; the written
free acids hash to the InChIKeys of palmitoleic and oleic acid, which is what the build
asserts rather than the convention.

\textbf{All 168 price, 120 by finding a compound eQuilibrator already holds and 48 through
the external p$K_a$ route.} The cache lookup is a route the coverage audit had no structure
to key on, and it checks the structures independently: 120 written InChIKeys resolve a
compound nobody here drew.

\textbf{What this buys is method consistency rather than coverage.} The eQuilibrator
recomputation moves from 66.35 to 86.27 percent of reactions, passing the shipped column's
84.85, and every one of those values now comes from one group-contribution method with an
uncertainty attached. \textbf{The union with the shipped table does not move at all},
staying at 3{,}716 reactions, because that table already prices these lipids by name out
of yETFL and ModelSEED. The recommended set gains 2 reactions and the flux-carrying
restriction gains none.

\textbf{A created compound is not on the same energy zero as a cached one, and that limits
what the 48 are good for.} On the 7 names where both routes produced a value at the same
proton count, the cache value sits \textbf{777.5\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$} below the created one,
and the two Legendre transforms agree to 0.14, so the whole gap is in the untransformed
group-contribution term. Section~\ref{sec:pka} localizes it: these structures were written
as the neutral parent, and resubmitting them as the pH 7 major microspecies closes the gap
to 0.13\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$. Where a cache compound exists its value is the one reported, 120
of 168 names and 289 of 347 metabolite rows, so a claim can take the cache-only figure.

One condition still separates these numbers from the built table. They are all at pH 7.2,
while \texttt{metabolite\_dgf\_}\allowbreak\texttt{prime.parquet} transforms each metabolite
at its own compartment's pH, so only the reaction counts above are comparable and a
re-transform is needed before any of them enters the flux layer.

\subsection{The kinetic ceiling is substrate structure, not the models\secstatus{todo}}

DLKcat, UniKP, EITLEM and EZKinetics cover exactly the same 3{,}552 of 3{,}728 catalytic
units, and DeepEnzyme covers 3{,}546. Four independently trained models agreeing to the unit
is not a coincidence about the models: every gene has a sequence, so the binding constraint
is whether the substrate has a structure to featurize. \textbf{The 95.3 percent ceiling is a
property of the inputs, and no better predictor moves it.} Raising it means resolving
substrate structures for the same lipid species that block the thermodynamics. TurNuP's
55.2 percent is a model limitation rather than an input one
(Section~\ref{sec:accuracy}).

The consensus tables take the per-unit median across predictors in log space and carry the
across-predictor spread beside it. For $K_M$ that spread has a median of 0.87 orders of
magnitude and the consensus is usable. \textbf{For $k_{cat}$ it is 1.14}, so the consensus
value and its uncertainty are the same size, and a $k_{cat}$ table should be treated as an
order-of-magnitude prior rather than a measurement. Both medians rose with the sixth
predictor, from 1.09 and 0.70: the most accurate predictor on the measured pairs is not the
one closest to the others.

\textbf{Selecting the predictors that fit yeast enzymes halves that spread, and it is the
only lever available.} Ranking on within-class plausibility keeps EZKinetics, UniKP and
EITLEM for $k_{cat}$ and EZKinetics and EITLEM for $K_M$, halving the per-unit spread at no
coverage cost (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}e,g,h). A $k_{cat}$ consensus at \textbf{0.71} is
still a factor of five.

\subsection{A p$K_a$ route that does not need the license\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:pka}

\textbf{Coverage of the native network and coverage of an inserted pathway are separate
problems, and only the first moves above.} A perturbation understood as an edit to the
cell's total genomic content adds reactions yeast-GEM never had, carrying compounds absent
from the compound cache, which is the one case the ChemAxon license governs. Adding such a
compound requires the p$K_a$ constants \texttt{cxcalc} supplies, without which
\texttt{LocalCompoundCache} opens read-only; that blocker also stands behind the 665
metabolites the coverage work leaves unpriced.

\paragraph{The bridge is an unreleased option, and it takes p$K_a$s and nothing else.}
\texttt{equilibrator-assets} master merged \texttt{pkas\_provided\_externally} on
2026-08-15; the last PyPI release, 0.6.0 from January 2024, does not carry it, so the route
runs from a clone pinned at commit \texttt{c79e371e}. The caller passes a dictionary keyed
by the exact structure string, valued by a list of \emph{macroscopic} constants, and the
option lifts read-only mode without ChemAxon. Charge and the proton ladder are then derived
from the structure rather than supplied: the atom bag comes from the SMILES through Open
Babel, and the microspecies mapping is indexed off the hydrogen count and the number of
supplied constants above pH 7. \textbf{The reference microspecies is therefore whatever
protonation state the caller wrote the structure in, and that choice is worth
776 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$.} Pricing 7 written lipids from their neutral structures left a residual
of $-777.5$ kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ against the same compounds in the cache; submitting the pH 7
major microspecies instead, the zwitterion pKasolver's own ladder places at pH 7, leaves
$+0.13$, range 0.07 to 0.15. The difference sits in two entries of the 163-long group
vector, one for the phosphate and one for the amine, so it is the untransformed
group-contribution term rather than the transform. \textbf{Submit the pH 7 major
microspecies.} The error is silent otherwise: standard InChI normalizes mobile protons, so
the two conventions share an InChIKey and a single local cache returns the compound the
first call created, which is why the comparison needs two caches that cannot see each other.

\textbf{Magnesium is accountable, but only from real tabulated values.} A constant cannot be
attached after creation: \texttt{Compound.transform} iterates microspecies rather than
constants, so an appended constant commits and changes nothing until the proton ladder is
regenerated from it, and each constant's proton count must match an existing rung.
eQuilibrator's own constants are a hand-curated 535-compound table keyed on MetaNetX
identifiers that a newly created compound does not have. Omitting them costs 0.0018 to
4.12 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ at pMg 3 on the 7 benchmark compounds that carry any, always in the
direction of under-stabilizing the compound, and up to 9.43 at pMg 2. Carrying a constant
over by substructure is worse than omitting it: bucketing the shipped table by ionizable
phosphate steps leaves a within-bucket standard deviation of 1.6 to 3.3 log units, and a
p$K_d$ off by 2 costs 10.87 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ on 5-formyl-THF and 11.14 on ADP against 4.12
and 1.10 for having no constant at all.

One interface defect belongs with these. The guard tests truthiness rather than presence, so
an empty list, the correct answer for a compound with no constant between pH 0 and 14, raises
rather than creating a single-microspecies compound.

\paragraph{The validation set is the cache's own constants, which is what makes it a test.}
No open-source predictor was trained to reproduce ChemAxon, so the cache is an independent
reference rather than a leaked one. Of the 694{,}324 compounds it holds, 468{,}752 carry a
dissociation-constant list; filtering to under 600 Da, to the elements C, H, N, O, P and S,
to 3 to 50 heavy atoms, to a present group vector and to 1 to 6 constants inside (0, 14)
leaves 74{,}101 eligible from 115{,}720 scanned, from which 300 are drawn at
\textbf{exactly 50 per constant-count stratum}. Every compound carries at least one of four
functional groups: amine 137, carboxylate 103, phenol 103, phosphate 44. Predictors are fed
the neutral parent rather than the cache's major microspecies, since the stored structure is
already deprotonated.

\textbf{Uni-pKa on the simple enumeration template is the tool to use.} It carries the
smallest energy error measured here, 0.91 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ mean against MolGpKa's 1.21, 0.16
against 0.34 at the median and 2.84 against 3.20 at the 90th percentile, over 299 of 300
compounds. It is also the only tool that forms a macroscopic constant itself, as a
log-sum-exp over the microstate free energies of an acid macrostate against a base
macrostate, so no aggregation of ours stands between the network and the number. For scale,
eQuilibrator's own uncertainty on comparable compounds runs 4.30 to 8.79 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$
across the five betaxanthin intermediates and 12.62 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ on the pilot
phosphatidylethanolamine. The weights are Bind Research's five-fold retrain, sha256-matched
to the registry the maintained inference package pins, rather than the authors' own release
(Table~\ref{tab:papers-pka}).

\textbf{The enumeration template, not the network, decides the answer.} The same weights and
the same code on the full template give 2.94 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, the worst of the three complete
tools, and neither template gets the ladder length right: the simple one returns 1.49
constants fewer per compound than the cache, the full one 1.42 more. Those two errors are not
symmetric in energy. The constants the simple template drops sit far from pH 7.2 and barely
move the Legendre transform, while the enumerator walks outward from the neutral parent, so
the rungs the full template adds are near pH 7 by construction, which is where the transform
is most sensitive (Figure~\ref{fig:external-pka}c). Reporting a Uni-pKa number without naming
the template names one of two answers that differ threefold.

\textbf{A ladder can also be checked without a reference, and that check ranks the tools the
way the energy column does.} Counting ionizable centers by SMARTS and flagging any prediction
carrying more constants inside (0, 14) than there are centers flags 8.7 percent of ChemAxon's
own lists, so 8.7 percent is the counter's false-flag floor rather than an error rate.
Against it the simple template is flagged on 1 compound of 299, 0.3 percent; MolGpKa on 145
of 300, 48.3 percent, and when flagged it returns 2.55 more constants than the structure can
support; the full template on 156 of 300, the highest rate. The check separates the two
Uni-pKa configurations without ever seeing ChemAxon's answer.

\paragraph{Reach is the binding limit, not accuracy.} Sorting the 665 unpriced metabolites
by what each actually needs gives four tiers. \textbf{16} need only the constants, which is
all a p$K_a$ predictor reaches on its own, 2.4 percent. \textbf{409} need a lipid structure
written first. \textbf{185} have a placeholder formula that no method can price, and
\textbf{50} have a structure the cache already holds but the group decomposer refuses; the
remaining 5 match only composition-mismatched candidates. \textbf{The structure writer,
not the p$K_a$ predictor, is what moves this number}, and it has now moved it:
Section~\ref{sec:lipids} prices 347 of the 409, so the measured reach is 347 of 665,
52.2 percent, against the 425 an earlier estimate put as a perfect writer's ceiling. On
written lipids the group-decomposition failure rate is \textbf{0 of 148}, against 2 of 5
pilot compounds; the two pilot failures were an iron porphyrin and an amino-acid ester,

labeled as needing the ChemAxon license. \texttt{pathway\_thermo.py} was matching the whole
InChI string, while the cache stores the InChI of the \emph{major microspecies}, whose final
