\notesec{A cell is not a pair}
The plan's joinability table, its spread and skew per source, and its cross-dataset
correlations are all computed on the pair: one gene under one compound, averaged over
every dose, screen and ploidy arm. Table~\ref{tab:units} computes the same statistics at
both units from the store. Computed at the pair, the store reproduces the plan: the spread
agrees to the third decimal for all four sources, and the pair counts differ by 596 for
Hillenmeyer and 443 for Hoepfner out of 1.65 and 0.86 million. Computed at the cell, the
two large sources change. Hillenmeyer has 1.57 cells per pair and Hoepfner 3.34, and their
spread is 15 and 25 percent wider at the cell than at the pair, because averaging over
doses and arms removes variance that a model trained on cells has to fit.

\notesec{The environment vocabulary}
Table~\ref{tab:vocabulary} gives the vocabulary an environment encoder sees. Three sources
are dense: an environment carries about 3{,}500 cells in Vanacloig and about 5{,}700 in
Hillenmeyer and Hoepfner, so an environment is a near-complete profile over the source's
genes. Wildenhain is sparse. Its 5{,}170 environments carry a median of 68 cells each,
between 7 and 185, out of 242 genes, so a Wildenhain compound is a partial profile and no
two compounds need share their genes. Hoepfner is the only source that revisits its
compounds: a compound appears in a median of three environments and at most thirteen, and
only one of its 148 compounds appears in a single environment.
