\section{The three parameter tables\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:parameters}

\subsection{What was there before, and what is there now\secstatus{todo}}

The module needs three tables and had almost none of them. Measured $k_{cat}$ covered 148
of 3{,}728 catalytic units, 4.0 percent, with the other 96 percent running on a single flat
default of 10.3\,s$^{-1}$. A catalytic unit here is a \textbf{(reaction, AND-term) pair},
not a distinct enzyme complex: \texttt{parse\_catalytic\_units} assigns an id inside the
per-reaction loop, so one complex catalyzing five reactions counts five times. The
deduplicated AND-term count is 1{,}065. Both numerator and denominator are counted the same
way, so 4.0 percent is internally consistent, but the denominator has to be named precisely
or the reader computes 148/1{,}065 and gets 13.9. $K_M$ had no table at all: the parameter existed as an enum
member and a protocol flag with nothing behind either. The thermodynamics came from a
shipped file of point values with no covariance, usable only for this organism.

Seven kinetic predictors were mirrored into
\texttt{\$DATA\_ROOT/models/kinetics/<name>/} with per-file \texttt{sha256}, retrieval
commands and git commits, and all seven now run over the enzyme-substrate pairs of yeast-GEM
9.0.2, with protein sequences taken from the genome object rather than from the table each
model bundles. The seventh, EZKinetics, is unpublished and pinned by repository and commit
(\texttt{le-yuan/EZKinetics}, revision \texttt{bf7a897}); it is the only one that
conditions on assay pH and temperature, and since yeast-GEM carries neither per pair it is
run at the authors' 7.0 and 25\,\textdegree C defaults, which keeps it comparable with the
six condition-blind predictors.

\textbf{Two of these models exponentiate base 2 and five base 10.} DLKcat and DeepEnzyme
are $\log_2$; UniKP, EITLEM, TurNuP, Boost\_KM and EZKinetics are $\log_{10}$. Reading either wrong
yields turnover numbers that still look entirely credible, which is why each was validated
against something its own authors published before its values were believed.

\subsection{Which tables are actually wired in\secstatus{todo}}

This distinction decides how any result from the module should be read, and it is easy to
lose.

\textbf{None of the three tables built in this work is consumed by any measured arm below.}
Every arm reported here ran on the Open Enzyme Database's 4.0 percent measured $k_{cat}$
plus a default, and on yeast-GEM's shipped formation energies. That is the sharpest thing to
carry out of this document, because the module diagram draws solid edges from the kcat and
thermodynamic tables into the box and the potential, and no arm below has those edges.

\textbf{The plumbing itself now exists and is under test.} \texttt{resolve\_kcat\_table}
takes a predicted tier and \texttt{resolve\_reaction\_delta\_g} a named energy route, and
GilaHyper cells are running the flux arms against them under a constraint warmup: job 1663
(\texttt{flux\_off}), jobs 1667 and 1668 (\texttt{flux\_capacity} and
\texttt{flux\_abundance} on the unit-corrected union, resubmitted after jobs 1664 and 1665
were found to be reading a mixed-unit table) and job 1669 (head normalization). None has
reached a selected epoch or a test score, so nothing here is a result for the predicted or
recomputed tables. $K_M$ still has no consumer at all.

\subsection{Why six predictors and not one\secstatus{todo}}

Re-keying the comparison from the enzyme-substrate pair to the \textbf{gene}, which is the
unit the capacity bound reads, gives the number that decides how a predicted table should
be used.

  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \caption[Per-gene disagreement]{Across-predictor spread per gene against the dynamic
  range of the quantity itself, in orders of magnitude, over genes every predictor covers. The
  $k_{cat}$ row is over six predictors and the $K_M$ row over four; before EZKinetics they
  were 1.23 against 0.94 (ratio 1.32) and 0.70 against 1.34 (ratio 0.52), so the sixth
  predictor widened both disagreements.}
  \label{tab:pergene}

  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
    \hline
    Quantity & Genes & Median spread & Consensus p10--p90 & Ratio \\
    \hline
    $k_{cat}$ & 980 & \textbf{1.28} & 0.87 & \textbf{1.47} \\
    $K_M$ & 1{,}150 & 0.81 & 1.22 & 0.67 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{For $k_{cat}$ the disagreement between predictors exceeds the quantity being
predicted, and a sixth predictor widened it.} The median gene moves 1.28 orders of magnitude between the
highest and lowest predictor, a factor of 19, and 71 percent of genes move by more than a
full order of magnitude, against a 10th-to-90th-percentile spread across genes of 0.87
orders of magnitude.
Choosing a different published predictor moves one gene's turnover number further than
real enzymes differ from one another. Gene-level Spearman between $k_{cat}$ predictors runs
0.01 (DeepEnzyme against EZKinetics) to 0.55 (EZKinetics against UniKP).

\textbf{For $K_M$ it does not.} The same construction gives 0.81 against 1.22, and
Boost\_KM agrees with UniKP at a gene-level Spearman of 0.75, higher than any pair of
$k_{cat}$ predictors reaches. Both are statements about agreement rather than accuracy,
since almost none of these pairs has a measured value.

That is why the registry in \texttt{torchcell/datasets/kinetics/} exists and why a config
string selects the predictor: predictor choice is a hyperparameter with a measured effect
size, not a settled detail.

\subsection{Which predictor is right, as far as that can be measured\secstatus{todo}}

Agreement is silent on accuracy. The Open Enzyme Database mirror carries measured
\emph{S. cerevisiae} values, and restricting to wild-type enzymes assayed between 20 and
40\,\textdegree C and joining on (UniProt accession, substrate) leaves \textbf{57} pairs
that are also among the catalytic units the predictors were run over.

  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \caption[Predictor accuracy against measured values]{Accuracy on the 57 measured pairs.
  Error is the median absolute $\log_{10}$ ratio in orders of magnitude, bias the median signed ratio,
  negative meaning the predictor runs low. Every predictor trained on BRENDA or SABIO-RK,
  from which the Open Enzyme Database is itself assembled, so an unknown fraction of these
  pairs was seen in training and the values are an upper bound on held-out accuracy.}

    $k_{cat}$ & DLKcat & +0.656 & 0.38 & $-0.14$ & 84.2\% \\

\textbf{The accuracy ranking is not the agreement ranking.} EZKinetics leads on rank
accuracy for both parameters while agreeing with DeepEnzyme at 0.01 on $k_{cat}$; EITLEM
leads on magnitude and places fourth on rank. So Table~\ref{tab:pergene} cannot be read as
a quality ordering, and the two accuracy axes disagree about which predictor is best. The
57 scored pairs are not the easy tail either: the across-predictor $k_{cat}$ spread on them
is 1.37 orders of magnitude against 1.27 over all 3{,}814 pairs, so the upper-bound caveat rests on
leakage alone. If one predicted table were to be wired into the capacity bound, EZKinetics
is the one these numbers point at for both parameters, with a $k_{cat}$ bias of $-0.14$
orders of magnitude to correct.

\textbf{Most of that correlation is the enzyme class rather than the enzyme, and this
bounds what wiring a predicted table can buy.} Measured on the whole Open Enzyme Database,
35{,}753 records across 4{,}555 enzymes, the median EC4 class spans 1.77 orders of magnitude between
its highest and lowest member against a measurement floor of 0.12 orders of magnitude, so knowing an
enzyme's exact four-digit EC number leaves 0.75 orders of magnitude of standard deviation unexplained
and accounts for under half the variance (intraclass correlation 0.46). A lookup table
that predicts each enzyme as the median of the others in its EC class, using no sequence
and no learning, scores a Spearman of \textbf{0.430} on the same 57 pairs: below TurNuP and
above DeepEnzyme. Centering both axes on the class mean collapses every predictor's Pearson
from the 0.81 to 0.93 range to between $-0.16$ and $+0.53$, and only EZKinetics has an
interval excluding zero.

The consequence for this module is specific. A flux layer already knows which reaction it
is parameterizing, so the between-class range is information it does not need; what the
capacity bound needs is the within-class ordering, which is the part these predictors are
measured to be weakest at. A predicted $k_{cat}$ table is therefore an order-of-magnitude
prior, and expecting the wiring to sharpen the bound is not supported by the accuracy
numbers. The full analysis, with its sample-size caveats, is in
\texttt{notes-tex/026-kinetics-thermodynamics}, Section~4.5.
