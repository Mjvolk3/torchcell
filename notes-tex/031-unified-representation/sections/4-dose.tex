\section{The dose axis does not resolve\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:dose}

A pooled model sees one dose field, and a field that means different things in different
rows is a nuisance variable rather than a feature. The five datasets use three mutually
non-convertible regimes, and the measurement is worse than a missing column.

\notesec{Vanacloig's dose is a potency, not a quantity}
Of its 143{,}218 records, 3{,}492 carry a numeric concentration and they are all one
compound, benomyl. The other 40 of 41 compounds carry a target-inhibition basis with the
concentration unstated. There is no molar value to pool.

\notesec{Wildenhain's dose is a constant}
Every one of its 5{,}170 compounds sits at the same fixed concentration, so its dose column
has zero variance and carries no information at all. This is harmless for a model that
ignores dose and fatal for one that regresses on it.

\notesec{Hillenmeyer is the only arm with real variation}
Its two arms carry a numeric dose on essentially every record, converting to molar for 84
and 96 percent of them, across a range spanning nine orders of magnitude, with up to seven
doses per compound.

\notesec{Hoepfner is the only possible bridge, and it is two compounds wide}
It is the only dataset stating both a molar concentration and a target-inhibition basis, on
100 percent of its records, with a median of two doses per compound. In principle that
calibrates what the basis means in molar terms, which is exactly what Vanacloig lacks. In
practice Vanacloig and Hoepfner share two compounds, so the calibration cannot reach the
panel.

\notesec{Shared compounds are not dosed alike}
Across the five datasets, 214 compounds are dosed in more than one with a molar value. The
median ratio between the highest and the lowest of those doses is 12.5, and the 90th
percentile is 100. A compound shared by two datasets is typically dosed an order of
magnitude apart in them.

\notesec{What follows}
Dose cannot be one shared continuous feature. Three routes exist and they are not
equivalent. A dose-basis token per dataset alongside log molar where it exists keeps the
information and accepts that the model cannot compare across bases. Dropping absolute dose
and training on within-condition gene profiles removes the problem and makes Wildenhain's
constant harmless. Learning a per-compound potency scale from the multi-dose compounds and
using it to place the single-dose ones is a modeling project rather than a preprocessing
step. The representation in Section~\ref{sec:representation} takes the first, because it is
the only one that keeps the information without a new model.
