\section{The question, and the panels that can answer it\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:question}

A \term{gene-level readout} is a profile with one number per gene: a transcriptome or a
proteome. A \term{metabolic phenotype} is a small vector of metabolite readouts or a
single titer. The question is whether the first carries the second: given only a
deletion's transcriptome or proteome, can its amino-acid pools or its pigment titer be
read off? If yes, a model that predicts the gene-level readout has already learned most
of what a metabolic head needs, and the two heads share an encoder for a reason. If no,
the metabolic head must find its signal elsewhere.

The knockout collection is the bridge. Three sources profile single-gene deletions of the
same collection: the Kemmeren 2014 microarray compendium
(\path{doi:10.1016/j.cell.2014.02.054}; 1{,}484 deletions, mRNA $\log_2$ ratio to wild
type over 6{,}169 reporters), the Messner 2023 knockout proteome
(\path{doi:10.1016/j.cels.2022.12.003}; 4{,}549 deletions after averaging duplicate
ORFs, $\log_2$ protein quantity over the HIS3 reference across 1{,}830 proteins), and the
Nadal-Ribelles 2025 perturb-seq atlas (\path{doi:10.1101/2025.04.30.651486}; 2{,}243
deletions with at least 50 cells, pseudobulk $\log_2$ fold change over 5{,}404 genes).
Six metabolic panels score deletions of the same collection: Mulleder 2016
(\path{doi:10.1016/j.cell.2016.09.007}; 19 intracellular amino acids in mM, 4{,}678
deletions), Cooper 2010 (\path{doi:10.1101/gr.105825.110}; 16 capillary-electrophoresis
peaks as a ratio to the plate mean, 4{,}313 deletions), Cachera 2023
(\path{doi:10.1038/s41467-023-42394-3}; betaxanthin corrected fluorescence, 4{,}719
deletions), Ozaydin 2013 (\path{doi:10.1016/j.ymben.2012.07.010}; beta-carotene colony
color score from $-5$ to $+5$, 4{,}474 deletions), Zelezniak 2018
(\path{doi:10.1016/j.cels.2018.08.001}; central-carbon metabolites in 95 kinase
deletions), and da Silveira 2014 (\path{doi:10.1016/j.molcel.2014.11.014}; 135 lipids in
127 deletions).

\notesec{Why the three sources are not interchangeable} The three profile the same
deletions and barely agree with one another. Messner protein against Kemmeren mRNA for
the same deletion reads a per-deletion median Pearson of $0.04$ and a per-gene median of
$0.08$; Messner against Nadal-Ribelles reads $0.01$ on both, and Nadal-Ribelles does not
replicate itself across its own batches (cross-batch $r$ of $0.04$). So the metabolic
read is a second, independent test of each source: a source that carries the metabolic
phenotype carries something real whether or not it agrees with the others, and a source
that carries nothing has no such defense.
