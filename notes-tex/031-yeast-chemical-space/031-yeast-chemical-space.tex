\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A full physical accounting of a yeast cell's chemical species would let us represent every
metabolite, medium component and dosed inhibitor in one molecular space, and the genome-scale
model is the obvious starting inventory. Yeast9 names 1{,}378 distinct species of which 894
carry a structure, but only 273 of those structures name a single molecule rather than a set
of stereoisomers, and a retrobiosynthetic expansion published in 2026 has already raised yeast
metabolome coverage from 7 to 92 percent using 21{,}921 reaction rules. Computing the dry-mass pools from the model's own biomass equation closes the accounting to
96 percent and puts DNA at 0.39 percent and the cofactor and ion pools at 0.73 percent, so the
chemical species that matter here are numerous and under one percent of the cell by mass. The
opening that remains is therefore not the expansion but two things nobody reports:
stereochemical resolution, and the lipid classes no reconstruction structures.
\end{abstract}
