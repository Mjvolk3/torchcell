### M2. Each deletion strain was measured once (n=1; the note says 4637 of 4678 have a single measurement) and there is no per-strain SE
- Previously stated by: note 2026.07.05 "`data_raw` shows 4637/4678 strains with a single measurement (170 with 2, 20 with 3, 4 with 4)"; note 2026.07.06 "n_replicates = 1, metabolite_level_se = None ... the genome-wide screen is a SINGLE culture per strain"; loader docstring lines 23-26 and lines 248-249.
- Verdict: PARTLY CONFIRMED.
- Evidence:
  - The paper never prints "n = 1" for the screen. What it says (paper.md lines 331, 341, 377, 395): "The 4913 deletion strains were arrayed on 62 96-well plates"; "Strains from the same batch were cultivated and their metabolites extracted on the same day and analyzed together by LC-MS/MS. Before analysis by LC-MS/MS the order of samples within the same batch was randomized. During analysis a quality control sample (QC) was assessed every 24 samples and in total 237 times. The QC was prepared by joining the extracts of the first batch including metabolites of 351 deletion strains. Eventually, it was used to monitor analytical performance and to calculate the technical variation (Figure S1B). In a second screening round, the 479 deletion strains that showed lowest metabolite concentrations were arranged on 6 additional plates and the analysis was repeated (batch 12)."; "We were able to obtain 4678 strain specific free amino acid profiles". Every "n =" in the paper is a validation or side experiment: Figure S1B "(n = 3)", Figure S1D "quality control sample (n = 237)", Figure S3 "untreated WT and tco89D (n = 3) and for treated WT (n = 6)", Figure S5A/C "(mean +/- SD, n = 3)", Figure S5B "WT strains (n = 12)", Figure 6C "(mean +/- SD, n = 4)".
  - Measured on the released workbook (`mulleder_xls_inspect.py`): `data_raw` has 5,290 rows, of which 237 have no ORF (identifiers `QC_001`..; the QC sample) and 4,831 unique ORFs. Rows per ORF over ALL 4,831: 1 row 4,637; 2 rows 170; 3 rows 20; 4 rows 4. Restricted to the 4,678 RELEASED ORFs: 1 row 4,487; 2 rows 167; 3 rows 20; 4 rows 4. So the note's "4637/4678" mixes two denominators: 4,637 is out of 4,831 raw ORFs; among the released strains 4,487 have one raw measurement and 191 have two to four.
  - Which measurement the released mM traces to (`mulleder_multi_measured.py`, `mulleder_multi_average_test.py`): for single-row strains, `conc_aa / raw_aa / batchfactor_aa` is constant across the 19 amino acids (median log-SD 0.0000), so the released value is one raw row times a per-batch, per-amino-acid factor times a per-sample scalar, exactly as the Methods describe. For the 191 multi-row strains the best single row fits poorly (median log-SD 0.084) while the mean of the per-row normalized values fits well (median 0.0067; better than the best single row for 180 of 191; below 0.02 for 157 of 191). Hypothesis supported by this diagnostic, not proven from a published statement: the released value for those 191 strains is the mean of their 2-4 normalized measurements.
  - No per-strain SE column exists in any sheet (sheets: `Overview`, `data_raw`, `intracellular_concentration_mM`, `robust_summary_statistics`, `Z-score`, `p_value_Z-test`, `Mahalanobis_distance`, `p_value_X2-test`; `robust_summary_statistics` is population-level `mean (mM)`, `SD (mM)`, `RSD (%)` per amino acid). CONFIRMED.
- Consequence for the loader / served records: `n_replicates = 1` is right for 4,487 records and understated for 191 (167 x 2, 20 x 3, 4 x 4); `metabolite_level_se = None` is right for all. A per-record `n_replicates` from the `data_raw` row count per ORF is derivable from the released file and would replace the constant.
