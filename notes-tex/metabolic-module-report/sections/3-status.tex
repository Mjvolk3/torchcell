\section{Where the training stands\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:status}

The 027 grid on Delta ran 144 cells, six arms over 24 seeds, on Cachera's betaxanthin
screen with the 019 nine-graph backbone. The anchored module scored $-0.020$ test Spearman
against $+0.027$ for no module. The prediction dumps show every cell is a constant
predictor: the largest prediction spread in any cell is 0.8 percent of the target
spread, the no-module arms sit at the target mean with a training loss of exactly 1.00 from
epoch 10, and the flux arms at wrong constants. The claim that the thermodynamic anchor
costs is therefore withdrawn and the module's effect is unmeasured; the same backbone
reached 0.434 under the 019 trainer, so the defect is in the 027 loop. Two defects precede
any relaunch: training uses yeast-GEM's shipped exchange bounds, which forbid all
amino-acid uptake, while the Cachera strains grew in a supplemented medium; and 170 of the
183 proton-translocating reactions carry a confidently wrong zero, the translocation term
being computed but off by default.

\textbf{Round 2 has one positive result, and it is not on betaxanthin.} Supervising the
flux layer's own learned whole-cell concentration, the volume-weighted mixture over the
compartments each amino acid occupies, as the amino-acid readout beats the pooled control at
all three seeds: $+0.0145$ mean per-feature Spearman on the pinned test split, standard
deviation 0.0093, at an epoch selected on validation and never a maximum over epochs, and
$+0.0198$ (s.d. 0.0136) with the constraint scale at 0. Wave 6's turnover readout on the
byte-identical arm collapses to a constant, $-0.0494$ against the same control at its two
seeds, so concentration minus turnover is $+0.0686$ at matched seeds. The learned whole-cell
medians match the measured medians to 0.036 to 0.037 decades over four orders of magnitude,
while the per-column held-out Spearman runs only $-0.09$ to $+0.17$ and the unsupervised arm
reads $-0.0031$, so the agreement is supervision rather than physics. Four caveats travel
with it: 10.63 percent of Mulleder's measurements sat above the shipped $10^{-2}$~M ceiling,
so the window was widened to 1~M before the round; five of the six fractional volumes are
assumed rather than sourced; $n = 3$ carries two degrees of freedom; and betaxanthin parity
on the one shipped kinetic table is $-0.055$ (s.d. 0.090, $n = 3$), a null rather than a
cost. Full account, contrasts and caveats in the 027 document.

\textbf{What each target can support, measured from each source's own repeats.} A model
cannot correlate with a measurement better than the measurement correlates with itself, so
the square root of a target's reliability is the highest Pearson correlation any predictor
of the true level can reach, and it is what a flat result has to be read against. Six
metabolite datasets enter the build, three of them decoded by a trained head
(Table~\ref{tab:target-reliability}). Betaxanthin is the strongest of the three at a
reliability of 0.84 and a ceiling of 0.92, because the CRI-SPA release averages a median of
15 colonies per gene; Cachera's own trial-to-trial correlation of 0.765, carried to four
screens by the Spearman-Brown relation, agrees at 0.93. The flat betaxanthin results are
therefore a property of the predictor and not of the label. The amino acids sit at 0.42 and
0.62, and the isobutanol screen at 0.11 and 0.33, which reverses the earlier reading that
called isobutanol the best-conditioned target: its widely quoted retest of 0.92 was computed
on the re-screened strong hits alone, and selecting both tails inflates it. Of the three
context datasets, the lipids and the metabolome could support a head at 0.91 and 0.79, while
the carotenoid screen's six-level visual score reaches only 0.33. The fourth endpoint,
essentiality, is bounded instead by how far two curated sources agree, which is 98.8 percent
of 1{,}121 genes against a majority rate of 86.0 percent.

\textbf{The parameter tables are closed and the trainer is not.} As of 2026-09-11 the
formation energies stand at 3{,}801 of 4{,}131 reactions and 2{,}724 of the 2{,}845 that can
carry flux, and the kinetic tables at 97 measured, 782 homolog, 2{,}831 predicted and 18
default over 3{,}728 catalytic units, with every value carrying a named route and every gap
a named failure class (Section~\ref{sec:parameters}). 291 of the 330 unpriced reactions are
permanently closed, blocked only by species that are not molecules, so no further method
moves them. Nothing in the parameter tables is now what holds the module back.

\textbf{A third defect precedes a relaunch, and it is now a config value rather than missing
code.} Every 026 and 027 run so far read
\texttt{metabolite\_dgf\_prime\_}\allowbreak\texttt{with\_external.parquet}, which predates
the composition check and therefore still carries the glycogen price taken from a
four-residue oligomer and the dolichol offset. \texttt{ThermoSource} now carries a
\texttt{verified} member that resolves the curated tables through their manifest's sha256
and raises rather than falling back when a hash does not match, and it is the only route in
which the composition guard has run. No arm asks for it yet.

\textbf{Order of work.} A trainer that reproduces 0.434 on the no-module arm with an abort
on prediction collapse; the module arms on it, on the dataset's actual medium, reading the
verified tables; then the merged $\Delta_f G$ and predicted $k_{cat}$ tables, on GilaHyper
jobs 1663, 1667, 1668 and 1669, carried to a selected epoch.
