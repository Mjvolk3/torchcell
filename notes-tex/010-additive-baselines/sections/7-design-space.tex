\section{The design space, and a defect found there\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:design}

Figure 1 of the Visani preprint is a scatter of network predictions against
additive predictions across the whole combinatorial space, and its force comes
from the near-identity of the two rankings: the variants selected for
construction were recoverable without the network. The same comparison on 010
gave a result that turned out not to be about additivity at all.

\subsection{The comparison as run\secstatus{todo}}

Checkpoint \texttt{c7671wgj} scored three separate 010 inference spaces:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{inference\_1}, 4{,}370{,}595 triples over a 526-gene panel,
\item \texttt{inference\_2}, 479{,}195 triples,
\item \texttt{inference\_3}, 465{,}735{,}532 triples genome-wide, which is the
  space the panel-12 and panel-24 selections consumed.
\end{itemize}

Scoring the same triples with B1 and streaming the 4~GB prediction file gives,
over the 325{,}631{,}845 triples of \texttt{inference\_3} whose three genes all
carry an additive coefficient, a Pearson correlation of $0.0018$ and a top-100
overlap of zero. Stratifying by the training support of each triple's
least-observed gene does not account for it: even where all three genes have 800
or more training observations, $n = 867{,}669$, the correlation is $0.036$.

On \texttt{inference\_1} the same comparison behaves completely differently. B1
against the three checkpoints gives $0.548$, $0.579$ and $0.511$.

\subsection{Why, and what it leaves standing\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:offby28}

The model is a deterministic function of the perturbed gene set, so a triple
scored twice by one checkpoint must receive the same number twice. Across these
runs it did not, and the additive coefficient $\hat{\beta}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:b1},
fit on measured labels and keyed on systematic gene name, is what showed which
run was wrong: a gene's mean predicted effect tracks $\hat{\beta}_g$ at 0.58 on
\texttt{inference\_1} and at 0.05 and $-0.01$ on the two runs that produced the
selection. The cause is a uniform shift of 28 in every gene index. Between the
runs the genome gene set went from 6{,}607 to 6{,}579 entries, the 28 absent
entries sort first, and the cell graph the indices are read from is rebuilt from
the live genome at every use. Re-scoring stored triples through the validated
path of Section~\ref{sec:verify} reproduces each run at correlation $0.999998$
under exactly one index map and not the other.

The symptoms, the decisive test, the second defect that scored panel triples as
doubles, the corrected panel predictions and the guard now in place are in the
companion document \emph{A Gene Index That Moved}
(\texttt{notes-tex/010-index-defect}); the additive anchor and the run-to-run
agreement tables that exposed the defect are reproduced there from
\texttt{inference\_run\_consistency.py}. What matters for this document is what
the defect leaves standing. Everything derived from the \texttt{inference\_2}
and \texttt{inference\_3} prediction files is invalid, which is the panel-12 and
panel-24 selections. The \texttt{inference\_1} artifacts are unaffected, and so
is every number in Sections~\ref{sec:results} and \ref{sec:paired}: they come
from the labeled split through the validated scoring path, whose index space was
checked against the recorded metrics before anything was read from it.

\subsection{Seed instability in the selection tail, separately\secstatus{todo}}

On \texttt{inference\_1}, where predictions do track gene identity, the three
checkpoints still disagree substantially about which triples are extreme.
Checkpoint-to-checkpoint Pearson is $0.524$ to $0.560$, no higher than each
checkpoint's $0.511$ to $0.579$ agreement with the additive model, and top-100
overlap between two checkpoints is $0.21$ to $0.35$. Two independent training
runs of one architecture on one split nominate largely different top-100
triples, so a selection taken from a single checkpoint's tail is not
reproducible across seeds even where the inference pipeline is sound.
