\suppnote{note:dcell-model}{DCell in TorchCell: the ontology-structured model in the shared notation\secstatus{todo}}

DCell (Ma et al., 2018) is the visible-neural-network baseline of Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}: hidden
units grouped into subsystems, one per Gene Ontology (GO) term, wired to each other as the
terms are wired in the ontology. It was reimplemented in TorchCell from the published
description rather than run from the released Torch7 code, so that it reads the same
experiment records and the same 6,607-gene S288C reference as every other model in the
comparison, and so that its one structural commitment, the ontology as the model graph, can be
stated in the notation of Table~\ref{tab:notation}. The published model was trained on single-
and double-deletion growth; extending it to trigenic interaction changes nothing in the
architecture, only which genes are zeroed. \suppfig{fig:dcell-model} draws the adaptation
and reports the ontology it is built on; the cost of training it is the subject of
\suppnoteref{note:dcell-training}.

\notesec{The graph is the ontology} Where the cell encoder $F_\theta$ reads a graph
$G=(N,E)$ over genes and takes the gene--gene relations $\{A^{(k)}\}$ only as soft priors on
attention (Eq.~\ref{eq:graphreg}), DCell has no gene--gene graph. Its graph is the GO directed
acyclic graph $G_{\mathrm{GO}}=(T,E_{\mathrm{GO}})$ whose nodes are terms $t\in T$ and whose
edges run from a child term to each of its parents (\texttt{is\_child\_of}; a term may have
several), together with the annotation relation $\mathrm{genes}(t)\subseteq[N]$ listing the
genes annotated directly to $t$. The three GO namespaces are joined under one synthetic
super-root \texttt{GO:ROOT}, as in the original. Each hierarchy edge is hard wiring, a block
of a weight matrix, not a regularizer: the model cannot pass information across an edge the
ontology does not contain, which is the sense in which it is visible.

\notesec{The perturbation enters as data} A strain with deleted set $p$ is a binary
gene-state vector $s\in\{0,1\}^{N}$ with $s_i=0$ if $g_i\in p$ and $1$ otherwise (the
published encoding is the complement; the sign is absorbed by the first linear layer).
TorchCell stores the reference's annotation relation as a table of 59,986 (term, gene) rows
with a state column; the data loader (\texttt{DCellGraphProcessor}) copies the table per
strain and zeros the rows of deleted genes. In the terms of \suppnoteref{note:equivalence}
and \suppnoteref{note:static-graph}, DCell is the rebuild route: no shared encoding $H$ and
no operator $\mathcal{T}_\psi$; each strain is a distinct input and the whole hierarchy is
re-evaluated for it, the $\mathcal{O}(B\,L\lvert E\rvert d)$ column of \supptab{tab:compute}.
A trigenic strain zeros three rows; that is the entire trigenic extension.

\notesec{Subsystems, readout, and losses} Every term $t$ owns a subsystem that reads the
outputs of its child subsystems $\mathrm{ch}(t)$ and the states of its own annotated genes,
\begin{equation}
I_t=\big[\,\Vert_{c\in\mathrm{ch}(t)}\,O_c\ \big\Vert\ s_{\mathrm{genes}(t)}\,\big],\qquad
O_t=\tanh\!\big(\mathrm{BN}(W_tI_t+b_t)\big)\in[-1,1]^{L_t},\qquad
L_t=\max\!\big(20,\lceil 0.3\,\lvert\mathrm{genes}(t)\rvert\rceil\big),
\label{eq:dcell-subsystem}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{BN}$ batch normalization. Two details differ from the published
$\mathrm{BatchNorm}(\mathrm{Tanh}(\mathrm{Linear}(I_t)))$: normalization precedes the
$\tanh$, so states are bounded, and $\lvert\mathrm{genes}(t)\rvert$ counts direct annotations
rather than the genes contained by $t$ and its descendants, the quantity the original sized
subsystems by. Subsystems run by stratum, the length of the longest path from
\texttt{GO:ROOT} to the term; every child has a strictly larger stratum than its parents, so
running strata from the deepest to $0$ supplies each parent its children's outputs, and one
stratum runs as one batch. The root readout $\hat y=w_r^{\top}O_{\mathrm{ROOT}}+b_r$ predicts
the trigenic interaction $y_{\mathrm{int}}$ directly, with no fitness intermediate (contrast
\suppnoteref{note:gi-from-fitness}). As in the original, every other subsystem carries a
head $\hat y_t=w_t^{\top}O_t+b_t$, and the loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}(\hat y,y)+\alpha\,\operatorname*{mean}_{t\neq r}\mathrm{MSE}(\hat y_t,y),\qquad \alpha=0.3,
\label{eq:dcell-loss}
\end{equation}
which averages the auxiliary terms where the original summed them over 2,526 subsystems. The
published $\lambda\lVert W\rVert_2$ penalty is replaced by AdamW weight decay; weights are
initialized uniformly in $[-0.001,0.001]$ as published. Whether the auxiliary term was
enabled in the run of Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}, with the batch size and schedule, is recorded in
\suppnoteref{note:dcell-training}.

\notesec{Building the ontology} The published 2,526 subsystems came from a 2017-era GO after
three filters: terms with evidence code IGI (inferred from genetic interaction, a
circularity), terms containing fewer than six of the genes disrupted in the training
genotypes, and terms redundant with their children, a removed term's children being
reconnected to its parents. TorchCell rebuilds that recipe from its own reference
(\supptab{tab:dcell-go-filter}): GO release 2025-03-16 restricted to terms that annotate a
reference gene, with annotations from the SGD gene records cached in TorchCell's SGD graph
(dated 2000-12-07 through 2023-10-04), giving 5,660 terms, 6,674 edges, and 66,404 direct
annotations that cover all 6,607 genes. The filters run in the order IGI, redundancy,
containment, with four deviations. IGI removal acts on annotations, not terms: 1,921 IGI
annotations are dropped and a term goes only when that empties it (119 terms). Redundancy is
judged against parents: a term whose gene set equals a parent's is removed (106). Containment
counts genes over the whole reference and the threshold is four, chosen so that the retained
2,655 subsystems approximate the published 2,526; this is the largest cut (2,780 terms). No
date cutoff is applied, so the ontology carries annotations that postdate the screens it is
trained on; a 2017-07-19 cutoff is implemented and would leave 1,408 subsystems (last row of
\supptab{tab:dcell-go-filter}), but it was not used in the trigenic run.

\notesec{The ontology, measured} The trigenic DCell has 2,655 subsystems on 3,208 hierarchy
edges in 13 strata, 1,527 of them leaves, with 59,986 annotations
(\suppfig{fig:dcell-model}b--d). Coverage is complete for a specific reason: 5,487
annotations with evidence code ND (no biological data) attach genes of unknown function
directly to the three namespace roots, 675 genes are in the hierarchy through those alone,
and every gene therefore sits in at least two subsystems (median 8, maximum 38). The same
annotations make the namespace roots the widest subsystems (molecular function: 2,444 direct
genes, $L_t=734$), while the median subsystem has 6 direct and 10 contained genes and the
floor width 20. The network has 61,429 hidden units (97,181 in the published model) and
20,613,037 parameters, 20,548,953 in subsystems and 64,084 in heads. That count follows from
Eq.~\ref{eq:dcell-subsystem} on the rebuilt DAG and equals the \texttt{model/params\_total}
logged by every DCell training run in both wandb projects (15 runs, one value), so the DAG
measured here is the one the baseline was trained on.
