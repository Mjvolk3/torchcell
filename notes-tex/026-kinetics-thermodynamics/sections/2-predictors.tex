\section{The predictors, and what running them taught\secstatus{todo}}

\subsection{One module per model, on the node-embedding pattern\secstatus{todo}}

Which predictor feeds the flux layer is a design choice to experiment over, not a pipeline
detail to settle once, so the kinetic predictors copy the shape the sequence-embedding
datasets already use: an abstract base, one module per model, and a name-to-class registry
that a config string selects.

One structural difference drives everything else. A node embedding is keyed by
\emph{gene}; a kinetic parameter is keyed by a \emph{pair}, because a turnover number is a
property of one enzyme acting on one substrate. The key is
$(\textrm{uniprot}, \textrm{substrate}, \textrm{predictor}, \textrm{parameter})$.
Collapsing to one row per gene would silently pick a substrate, and collapsing to one row
per catalytic unit would silently pick a subunit, so the store holds pairs and aggregation
lives in the resolver.

\subsection{Sequences come from the genome, not from the model's bundled table\secstatus{todo}}

yeast-GEM ships a \texttt{swissprot.tsv} with a sequence column, and it sits conveniently
beside the model. It is the wrong source. Protein sequences are read from
\texttt{SCerevisiaeGenome} through \texttt{genome[orf].protein.seq}, the same path the ESM2
embedding dataset uses, so everything stays on the derivation chain from the S288C
reference FASTA plus the GAF. The difference is provenance rather than content: a sequence
from a third-party table is a claim by that table's compiler, while the same sequence from
the genome is the one the perturbation ontology edits, so a strain's genotype and the
protein a predictor sees cannot silently disagree.

All 1{,}161 yeast-GEM genes resolve as \texttt{current} through the shared gene-name
reconciler, and all 1{,}161 have a protein sequence, so nothing is lost by the switch.
Rebuilding DLKcat on genome sequences moved the median $k_{cat}$ from 4.0125 to 4.0116
s$^{-1}$.

Two failures are worth recording because both are silent. \texttt{orf in genome} looks
safer than indexing and is not: the genome defines no \texttt{\_\_contains\_\_}, so Python
falls back to integer indexing and calls \texttt{genome[0]}, raising before the real lookup
happens. And \texttt{go\_root} is a separate constructor argument defaulting to a relative
path; left unset it misses and the constructor tries to fetch \texttt{go.obo} from
\texttt{current.geneontology.org}, which answers 403.

\subsection{Validation caught a log base\secstatus{todo}}

The DLKcat implementation reproduces the authors' three released example values to four
decimal places, and that check caught two defects that would otherwise have been invisible
in a plausible-looking table. The first is the exponentiation base, which
Section~\ref{sec:logbase} tabulates for every predictor whose base was checked.

\textbf{Multi-component SMILES are refused by the model itself.} Its fingerprint pass
indexes a node list built only from bonded atoms, so a disconnected fragment, such as the
lone \texttt{[Fe+2]} in the heme SMILES yeast-GEM gives for ferricytochrome $c$, walks off
the end of the list.

\subsection{Embedding on four GPUs\secstatus{todo}}

UniKP's protein half is ProtT5-XL~\cite{elnaggarProtTransUnderstandingLanguage2022}, a
3-billion-parameter encoder, and its pinned environment holds CPU-only torch. The embedding is therefore produced once on GPU and
handed across as a content-addressed cache, keyed by a hash of the sequence so that any
predictor wanting a ProtT5 representation can reuse it and two ORFs with one sequence
resolve to one vector.

Sequences are split by index across the four GPUs and merged; within a shard they are
sorted by length and batched, which is where most of the gain comes from, and a
per-sequence masked mean keeps the result identical to unbatched inference. The 1{,}154
distinct sequences finish in under 40 seconds.

The genome is read exactly once, by a dump step, and the shards read that dump. Four
processes opening the same gffutils SQLite database concurrently raced and made it briefly
unreadable; since the shards need the genome for nothing but a sequence lookup, the
dependency was removed rather than serialized.

\subsection{What a predictor is actually missing is a substrate structure\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:substrate-structures}

A predictor takes a protein sequence and a substrate structure. Every gene has a sequence,
so nothing a predictor fails to price fails for want of a protein, and an audit of the 176
catalytic units no admitted predictor could price found the same blocker on all 176: no
structure for any substrate the reaction consumes. None was blocked by an accession, a
sequence, the complex rule or a predictor crash.

\textbf{Three routes recovered 165 of the 176, and each of them is a mapping rather than a
model.} An annotation cross-reference priced 27, following the identifier yeast-GEM carries
through MetaNetX's own cross-reference table to a current entry: the model annotates an
older MNXref release, so \texttt{MNXM417} for fructose 1,6-bisphosphate is not a row in
MNXref 4.5 and three reactions had no turnover number for that reason alone. The lipid
structure writer of Section~\ref{sec:lipids} supplied 48 more from structures it had already
composed for the thermodynamics. A substrate writer built the remaining two by constructing
every chain length and keeping the one whose RDKit formula equals the model's, which selects
dolichol-4 uniquely for dolichyl D-mannosyl phosphate out of $n = 2$ to $11$. Eleven units
none of the three reached.

\textbf{Acceptance is composition, not similarity.} A candidate structure is admitted only
when its RDKit formula equals the model's formula with both moved to the neutral species by
adding one hydrogen per negative charge. That rejects an R-group placeholder outright, since
\texttt{C4H7O2SR} never equals \texttt{C4H7O2*}, and it is what caught the structures the
tables were already resting on: of the 1{,}425 distinct substrate structures the first round
of predictions used, \textbf{97 fail the check}, 35 of them tRNA species the model gives the
formula \texttt{RH}. Repairing 31 and dropping the rest cost 8 catalytic units and moved
none of the surviving values by more than one decade for $k_{cat}$.

\textbf{Two table versions are kept and they are not interchangeable.} The \texttt{processed}
tables are the first round, 3{,}717 units on unchecked structures; the \texttt{verified}
tables are 3{,}709 units, 1{,}359 distinct structures and none failing the composition
check. Every predictor was rerun on the verified pairs and both versions are hashed per
predictor and per parameter in \texttt{results/kinetic\_table\_versions.json}, so a stated
tier count names the version it belongs to. Section~\ref{sec:kinetic-tiers} reports the
verified ones.

\subsection{CatPred, the eighth predictor and the only one that returns a variance\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:catpred}

CatPred~\cite{boorlaCatPredComprehensiveFramework2025} was not in the set Wu et al.\ name and
was added afterwards. It is a fork of chemprop: the substrate is a directed message-passing
network over its 2D atom-bond graph, the enzyme is a learned residue embedding carrying
rotary positional information through multi-head self-attention concatenated with ESM2-650M
features, and an attentive pooling layer reduces the pair to a fully connected head. No EC
number is an input, and the structural branch belongs only to the released $K_i$ model.

\textbf{What makes it worth the seat is the head, not the encoder.} It outputs a mean AND a
variance and is trained under a negative log-likelihood loss, and the release is an ensemble
of ten differently seeded copies, so one pass gives three numbers: the prediction, an
aleatoric variance which is the mean of the ten predicted variances, and an epistemic
variance which is the variance of the ten means. Every other predictor here returns a point
value, so this is the only place in the parameter tables where a value arrives with a stated
confidence.

Three checks were needed before any number was written. The output base is $\log_{10}$,
settled by reproducing the repository's own shipped demo prediction, 0.1875471 against its
recorded 0.1875500. The pinned \texttt{rotary\_embedding\_torch} version is load-bearing: a
later release changes a cached-frequency shape and the checkpoints refuse to run at all. And
the repository's own postprocessing broadcasts the first row's uncertainty to every row, so
the three terms are recomputed from the ensemble columns rather than read from its output.
One predictor property follows from the architecture: a substrate with no heavy atom has no
graph for the message passing, so the bare proton yeast-GEM writes as \texttt{[H+]} is
refused, which costs 1{,}025 of 7{,}456 pair rows and only 5 catalytic units.

\textbf{For CatPred alone the training overlap is a measurement rather than a caveat.} It
released its training tables with a UniProt accession on every row, and its production
weights were fit on train plus validation plus test, which its own \texttt{args.json}
records. Matching the 58 measured yeast pairs on accession and InChIKey connectivity block,
51 of the $k_{cat}$ pairs and 44 of the $K_M$ pairs are in that table. The concentration is
the point: across the whole model only 109 and 193 of 1{,}148 proteins are in it, so the
enzymes that can be scored are almost all seen while the model as a whole is extrapolating.
The same leakage is expected for the other seven and cannot be quantified for them.

Admitting it for $K_M$ changes the admitted set from EZKinetics alone to EZKinetics and
CatPred, which moves 2{,}824 of 3{,}728 catalytic units by a median of 0.151 decades, 667 of
them by more than a factor of two. \textbf{No capacity bound moves at all}, and that is
structural: the enzyme constraint is $|v_j| \le k_{cat} E$, saturation-free, so the bound
scales with $k_{cat}$ alone and the $k_{cat}$ admitted set is unchanged. The tier counts are
identical on both parameters.

The variance is under-dispersed and it ranks. Coverage of the total predicted interval on
the 58 measured pairs is 0.603 at one standard deviation against a nominal 0.683 for
$k_{cat}$ and 0.621 for $K_M$, giving a root mean square standardized residual of 2.62 and
1.22, so a $k_{cat}$ interval is off by about a factor of two and a half while a $K_M$
interval is close to honest. What does hold is the ordering: the rank correlation between the
predicted standard deviation and the absolute error is $+0.570$ for $k_{cat}$ and $+0.621$
for $K_M$, and the median absolute error rises monotonically across the quartiles of the
predicted standard deviation, from 0.115 to 1.118 decades for $k_{cat}$. A flux layer can
therefore spend the variance as an ordering, by widening a bound where the ensemble is
unsure, long before it can spend it as a calibrated interval.
