\section{Data and the two splits\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:data}

\notesec{The label}
Write $f_i$, $f_{ij}$ and $f_{ijk}$ for the fitness of the single, double and triple
mutant relative to wild type, and $\varepsilon_{ij} = f_{ij} - f_i f_j$ for the
digenic interaction. The trigenic interaction score is
%
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{ijk} \;=\; f_{ijk} - f_i f_j f_k
  - f_i\,\varepsilon_{jk} - f_j\,\varepsilon_{ik} - f_k\,\varepsilon_{ij} ,
  \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
%
\external{Kuzmin et al.\ 2018, \texttt{10.1126/science.aao1729}; the definition of
the released adjusted score, not re-derived here}
a third-order residual: the multiplicative part of fitness and every pairwise term are
subtracted before the number is reported, so $\tau$ is by construction the part of the
triple phenotype that no single or double predicts. The loaders store it as
\texttt{gene\_interaction}.

\notesec{The records}
Subset S0 of the 025 build is its 376{,}732 trigenic records over 4{,}352 genes,
the 010 build record for record: matching by genotype recovers every one, 376{,}526
labels are bit-identical and the remaining 206 differ by at most
$5.6 \times 10^{-17}$ (\file{experiments/025-solid-growth/scripts/label_parity_010_vs_025.py}).
Every model below is fit on these records and nothing else.

\notesec{The screen structure}
Counting unordered gene pairs over the records finds 739{,}315 distinct pairs, of
which 420 occur five or more times. Those 420 cover 376{,}733 pair-instances, one more
than the record count, so every record contains exactly one recurring pair and a
single record contains two. The 420 pairs are the Kuzmin query doubles. Grouping
records by them recovers the screen exactly, and any model with a per-pair offset can
absorb whatever is common to a screen.

\notesec{The two arms}
Arm R is 010's own split, random over records, carried into the 025 index space by
genotype identity. Arm Q assigns whole query pairs to parts, so no screen appears in
more than one part. The two arms hold out nearly the same number of records and differ
only in what is held out.

Under arm Q a held-out record's query pair has never been seen, but its genes have:
every array gene of the validation and test parts appears in training, as do 78 of 86
validation and 88 of 92 test query-pair genes, so 81 percent of validation and 92
percent of test records have all three genes in the training set. The split holds out
the pair, not the genes.
