\title{\vspace{-1.5em}The S3 Closure Pool Against Its Own Definition\\
  {\large Recomputing every interaction score and every p-value from the singles,
   doubles and triples the 025 build joined, and the same on the deletion-only 029 build}}
\author{Michael Volk}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\thispagestyle{empty}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The S3 closure pool holds, for every one of its 376{,}732 measured triples, every
term of the equation that defines the triple's interaction score: the three singles,
the three doubles and the triple itself, 1{,}121{,}645 records in all. Inside any one
source screen that equation is exact: recomputing the digenic score from the fitness
columns of the same row reproduces the reported score at $r = 0.999$ in Costanzo 2016
and $r = 1.000$ in Kuzmin 2018. Inside the build it is not. The build's own fitness
reproduces its own digenic scores at $r = 0.445$ and its own trigenic scores at
$r = 0.230$, and the loss is traceable to the join rather than to the formula. Four
mechanisms account for it. SGD essentiality enters as a fitness of 0 and is averaged
with the measured fitness of the same gene's allele, which pulls 907 singles down by a
median of 0.16 and shifts every score built on them by 0.12; taking the 0 back out
halves the digenic error on the 139{,}461 doubles those singles touch. Screens are
averaged across their own controls, so a single that Costanzo measured against 350
control screens is combined with the same gene's Kuzmin estimate and then used in an
identity that was only ever true within one screen. Duplicate screens of the same
genotype, 94 percent of the closure doubles, are merged into one record whose p-value
is a $t$-test over the two or three duplicate scores with one or two degrees of
freedom, which discards the source p-values entirely: the merged p correlates with the
median source p at $\rho = 0.05$, and 1{,}754 doubles significant in every source are
not significant in the build. And 99.6 percent of the Kuzmin 2020 digenic rows carry no
query fitness, which the source scoring replaced by 1.0 before computing the score, a
substitution that is recorded nowhere in the released table but is recovered here
exactly. Where the build kept a single screen, its p-value is the source's and is
reproducible from the double's own colony standard deviation at $\rho = 0.984$;
the trigenic p-values are not reproducible from anything the build holds, because
their null variance comes from control screens that were never released. The
consequence for training on S3 is stated at the end: the fitness the model is asked
to learn and the interaction it is asked to learn are not consistent with each other
in this build, so the identity cannot be the mechanism by which fitness supervision
helps, and every p-value in a merged record should be treated as unlabeled. A second
build on the same graph, 029, keeps deletion alleles only and drops the merge, so the
value a record carries becomes a label policy chosen at read time; under four such
policies the identity reproduces each screen's own scores and no other's, Costanzo's
digenic scores at $r = 0.90$ and $0.75$ under a Costanzo-first policy and Kuzmin's 2018
trigenic scores at $r = 0.52$ under a Kuzmin-first one, while the policy that is best for
one order is worst for the other. Which screen's standard a model learns is a decision
the query and the policy make, and the cost of each choice is stated per screen.
\end{abstract}
