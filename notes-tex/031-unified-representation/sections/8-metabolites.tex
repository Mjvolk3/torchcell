\section{Whether the metabolites can join\secstatus{tent}}
\label{sec:metabolites}

The plan is to represent media components and metabolic-model metabolites as molecules in the
same embedding space as the dosed inhibitors. Two things decide whether that works: how many
Yeast9 species have a structure at all, and whether the encoders can embed them.

\notesec{The encoders are not the constraint}
Eleven of the twelve registered encoders embed every structure-bearing species with no
failures (Table~\ref{tab:yeast9enc}). The three-dimensional encoder reaches 836 of 894, its
refusals being monatomic ions with no conformer and planar or linear molecules, together with
the inositol-phosphoceramide sphingolipids. The descriptor block emits undefined entries on 18
rows, so a caller imputes before taking any distance.

\notesec{The structures are the constraint}
Yeast9 holds 2{,}806 compartment-specific entries that collapse to 1{,}378 distinct species,
and only 894 of those carry a SMILES, all from the release's own name-keyed table. No species
carries an InChI, so every key used here is derived from the shipped SMILES rather than
sourced. Two quality problems matter for a shared space. Every shipped SMILES is
stereochemically flat, with 621 of 894 carrying an unassigned stereocenter, so L and D forms
collapse onto one key. And 36 do not encode the species the model declares, judged against the
model's own formula, two of them being outright wrong molecules.

The 484 species without a structure split into two halves that need different treatment
(Table~\ref{tab:yeast9cls}). About 167 have no single structure and never will, being transfer
RNAs, pooled lipid classes, protein species, glycan accessions and biomass pseudo-metabolites.
Those need a non-molecular slot regardless of curation. The remaining 317 have a definite
structure absent from the release table, dominated by acyl-resolved lipids, and 295 of the 484
carry a cross-reference identifier, so a lookup route exists.

The consequence for anything reaction-level is that 2{,}156 of 4{,}131 reactions, 52 percent,
have a structure for every participant.

\notesec{The axes are related but not the same}
Against the full five-dataset compound set, 49 exact keys and 103 connectivity skeletons are
shared with Yeast9, so 103 metabolites are dosed as exogenous compounds somewhere in the five
datasets. They are the central cofactors and metabolites rather than the drug-like library.
The media axis is a different story: 30 of 37 media components are Yeast9 metabolites, which
is expected since media components are metabolites by construction and is the strongest
argument for one shared space. Because Yeast9 is stereo-flat while the curated compound keys
carry stereochemistry, any join between the axes is on the connectivity skeleton with the
ambiguity recorded.
