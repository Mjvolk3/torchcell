\suppnote{note:models}{What each epistasis model estimates, and what the choice costs\secstatus{todo}}

An interaction score is a residual, and a residual is only defined once the expectation is
written down. The four models in this document write down four different expectations for
the same seven measured strains, so a triple can be an interaction under one and not under
another without either fit being wrong. This Note gives each expectation explicitly and
names what the reader buys and gives up by choosing it.

\notesec{Setting} Write $f_S$ for the titer of the strain carrying deletion set $S$,
divided by the titer of the base strain in the same experiment, so $f_\emptyset = 1$ by
construction. Single, double and triple deletions are $f_i$, $f_{ij}$ and $f_{ijk}$. All
four models are fit on these same normalized quantities, and none of them sees a
combination the design did not build.

\notesec{Multiplicative} The expected phenotype of a combination is the product of its
parts, which is the null of the yeast genetic interaction maps:
%
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\times}_{ij} = f_{ij} - f_i f_j ,
\qquad
\tau^{\times}_{ijk} = f_{ijk} - f_{ij} f_k - f_{ik} f_j - f_{jk} f_i + 2 f_i f_j f_k .
\label{eq:si-mult}
\end{equation}
%
Independence on this scale means that a deletion multiplies titer by the same factor
whatever else is deleted. The trigenic term is the one used throughout the main text.

\notesec{Additive} The same construction with sums in place of products:
%
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{+}_{ij} = f_{ij} - f_i - f_j + 1 ,
\qquad
\tau^{+}_{ijk} = f_{ijk} - (f_{ij} + f_{ik} + f_{jk}) + (f_i + f_j + f_k) - 1 .
\label{eq:si-add}
\end{equation}
%
Independence here means that a deletion shifts titer by the same amount whatever else is
deleted. The $+1$ and $-1$ are the base strain entering at $f_\emptyset = 1$.

\notesec{GLM log-link} A generalized linear model with a Gamma family and a log link, fit
on the replicate-level titers rather than on strain means, with $x_i \in \{0,1\}$ marking
deletion $i$ and $r$ indexing replicate:
%
\begin{equation}
\log \mathbb{E}[y_{S,r}] = \mu + \sum_i \alpha_i x_i + \sum_{i<j} \beta_{ij} x_i x_j
+ \sum_{i<j<k} \gamma_{ijk} x_i x_j x_k + \rho_r .
\label{eq:si-glm}
\end{equation}
%
The interaction is $\gamma_{ijk}$. The base strain is excluded from the fit and enters
through $\mu$. Gamma sets the variance proportional to the square of the mean, which is
the right shape for a titer that cannot go negative and whose spread grows with its level.

\notesec{Log-OLS} Ordinary least squares on log titer differenced against the base strain
within each replicate, with $\delta = 10^{-6}$ a pseudocount:
%
\begin{equation}
g_{S,r} = \log(y_{S,r} + \delta) - \log(\bar{y}_{\emptyset,r} + \delta) ,
\label{eq:si-logols}
\end{equation}
%
regressed on the same saturated design as \suppeq{eq:si-glm}. Its three-way coefficient
estimates the saturated log-scale interaction
%
\begin{equation}
\tau^{\log}_{ijk} = g_{ijk} - g_{ij} - g_{ik} - g_{jk} + g_i + g_j + g_k ,
\qquad g_S = \log f_S ,
\label{eq:si-logtau}
\end{equation}
%
and that equivalence is checked in code rather than assumed: the scale test recomputes
\suppeq{eq:si-logtau} from the strain means and raises unless it agrees with both fitted
log-scale models at $r > 0.99$.

\notesec{The scale is the first tradeoff, and it is not a matter of taste} Equations
\ref{eq:si-mult} and \ref{eq:si-logtau} are different quantities, not two estimates of one
quantity. They agree in magnitude across the 119 testable triples at $r = 0.91$ and
Spearman $\rho = 0.84$ and disagree near zero, which is exactly where a call is made: 15 of
the 27 triples with a positive linear-scale score are non-positive on the log scale, and
none of the reverse. A three-way interaction is not invariant under a monotone transform of
the phenotype, so a positive trigenic interaction on titer is a linear-scale statement and
has to be reported as one. Which scale is appropriate depends on what the reader will do
with the number. A campaign that asks how many milligrams per liter a third deletion adds
is asking a linear-scale question; a campaign that asks whether a deletion's fold effect is
preserved in a new background is asking a log-scale one.

\notesec{The second tradeoff is what carries the pairwise information} The multiplicative
trigenic score can be written with the digenic scores in it,
%
\begin{equation}
\tau^{\times}_{ijk} = f_{ijk} - f_i f_j f_k
- \left( \varepsilon^{\times}_{ij} f_k + \varepsilon^{\times}_{ik} f_j
+ \varepsilon^{\times}_{jk} f_i \right) ,
\label{eq:si-tau-eps}
\end{equation}
%
which is Eq.~\ref{eq:tau-eps} of the main text and is an identity, not an approximation.
Each pairwise deviation enters weighted by the remaining single-deletion phenotype, so a
pair that interacts strongly in a background where the third deletion is nearly neutral
contributes almost its full $\varepsilon$, and the same pair contributes little when the
third deletion is severe. The log-scale interaction in \suppeq{eq:si-logtau} weights every
term equally. Neither weighting is more correct; they encode different ideas of how a pair
is expected to act in the presence of a third deletion, and the weighted form is the one
that keeps comparability with the published yeast interaction maps.

\notesec{The third tradeoff is power against assumption} The two linear-scale models are
fit on strain means and propagate the replicate standard errors through
Eqs.~\ref{eq:si-mult} and \ref{eq:si-add} by the delta method, so they assume nothing about
the shape of the noise beyond a standard error per strain, and they pay for it in degrees
of freedom: a trigenic score combines seven independently measured strains at three
replicates each, giving a median Welch-Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom of 4.28.
The two regression models are fit on the replicate-level values and borrow strength across
the whole design, which raises power and buys a mean-variance assumption in exchange, Gamma
for \suppeq{eq:si-glm} and homoscedastic on the log scale for \suppeq{eq:si-logols}. A
count of significant interactions is therefore not comparable across the two families
without saying which is which, and that is why the main text reports agreement on the
identity of the interacting triples separately from agreement on their sign.

\notesec{What this document uses, and why} The multiplicative model is the reported one
throughout, for comparability with the trigenic screens the field already has, and every
claim that rests on a sign is stated with the scale attached. The other three exist here to
answer how much of a claim is the modeling choice, which is a question the reader can only
settle if all four expectations are on the page.

\suppnote{note:digenic}{The digenic layer the trigenic score is measured against\secstatus{todo}}

The main text is a claim about the third deletion. That claim is made against what the
first two do, so what the pairs do is worth stating on its own, and it is the opposite of
the trigenic result.

\notesec{The pairs are positive} On total titer, 43 of the 45 double deletions have a
positive multiplicative digenic score and the median is $+0.40$. Twenty-five of the 45
reach a 5\% false discovery rate within the readout, and every one of the 25 is positive
(\suppfig{fig:si-digenic}a). A pair of deletions therefore tends to produce more titer than
the product of its singles, which is the behavior a campaign would want and would find if
it stopped at two.

\notesec{No factor carries it} Drawing all 45 scores as a matrix over the ten factors
(\suppfig{fig:si-digenic}b) shows the positive scores spread across the panel rather than
concentrated in the rows of one or two factors. The largest score in absolute value is
0.98. Every one of the ten factors takes part in at least one called pair, from GCN5 in 1
of its 9 to TFC7 in 8 of its 9, so no single factor carries the positive digenic signal
and none is exempt from it.

\notesec{The third deletion undoes it} Writing the multiplicative expectation for a triple
with the pairs included, as in \suppeq{eq:si-tau-eps}, gives a prediction of the triple
from its singles and its pairs alone. Ninety-three of the 120 triples fall below that
prediction (\suppfig{fig:si-digenic}c), and the vertical distance to the diagonal in that
panel is $\tau$. The negative trigenic median reported in the main text is therefore not a
continuation of a downward trend the pairs already set. The pairs trend upward, and the
third deletion reverses the direction.

\suppnote{note:species}{The five species under the summed total\secstatus{todo}}

Total titer is the sum of five measured fatty acids, and summing is a modeling choice in
the same sense that the null is. The five species were measured separately, so the choice
can be audited rather than argued about.

\notesec{Every species interacts, and they do not agree on the sign} Recomputing the
false-discovery-rate correction within each readout gives 3 to 80 called trigenic
interactions per species against 86 on the summed total
(\suppfig{fig:si-species}a). C16:0, C18:0 and C18:1 behave like the total, with 60 to 67 of
their calls negative. C14:0 does not: all 26 of its calls are positive and its median
trigenic score is $+3.90$, the largest of any readout. C16:1 is nearly silent, with 2
digenic and 3 trigenic calls, so a claim about it would be a claim about a readout this
design cannot resolve.

\notesec{C14:0 reverses between orders} On C14:0 all 15 digenic calls are negative with a
median of $-1.40$, and all 26 trigenic calls are positive with a median of $+3.90$
(\suppfig{fig:si-species}c). The direction of the interaction on that species depends on
how many deletions are counted. Three of the five species move down from the digenic to
the trigenic median and two move up, so the reversal the main text reports on the summed
total is a majority behavior rather than a universal one.

\notesec{The summed total is not a faithful summary} Correlating $\tau$ across readouts
over the same 119 triples (\suppfig{fig:si-species}b) puts the total close to C16:0 and
C18:1 at $r = 0.91$, further from C16:1 and C18:0 at $r = 0.54$ and $0.40$, and on the
other side of zero from C14:0 at $r = -0.28$. The two most negatively related species are
C14:0 and C18:1 at $r = -0.41$. Summing therefore reports the species that carry the most
mass and cancels a species whose interactions run the other way, which is the mechanism
behind the main text's observation that 31 positive consensus interactions exist across the
five species and none survives on their sum.

\notesec{What follows for a campaign} A campaign that optimizes one species is not
optimizing a scaled version of the total, and the interaction structure it would have to
navigate is not the one reported here. The reachability result of the main text is stated
on total titer, and repeating it per species is a separate analysis this document does not
carry out.
