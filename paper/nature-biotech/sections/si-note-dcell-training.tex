\suppnote{note:dcell-training}{DCell training cost, instability, and the checkpoint-based baseline\secstatus{todo}}

The DCell value in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d ($r=0.157\pm0.009$) comes from one training run,
not from replicates. DCell in TorchCell (\suppnoteref{note:dcell-model}) took 30.8 days
on four GPUs to reach epoch 333, passed its best validation epoch after 14 days, was
unstable throughout, and was run to completion once within the compute available. The
three values behind the mean and error bar are validation evaluations of that run at
epochs 144, 148 and 150, so the baseline is the best DCell result obtainable here, with
an error bar that is not a replicate error bar.

\notesec{Training run} One full run (wandb \texttt{eni948by}, SLURM job 1922684) trained
the 2,655-term, 20.6~M-parameter DCell on the trigenic interaction task with the DCell
loss (auxiliary losses on, $\alpha=0.3$), AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight
decay $10^{-6}$, batch 600 per GPU on four GPUs under DDP (global batch 2,400), bf16
mixed precision, and gradient clipping at 10. The reduce-on-plateau schedule had its
floor set equal to the initial rate, so the learning rate never changed
(\suppfig{fig:dcell-training}b). Validation Pearson~$r$ was evaluated once per epoch
over the full validation split, and the run was stopped after 333 of the configured
1,000 epochs. By optimizer steps it iterated over 266,393 training records per epoch,
the 80\% split of the experiment-006 build (332,313 records), whereas the CGT replicates
in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d trained on the experiment-010 build (376,732 records;
\supptab{tab:dcell-training-cost}). A second configuration without auxiliary losses
(job 1921740) was stopped after 10 epochs (23~h) at $r=0.009$ and is partial. The
training script attaches no profiler, so no per-operation trace of the cluster run
exists.

\notesec{Checkpoint statistic and its caveat} The three values in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d
are the validation~$r$ at epochs 144, 148 and 150: 0.155, 0.142 and 0.173
(\supptab{tab:dcell-training-checkpoints}). Epoch 150 is the maximum over all 333
evaluations, so the mean of the three (0.157) is an upward-biased order statistic rather
than an expected performance, and the SD over them (0.016; SEM 0.009) measures
epoch-to-epoch variation of one run, not replicate variance. The checkpoint the training
script saved, the minimum validation MSE at epoch 142, scores $r=0.127$; the final epoch
scores 0.038. As a curve (\suppfig{fig:dcell-training}a), validation~$r$ rose from zero
over the first 100 epochs, fluctuated between 0.04 and 0.17 thereafter (mean 0.088, SD
0.028 over epochs 100--332, consecutive epochs differing by up to 0.10), peaked at epoch
150, and drifted down to 0.038 by epoch 332 while the training loss kept falling by an
order of magnitude (\suppfig{fig:dcell-training}b), overfitting under a constant learning
rate. Whether a decaying schedule would have stabilized the run is untested.

\notesec{Cost} DCell reached its best validation epoch after 1,340 GPU-hours (335~h on
four GPUs), against 97--101 GPU-hours for each CGT replicate (24--25~h on four GPUs,
best at epoch 24--25) and 8--12 GPU-hours for the two DANGO runs on the same build as
DCell (\supptab{tab:dcell-training-cost}, \suppfig{fig:dcell-training}c). Per epoch,
DCell processed 33 training samples per second (2.22~h per epoch), CGT 89, and DANGO
about 1,200; DCell also needed six times as many epochs as CGT to peak. Three replicate
runs of the same length would cost about 8,900 GPU-hours at the observed rate, which is
why one run is reported and labeled as such.

\notesec{Where the time goes} The DCell forward pass visits the 2,655 GO-term subsystems
one at a time in a Python loop ordered by stratum, each subsystem a linear layer, batch
normalization and $\tanh$ of its own, so every step launches thousands of small kernels
irrespective of batch size. The speed-up work recorded in
\texttt{notes/experiments.006-kuzmin-tmi.dcell-speed-up.md} (four GPUs, batch 256 per
GPU, a shared workstation; \suppfig{fig:dcell-training}d) measured 128~s per optimizer
step for the first working implementation, 82~s after removing a duplicated forward pass,
23~s after caching the GO strata tensors, and 20~s with bf16 mixed precision;
\texttt{torch.compile} with the recompilation limit raised to 64 gave 49--59~s per step
at batch 500--600, no gain in samples per second over the uncompiled bf16 run (40 versus
50). Two reruns of the bf16 configuration on later days measured 99--119~s per step, so
day-to-day variation on the shared machine is as large as several of the optimizations.
A rewrite that spreads a stratum's per-term calls over four CUDA streams
(\texttt{dcell\_opt}), still one module call per term, ran at 13\% GPU utilization and
80--100~W of 300~W at 1~h~40~min per epoch, slower than the loop, and was not used. The
cluster run's 72~s per step at global batch 2,400 (33 samples per second) sits in the
range of the workstation measurements at batch 2,000--2,400 (40 samples per second).
Hypotheses, untested: the step is bound by kernel launches in the per-term loop rather
than by data loading, which the measurements above cannot separate; if so, one
block-diagonal or grouped matrix multiply per stratum and CUDA graphs for the fixed
per-stratum shapes would remove most of the launch overhead. The per-operation profile
in \suppfig{fig:dcell-training}e requires a GPU run and is left as a placeholder.
