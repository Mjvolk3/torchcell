## 2026.08.25 - The joint ablation at n=50, and why it is still not a verdict

Generated by `experiments/019-simb-multimodal/scripts/analyze_energy_rank_ablation.py`
(CPU only, reads W&B). Data: `experiments/019-simb-multimodal/results/energy_rank_ablation.json`.

### Why this exists

`pearson_per_feature` is computed from `DistHead.point()` = $\mu$, while the energy head's
factor $V$ enters only through

$$\Sigma = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma^2) + VV^\top,$$

which never touches $\mu$. So the `energy_rank` $\in \{0, 32\}$ ablation is **structurally
invisible** to the metric the sweep ranks on -- a null there is what the mathematics predicts,
not a verdict. The only recorded quantity that can see $V$ is the energy score itself, logged
as `val/loss` for `dist=energy` runs. Restricted to that one arm: `val/loss` is a different
scoring rule per distributional arm, so it is comparable **within** an arm and meaningless
across arms.

### Results at n=50 (27 at rank 0, 23 at rank 32)

| quantity | rank 0 (diag) | rank 32 (joint) | Mann-Whitney p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| energy score (mean, **lower better**) | 70.25 | **68.39** | 0.815 |
| energy score (median) | 82.21 | **64.17** | -- |
| pearson (mean, higher better) | 0.0238 | **0.0339** | 0.815 |
| pearson (median) | **0.0140** | 0.0130 | -- |
| \|cov50 − 0.50\| | **0.0606** | 0.0612 | 0.953 |
| \|cov80 − 0.80\| | 0.0187 | **0.0165** | 0.546 |
| pit_ks | 0.0417 | **0.0409** | 0.654 |

**Nothing is significant.** Every p is ≥ 0.55.

### What changed from the n=14/n=18 read

Both of the earlier headline effects dissolved as n grew, which is the expected behaviour of
noise and is worth recording as such:

- The energy-score gap favouring the joint head was 8.11 at n=14 and 11.65 at n=18; at n=50 it
  is **1.86**, p = 0.815.
- Pearson was trending **against** rank 32 at n=18 (0.0155 vs 0.0283) and was flagged then as
  the one result worth watching. At n=50 the mean flips to favour rank 32 (0.0339 vs 0.0238)
  while the medians are effectively tied (0.0130 vs 0.0140). It was noise, not $V$ costing the
  mean.

### The caveat that governs all of it

These runs come from the `_007` round, which was **declared void**
([[experiments.019-simb-multimodal.expression-round-retrospective]]): the compared runs have a
**median final epoch of 71** (range 23--199), because early stopping watched `val/loss`, a
series measured to move *opposite* the ranked metric on this task -- loss bottoms at epoch
~103--136 while val Pearson climbs to a project-best 0.1980 at epoch 1367.

So this is a comparison of **truncated models**, cut off roughly 1,200 epochs before the model
the architecture actually reaches. The method is sound and the apparatus is reusable; the
numbers are not citable. The `epoch` column and the `caveat` field are written into the JSON
so the truncation travels with the data rather than living only in prose.

**To make this a verdict:** re-run against a round trained to saturation (no early stopping,
`max_epochs` 10,000 / `max_time_s` budget), which is now the default -- see
[[experiments.019-simb-multimodal.scripts.plot_residual_covariance]] for the companion finding
that $\Sigma$ is global rather than conditional, i.e. that a single fixed $\Sigma$ is the right
model and the effect being measured here is expected to be real but small.
