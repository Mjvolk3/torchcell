\section{Open\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:open}

The held-out number is close to saturated: the round-12 ensemble's interval, 0.893
[0.84, 0.94], spans what 195 genes with 31 essential can resolve, and every lever on the
transformer side has been moved (width, depth, the preprocessor, the budget, the class
weight, six encodings). What remains is attribution and the module.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The pathway multi-hot carries the round-9 gain (rounds 14 and 22, and the split
    on the cheap models); the deletion's own flux vector does not substitute for it
    (round 35: 0.868 on ProtT5 alone against 0.912 with the block, and the block
    without the vector 0.890), so the two carry different information. Which
    pathways, and what in the annotation the solve cannot see, is not measured.
  \item The round-16 cell (the round-11 affine on the round-9 backbone) and the round-9
    cell are one cell to the resolution of 195 genes (rounds 16 and 17); a larger
    held-out set, such as the genome-wide labels scored on genes the released split
    never touched, is what would separate them.
  \item The MLP, the graph convolution and the transformer on the same three inputs are
    one number as fold ensembles (0.886, 0.888, 0.893), and graph convolution on GO
    alone is 0.917 above them with overlapping intervals. On the 4,674 genome-wide
    genes the released split never touched (round 30) the architectures separate:
    the graph convolution transfers at 0.75 and the GO margin does not survive
    (0.70), while the MLP and the transformer's recipe read 0.51 and 0.57. Open on
    that set: whether a model trained on the metabolic genes transfers because it
    reads essentiality or because it reads what the graph encodes about SGD-annotated
    against deletion-collection genes (label origin coincides with class there, and
    for a viable deletion the two are close to one definition). Degree and clustering
    alone reach the 0.75 on that set, so the question is what degree in the physical
    graph measures there; a genome-wide set whose nonessential labels come from the
    same source as the essential ones would separate origin from class, and the
    physical graph's own coverage of the deletion collection against the SGD
    inviable set is the first thing to count.
  \item The GO multi-hot is the ladder's strongest encoding and the cheap models take
    it (graph convolution 0.887), and the transformer does not (0.833 beside ProtT5,
    a cost of 0.04 beside the pathway block, round 26), and a cell 350 times wider
    reads it the same (round 28), so capacity is not the reason. What separates 173
    sparse pathway columns from 2,362 dense GO columns in this backbone is not
    measured; a per-block projection, the GO vector entering as its own token, and
    the pooled readout itself are the untested constructions, and the answer decides
    whether annotation vectors scale as inputs to this backbone.
  \item The forced growth column is closed by the dual-overlap probe
    (Section~\ref{sec:dual-overlap}): the ceilings that bind are the same for every
    genotype, so no step size, head or reach trains it (rounds 18 to 20). A construction
    that changes which bounds bind per genotype, such as ceilings the deleted gene's
    neighbors own, or an objective other than biomass, since the layer that solves
    accepts any objective column, is untested.
  \item The head over the solved flux is the module's number, and the form that scales
    (round 27: the program solved once per genotype, nothing learned in the layer, a
    zero-initialized head) reads 0.90 to 0.92 on eight runs of nine and misses the
    fitting transition on one, where the learned-ceiling form misses it on three cells
    in seventeen, and reads 0.912 [0.86, 0.95] under the fold protocol with all five
    folds fit. Open on it: what in the forward makes the transition a chance event of
    the run rather than of the seed (round 29: the same seed and program fit at epoch
    70 in one run and never in another, the two diverging from the first epoch, and
    the scalable form repeats it with the solve cached and nothing learned in the
    layer); round 32 closed it: pinning the card's kernels makes two runs of the seed
    one trajectory, so the chance was the kernels, and pinned, six seeds fit six
    times, so the miss is a property of the free run and not of any seed; the pooled
    layer-free cell's two pinned runs are not one trajectory (round 45), with no
    operation warning under the deterministic mode, and round 46 places the
    divergence with the pathway multi-hot in the pooled arm (the ProtT5-alone
    layer-free pair is one trajectory, the pathway-and-ProtT5 affine pair with no
    solve columns is not, and the LP cell carries both embeddings under another arm
    and was one trajectory), and round 49 with the pathway embedding itself (the
    pathway multi-hot alone with the affine and pathway-and-ProtT5 with the plain
    pooled head both diverge pinned, GO-and-ProtT5 with the affine is one
    trajectory); the pathway dataset was rebuilt at every launch and enumerated its
    173 columns from a Python set, so each process served a column permutation the
    previous one wrote, and with the enumeration sorted and the rebuild removed both
    cells pinned are one trajectory (rounds 51 and 52), so the chance in the pooled
    cell is closed and it was an input, not a kernel;
    the cache's hit rate and where the wall time goes when the solve is cached, since
    six cached cells on one card ran no faster per cell than three uncached; and the
    multi-phenotype form, the flux in the cell representation rather than on the
    logit: a hidden layer over the vector reads no more than the linear affine
    (round 41), the raw vector as a node embedding through the preprocessor reads
    0.03 under the logit form and under no vector at all (round 43), and reduced to
    64 or 16 principal components it fits on time and reads the affine-alone number
    on the node and the logit form's number only when the vector is in the affine as
    well (round 44). So the representation path carries the vector without cost once
    it is compact and reads nothing from it that the logit does not; what is open is
    a head that reads a representation-borne vector, the deleted gene's own token
    rather than the pooled context, and the vector as its own token.
  \item The two model families, annotation through the graph and the solve through
    the transformer, miss different genes, and their post hoc average reads 0.954 under
    the fold protocol (the GO convolution with the scalable form). The one model
    tried, the solve's columns on the node beside GO under the convolution, reads the
    GO-alone number (0.909), so the average's gain is not the graph model's to take
    from those columns, and the transformer with GO on the node beside the solve in
    the affine reads 0.866, the round-26 cost with the solve as with the ratios
    (round 47); GO on the logit through the affine, the path the solve takes, costs
    nothing and adds nothing (0.901 with the vector, round 50), the affine
    memorizing the training genes through 2,362 binary columns by epoch 20, and
    neither weight decay (round 59) nor reducing GO to 64 components before the
    affine (round 60: 0.906, the solve-alone number, fitting on time) recovers any of
    the average; so on this backbone the two families stay two models, and what is
    untested is a learned weighting of their logits and the transformer's affine
    read of the solve beside a graph-convolved GO token, while the average's number
    carries the selection of the best of eleven.
  \item The layer that solves has the wrong shape for a training loop: the program runs
    on the CPU inside the forward pass, the GPU waits on it (0 to 13 percent
    utilization sampled during round 19, against a 1.6 hour cell where the alternating
    layer takes 19 minutes), and the bytes moved are not the cost, the synchronization
    is. With nothing learned in the solve (round 27) the cost leaves the loop
    entirely: the program is solved once per distinct genotype and medium, offline,
    and stored as a feature, and no solver runs in training. Round 31 measured that
    form: the deletion's pFBA vector precomputed and appended to the affine reads
    0.871 pooled alone and 0.901 with the solve statistics at 31 minutes per cell
    against 83 for the solve inside the graph, under the cached-solve head's 0.917,
    with four of six vector cells selecting an epoch before the fitting transition;
    round 33 closed the 0.05 with the encoding alone: as $\log(1 + |v|)$ the vector
    reads 0.896 to 0.919 on twelve of twelve runs, 0.912 pooled, with nothing in the
    graph and 11 minutes a cell, and 0.920 as a fold ensemble. What remains open on
    the form is why standardized columns make the backbone peak before it fits (and
    why one $\log(1 + |v|)$ fold cell in seventeen still did), and the count that
    sizes the store, the distinct (genotype, medium) pairs across the database, which
    is not measured; on this endpoint the store solved on synthetic complete medium
    reads the YPD store's number (round 42), so whether the medium axis of the store
    carries anything is a question for a phenotype scored across media.
    Where a solve must stay in the loop, the fine-tuning form with learned ceilings,
    three constructions would remove the synchronization, in order of how much they
    change: a batched first-order solve on the card, which NVIDIA's cuOpt offers as
    PDLP with a batch mode for many small programs at a benchmark tolerance of
    $10^{-4}$ relative; a per-genotype basis warm start of the simplex through HiGHS's
    own interface, which keeps the exact solve and the exact duals; a smooth
    interior-point solve at a fixed barrier, which changes what the layer computes
    and belongs to its own rounds. The read that had to come first is in
    (Section~\ref{sec:solve-tolerance}): the program's argmax is degenerate, another
    exact vertex already breaks a head trained on the reference vertex (the probe at
    chance), and a point inside the optimal face, PDLP's or the interior point's, is
    as rankable as a vertex when the head is trained on it, and rounds 36 and 38
    trained the cell over an interior-point store to 0.911 and over the PDLP store,
    the card's algorithm, to 0.907, the pFBA store's number, where the growth
    program's own vertex trained to 0.840. So the solver is part of the module's
    definition, the store and the head must be built from one solver, the card's
    first-order point is a store, and across tolerances of one first-order solver
    the point is the same point: PDLP at $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-6}$ move 0.6 percent of
    the reactions against each other and a probe fitted on either scores the other
    at its own within-solver number (0.709 and 0.717 against 0.716 and 0.702), where
    across solvers the same probe loses 0.05 to 0.35 (Section~\ref{sec:solve-tolerance});
    the cell trains to the same number over the $10^{-6}$ store (round 53), and the
    $10^{-8}$ store with 320 programs uncertified reads 0.02 under it until those rows
    are filled from the looser solves (rounds 55 and 57), so the store's cost is its
    uncertified fraction and a second pass recovers it; what is open is why the dual
    simplex's vertex is the worst store of the four.
  \item The module's own hyperparameters have not been moved on this endpoint: the
    $k_{cat}$ tier (measured against predicted) and the thermodynamic source. Given
    round 15, the $k_{cat}$ tier is the one that sets whether the capacity box holds a
    growing flux at all.
\end{itemize}
