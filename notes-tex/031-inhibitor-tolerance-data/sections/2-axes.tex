\notesec{The cell axis}
A Vanacloig genotype is never a single deletion. The library is the drug-sensitized
\emph{3DeltaAlpha} collection, so every strain carries the queried barcoded deletion on
top of \emph{pdr1}, \emph{pdr3} and \emph{snq2} deletions, which the loader stores as
three constant background perturbations: four genes per genotype, haploid, on S288C.
Hillenmeyer HOM is one KanMX deletion in a homozygous diploid; HET is one copy of a gene
removed from a diploid, a dosage halving that reaches the essential genes HOM cannot. A
model that reads the genotype as a gene set therefore sees four genes per Vanacloig
strain and one per Hillenmeyer strain, and the three background genes are absent from
Vanacloig's queried set. The cell representation differs on background, ploidy and
perturbation class at once, before any environment is considered.

\notesec{The environment axis}
Two axes are constant within each dataset and opposite between them. Vanacloig is
anaerobic in SynBase, a defined synthetic hydrolysate mimic at pH 5.0 set with HCl;
Hillenmeyer is aerobic in YPD on 86\% of HOM records and 99\% of HET records, with SC
dropouts, SD and YP glycerol for the rest. Neither oxygen nor base medium varies inside
its own dataset, so a joint model cannot learn their effect from these two datasets; it
can only learn a dataset offset. Temperature is 30\,C on every Vanacloig record and a
typed provenance gap on 98\% of Hillenmeyer records. Duration is in generations on both
sides on different scales, 6.5 against mostly 20. Dose is the third asymmetry: Vanacloig
records an IC30 basis with no molar value for 39 of its 41 compounds, Hillenmeyer a
numeric dose on 98\% of records, so a dose feature is a different kind of number in the
two sources. Every Vanacloig record carries exactly one dosed compound; 18\% of HOM
records carry none (media swaps, temperature, radiation, pH) and 1\% carry two.

\notesec{The readout axis}
All three are pooled competitive-growth barcode assays, but the statistic differs:
Vanacloig stores a log2 ratio of treated to control CPM, averaged over three batch
replicates with a sample SD on every record; HOM stores a z-score against a matched
control set and HET a log2 ratio, with a served SD on 35\% and 24\% of records because
65\% and 76\% of records come from a single array. The sign is opposite to Vanacloig's
(positive is a defect in Hillenmeyer). The label must be per dataset, or reduced to a
sign or a rank, before anything is pooled.

\notesec{Overlap}
Genes overlap almost completely and compounds almost not at all
(Table~\ref{tab:overlap}). Sodium acetate (Vanacloig) and acetic acid (Hillenmeyer) are
different compound entities and do not join; the same holds for sodium butyrate against
any butyric acid record. Benomyl is dosed at 10\,$\mu$g/mL on both sides.
Hillenmeyer's ferulic acid is 3.95\,$\mu$M in HET only, against Vanacloig's IC30.
