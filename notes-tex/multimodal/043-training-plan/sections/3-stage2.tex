\section{Stage 2: fine-tune per target\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:stage2}

\subsection{The starting point\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:ftstart}

A fine-tune starts from $\theta^{(1)}$, the parameters of a stage-1 run at its last epoch,
which the trainer already checkpoints as the end of the registered window; validation never
picks the checkpoint. The target's standardization is the stage-1 record, matched by its
sha256, since the target's training rows are the same in both stages. The dataset token,
the per-dataset bias and the head carry over; the optimizer state does not: AdamW restarts at
learning rate $\mathrm{lr}_2$ with 100 warmup steps, then constant. The main setting is
$\mathrm{lr}_2 = 10^{-4}$, a third of the stage-1 rate, with nothing frozen; the freezing
policies $\mathcal{F}$ are none, trunk frozen (the token projections and $F_\theta$ fixed, the
operator and heads training) and heads only. These are choices, not findings; the Aurora block
(Sec.~\ref{sec:aurora}) tests them, and decision 16 holds the outcome.

\subsection{The order of targets\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:fttargets}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Kemmeren 2014 alone}, read on Kemmeren's validation fold and on the 72 Sameith
doubles.
\item \textbf{The Sameith singles}: the 82 Kemmeren training records whose genes are the
parents of the 72 doubles. Sameith 2015 Sm is the same arrays and stays excluded; the singles
are Kemmeren's records, pinned to the training folds by primer decision P2. Fine-tuning on the
parents alone asks whether narrowing to them helps the doubles more than the full Kemmeren set.
\item \textbf{The segregant panels}, N'Guessan 2025 then Boocock 2025 pooled, read on their
own validation folds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The matched-steps comparison\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:ftcompare}

For target $s$, three runs share the seed of the stage-1 run they are compared with,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ft}(k):&\quad \theta^{(1)} \text{ after } k_1 \text{ stage-1 steps, then } k \text{ steps on } s,\\
\mathrm{alone}(k_{\mathrm{tot}}):&\quad k_{\mathrm{tot}} = k_1 + k \text{ steps on } s \text{ from scratch},\\
\mathrm{alone}(k):&\quad k \text{ steps on } s \text{ from scratch},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ftruns}
\end{equation}
and the two gains are
\begin{equation}
\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \overline{\operatorname{stat}}^{\,\mathrm{ft}(k)}_s - \overline{\operatorname{stat}}^{\,\mathrm{alone}(k_{\mathrm{tot}})}_s,
\qquad
\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{k} = \overline{\operatorname{stat}}^{\,\mathrm{ft}(k)}_s - \overline{\operatorname{stat}}^{\,\mathrm{alone}(k)}_s,
\label{eq:ftgain}
\end{equation}
each a registered-window mean (\deq{28}) over its own run's epochs. $\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is
the headline: positive means pretraining bought more than the same optimizer steps spent on the
target. $\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{k} > 0$ with $\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{\mathrm{tot}} \le 0$ means the
start helps per fine-tune step but not per total step. One stage-1 run serves every target, so
charging all of $k_1$ to each target makes $\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ the conservative
comparison. The stage-1 model's own window mean on $s$, with no fine-tuning, is reported beside
both.

\notesec{The arithmetic for Kemmeren}
With 151 steps per epoch at $B = 8$, the main fine-tune is $k = 210 \times 151 = 31{,}710$
steps, evaluated every 5 epochs, so its window (epochs 169 to 210) holds 9 evaluations. With
the estimate $k_1 \approx 353{,}150$ of Sec.~\ref{sec:armall}, $k_{\mathrm{tot}} \approx
384{,}860$, or 2{,}549 Kemmeren epochs with the last one cut (\texttt{budget.max\_steps}); both
are fixed once \texttt{all} is prepared. The nearest measured point is the overnight alone run
at 317{,}300 steps: 0.110 on Kemmeren, 0.335 on the doubles (seed 0, job 6152). The singles take
$k = 1{,}100$ steps (100 epochs of 11 steps); their $\mathrm{alone}(k_{\mathrm{tot}})$ control is
the Kemmeren one, which has about 9\% more steps than $k_1 + 1{,}100$, a difference that favors
the control. The segregant panels take $k = 31{,}710$ as well: about 143 epochs of N'Guessan's
1{,}770 training records and 1{,}057 of Boocock's 235.

Three statements are registered before the first fine-tune, each under the rule of
Sec.~\ref{sec:rule} with the interval resampled over the 72 doubles:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Fine-tuning helps the doubles}: $\Delta^{\mathrm{ft}}_{\mathrm{tot}} \ge 0.02$ on the
doubles statistic, positive in at least 5 of 6 seeds, interval excluding zero.
\item \textbf{The fine-tuned model clears the additive null}: its doubles window mean exceeds
0.440, with the interval of the paired difference excluding zero. The singles are in training,
so the null and the model see the same measured parents.
\item \textbf{It clears the fitted rung}: the same against 0.494.
\end{enumerate}
The model's own additive prediction, the sum of its predictions for the two singles, is
reported beside each, so a gain can be split into better singles and better combination. The
doubles are split B and a test set: they are logged during training but enter only these three
statements, read once at the end of the campaign; no choice of arm, $\mathrm{lr}_2$, $\mathcal{F}$
or $k$ reads them, all of which are made on Kemmeren's validation fold.
