\section{Findings\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:findings}

The two questions the document opened with have the following answers on the released
Merzbacher 2025 split, every number at a validation-selected epoch on the 195 held-out
genes with a gene bootstrap, and every gain attributed by a paired, pre-registered read.

\begin{description}
  \item[The held-out number is 0.893 {[0.84, 0.94]}.] A width-12 cell graph transformer
    reading the chromosome-pathway indicator and ProtT5, with FBA's four growth ratios on
    its logit, under Flux Cone Learning's own five-fold ensemble protocol; 0.15 above
    Flux Cone Learning's 0.742 on the same genes and 0.06 above the classical ladder's
    best rung. Its per-cell mean over seven seeds is 0.869.
  \item[The inputs are the number.] The gain over the backbone alone (0.774) is the four
    solver ratios (to 0.821) and the pathway multi-hot beside the protein embedding (to
    0.871); the five gene properties in the same encoding, expression included, add
    nothing, and neither do width, depth, the preprocessor, ESM2, codon frequency or the
    species-LM composite. A two-layer MLP on the same three inputs reads 0.870 per cell in
    six seconds and 0.886 as a fold ensemble; graph convolution over the nine gene graphs
    reads 0.888. Three architectures are one number at this resolution.
  \item[The pathway multi-hot is the indicator.] Split into its blocks, the 197-column
    encoding's gain is the 173 pathway columns: with ProtT5 and the ratios they read
    0.880 on the MLP and 0.890 on the transformer, above the full encoding (0.870 and
    0.871), while the chromosome one-hot (0.809, 0.822) and the seven properties
    (0.798, 0.824) sit at the level of ProtT5 with the ratios. On the transformer the
    pathway columns need the sequence embedding beside them (0.766 alone); on the MLP
    they do not (0.828). The pooled transformer on ProtT5 with the pathway multi-hot and
    no readout reads 0.860. The solve does not substitute for the annotation: the
    deletion's flux vector on ProtT5 alone reads 0.868 pooled, the same vector with
    the pathway block 0.912, and the block with the ratios alone 0.890
    (Section~\ref{sec:round35}).
  \item[Keeping the edge type is the one architectural choice that registers.] Of the
    message-passing forms of MATH-490 and Gene\_Graph on the transformer's three inputs,
    relational attention over the nine graphs as nine edge types reads 0.889, GraphSAGE
    0.887, relational convolution 0.860, graph convolution over the untyped union 0.847
    and the graph isomorphism network 0.705, against the MLP's 0.870. The 1-WL-expressive
    aggregator is the weakest, and the typed-edge attention is level with the
    transformer's best single cell.
  \item[GO through the graph is the highest number in the document.] Two layers of
    graph convolution over the union of the nine gene graphs on the propagated GO
    multi-hot alone, no sequence and no solve, read 0.912, 0.910 and 0.904 at 400
    epochs, 43 seconds per seed, and under the reported five-fold protocol 0.917
    [0.87, 0.96] against the transformer's 0.893 [0.84, 0.94], the folds above on four
    of five and the same 0.917 with three seeds per fold; the curve is flat past 400
    epochs, the sequence embedding and the
    ratios add nothing to it, and on six seeds it reads 0.898 to 0.912 (mean 0.905,
    sd 0.005) with no run that misses. On the ladder GO is
    the strongest single encoding (0.808 linear, 0.844 with a random forest and the
    ratios). The transformer is the one model that does not take it: 0.833 beside
    ProtT5 and a cost of 0.04 beside the pathway block.
  \item[The genes every model misses are the ones the solve misses.] Thirteen of the
    fifteen hardest essential held-out genes have a knockout growth ratio of 1.0 (CDC19
    covered by PYK2, PET9 by AAC1 and AAC3), and the hardest nonessential genes are
    metabolic paralogs (TDH1, TDH2, TDH3, PFK1, PFK2) the annotation vectors make look
    essential; the six transformer arms agree on their failures at a Spearman of 0.4
    to 0.9, and the GO convolution fails elsewhere (0.15 to 0.4 with them), halving the
    misrank on
    FRQ1, AUR1, PGA3, LCB1 and LCB2 and repairing twelve of the misranked paralogs.
    Isozyme redundancy, read the wrong way in both directions, is where the remaining
    AUROC sits, and the two model families miss different halves of it
    (Section~\ref{sec:hard-genes}). Averaging their existing predictions with equal
    weight and nothing fitted scores 0.948 [0.91, 0.98] on the split-file cells (the
    six-seed GO convolution with the six-seed layer-free form, from 0.905 and 0.910
    alone; 0.946 with the scalable form) and 0.954 [0.92, 0.98] under the fold
    protocol, from families at 0.917 and 0.922 alone (the GO convolution's fold models
    with the scalable form's): the maximum of the thirteen and nine post hoc averages
    tried, reported as such; one model over both inputs, the solve's columns beside
    GO under the convolution, reads the GO-alone 0.909 and recovers none of it
    (Section~\ref{sec:family-ensembles}).
  \item[Off the metabolic genes, only the graph transfers.] On the 4,674 genome-wide
    genes the released split never labeled (974 essential by SGD, 3,700 nonessential by
    a fitness record, none with a GEM reaction, so FBA reads exactly 0.5 and label
    origin coincides with class), models trained on the 629 released genes read: the
    MLP on the transformer's inputs 0.509, the transformer's reported recipe 0.574,
    the scalable module form 0.542 (its flux is the wild type on every one of them),
    graph convolution on ProtT5 alone 0.749, on the three inputs 0.744, on GO alone
    0.702, degree and clustering alone through the physical graph 0.753, and the raw
    degree in the physical graph, nothing trained, 0.740 (0.594 on the released 195).
    The GO convolution's margin is a property of the metabolic genes; off them the
    number is the gene's degree in the protein interaction graph, a genome-wide signal
    the metabolic genes barely vary in, which any input through the graph reaches and
    no input without it does. The other way, the
    genome-wide labels as training data leave the GO convolution at 0.913 on the 195
    and cost the three-input models 0.03 to 0.05 (Section~\ref{sec:round30}).
  \item[Structure is not the signal.] The 1-WL test separates 1,118 of 1,121
    single-deletion graphs on the physical graph and all of them on the union, so
    expressivity does not bound a message-passing model here, and the WL kernel, all-ones
    and degree inputs all rank at chance. What is written on the nodes carries the number.
  \item[Accuracy is the wrong statistic.] yeast-GEM's own benchmark reports 0.902 on a
    gene set whose majority class alone gives 0.856, with recall 0.41; FBA on 9.0.2
    reproduces it at 0.907, the released labels agree with the Stanford list on 1,102 of
    1,104 shared genes, and the transformer's balanced accuracy at the loss's decision
    boundary is 0.78 against 0.63 to 0.72 for the solvers.
  \item[The solve carries the module's value, and the alternating layer cannot compute it.]
    Routed through the module's growth column the prediction never beats the backbone
    alone. The layer's alternating projections never reach the polytope the solve
    optimizes over: with the balance exact the box is violated on 58 percent of reactions,
    the second law on half, the protein pool by up to seven times, the dissipation limit
    by three; the growth column is a free signed coordinate the readout learns to use
    with either sign; the $k_{cat}$ content is unread (the shuffled table matches); and
    handed the solve's own point, the anchored constraint set discards it.
  \item[The enzyme-capacity box as parameterized contains no growing flux.] A linear
    program under the layer's bounds is infeasible for every genotype, because the
    predicted $k_{cat}$ tier times the nominal abundances caps the median reaction at
    0.084 mmol/gDW/h. Every anchored arm in this document read a flux off the polytope
    for that reason. Scaled by 30 or more the box admits growth; the ceilings still bind
    up to a scale near 3,000.
  \item[On the polytope, the module helps.] On the GEM's own box the solve survives the
    projections and the program that solves is feasible and ranks the held-out genes at
    0.73 before training, above FBA's 0.66; the gated module cell reaches the level of the
    solve appended as an input, 0.816 by warm start and 0.824 by the layer that solves,
    three of three seeds above the round-4 gated cell, with the growth column entering
    through a positive gate. On six seeds the two gated forms read 0.814 (GEM box) and
    0.804 (elastic program, feasible by construction), twelve seeds of twelve above the
    gated cell without the solve and 0.01 to 0.02 under the affine.
  \item[The ceilings cannot carry the ranking, so the forced growth column cannot be
    trained.] With the program elastic, feasible for every genotype and its gradient
    arriving on nine genotypes in ten, the forced column still reads 0.70 to 0.74 and
    its training loss never leaves 1.25, under ten times the learning rate, a deeper
    head, a wider reach and two ceiling scales. The dual-overlap probe measures why: the
    same six gene-owned ceilings bind whichever gene is deleted (median Jaccard 1.0), and
    the deleted gene's own reactions never bind because the deletion has closed them, so
    the head can move one growth level for every genotype and cannot move one genotype
    against another.
  \item[The solve's flux vector, read by a linear head, is the module's number.] The
    gated elastic cell with a zero-initialized linear head over $\log(1 + |v^*|)$ of the
    solved flux reads 0.883, 0.853 and 0.868 on the ProtT5 backbone, the first module
    cell to pass a pre-registered read, 0.047 above the FBA affine on that backbone and
    with the growth column's gate ending near zero. Over the 274 exchange fluxes alone it
    reads 0.835. On ProtT5 with the pathway multi-hot the same cell reads 0.897 and 0.903
    on two seeds, the two highest single cells in the document and above the affine on
    that input (0.890), and 0.716 on a third seed whose training loss never fell. The
    vector carries it: with nothing learned in the layer the same head reads 0.852,
    the ceilings add 0.01 to 0.02, and the gene-owned columns hold it (0.861 against
    0.835 for the exchanges). Under the fold-ensemble protocol on the pathway input it
    reads 0.897 [0.84, 0.95], one number with the reported 0.893, from three fold
    models at 0.90 and two that never fit; three of this form's seventeen cells miss
    the fitting transition within 200 epochs, and the miss is a chance event of the run
    and not of the seed: the unfit seed rerun under the same program fits at epoch 70
    and reads 0.889, the two runs diverging from their first epoch
    (Sections~\ref{sec:round24}, \ref{sec:round25} and~\ref{sec:round29}). The chance
    is the card's kernels: with cuDNN's autotuner off, deterministic kernels on and
    cuBLAS's workspace fixed, two runs of that seed are identical to four decimals at
    every epoch, both fit at epoch 86 and read 0.894, where two free runs of the same
    seed read 0.920 and 0.782, at a five percent cost in wall time
    (Section~\ref{sec:round32}). The form
    that scales, the program solved once per genotype with nothing learned in the
    layer and its flux read through a zero-initialized head, reads 0.897 to 0.917 on
    eight of nine runs over six seeds (pooled 0.917 [0.87, 0.96] on the six-seed
    rerun, above the elastic form's 0.910), and misses the transition on the ninth,
    a seed it had fit in the previous attempt, so the chance is not in the ceilings;
    under the fold protocol it reads 0.912 [0.86, 0.95] with all five folds fit, one
    number with the reported 0.893 (Section~\ref{sec:round27}). Metabolism enters the cell as a
    perturbation-conditional transform that holds mass balance and the bounds exactly,
    costs nothing on the card, and cannot hurt at initialization. With no layer in the
    graph at all, the deletion's pFBA vector precomputed and appended to the affine's
    input (round 31), the cell reads 0.871 pooled alone and 0.901 with the solve
    statistics beside it, at 31 minutes per cell against 83, and four of the six
    vector cells select an epoch before the backbone's fitting transition over a
    default-initialized 4,135-column affine, where the two that select after it read
    0.923 and 0.911 (Section~\ref{sec:round31}). The encoding is the whole of that
    gap: the same vector as $\log(1 + |v|)$ reads 0.896 to 0.919 on twelve of twelve
    runs (0.912 pooled; zero initialization changes nothing, standardized columns
    zero-initialized still peak early), the cached-solve head's number with no layer,
    no solver in training, no missed transition and the lowest spread in the
    document, at 11 minutes alone on a card, and 0.920 [0.87, 0.96] as a fold
    ensemble, the document's highest transformer ensemble, one number with the GO
    convolution's 0.917 (Section~\ref{sec:round33}).
  \item[The solve's argmax is not unique, and the head reads the solver's point.]
    The round-27 program solved for every labeled gene by five solvers gives one
    growth value (to $10^{-5}$, FBA's 0.60 to 0.68 on the 195 whichever solver) and
    five flux vectors: another exact vertex moves 4 percent of the reactions in the
    head's encoding, a point inside the optimal face 13 to 16 percent. A probe fitted
    on the reference vertices reads 0.756 on them and 0.50 to 0.69 on the other
    solvers' vectors, at chance on the other exact vertex; fitted within each solver it
    reads 0.65 to 0.78, the interior point's 0.776 level with the vertex's. The store
    and the head must come from one solver, and a point inside the face, which is
    what a first-order solve on the card returns, ranks as well as a vertex
    (Section~\ref{sec:solve-tolerance}). Trained over such a store the layer-free
    cell reads 0.911 on six of six seeds, the pFBA store's number, and 0.912 as a
    three-seed fold ensemble against the pFBA store's 0.919; over the PDLP store, the
    card's algorithm on the CPU, 0.903 on six of six (pooled 0.907) and 0.909 as a
    fold ensemble; where the growth
    program's own dual-simplex vertex reads 0.840 with two early peaks
    (Section~\ref{sec:round36}): the store can be built by the batched solver on the
    card, and not every point of the face is a good store.
  \item[Where the gradient goes is not the problem.] On the alternating layer the
    gradient reaches the flux layer on every arm that lets it; the backbone's
    preprocessor, three quarters of the parameters, is where fitting continues after the
    validation curve peaks.
\end{description}

What carries forward to a model trained over every phenotype in the database is the
part of this that did not depend on the 195 genes. Metabolism enters as a
perturbation-conditional data transform: the genome-scale program solved once per
genotype and medium on the GEM's own box, cached, and read through a zero-initialized
head over $\log(1 + |v^*|)$, which cost nothing on the card, held mass balance and the
bounds exactly, read 0.90 to 0.92 on eight runs of nine where the affine read 0.89, and
cannot hurt at initialization; the learned enzyme-constrained layer, whose ceilings
added 0.01 to 0.02 at five times the wall time and whose forced growth column could
not be trained, is a fine-tuning module and not a backbone component. Annotation
vectors enter through message passing over the gene graphs and not through the
transformer's token: the GO multi-hot read 0.917 through two layers of convolution
and 0.833 through the transformer, and the pathway block's gain on the transformer
was the one annotation it took. The gene graphs enter as a message-passing input and
not as a regularizer, since off the metabolic genes the models without the graph as
an input fell to chance and the ones with it held 0.75. What the 195 genes could not
decide, which of three architectures on the same inputs is better, the genome-wide
set decided for the graph; what neither set can decide is whether that transfer is
essentiality or the deletion collection's coverage.
