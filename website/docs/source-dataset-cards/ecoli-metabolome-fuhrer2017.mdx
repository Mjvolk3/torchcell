<CardBlurb>

Each record is one strain of the Keio collection, *Escherichia coli* K-12 with one gene
deleted. An extract of the growing culture was measured by flow-injection
time-of-flight mass spectrometry, and each detected ion was scored against its level
across all strains. The dataset holds 3,806 records.

</CardBlurb>

<CardNotation>

$$
g = \{\Delta i\}, \quad i \in \mathcal{G}
\qquad\qquad
(g, e) \mapsto y \in \mathbb{R}^{T}, \quad T = 7{,}534
$$

The genotype holds one deletion $\Delta i$. The environment $e$ is the same for every
record. The phenotype $y$ is one modified z-score per ion: 3,169 ions of the negative
ionization mode and 4,365 of the positive mode.

</CardNotation>

<CardDetails>

Source: Fuhrer et al. 2017, *Mol Syst Biol* 13:907,
[doi:10.15252/msb.20167150](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.20167150). The per-strain
values are deposited in BioStudies under accession S-BSST5.

| Detail | Value | Read from |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Strain collection | Keio knockout collection | Methods, "Biological samples" |
| Reference strain | *E. coli* K-12 BW25113. Fuhrer 2017 does not name it; the cited collection paper does | Baba et al. 2006 |
| Perturbation type | Single-gene deletion: the coding region replaced by a kanamycin cassette | Baba et al. 2006 |
| Medium | Mineral salts medium with glucose and amino acids, liquid | Fuhrer 2017 |
| Temperature | 37 °C | Fuhrer 2017 |
| Culture | 1 ml in 96-deep-well plates, shaking at 300 rpm, harvested in mid-exponential growth | Fuhrer 2017 |
| What $y$ is | Modified z-score of each ion: the ion's intensity minus its median, divided by its standard deviation, both over the whole dataset | Fuhrer 2017 |
| Unit of $y$ | None. No internal standards were used, so there is no absolute concentration | Fuhrer 2017 |
| Replicates | Two independent clones per strain, each extract measured twice, summarized into one score per strain | Methods, "Reproducibility between biological replicates"; BioStudies S-BSST5 |
| Uncertainty reported | None per strain. Across the dataset, 99% of the variability between biological replicates is below a z-score of 2.765 | Fuhrer 2017, Figure EV2A |

</CardDetails>

</DatasetCard>
