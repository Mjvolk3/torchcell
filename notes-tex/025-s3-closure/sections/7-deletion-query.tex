\section{The same identity on a deletion-only query\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:deletion-query}

A second build, 029 (\texttt{experiments/029-solid-growth-ko/}), changes the query and
the build in the way Section~\ref{sec:consequences} asked for and nothing else. The
query keeps only deletion alleles, \texttt{sga\_kanmx\_deletion} and
\texttt{sga\_natmx\_deletion}, at both screening temperatures, and drops the
temperature-sensitive, DAmP and suppressor alleles that the 025 build merged under the
same gene name; the SynthLethDB block requires two perturbation nodes, which removes
the self-pairs that carried a single-gene fitness of 0. The build drops the mean-merge:
every source entry of a genotype is kept beside the others, with its screen, its
temperature, its marker, its standard deviation and its p-value. Figure~\ref{fig:stages}
sets the two builds side by side; Table~\ref{tab:survival} counts what each holds.

\tcfigfit[0.98]{figures/closure_label_policy.pdf}{The two builds from the same served
graph. Right: 025 as built, five full copies on disk, the mean-merge in red discarding
the source values, screens, temperatures, alleles and every source p-value at the step
marked. Left: 029 build 001, deletion alleles only, no merge, every source entry kept
through aggregation, so that the value the trainer reads is chosen by a label policy
at read time rather than fixed at build time.}{fig:stages}

\notesec{A policy in place of a merge}
With every entry kept, a record no longer holds one fitness. A single carries its
Costanzo entries at 26 and 30 degrees under both markers, its Kuzmin query fitness
where the gene was a Kuzmin query, and the converted 0 where SGD lists it as
essential; a double carries both Costanzo orientations and every Kuzmin digenic
screen of the pair; a triple carries one entry per Kuzmin screen. What enters the
identity is a choice among them, and the recompute runs the trigenic identity of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} under four such choices:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{mean of every entry}: the mean over all entries, converted 0 included.
  This is the 025 join replayed on the deletion-only universe.
\item \emph{measured, 0 only if none}: the mean over measured entries; the converted
  0 only for a gene with no measurement at all.
\item \emph{Kuzmin first}: the Kuzmin screen of the triple's own year, then the other
  Kuzmin screen, then Costanzo at 30 degrees, then 26, then the converted 0.
\item \emph{Costanzo first}: Costanzo at 30 degrees, then 26, then Kuzmin, then the
  converted 0.
\end{itemize}

Each policy yields one fitness per gene set, and the recomputed score is compared with
every stored interaction entry; a stored entry is one source row, so the comparison
can also be read per screen. The 299{,}146 triple gene sets carry 309{,}122 stored
trigenic entries, because a gene set reached by both Kuzmin screens keeps both, and
289{,}062 of those have every term of Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} present under a policy:
15{,}229 of the rest are missing one of the three doubles and 8{,}545 one of the three
singles. The comparable count in the 025 build is 352{,}505 of its 376{,}732 triples.

\notesec{What the policies reproduce}
Table~\ref{tab:query-comparison} and Figure~\ref{fig:query} give the result.
Replaying the 025 join on the deletion-only universe, the mean of every entry, gives
$r = 0.435$ digenic and $r = 0.193$ trigenic, within 0.04 of the 025 build's 0.445
and 0.230: the deletion filter alone changes little, because the loss was never in the
alleles. Taking the converted 0 out of every gene that has a measurement moves both
to 0.453 and 0.207. The two ordered policies then pull apart. Kuzmin first gives the
best trigenic reproduction, $r = 0.245$ with slope 0.71, and the worst digenic,
$r = 0.227$; Costanzo first gives the best digenic, $r = 0.508$, and the worst
trigenic, $r = 0.137$. No policy is best at both orders, and the per-screen columns of
Table~\ref{tab:query-comparison} and Figure~\ref{fig:query}b say why. Each screen's
stored score is reproduced by that screen's own fitness and by little else: the
Costanzo 26 degree digenic scores at $r = 0.90$ and the 30 degree scores at 0.75 under
Costanzo first, against 0.01 and 0.08 under Kuzmin first; the Kuzmin 2018 digenic
scores at 0.89 under Kuzmin first, against 0.33 under Costanzo first; the Kuzmin 2018
trigenic scores at 0.52 with slope 0.93 under Kuzmin first, against 0.30. The Kuzmin
2020 rows stay at 0.41 digenic and 0.29 trigenic under every policy, which is hazard
H5 of Section~\ref{sec:hazards}: their scores were computed with a query fitness of
1.0 that no policy over measured entries can select.

What keeps the Kuzmin-first trigenic $r$ near 0.5 rather than near 1 is one missing
measurement, and the published form of the identity can now be run directly to show
that the formula's shape is not the cause. Roles are recoverable from the build: on a
triple exactly one perturbation carries an array strain identifier and the other two
carry the query token, which resolves the array gene, the query pair and the query
strain on all 299{,}146 triples. Evaluating the published asymmetric form against the
symmetric one on identical records (Table~\ref{tab:asymmetric}), the 2018 screen moves
from $r = 0.504$ to $0.511$, a difference of 0.007. The shape is therefore excluded and
the whole remaining gap on that screen is the substitute the closure is forced into for
$f_{ij}$, the pair's own digenic array screen in place of the query strain's fitness,
which is not a record anywhere in either build.

On the 2020 screen the shape does matter, $r = 0.278$ symmetric against $0.325$
published, with the error falling from 0.212 to 0.165, and the reason is the convention
of Section~\ref{sec:strength}. Kuzmin 2020 releases a query fitness on 0.4 percent of
its control rows and scored with 1.0 throughout. The published form needs no query
single at all, so it asks the build for exactly what the source used, while the
symmetric form needs all three and reconstructs values the source never had. The screen
where the source substituted 1.0 is the screen where using the source's own form pays.
It is also computable on more records, 236{,}225 against 231{,}611, because it needs one
double and one single where the symmetric form needs three of each.

That the identity is sensitive to its terms at all is worth checking rather than
assuming. Forcing the array single fitness to 1 as a control costs 0.084 on the 2018
screen and 0.052 on 2020 and drives the 2018 slope to 1.23, so the reconstruction does
respond to a wrong term, and the query double is specifically the one that is missing.

The converted 0 changes character on this query. In 025, 907 of the 1{,}140 essential
genes carried a measured fitness beside the 0, because the temperature-sensitive
alleles were their measurements; in 029, 1{,}116 of them carry nothing else, so the 0
is the only value and every double or triple that would touch one is absent from a
deletion screen by construction. The 0 no longer taints a measured mean; it is a label
for a gene with no record. And with no merge, every one of the 1{,}848{,}623 stored
interaction entries carries its source p-value, so the merged-record hazard H4 does
not exist in this build: the stored p-value orders the normal test on the double's own
colony standard deviation at $\rho = 0.87$ over every digenic entry, and at 0.20 over
the trigenic entries, whose source null pools variances that were never released.

\notesec{Where the bias sits}
The identity does not fail in the join; it fails wherever a score from one screen is
tested against fitness from another. Costanzo's and Kuzmin's scores are each exact
against their own controls and each about as far from the other's as the 025 build's
merged values were from both, so a build that averages the screens reproduces neither,
and a build that ranks them reproduces one. Which one is a decision the query and the
policy make on the modeler's behalf, and Figure~\ref{fig:query}b is the cost of each
choice stated per screen. For the trigenic target the Kuzmin-first policy is the
consistent one: the triples are Kuzmin's, their digenic terms are Kuzmin's, and the
singles are Kuzmin's where Kuzmin measured them, which is what the source did. For a
digenic target the same policy discards Costanzo's standard for the 902 thousand
Costanzo digenic entries in the closure, at $r = 0.08$. A model trained on both orders under one
policy is asked to hold two standards for the same gene at once, and no fitness
representation satisfies both; the per-order metrics of the S3 arm are where that
shows first.

The pinned splits transfer at 79 percent. Of the 37{,}673 validation and 37{,}673 test
triples of the 010 random split, 29{,}842 and 29{,}641 exist in 029; the rest were
measured only with a temperature-sensitive array allele or a non-deletion query.
A 029 arm evaluates on the surviving triples, and its comparison with the 025 arms is
restricted to the same 59{,}483 triples on both sides.
