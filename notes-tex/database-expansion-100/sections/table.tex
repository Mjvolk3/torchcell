\section{What the list adds up to\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:counts}

\input{tables/counts}

All 168 rows carry roughly $1.8\times10^8$ genotype-by-environment instances and
$5.8\times10^8$ individual measurements. Both are lower bounds: rows whose axes could not
be pinned down contribute nothing to either total, and the instance figures marked
$\dagger$ are products of the two axes rather than counts the papers report.

The totals are dominated by a handful of rows, so quoting them without naming those rows
would mislead. de Boer 2020 alone supplies $10^8$ instances, Turco 2023 $3.8\times10^7$ and
Lee 2014 $1.3\times10^7$. Of those three, Turco's contribution is not net new, since it is a
meta-aggregation of screens this table lists separately, and Lee's is not currently reachable
(Sec.~\ref{sec:status}). de Boer's stands, and it is a different kind of instance from the
rest: an 80-base promoter with an expression readout, not a strain with a phenotype.

The honest summary is that this list contains one dataset larger than everything already
built, one larger than everything else here, and about $2\times10^7$ instances spread across
the remaining 157 rows.

Wave 1 reads differently from the list as a whole, which is the point of banding it. Its
class mix is 20 expression and single-cell rows, 10 natural variation, 8 CRISPR library
screens, 5 modality rows and 5 metabolite rows, against a full-list mix in which tolerance
screens are the largest group; only 2 tolerance rows reach wave 1. Its sequence bases shift
the same way: 15 of the 50 are \term{reference-only}, meaning the environment rather than a
strain edit carries the perturbation, which is what a condition-crossed expression layer
looks like.

Instance counts invert across the two waves and the inversion is worth stating before
someone reads it as an error. Wave 1 holds about $1.8\times10^6$ instances against
$3.9\times10^8$ measurements, because its rows are vector-valued: a few hundred strains
each carrying a transcriptome or a proteome. Wave 2 holds far more instances and fewer
numbers per instance. Ranking on instances would therefore have ordered these two waves the
other way round, which is the same argument the measurements column exists to settle.
