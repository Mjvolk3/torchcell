\section{Predicting the last Carruthers round\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:isoprenol}

The task these rows were surveyed for: predict the final design-build-test-learn round
of the Carruthers isoprenol campaign from its earlier rounds, using whatever other
isoprenol data is served. Carruthers is a collaborator, so the campaign's own data is
available; the question is which single dataset from outside it helps.

\subsection{What can help the campaign\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:iso-transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:pcstransfer} is the answer in one page. It holds the thirteen served
isoprenol datasets that are not Carruthers', ranked by how much of that campaign's
target set each one reaches. Carruthers' own four stores are the thing joined to, so
none of them is a row.

The ranking column is a join, not a size. Of the 135 CRISPRi targets the campaign
knocks down, it counts how many each dataset also \emph{perturbs} and how many it
reports a value \emph{for}, and the two are kept apart because they buy different
things. A perturbed target gives a second measurement of that target's effect, which
is what tests a model. A measured target gives that target a feature, which is what
feeds one.

Three groups come out of it, and the order is not the order of record counts:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Yunus 2026 alone perturbs the target set}, 120 of 135, which made it the
transfer candidate (Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-yunus}). Measured, its titer does not rank those
targets the way the campaign does (Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-corr}).
\item \textbf{Three proteome datasets measure most of it}, 111, 111 and 100 targets,
in conditions the campaign never ran. They are feature sources, not labels.
\item \textbf{The two transposon compendia cover 86 targets} with a fitness value for
growth on isoprenol, which is a per-target prior available without a new experiment,
and separately name the catabolism genes (Sec.~\ref{sec:transposon}).
\end{itemize}

Everything below those reaches four targets or fewer. Those rows earn their place by
design rather than by overlap, and the last column says which. Overlap is a
precondition, not evidence of transfer: whether any of these values moves with the
campaign's titer is tested in Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-corr}, and none does reliably.

\subsection{What the served data holds on isoprenol\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:iso-all}

Seventeen of the 32 served \org{P. putida} datasets carry isoprenol, over 2.59 million
records of which 20,264 are isoprenol records. Table~\ref{tab:pcsisodata} is the short
list: what each one would be used for, what one label is, the held-out unit a score
would be read on, and the other datasets posing the same task. It bands by the role
isoprenol plays, because the role decides the question the dataset can answer and only
one of the four is a label for this task:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{As the measured product}, in five titer campaigns. These are the only
candidate labels. Kang 2026 is in this group on a technicality worth knowing: it is an
isoprenyl acetate campaign built on the isoprenol chassis, and 7 of its 26 records
carry isoprenol as the product while 19 carry the ester.
\item \textbf{As the sole carbon source}, in both transposon compendia, which is the
largest isoprenol block in the served data and measures catabolism rather than
production (Sec.~\ref{sec:transposon}).
\item \textbf{As a stressor}, in the three Lim 2025 datasets.
\item \textbf{As the pathway the strain carries}, in the proteome, metabolite and
selection datasets, which measure something else on a strain whose genotype names the
cassette. These join to a campaign by strain rather than carrying a titer.
\end{itemize}

Of the four titer campaigns outside Carruthers, three are ruled out before any
comparison of conditions. Banerjee 2024 holds 20 records across five condition
families, and none of its strains is a CRISPRi knockdown, so there is no target set to
share. de Siqueira 2025 holds 10 records on acetate-tolerized isolates, again not
CRISPRi. Kang 2026's 7 isoprenol records are controls in an ester campaign. That
leaves one.

\subsection{The candidate: Yunus 2026\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:iso-yunus}

\sym{IsoprenolTiterYunus2026} is the candidate on design, and Table~\ref{tab:pcsiso}
is why. Yunus 2026 (doi:\allowbreak 10.1016/\allowbreak j.ymben.\allowbreak
2025.\allowbreak 11.007) selects CRISPRi knockdown targets by FluxRETAP against intuition, in the
engineered isoprenol-producing strain IY1452, and Carruthers is a co-author. Three
things line up and two do not. The measured test of whether it transfers is
Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-corr}, and it does not.

\notesec{The target sets nearly coincide} Every target in the Yunus campaign but one
is also a Carruthers target (Table~\ref{tab:pcsiso}). That is what makes the dataset
unusual: it is not a different experiment that happens to share a product, it is a
second measurement of nearly the same target set.

\notesec{The readout is the same quantity} Same product, same unit, same
quantification method, same 48 hour culture. No unit conversion and no method bridge
stands between the two campaigns. The replicate structure is the one readout axis that
differs, and the difference is in what was released rather than in what was run.
Carruthers reports a per-strain count, 2 to 6 biological replicates, and every record
carries a sample standard deviation. Yunus releases one number per strain with the
replicate count given only as a 3-to-6 range and the dispersion only as error bars, so
the loader stores the conservative lower end of that range and records the missing
dispersion as a typed gap. The uniform 3 in Table~\ref{tab:pcsiso} is therefore a
resolved lower bound, not a reported count, and a weighting scheme must not read it as
one.

\notesec{The modality is the same, the chassis designation is not} Both repress a
native gene by CRISPRi in an isoprenol-producing strain. The served records pin
different chassis, IY1449b for Carruthers and IY1452 for Yunus (Table~\ref{tab:pcsiso}),
and the Yunus loader deliberately does not borrow the IY1449b genotype for IY1452,
because the two are different designations and the paper states no genotype for its
own. A target's effect therefore need not mean the same thing in both.

\notesec{The paired features exist} \sym{CrispriPanelProteomeYunus2026} carries an
absolute Top3 proteome over 253 accessions on the same strains, which is the same
feature modality as \sym{CampaignProteomeCarruthers2025} in
Table~\ref{tab:pcsrows}. A proteome-to-titer map can be fit on both.

\notesec{The conditions differ, and this decides how it is used} Carruthers runs at
24\,\textdegree C in M9-NREL with 20 g/L glucose; Yunus runs at 30\,\textdegree C in
plain M9 salts. The titer ranges differ accordingly, and the Yunus records carry no
uncertainty, because only one number per strain was released and the loader records
that as a typed gap rather than inventing a dispersion.

So Yunus is not extra training rows for absolute titer. The two uses it was a candidate
for were a held-out condition, where a ranking learned on the earlier Carruthers cycles
is scored on Yunus, and a reproducibility bound on the label. Both rest on the two
campaigns ranking the shared targets alike, and they do not: rank agreement over the
120 shared targets is indistinguishable from none (Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-corr}). The
reproducibility question has a different answer than expected. Within Carruthers'
first round the label is highly reproducible, and between the two campaigns it is not,
so the target effect is bound to the condition, the chassis or both, and the served
data cannot say which.

The runner-up is \sym{IsoprenolSelectionMenasalvas2025} in
Table~\ref{tab:pcsrows}, the only other CRISPRi and isoprenol pairing. Its readout is a
categorical biosensor enrichment call rather than a titer, so it is a weaker label and
not the first choice.

\subsection{Does any served readout track the campaign?\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:iso-corr}

Overlap says another dataset reaches a target. It does not say that dataset's value for
the target moves with the campaign's titer, and Table~\ref{tab:pcscorr} tests that
directly.

\notesec{The campaign's effect is a real signal} The per-target effect is the titer of
the campaign's single-knockdown strains, 123 strains over 121 targets. Matched to the
raw mirror's cycle column by target set, 119 of them are DBTL0 and the other four match
no cycle or several, so the effect is a within-round estimate with no batch term.
Replicate error accounts for under 2 percent of the spread between targets: the mean
released standard error is 7.0\,ug/mL against a spread of 73.7\,ug/mL, a reliability
of 0.985. A weak correlation below is therefore not caused by noise in the campaign.

\notesec{No served readout tracks it} Every served gene-level dataset outside the
campaign was read as gene vectors, one per experiment, sample or strain, and each
vector was correlated with the effect over the shared targets. None clears the bar once
the number of vectors a dataset offers is accounted for:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Yunus 2026}, the largest overlap, ranks the 120 shared targets in
unrelated order, $\rho = -0.10$. Its top quartile shares 8 of 30 targets with the
campaign's, against 7.5 by chance, and its bottom quartile shares 3. Its titers run
about five times higher, a median of 858 against 163\,ug/mL. Its own knockdown depth
does not predict the campaign's effect either.
\item \textbf{Growth on isoprenol} in the transposon compendia gives $\rho$ between
$-0.06$ and $+0.01$ over 77 targets (Table~\ref{tab:pcspriors}).
\item \textbf{The best of hundreds} is what hundreds of unrelated vectors produce. The
best of 234 and 310 compendium experiments reaches $\rho = +0.28$ with a best-of
\emph{p} near 0.8, and the best of 321 PRECISE321 samples reaches $+0.25$ at 0.09.
\item \textbf{Lim 2025's stress proteome fold change} is the one best-of \emph{p} below
0.05, $\rho = +0.27$ at 0.028 over four lineages. Against the all-strain fit it falls
to $+0.18$, so it is a lead, not a result.
\end{itemize}

\notesec{One weak lead among the priors} Targets whose transposon mutants show a strong
defect in many experiments give higher first-round titer, $\rho = +0.27$ and $+0.24$ in
the two compendia, and essential targets have a higher median titer than nonessential
ones (Table~\ref{tab:pcspriors}). It does not stand as a finding. The priors were not
chosen in advance, the two compendia share most of their experiments, and the
correlation falls below $+0.10$ against the all-strain fit. Hypothesis (untested):
knocking down a growth-limiting gene diverts carbon from biomass to product, which
would make a fitness prior useful for ranking first-round targets.

\notesec{What it leaves} For predicting the last round, the served data outside the
campaign offers features with no measured relation to the label, and the campaign's own
earlier rounds remain the training signal. The cycle label that split needs is the
blocker of Sec.~\ref{sec:iso-cycle}.

\subsection{The blocker: the cycle label is not in the graph\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:iso-cycle}

The task needs a cycle label, to train on the earlier rounds and test on the last one.
No served Carruthers record carries one. Table~\ref{tab:pcscycle} searches every record
in all four Carruthers stores for a DBTL token anywhere in its serialization and finds
none, and \texttt{screen\_id} is empty throughout.

The loader's record identity is one per construct and DBTL cycle, so the cycle is
implied by the record set and was never written onto a record. From the graph alone the
final round cannot be separated from the earlier ones, which means the task as stated
cannot be set up from a query.

It is recoverable. The raw mirror's Dryad culture metadata carries a
\texttt{DBTL\_Cycle} column, and Table~\ref{tab:pcsrawcycle} gives its distribution.
The join is on the construct name, so recovering the label is a preprocessing step
rather than a re-ingest. It is still a loader gap worth filing: a campaign whose entire
structure is the cycle should not lose the cycle on ingest.
