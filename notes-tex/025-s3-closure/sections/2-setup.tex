\notesec{The sources}
Costanzo 2016 contributes single-mutant fitness at 26 and 30 degrees, double-mutant
fitness with a colony standard deviation, and a digenic score with a p-value, one row
per query-array pair per screen, 12.2 million double rows over four released files.
Its single-mutant fitness is not screen-specific: the supplementary text
(\texttt{costanzoGlobalGeneticInteraction2016/si/si1.md} line 94) states that array
strains were ``based on an average of 350 replicate control screens'' and query
strains on 17, so $f_a$ and $f_b$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} are genome-wide standards.
Kuzmin 2018 and Kuzmin 2020 contribute single, double and triple fitness, digenic and
trigenic scores and their p-values, one row per query-array pair, 501 thousand and
1.4 million rows. Their array single-mutant fitness is taken from Costanzo 2016
(\texttt{kuzminSystematicAnalysisComplex2018/si/si1.md} line 135: ``single mutant
fitness estimates available from a previous study''), and the two digenic terms of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} come from the single-mutant control query screened ``in the same
batch'' as the double-mutant query (line 75). SGD essentiality contributes a label
with no fitness measurement, and SynthLethDB a synthetic-lethal pair with none.

\notesec{The build}
\texttt{CompositeFitnessConverter} turns an essentiality or synthetic-lethality entry
into a fitness of 0. \texttt{MeanExperimentDeduplicator}
(\texttt{torchcell/data/mean\_experiment\_deduplicate.py}) then merges every entry
that shares an experiment type and a sorted gene set: fitness becomes the mean and
its standard deviation the root mean square (lines 308 to 316); an interaction score
becomes the mean, and its p-value becomes a two-sided $t$-test of the merged scores
against zero with $k-1$ degrees of freedom (lines 480 to 494), which does not read the
source p-values at all. \texttt{GenotypeAggregator} groups what remains into one
record per genotype, 13{,}525{,}071 of them. The trainer reads one fitness value and
one interaction value per record; a record that still carries two fitness values,
which happens when a SynthLethDB entry lists the same gene twice and so escapes the
merge key, is masked (three records, Section~\ref{sec:hazards}).

\notesec{The pool}
Table~\ref{tab:composition} lists what the join produced, by perturbation order and by
the set of sources behind each record's fitness. Of the 5{,}694 singles, 1{,}140
carry an SGD essentiality entry, and 233 of those carry nothing else. Of the
739{,}219 closure doubles, 697{,}825 (94.4 percent) are merged from two or more
screens, most often the two orientations and two temperatures at which Costanzo
screened the same pair. Of the 376{,}732 triples, 12{,}914 are merged, mostly the
same triple reached from two different double-mutant queries.
