\section{Thermodynamic coverage, and two corrections\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:coverage}

\subsection{The recomputation\secstatus{todo}}

Formation energies were recomputed from eQuilibrator's component contribution, which
returns a covariance and applies to any organism because it works from structure rather
than from a curated per-organism file. Summing the resulting table reproduces
eQuilibrator's own reaction call to $6.8\times10^{-13}$\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, so the table is
arithmetically correct. On the chemical reactions both tables cover it agrees with the
shipped file to a median of 16.25\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, which is worse than the shipped file
agrees with itself, and is expected once that file is read for what it is: yeast-GEM's
metabolite and reaction columns come from three sources (yETFL, ModelSEED, dG-predictor)
under three different matching keys, one of them row position.

\textbf{The shipped columns are also in kilocalories and labeled kilojoules.} They are
yETFL's values, from Oftadeh et al.\ 2021, copied by yeast-GEM's own curation script with
no conversion, and yETFL carries \texttt{thermo\_units = 'kcal/mol'};
regressing our kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ formation energies on yETFL's over 1{,}927 shared metabolites
gives a slope of 4.187 at Pearson $r = 0.987$. The 33 reactions taken from dG-predictor are
in kilojoules, so the reaction column mixes two unit systems. Correcting the unit and
matching the compartment conditions closes the \emph{transport} arm of the disagreement,
median 17.4 to 5.6\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ at $r$ 0.30 to 0.75, and leaves the single-compartment
cloud where it was, 16.25 to 17.60. Only the second is a disagreement about thermodynamics.
Every arm below read that column as kilojoules; \texttt{\_read\_delta\_g\_csv} now
multiplies by 4.184 at parse time, so no later code path can hold a kcal number and call it
kJ. The 33 reaction-column values that came from dG-predictor are already kJ and are
over-converted by that factor; they supply 6 of 4{,}131 reactions in the union's
last-resort fill and nothing else.

The gain is 785 reactions with a real standard error, median 2.30\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$, and the
rank-590 covariance factor the layer had recorded as unavailable. The cost is reaction
coverage: 66.4 percent against the shipped file's 84.9.

\subsection{Correction: most of the shortfall was the proton\secstatus{todo}}

Reaction coverage was 42.5 percent, and that number was read as the price of an
organism-independent method. Ranking metabolites by how many otherwise-priceable reactions
each blocks says otherwise: the top twelve blockers were all H$^+$, in twelve compartments,
blocking 1{,}477 reaction slots between them.

\textbf{The proton is the reference of the Legendre transform, not a compound to be priced
by it.} \texttt{standard\_dg\_formation} returns nothing for it, correctly, because a bare
proton has no group decomposition. Left as a missing value it deleted every reaction that
moves a proton. Setting it to zero, which reproduces eQuilibrator's own reaction call to
0.0000\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ on four proton-carrying balanced reactions, takes coverage from
1{,}755 reactions to \textbf{2{,}741}, a gain of 986. It does not move the union with the
shipped table, which stays at 3{,}716: every added reaction was already covered, so what
improved is independent confirmation rather than coverage.

The fix carries a known error that is worth more attention than the gain. Zero is exact
within one compartment and wrong across a membrane, where $RT\ln(10)\,\Delta\mathrm{pH}$
per proton is the entire driving force. \textbf{183 reactions move a proton between
compartments and 170 are now priced}, each missing that term: previously unpriced, now
confidently wrong.

\textbf{That term now exists as a separate table.}
\texttt{torchcell/metabolism/transport.py} evaluates
$\Delta_r G^t_j = -N_H RT\ln(10)\,\Delta\mathrm{pH} - Fq\,\Delta\Phi$ per reaction from the
stoichiometry, each metabolite's compartment and its charge, giving a nonzero term on 326
of the 1{,}468 reactions that cross a membrane. Against yeast-GEM's own column, converted
out of kcal, the sign agrees on 84.7 percent of the 287 reactions nonzero in both at
Pearson $r = 0.804$ (\texttt{results/transport\_terms.json}). It is a configuration field,
\texttt{transport\_delta\_g\_source}, and it defaults to \texttt{off}, so every arm reported
in this document still carries the zero. The 613 reactions with a nonzero shipped term and a
zero computed one are the protonation contribution to $N_H$, which needs a species
distribution per transported compound and is not computed.

\subsection{The remaining shortfall is not a mapping gap\secstatus{todo}}

The next reading is that identifiers are failing to resolve, and that better mapping would
close what is left. An audit that implements every available recovery route measures
otherwise.

\textbf{There are two loss channels, and the summary reported only one.} The 332
``unresolved'' metabolites are the accession-match failures. A further \textbf{361
metabolites match a cache record that carries no structure and no group vector}, so
\texttt{standard\_dg\_formation} returns nothing while the tier histogram counts them as
resolved. Total unpriced is \textbf{693}, not 332. The baseline is 2{,}113 of 2{,}806
metabolites, 75.30 percent, and 2{,}741 of 4{,}131 reactions, 66.35 percent.

\textbf{Total recovery is 28 metabolites and 46 reactions, 1.12 percentage points.} The
extra namespaces are worth exactly one chemical, hydrogen sulfide. The census explains why:
the model carries chebi on 2{,}402 metabolites and metanetx on 2{,}251, but biocyc on 29,
seed on 16, reactome on 2, and no hmdb, lipidmaps or swisslipid at all. Of the 332
unmatched, \textbf{181 of the 192 distinct chemicals carry no chemical annotation
whatsoever}, and the class breakdown is overwhelmingly lipid: phosphatidylinositol 135,
cardiolipin 62, triacylglycerol 34, phosphatidate 25.

Of the 665 still unpriced, 235 (35.3 percent) are a real ceiling: 185 carry a placeholder
formula with an R, X or $*$ so no definite structure exists, and 50 are known to
eQuilibrator but have no group decomposition. The other 430 (64.7 percent) have a definite
formula and no usable structure anywhere, which is a structure-curation gap rather than a
limit of the method.

\textbf{Most of that gap is now closed.} 409 of the 430 are lipids whose acyl composition
is written into the name, and \texttt{lipid\_structure\_writer.py} parses 168 of the 206
distinct names, writes a structure for each, and reproduces yeast-GEM's own formula on all
168. \textbf{347 of the 409 metabolite rows are priced}, 289 by finding a compound
eQuilibrator already holds and 58 through the external p$K_a$ route
(\texttt{results/lipid\_coverage.json}). The eQuilibrator recomputation moves from 66.35 to
86.27 percent of reactions and passes the shipped column's 84.85, while the union with the
shipped table does not move at all, since that table already prices these lipids by name.
The gain is method consistency rather than coverage. Two conditions hold the values out of
the built table for now: they are all at pH 7.2 and need re-transforming per compartment,
and the 48 names priced only through the external route sit 777.5\,kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ off a
cache value because they were written as the neutral parent rather than the major
microspecies, a fix that is in progress.

\subsection{A correctness bug in the shipped build, found by the same guard\secstatus{todo}}

The InChIKey route initially reported a gain six times larger, and it was wrong. MetaNetX
writes \texttt{[*]} dummy atoms for polymers and conjugates; RDKit returns an \emph{empty}
InChIKey for these rather than failing; an empty key reaches the cache as \texttt{LIKE
'\%'} and matches the entire compound table. \texttt{Ala-tRNA(Ala)} was being priced as
water.

The guard that fixes it, comparing heavy-atom composition against the GEM formula with
hydrogen excluded because protonation state legitimately differs, then finds the same class
of error in the build already in use: \textbf{31 metabolites currently carry a formation
energy from a compound whose heavy-atom composition disagrees with the GEM formula}, and 14
of the covered reactions touch one. Every dolichol species is matched to a C25
pentaprenyl against the GEM's C20 tetraprenyl; \texttt{glycogen} is matched to a C24
tetramer against a C6 monomer; \texttt{protein}, \texttt{carbohydrate} and \texttt{sterols}
are matched to concrete molecules.

This does not raise coverage and it is worth more than coverage: it removes 31 wrong
numbers. It is not yet applied to \texttt{resolve\_compound}, and it should be.

\subsection{Where the ChemAxon license actually binds\secstatus{todo}}

eQuilibrator's component contribution needs a pKa and majority-microspecies decomposition
for any compound it has never seen, and it obtains that by shelling out to ChemAxon's
\texttt{cxcalc}. A compound already in the compound cache ships with that decomposition
precomputed, so no license is involved. The license binds only on compound \emph{creation}.

\textbf{Two corrections are recorded here rather than folded away.} An earlier account of
this work stated that the blocker does not apply to betaxanthin because all of the pathway's
intermediates are in the cache. A probe on 2026-09-04 then reported three of the five as
absent. The probe was wrong for a reason of its own: it matched the full InChI string, and
the cache stores the InChI of the major microspecies at pH 7, whose final InChIKey block
records the protonation state. Matching on the first two InChIKey blocks, the skeleton and
stereochemistry, finds all five, each with exactly one cache compound, a group vector and
ChemAxon pKas. The first account was right in its conclusion and wrong in its evidence; the
probe was wrong in both.

\textbf{All five are priced, so the license binds nowhere on this pathway.} A thermodynamic
statement about the betaxanthin route is available from the cache alone, with the three
pathway-specific compounds carrying a standard deviation near 8.5 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ against
4.3 to 7.5 for the two native precursors.

This does not affect any number reported elsewhere in this document, because the flux layer
reads yeast-GEM's shipped table and the pathway is heterologous, hence absent from the GEM
entirely.

For a genuinely novel compound the constraint has also loosened. Upstream
\texttt{equilibrator-assets} merged support on 2026-08-15 for creating compounds without
ChemAxon when pKa values are supplied, via \texttt{pkas\_provided\_externally} and
\texttt{specified\_pkas}. That is master-only; the pinned release 0.6.0 does not contain it.
What the interface needs is an unannotated list of macroscopic pKa floats, which is a
weaker requirement than the site-annotated micro-pKa the harder tools produce. That route
was benchmarked on 2026-09-10 against 300 cache compounds whose stored ChemAxon constants
serve as ground truth: Uni-pKa on its simple enumeration template (Luo et al., \emph{JACS
Au} 4:3451--3465, 2024), run locally on Apache-2.0 weights, moves the pH 7.2 formation
energy by 0.91 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ mean absolute error, median 0.16 and 90th percentile 2.84,
well inside the 4.3 to 8.8 kJ\,mol$^{-1}$ uncertainty eQuilibrator itself attaches to the
betaxanthin compounds. MolGpKa reads 1.21 and pKasolver-lite 2.52. So the license is
replaceable. What the route cannot do on its own is enlarge the
model: only 16 of the 665 unpriced yeast-GEM metabolites lack nothing but pKas, and the 409
that could follow needed the lipid structure writer above. With it, the measured reach is
347 of 665, 52.2 percent. The benchmark, the bridge audit and the reach analysis are in the
notes \texttt{experiments.026-metabolism-flux.external-pka-route} and
\texttt{experiments.026-metabolism-flux.lipid-structure-writer}.
