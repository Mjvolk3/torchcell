\section{What the evidence leaves open\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:next}

\pfstep{A plate-aware null} Mulleder 2016 and Messner 2023 come from one laboratory
on one prototrophic knockout collection, and both were measured plate by plate. A
plate effect shared by the two would read as a protein-to-amino-acid signal under a
deletion-label permutation, because permuting deletions also permutes plates. The
enzyme identities at the top of every strong read argue for biology, but the test is a
permutation within plate, which needs the plate assignment of each strain on both sides
and has not been run.

\pfstep{The encoder's cell vector} The proteome carries the amino-acid pools; whether
the v14 encoder's hidden state does is the question that decides the joint head. The
read is the same ridge, from the encoder's cell vector for each deletion to the
Mulleder amino acids on the same shared deletions, against the same permutation null.
A vector that reads near the proteome's $0.4$ has learned the metabolic phenotype on the
way to the proteome; one that reads near zero has not, and the metabolic head would be
fitting the encoder rather than reading it.

\pfstep{A joint head on the shared deletions} Some 4{,}400 deletions carry both a
Messner proteome and a Mulleder amino-acid vector. A second node-level head on the
amino acids, trained with the proteome head on those deletions, is the direct test of
whether the two heads help one another, scored by the proteome's per-feature Pearson
and the amino acids' out-of-fold Pearson against each head alone.

\pfstep{Spearman for the single-gene read} The maximum $|r|$ over features widens its
permutation null under heavy-tailed metabolite values, which is what makes several
Kemmeren amino acids show hundreds of features past FDR with a maximum under its null.
A rank correlation removes the tails; it is the fix if the single-gene read is ever
needed on its own rather than as the illustration it is here.
