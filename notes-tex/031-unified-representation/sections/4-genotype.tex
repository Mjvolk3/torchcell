\section{The genotype axis resolves\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:genotype}

A homozygous diploid deletion can be written as the haploid genotype plus a statement of
ploidy, because both copies are gone. A heterozygous deletion cannot, because one copy
survives and the gene sits at half dose. The question is whether the schema can express that
and whether the modeling stack can consume it. The two answers differ.

\notesec{The schema already carries it}
Heterozygosity is stored in two parts. A genome-wide baseline sits on the reference genome's
ploidy field, which is the copy number of every unperturbed gene, and a per-locus deviation
sits on an engineered copy-number perturbation carrying the copies present and the copies in
the reference. That extension was made for exactly this case. Read from the built stores,
all five datasets round-trip: Vanacloig and Wildenhain are haploid with deletion leaves,
the Hillenmeyer homozygous arm and the Hoepfner homozygous arm are diploid with deletion
leaves, and the Hillenmeyer heterozygous arm and the Hoepfner heterozygous arm are diploid
with a one-of-two copy-number leaf marked present. Heterozygous and homozygous records are
distinguishable from the stored record alone on three redundant signals: the perturbation
class, the state field, and whether the copy-number fields exist.

\notesec{The tensor layer discards it, and that is a correctness problem}
The processor that turns a genotype into model input reads exactly one field off every
perturbation, the systematic gene name. Neither copy number nor ploidy appears anywhere in
the dataset-to-tensor layer. A heterozygous record and a homozygous record for the same gene
therefore produce byte-identical input, one index and one boolean each.

That matters quantitatively because Hoepfner measures both. Its two arms share 4{,}885 genes
and 147 compounds, and \textbf{648{,}977 gene-by-compound cells are measured both
heterozygous and homozygous}. Under the current representation those 648{,}977 pairs are
identical inputs carrying two different measured responses, which is label noise rather than
a missed feature.

\notesec{One channel closes it}
A per-gene functional dose, copies present over copies in the reference, is lossless for
these five datasets on the dosage axis, because every perturbation in all five is either a
full deletion or a one-of-two engineered variant. It needs no separate ploidy feature,
because the vector's background level is the ploidy: a haploid deletion sits at zero against
a background of one and a homozygous diploid deletion sits at zero against a background of
two, so the two remain distinguishable. It is a strict generalization of the present
boolean, since every current dataset emits zero for a deletion and reproduces current
behavior exactly.

Three things the channel does not carry, all measured rather than supposed. Cassette identity
is lost, and this is not a detail: a KanMX replacement inserts a heterologous gene that is
not in the S288C universe, so no vector over yeast genes can hold it. Barcode and collection
are lost, and they are the readout identity in a pooled assay. And perturbation cardinality
becomes a strain property rather than a constant, since Vanacloig contributes four indices per
record against one for the others. Allele classes off the dosage axis, temperature-sensitive,
DAmP and CRISPR interference, sit at full copy number and are functionally compromised, so a
dose vector cannot hold them either; none of the four datasets contains one, and they are out
of scope for this phase (Section~\ref{sec:question}). When they are needed the natural
extension is a second per-gene channel for the expression-modulation axis the schema already
distinguishes from the dosage axis.

\notesec{A flagged risk for the metabolic module}
The flux layer hard-zeros a deleted gene's slot in the genome-scale model. A heterozygous
deletion is not a zero there, so admitting heterozygous data without changing that step
asserts a full knockout of a gene that still has a working copy. The fix is the same quantity
the operator uses: the layer reads the functional dose $d_i$ rather than a presence bit
(Section~\ref{sec:model}).
