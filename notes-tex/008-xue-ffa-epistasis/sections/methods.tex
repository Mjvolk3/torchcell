\section{Methods\secstatus{todo}}

\subsection*{Data and normalization}

Titers for five fatty acid species and their sum were taken from the source study's
supplementary tables for the base strain and for all 175 deletion combinations. Each strain
contributes three replicate measurements per species, in milligrams per liter at a single
endpoint. Every titer is divided by the base strain's titer for the same species in the
same experiment, so all values below are ratios and the base strain is 1 by construction.
The sum, referred to as total titer, is formed per replicate before averaging rather than
by summing the five published species means, so it is a measured quantity and not a sixth
derived one. Loading and normalization are in
\file{experiments/008-xue-ffa/scripts/free_fatty_acid_interactions.py}.

The released measurements carry no biomass or optical density column, so no quantity in
this document is normalized to cell number. Every $f$ is a volumetric titer ratio, and a
deletion that lowers final biomass is not separable from one that lowers output per cell.
This is a property of the released data rather than a choice made here, and it bounds what
the interaction scores mean: they are interactions on volumetric titer, which is the
quantity a titer-screened campaign selects on.

\subsection*{Epistasis models}

An interaction score is a residual, and a residual is only defined once the expectation is
written down. The four models below write down four different expectations for the same
seven measured strains, so a triple can be an interaction under one and not under another
without either fit being wrong. Figure~\ref{fig:model-intuition} draws the four side by
side on one worked pair of deletions.

\notesec{Setting} Write $f_S$ for the titer of the strain carrying deletion set $S$, divided
by the titer of the base strain in the same experiment, so $f_\emptyset = 1$ by
construction. Single, double and triple deletions are $f_i$, $f_{ij}$ and $f_{ijk}$. All
four models are fit on these same normalized quantities, and none of them sees a
combination the design did not build.

\notesec{Multiplicative} The expected phenotype of a combination is the product of its
parts, which is the null of the yeast genetic interaction maps:
%
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{\times}_{ij} = f_{ij} - f_i f_j ,
\qquad
\tau^{\times}_{ijk} = f_{ijk} - f_{ij} f_k - f_{ik} f_j - f_{jk} f_i + 2 f_i f_j f_k .
\label{eq:mult}
\end{equation}
%
Independence on this scale means that a deletion multiplies titer by the same factor
whatever else is deleted. These are Eqs.~\ref{eq:eps} and \ref{eq:tau}, and this is the
model every figure uses unless it says otherwise
(\file{free_fatty_acid_interactions.py}).

\notesec{Additive} The same construction with sums in place of products:
%
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon^{+}_{ij} = f_{ij} - f_i - f_j + 1 ,
\qquad
\tau^{+}_{ijk} = f_{ijk} - (f_{ij} + f_{ik} + f_{jk}) + (f_i + f_j + f_k) - 1 .
\label{eq:add}
\end{equation}
%
Independence here means that a deletion shifts titer by the same amount whatever else is
deleted. The $+1$ and $-1$ are the base strain entering at $f_\emptyset = 1$
(\file{additive_free_fatty_acid_interactions.py}).

\notesec{GLM log-link} A generalized linear model with a Gamma family and a log link, fit
on the replicate-level titers rather than on strain means, with $x_i \in \{0,1\}$ marking
deletion $i$ and $r$ indexing replicate:
%
\begin{equation}
\log \mathbb{E}[y_{S,r}] = \mu + \sum_i \alpha_i x_i + \sum_{i<j} \beta_{ij} x_i x_j
+ \sum_{i<j<k} \gamma_{ijk} x_i x_j x_k + \rho_r .
\label{eq:glm}
\end{equation}
%
The interaction is $\gamma_{ijk}$. The base strain is excluded from the fit and enters
through $\mu$. Gamma sets the variance proportional to the square of the mean, which is the
right shape for a titer that cannot go negative and whose spread grows with its level
(\file{glm_log_link_epistatic_interactions.py}).

\notesec{Log-OLS} Ordinary least squares on log titer differenced against the base strain
within each replicate, with $\delta = 10^{-6}$ a pseudocount:
%
\begin{equation}
g_{S,r} = \log(y_{S,r} + \delta) - \log(\bar{y}_{\emptyset,r} + \delta) ,
\label{eq:logols}
\end{equation}
%
regressed on the same saturated design as Eq.~\ref{eq:glm}. Its three-way coefficient
estimates the saturated log-scale interaction
%
\begin{equation}
\tau^{\log}_{ijk} = g_{ijk} - g_{ij} - g_{ik} - g_{jk} + g_i + g_j + g_k ,
\qquad g_S = \log f_S ,
\label{eq:logtau}
\end{equation}
%
and that equivalence, which Fig.~\ref{fig:models}c,d rests on, is checked in code rather
than assumed: \file{linear_vs_log_scale_sign_test.py} recomputes Eq.~\ref{eq:logtau} from
the strain means and raises unless it agrees with both fitted log-scale models at
$r > 0.99$ (\file{log_ols_wt_differencing_epistatic_interactions.py}).

\notesec{The scale is the first tradeoff, and it is not a matter of taste} Equations
\ref{eq:mult} and \ref{eq:logtau} are different quantities, not two estimates of one
quantity. They agree in magnitude across the 119 testable triples at $r = 0.91$ and
Spearman $\rho = 0.84$ and disagree near zero, which is exactly where a call is made: 15 of
the 27 triples with a positive linear-scale score are non-positive on the log scale, and
none of the reverse. A three-way interaction is not invariant under a monotone transform of
the phenotype, so a positive trigenic interaction on titer is a linear-scale statement and
has to be reported as one. Which scale is appropriate depends on what the reader will do
with the number. A campaign that asks how many milligrams per liter a third deletion adds
is asking a linear-scale question; a campaign that asks whether a deletion's fold effect is
preserved in a new background is asking a log-scale one.

\notesec{The second tradeoff is what carries the pairwise information} The multiplicative
trigenic score can be written with the digenic scores in it, which is Eq.~\ref{eq:tau-eps}
and is an identity rather than an approximation. Each pairwise deviation enters weighted by
the remaining single-deletion phenotype, so a pair that interacts strongly in a background
where the third deletion is nearly neutral contributes almost its full $\varepsilon$, and
the same pair contributes little when the third deletion is severe. The log-scale
interaction in Eq.~\ref{eq:logtau} weights every term equally. Neither weighting is more
correct; they encode different ideas of how a pair is expected to act in the presence of a
third deletion, and the weighted form is the one that keeps comparability with the
published yeast interaction maps.

\notesec{The third tradeoff is power against assumption} The two linear-scale models are
fit on strain means and propagate the replicate standard errors through
Eqs.~\ref{eq:mult} and \ref{eq:add} by the delta method, so they assume nothing about the
shape of the noise beyond a standard error per strain, and they pay for it in degrees of
freedom: a trigenic score combines seven independently measured strains at three replicates
each, giving a median Welch-Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom of 4.28. The two
regression models are fit on the replicate-level values and borrow strength across the
whole design, which raises power and buys a mean-variance assumption in exchange, Gamma for
Eq.~\ref{eq:glm} and homoscedastic on the log scale for Eq.~\ref{eq:logols}. A count of
significant interactions is therefore not comparable across the two families without saying
which is which, and that is why the Results report agreement on the identity of the
interacting triples separately from agreement on their sign.

\notesec{What this document uses, and why} The multiplicative model is the reported one
throughout, for comparability with the trigenic screens the field already has, and every
claim that rests on a sign is stated with the scale attached. The other three exist here to
answer how much of a claim is the modeling choice, which is a question the reader can only
settle if all four expectations are on the page.

\subsection*{Uncertainty and multiple testing}

Standard errors propagate through the interaction expression by the delta method, using the
per-strain standard errors of the replicate means. The resulting $t$ statistic is referred
to the Welch-Satterthwaite effective degrees of freedom of the linear combination, computed
from each term's contribution to the combined standard error. This matters more than it
usually does. A trigenic score combines seven independently measured strains, and referring
its $t$ statistic to the degrees of freedom of the smallest single input, as an earlier
version of this analysis did, gives 1 or 2 rather than a median of 4.28, and suppresses
nearly every call. Across the 990 multiplicative tests of the whole run, none was
significant at a 5\% false discovery rate before the correction and 456 are after it
(audit record: \file{notes/plan.008-xue-ffa-epistasis-audit.2026.08.15.md}).

Benjamini-Hochberg controls the false discovery rate. The family matters and is stated
wherever a count appears. Counts in Figs.~\ref{fig:landscape} and \ref{fig:models} correct
over the tests of a single readout, since that is what those figures claim about. The
enrichment analysis in Fig.~\ref{fig:models}b uses the stored per-test $P < 0.05$ over all
six readouts pooled, which is the family the enrichment scripts were written against.

\subsection*{Path enumeration and monotonicity}

For each triple, all six orders of introducing its three deletions were expanded into a
four-rung trajectory: base strain, one deletion, two deletions, three deletions, taking the
measured strain at each rung. A route is monotone when every step is strictly positive, and
monotone within one standard error when every step exceeds the negative of that step's
propagated standard error. Valley depth is $1 - \min(\text{rungs})$ on a route, and a
triple's valley depth is the minimum over its six routes, meaning its shallowest available
approach. The greedy campaign in Fig.~\ref{fig:inaccessible}b starts at the base strain,
evaluates every combination reachable by one further deletion, takes the highest if it
exceeds the current strain, and stops otherwise. Enumeration and monotonicity are in
\file{ffa_epistatic_path_panels.py}; the greedy simulation is in
\file{perspective_figure_panels.py}.

\subsection*{Interaction graph enrichment}

Ten interaction graphs were queried for each triple: physical, regulatory and genetic
interactions, TFLink, and the neighborhood, fusion, co-occurrence, coexpression,
experimental and database channels of STRING 12.0. A triple counts as overlapping a graph
when its three genes induce a connected subgraph of that graph. Fold enrichment is the ratio
of the overlapping fraction among significant triples to the overlapping fraction among the
rest, and significance is Fisher's exact test on the corresponding two-by-two table. Three
of the ten graphs contain no connected triple among these ten genes at all and are omitted
from Fig.~\ref{fig:models}b, which is a statement about those graphs' coverage of this gene
set and not about epistasis
(\file{interaction_graph_enrichment_analysis.py}).

\subsection*{Scoring the triples with a growth-trained model}

Each of the 120 triples was scored by the torchcell equivariant cell graph transformer
trained on the Kuzmin 2018 and 2020 trigenic growth screens, as a three-gene deletion in a
wild-type background, the perturbation form the model was trained on. The three chassis
deletions were not part of the scored genotype, and no single or double was scored. Three
independently trained checkpoints were scored on CPU with a parity guard against stored GPU
predictions of the same genotypes (18 values, largest deviation 0.0012 at a tolerance of
0.005), and the prediction is the ensemble mean of the three. The predicted quantity is the
trigenic growth interaction $\tau$ on the synthetic genetic array selection medium. It was
compared with the measured multiplicative titer interaction by Pearson and Spearman
correlation, by a Kruskal-Wallis test of measured $\tau$ across quartiles of the prediction,
by Fisher's exact test of the predicted sign against the measured sign, and by Fisher's
exact test of the top predicted quartile against the false-discovery-rate call
(\file{experiments/010-kuzmin-tmi/scripts/ffa_panel_model_score.py}, tables in
\file{experiments/010-kuzmin-tmi/results/ffa_panel_model_score.\{csv,json\}}).

\subsection*{The metabolic map panel}

Figure~\ref{fig:network}a is KEGG's global map of yeast metabolism, redrawn from the
coordinates KEGG publishes for it and overlaid with the yeast genome-scale model. No part
of it is hand-placed. The generating script is
\file{experiments/008-xue-ffa/scripts/ffa_kegg_map.py} and every response it reads is
stored with its sha256 under \file{experiments/008-xue-ffa/results/kegg_map/}.

\notesec{Why a reference map and not a layout of the model} A metabolic map that reads as
one, with the citrate cycle drawn as a circle and glycolysis as a spine, is a hand-drawn
artifact. No layout algorithm produces one from a stoichiometric model, the yeast
genome-scale model ships no map, and the only published yeast map in Escher is of an
earlier model and covers central carbon alone. What is available is the coordinates of a
map somebody already drew. KEGG publishes its yeast global map \texttt{sce01100} as KGML,
which carries the vertex list of every reaction line and the center of every compound
circle, so the drawing can be taken and the rendering replaced.

\notesec{What is fetched} Three kinds of response, each cached and hashed, and re-fetched
only under \texttt{-{}-refresh}, which raises rather than overwrites if the bytes have
changed upstream. The KGML of \texttt{sce01100} gives 3{,}878 polylines and 3{,}562
compound circles on a canvas of 4{,}858 by 3{,}137 units. One gene list per pathway
(\texttt{link/sce/<pathway>}) gives the yeast genes KEGG assigns to each of the seven
modules the route passes through. One lookup per named gene (\texttt{find/sce/<name>})
resolves a gene name to its systematic name, preferring the entry whose first symbol is
that name, which is what distinguishes TFC7 as YOR110W from the YNL039W that carries TFC7
only as an alias.

\notesec{What is overlaid} The yeast genome-scale model, Yeast9 (yeast-GEM 9.0.2), is read
from its SBML with cobrapy, and its gene identifiers, its \texttt{kegg.reaction}
annotations and its \texttt{kegg.compound} annotations are cached under the sha256 of the
model file. A drawn line counts as being in the model when any gene KEGG attaches to it is
a gene of the model or any reaction it carries is one of the model's KEGG reaction
identifiers; a drawn circle counts when its compound identifier is one of the model's KEGG
compounds. The map draws 814 yeast genes, of which 745 are in the model, and 889 of its
3{,}878 lines and 684 of its 3{,}562 circles are in the model. The remainder is the
reference map's coverage of chemistry the model does not carry, and it is drawn faint
rather than dropped, since the panel is about where the measured chemistry sits in
metabolism rather than about the model's boundaries. The model's own transport, exchange
and lipid-chain reactions, 1{,}110, 274 and 188 of its 4{,}131, have no place on a map of
chemistry and are part of what Fig.~\ref{fig:network}b carries instead.

\notesec{The route} The colored modules are KEGG's gene lists for glycolysis and
gluconeogenesis (\texttt{sce00010}), pyruvate metabolism (\texttt{sce00620}), the citrate
cycle (\texttt{sce00020}), fatty acid degradation (\texttt{sce00071}), fatty acid
biosynthesis (\texttt{sce00061}), fatty acid elongation (\texttt{sce00062}) and
unsaturated fatty acid biosynthesis (\texttt{sce01040}), the last three sharing one color.
A gene on more than one list takes the first list it appears on, in that order, and the
order is load-bearing in both directions. Degradation has to outrank the biosynthetic
lists because KEGG assigns POX1, the acyl-CoA oxidase the chassis deleted, to the
unsaturated fatty acid list as well, and ordered last it takes the biosynthetic color and
the whole beta-oxidation arm goes with it. Degradation must not outrank central carbon,
because KEGG's degradation list also carries the alcohol and aldehyde dehydrogenases, and
above glycolysis it takes twenty of that module's lines with it.

\notesec{Species the map does not draw} Four of the five measured species have a compound
circle on the map. Oleate has none, as the free acid or as either C18 acyl-CoA, so it is
attached along the shortest path of the same genome-scale model from a compound the map
does draw. That path is computed on the pathway subgraph of the model with currency
metabolites removed and with substrates joined to products only, so a shared cofactor
cannot make two species adjacent; every anchor at the minimal path length is kept as a
candidate and the choice among them is made by the placement search below. Oleate resolves
to stearate by way of stearoyl-CoA and oleoyl-CoA, and the genes of that path, FAA1, FAA2,
FAA4 and OLE1, are printed under its name. An attached species is drawn as an open ring so
it cannot be read as a node of the map.

\notesec{Where the labels go} Every drawn element is sampled onto a half-millimetre grid,
the route weighted an order of magnitude above the faint background, so that a query
returns how much ink lies under a rectangle or along a line. A label tries sixteen
directions at four leader lengths from its node and takes the candidate whose plate and
leader cover the least ink. A candidate is rejected outright if the plate leaves the
panel, overlaps a placed plate, covers a labeled node, sits on a leader already drawn, or
if its leader passes through a placed plate or crosses another leader. A crowded group is
placed in the best of all orders rather than greedily, since a fixed order strands the
last label of a cluster. The attached species go into the window of the map whose rings
and links cover the least ink, scored over both sides of the column. Labels may use a
5 mm gutter to the left of the drawing, which the drawing itself may not, because the
compounds this panel names sit at the left end of the fatty acid comb. One label, pyruvate,
has its search narrowed to upward directions after review; the search still chooses among
what is left, and the narrowing is recorded in the script.

\notesec{What the caption's counts are} Every count in the caption of
Fig.~\ref{fig:network}a and in the panel's own key is computed by the run that draws the
panel and written to \file{results/kegg_map/provenance.json}, together with the sha256 of
the KGML, of the model file and of every stored response, the resolved systematic name of
each of the twenty-three named genes, and the number of map lines each one draws. That the
ten deleted regulators draw nothing is read off the KGML's own gene entries rather than
asserted: none of the ten appears on any line, and all thirteen pathway genes do.

\notesec{The earlier version of this panel} The same panel was first drawn on iPath3's
reference map, which is the map Wu et al.\ use, requested from the iPath3 web service with
a selection of KEGG identifiers. That drawing is older and thinner than KEGG's current
one: probing it one identifier at a time showed it has no node for palmitoleate, stearate
or oleate, as free acids or as acyl-CoAs, and draws nothing for ELO1, ELO2, ELO3 or OLE1,
so four of the thirteen pathway genes and three of the five measured species were missing
from it. The KGML redraw carries all thirteen genes and four of the five species. The
iPath3 client is kept at \file{experiments/008-xue-ffa/scripts/ffa_ipath_map.py} with its
stored responses, and the two scripts share the label placement module
\file{map\_labels.py}.
