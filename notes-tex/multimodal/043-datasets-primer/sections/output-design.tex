\section{The output side: readouts, heads, tokens, losses and the evaluation\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output}

What follows is a design for the group to accept, change or reject. The owner's direction
fixes three things: every gene-level readout is trained on; a readout is read off the
perturbed graph, ``from the node itself'' for a gene-level value; and the question to
answer is whether the model improves on held-out single deletions and on the Sameith
double deletions, with the isolates as a further test and Ohya 2005 CalMorph as a probe of
the learned state and never a training target. Everything else below is a choice with its
facts, Fig.~\ref{fig:output-side} draws the path, and Sec.~\ref{sec:output-decisions}
lists the choices the owner has to make.

\subsection{Readout sites and the per-gene readout\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-sites}

The trunk returns three tensors per record after the perturbation operator: the perturbed
class token $h \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the perturbed gene states $H \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$
over the $N = 6{,}607$ gene nodes, and the summary $z_S$. A gene-level value for gene $g$
is read from that gene's own state,
\begin{equation}
\hat z_{g} = f_\theta\!\big([\,H_g \,;\, t\,]\big) + b_{d},
\qquad t = [\,t_{\mathrm{type}} \,;\, t_{\mathrm{data}} \,;\, t_{\mathrm{env}}\,],
\label{eq:pergene}
\end{equation}
with $f_\theta$ one head shared by every gene, every dataset and every readout type of the
gene level, $t$ the record's tokens (Sec.~\ref{sec:output-tokens}), $b_d$ the bias of
dataset $d$ and $\hat z_g$ the prediction on the dataset's standardized scale
(Sec.~\ref{sec:output-losses}); under the binned loss $f_\theta$ emits $K$ logits and
$b_d$ is the vector $c_d$. The prediction for gene $g$ depends on $H_g$ and on nothing
else of $H$; the strain reaches it through the trunk's attention and the perturbation
operator, which is what ``from the node itself'' means in the equation. Four forms of
$f_\theta$ are on the table.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Shared affine.} $f_\theta(x) = w^\top x + c$. The 019 head round measured
the linear per-gene readout within noise of the two-layer one on Kemmeren expression
($+0.002$ over four seeds, \texttt{H\_linear} against \texttt{H\_ref},
\texttt{2-readouts.tex}), so the nonlinearity is not known to carry anything there.
\item \textbf{Shared MLP with the per-dataset bias} (Eq.~\ref{eq:pergene}; the prototype
\texttt{PerGeneReadout}).
\item \textbf{Shared MLP with the strain vector concatenated.} 019's \texttt{H\_concat}
feeds $[H_g ; c]$ with $c$ the strain context, and was the one arm above the reference in
every seed, $+0.0135$ with a seed sd of $0.0026$ on the difference (\texttt{2-readouts.tex},
Table \texttt{tab:readouts-head}). In Eq.~\ref{eq:pergene} this is $t$ extended by $z_S$.
\item \textbf{Cross-attention head.} One learned query per output feature over every token
(019 \texttt{M\_xattn}). On morphology it escaped under the pinball (4 of 4, $0.121 \pm
0.029$) and not under MSE (1 of 4) (\texttt{11-inductive.tex}). It reads the whole token
set, so it is not a readout from the node itself; it is the alternative if the per-gene
form stalls.
\end{itemize}

A record-level value (Vaishnav 2022, one level per promoter insert) is read from
$[h ; z_S ; t]$ through an MLP, the \texttt{GlobalHead} with \texttt{use\_pert\_summary}
that gave the 019 morphology result (12 of 12 escapes with MSE, $0.149 \pm 0.022$ over the
registered window, \texttt{11-inductive.tex}). The plain CLS escaped 0 of 8 under MSE at
two learning rates, so $h$ alone is not a readout site this design uses for a trained
target; it remains a probe input (Sec.~\ref{sec:output-protocol}).

\subsection{Heads by level, by type or by dataset\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-heads}

Two facts bound the choice. First, the datasets report different quantities on different
scales (Table~\ref{tab:output-scales}): a two-color $\log_2$ ratio to a wild-type pool, an
error-model X score, a pseudobulk $\log_2$ fold change with sentinels at $\pm 23$ to
$\pm 33$ in 20.2\% of cells (028, \texttt{2-readouts.tex}), UMI counts, TPM, a linear
MaxLFQ abundance, a log SWATH signal, a degradation rate per hour, a sort-seq bin level.
Second, datasets of one quantity agree weakly where they share a genotype
(Table~\ref{tab:cross}): Kemmeren against Hu 0.107, against Jackson 0.043 to 0.076,
against Nadal-Ribelles 0.002 per genotype; Messner against Zelezniak 0.049; Kemmeren mRNA
against Messner protein 0.031. Standardization and a per-dataset bias reconcile the first
fact and cannot reconcile the second: a head shared by two datasets that agree at 0.002
is asked to emit two unrelated vectors from the same $H_g$, told apart only by the tokens.

\notesec{One head per readout level, the main design}
Two heads: one gene-level head over every gene-level readout of the eleven gene-level
training datasets, one record-level head over Vaishnav and any other scalar readout, each
conditioned at the head by the type token of Sec.~\ref{sec:output-tokens}, with targets
standardized per dataset and a learned bias per dataset. The type token carries what a
by-type head would carry, so a readout type unseen in training lands on an existing path
of the ontology and is scored by a head that already exists; a head per type cannot serve
a type it never saw. What a shared head buys is the transfer of the readout function
across datasets and types, which is the premise of the exercise and is unmeasured; what it
risks is that a large low-agreement dataset pulls the shared function away from the one
with a ceiling, which the equal per-dataset weighting of Sec.~\ref{sec:output-losses}
limits.

\notesec{Six heads by type, the control arm}
Expression log ratio; absolute expression; expression score; protein abundance; protein
turnover; promoter level. The datasets that would share a head and the agreement that
supports it: Kemmeren and Sameith Dm (the same arrays and procedures; Sameith's Methods
defer to Kemmeren, Sec.~\ref{sec:between}; the Kemmeren ceiling 0.729 is the only
within-type ceiling) and, by scale alone, Nadal-Ribelles at 0.002; Jackson, Boocock,
N'Guessan and Caudal under absolute expression (no shared genotype among them; Boocock and
N'Guessan share no segregant, Sec.~\ref{sec:within}); Messner, Zelezniak and Muenzner under
protein abundance (Messner against Zelezniak 0.049; Muenzner shares no genotype with
either). The paired difference against the main design on the split-A score is the answer.

\notesec{Split by dataset}
Fifteen heads, no sharing below the trunk. Datasets then meet only in $H$, which is what
every 019 round did with two array datasets (Kemmeren and Sameith) that are the same
arrays. Nothing is known about the trunk's transfer between readout types from this
campaign: proteome from genotype reached $0.085 \pm 0.008$ (v16, 12 runs) and expression
$0.180 \pm 0.006$ (v12, 4 runs) in separate trainings (\texttt{10-data-review.tex}); a joint
training of both was not scored on both. The alone runs of the transfer statistic
(Sec.~\ref{sec:output-protocol}) measure what the split form would.

\subsection{The experiment-type, dataset and environment tokens\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-tokens}

Three learned vectors travel with every record. The \textbf{experiment-type token} is the
record's ontology phenotype class: \texttt{Microarray\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Phenotype} (Kemmeren,
Sameith), \texttt{Microarray\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Score\allowbreak Phenotype} (Hu),
\texttt{Single\allowbreak Cell\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Phenotype} (Jackson, Boocock, N'Guessan),
\texttt{Pseudobulk\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Phenotype} (Nadal-Ribelles), \texttt{RNASeq\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Phenotype}
(Caudal), \texttt{Protein\allowbreak Abundance\allowbreak Phenotype} (Messner, Zelezniak, Muenzner),
\texttt{Protein\allowbreak Turnover\allowbreak Phenotype} (Muenzner) and \texttt{Pooled\allowbreak Sort\allowbreak Seq\allowbreak Expression\allowbreak Phenotype}
(Vaishnav), the classes of Table~\ref{tab:store}'s rows. The \textbf{dataset token} is the
dataset's id. The \textbf{environment token} is the record's medium, temperature and
environmental perturbation, which separates Jackson's eleven conditions and Nadal-Ribelles'
two.

\notesec{The precedent}
Experiment 035 gave the cell graph transformer a compound token $z_E$ from a fingerprint
MLP and asked whether it predicts Vanacloig 2022 for an unseen compound. Three entry points
were measured on 123 compound-evaluations (\texttt{experiments.035}, rounds 9 and 10): a
bilinear factor at the head ($-0.007$ paired against ridge, 95\% CI $-0.028$ to $+0.015$),
a gate on every gene's state ($-0.025$), and a token the genes attend to for one further
layer ($+0.008$, CI $-0.026$ to $+0.045$; $+0.015$ against the head form, CI through zero).
The ablation was the arm with no compound input (\texttt{cgt\_genes}), whose centered score
is zero by construction. Experiment 030 put a one-hot over its 15 source datasets,
projected to 8 dimensions, at the readout of both heads; seed 0 on the random split
converged at a validation interaction Pearson of 0.5655 by epochs 164 to 181 (W\&B
\texttt{xy3xnpau} and its continuations), and its encoder-side placement never ran.

\notesec{Where the tokens enter, and the per-dataset bias}
Three forms, for gene $g$ of a record from dataset $d$ with experiment type $\tau(d)$:
\begin{equation}
\text{(a)}\;\; \hat z_g = f(H_g) + b_d, \qquad
\text{(b)}\;\; \hat z_g = f\big([\,H_g \,;\, t_{\tau(d)} \,;\, t_d \,;\, t_{\mathrm{env}}\,]\big) + b_d, \qquad
\text{(c)}\;\; H' = \mathrm{Layer}\big([\,t_{\tau(d)};\,t_{\mathrm{env}};\,H\,]\big),\;\; \hat z_g = f(H'_g) + b_d.
\label{eq:token-forms}
\end{equation}
Duplicate encoding with a linear bias is the pairing in (b): the record's provenance is
encoded once semantically, as the type and environment tokens shared across datasets, and
once as free parameters, the dataset row and its bias, which absorb the instrument's scale
and offset. With standardized targets the scale is fitted from the data and only the bias
is learned; under the binned loss it is a bias vector $c_d \in \mathbb{R}^K$ on the logits,
a learned prior histogram per dataset. Form (b) contains (a), since a one-hot $t_d$ read by
a head that is linear in it is a per-dataset bias.

The main design is (b), the 030 per-entry readout token's form: cheapest, measured to
convergence on one seed, and it keeps every token out of the trunk. Arm 1 is (c), type and
environment attended in one layer after the operator, the 035 environment-encoder form, the
only one of 035's three with a positive paired mean against ridge, and the form a
compound-structured environment will need, since a token the genes see can change which
genes respond. The ablation is (a) with no token. The dataset id never enters the trunk
in any arm, so the morphology probe reads a latent built without it. A new dataset is one
new row of $t_d$ and one new bias; a new dataset of a known type can be scored before
training with $t_d$ at its type's mean row and the bias at zero (hypothesis, untested,
that this read is above the per-gene mean). The interface the head needs from the trunk is
$H$, $z_S$, $h$ and the three integer ids \texttt{collate\_043} already emits; the isolate
context and the change-type marks enter the operator and never the head, or the
alone-against-joint transfer on the isolates cannot be attributed to the trunk.

\notesec{Ontology path or flat id}
A flat token is one row per class. A path token sums one row per node of the class's
ontology path, so \texttt{MicroarrayExpressionPhenotype} and
\texttt{RNASeqExpressionPhenotype} share an expression row and differ in their own
(prototype \texttt{ExperimentTypeEmbedding}, \texttt{mode="dag"}). The served graph has no
such path: every phenotype class is a direct child of \texttt{phenotype} in
\texttt{biocypher/config/torchcell\_schema\_config.yaml} (the \texttt{phenotype member of}
edge lists all 34 leaves), and in the pydantic schema only the two microarray classes share
a parent below \texttt{Phenotype} (\texttt{TwoChannelMicroarrayExpressionPhenotype}). The
main design writes the grouping on the modeling side: expression, protein, promoter,
fitness, interaction, metabolite, morphology, with the served leaf below its group. This
is the owner's tokenizing of the ontology; when the ontology unification lands a real
path, \file{dag\_paths} reads it and nothing else changes. The flat id is the ablation,
which says whether the shared group row carries anything (hypothesis, untested: with 15
datasets over 8 classes the type and dataset rows are nearly collinear, and the type row
earns its place only on a dataset unseen in training).

\subsection{Losses\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-losses}

Each readout has one loss over the measured entries of its records only: a missing gene is
\texttt{NaN} in the target tensor, contributes no value and no gradient, and a type with
nothing measured in a batch returns a connected zero (prototype \texttt{MaskedTypedLoss},
the convention of \texttt{masked\_mean} in \texttt{torchcell/losses/distributional.py}).
Nadal-Ribelles sentinels are set missing first.

\notesec{Standardization}
Every target is standardized per dataset before the loss, $z = (y - \mu_d)/s_d$ with
$\mu_d$ and $s_d$ fitted on the training rows of dataset $d$, pooled over genes so a
gene's deviation keeps its size. Every linear quantity is logged first and every panel
quantity centered per gene over the training isolates or segregants
(Table~\ref{tab:output-scales}). The learned per-dataset scale of the prototype is thereby
fixed from the data; what stays learned per dataset is the bias of
Eq.~\ref{eq:token-forms}. The statistics are fitted on training records only and frozen
for every linear evaluation, or they leak the held-out set's scale.

\notesec{Binned prediction, the main design}
The main loss is HL-Gauss (Farebrother et al.\ 2024): the standardized target is a
Gaussian of width $\sigma$ integrated over $K$ bins and renormalized, the loss is the
cross-entropy against that soft target, the point prediction is the expectation over bin
centers, read back in released units as $\mu_d + s_d\, \mathbb{E}[z]$, and the bin entropy
is the uncertainty. Bins are shared in $z$ units: $K = 32$ equal bins over $[-4, 4]$,
width 0.25, the two edge bins open, $\sigma = 0.75$ bin widths, so a target smears over
about three bins. Vaishnav's released level is already a mean over 18 sort bins and is
treated as a continuous scalar after standardization. The logits are $[B, 6{,}607, K]$,
6.8 million floats or 27 MB in fp32 at $B = 32$, against the 19-knot pinball's 16 MB; the
attention over 6{,}607 tokens dominates either. The cross-entropy per entry is masked
exactly as the MSE is; the soft target is built from the zero-filled safe target under the
mask, so no \texttt{NaN} reaches the backward pass. Scalar heads (trigenic interaction,
betaxanthin, the promoter level) use the same bins on their own standardized target.
Pearson and Spearman are computed on $\mathbb{E}[z]$; accuracy within one bin and the mean
entropy are reported beside them, the classification reading of the same prediction.
Hypothesis (untested): the binned head escapes the marginal plateau at least as reliably
as MSE on $z_S$, because its gradient at the marginal is the full mismatch of two
histograms and not a shrinking residual. No binned head has run in this codebase, so
every gain is a hypothesis until the arms below have their seeds.

\notesec{Pinball and MSE, the arms}
019 trained expression with the 19-quantile pinball and read the median; on morphology, MSE
on \texttt{M\_zs} escaped 12 of 12 runs by epoch 410, the pinball on the same readout 8 of
10 and late (epochs 230 to 770), MSE on the CLS 0 of 8, CRPS 0 of 8
(\texttt{11-inductive.tex}). Nothing is measured on a per-gene head with any loss on these
data. The pinball is arm 1, on the same seeds as the main design, and is the owner's
stated preference over MSE. One MSE cell on the per-gene head is the reference: it is the
only loss with a 12 of 12 escape on record (on $z_S$), so it tells a bins failure from a
readout failure. The Pearson loss and ListMLE are not arms: five of six batch-32 Pearson
runs collapsed to a constant output and the anchored ListMLE runs sat on the mean by epoch
250 (\texttt{2-readouts.tex}). A two-hot target is HL-Gauss at $\sigma \to 0$ and is
covered by a $\sigma$ sweep over 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 bin widths if the main design is in
doubt.

\notesec{Weighting across datasets}
By record the store is 173{,}617 samples, of which Boocock holds 79{,}516 and Vaishnav
71{,}158 (Table~\ref{tab:store}); by gene-level value Boocock's single cells hold 52\% of
the training values (Table~\ref{tab:data-per-part}); Kemmeren is 1{,}482 records. The main
design weights each dataset's mean masked loss equally. Agreement weighting is dropped: it
writes a judgment about which datasets are poor into the objective, and the arm without
the low-agreement datasets (Sec.~\ref{sec:output-protocol}) answers the same question
without it. Record-count weighting is the arm that shows what the two large panels do to
the deletion score, and decision P1 settles whether Boocock's de novo cells train at all.

\subsection{Linear evaluations first\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-linear}

Nothing trains on a card before the ladder is on file. Every rung reads the fold file of
Sec.~\ref{sec:output-protocol}, scores the same held-out units the transformer will, and
runs on CPU (\file{linear\_evals\_043.py}, results in \file{results/linear\_evals\_043.json};
neither exists yet). The reason is the 031 operator audit: on single deletions the
transformer's score was a learned affine read of the deleted gene's own token, reproduced
by a ridge from that row, and the ridge on the raw ProtT5 token read 0.082 on the same
pin. The ridge from the deleted gene's frozen composite token to its expression profile is
therefore the real split-A bar, and it has never been measured on expression. 035 found
nested ridge on the raw fingerprint, 0.303, the bar no model beat with an interval clear
of zero; 030 found a zero-parameter lower-order null at 0.577 above the joint
transformer's 0.562.

\notesec{Strain vectors}
A single deletion is the deleted gene's frozen composite token $x_g$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:input-token}), and each member of it alone, so the ladder says which block
carries what. A double is $x_a + x_b$ and $[\,x_a + x_b \,;\, x_a \odot x_b\,]$; since no
double trains, the inductive additive null is $f(x_a) + f(x_b)$ from the single-fit ridge.
An isolate is, in order of richness, the 0/1 vector of its absent and loss-of-function
genes, the genotype kinship matrix over the 979 isolates from the sequence-variant calls
(kinship ridge is the GBLUP bar), and the change-type-weighted sum of $x_g$ over its marked
genes. A segregant is its parent-of-origin vector over genes ($+1$, $-1$, 0 for
unassigned) from the posterior 0.9 blocks. A promoter is its one-hot 80-mer and $k$-mer
counts, $k \le 6$, and the frozen $f_{\mathrm{up}}$ window once the plasmid is in.

\notesec{Rungs}
N0, the per-gene mean, the training-mean profile for every strain; ridge, multi-output and
closed form, penalty on the inner fold, inputs and targets $z$-scored on training rows; kNN,
the mean profile of the $k$ nearest training strains by cosine on $x_g$, $k \in \{5, 10,
20\}$; GBLUP for isolates; the marker ridge for segregants; the $k$-mer ridge for
promoters; and for the doubles a four-rung ladder that sees only the double's components:
the per-gene mean, the stronger single, $M_x + M_y$, and the fitted $a M_x + b M_y + c$
(leave-one-out over the 72). Every difference is paired by held-out unit with a 95\%
bootstrap interval resampled over strains (per-strain statistics) or genes (per-gene
statistics); the strain interval is the one quoted, as the compound interval was in 035.
A ridge over 1{,}400 strains by 3{,}328 inputs to 6{,}169 targets is one solve per penalty
per fold, seconds on CPU; the whole ladder is a CPU job.

\notesec{What would say the embedding carries the signal}
Per dataset, ridge or kNN on $x_g$ above N0 on the median per-gene Pearson with the strain
interval clear of zero. For Kemmeren the reference points are the 019 window mean $0.180
\pm 0.006$ and the ceiling 0.729; no number is predicted for the token ridge. For the
isolates, GBLUP against N0 on Muenzner is the bar the transformer must clear, with Caudal
against Muenzner (0.208 per isolate, 0.160 per gene, Table~\ref{tab:cross}) the reference
for another readout of the same strain. For the doubles, a model counts only above the
fitted combination with at least 5 of 6 seeds up, on the 72 and on the restricted genes.

\subsection{The evaluation protocol\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-protocol}

One fold file, five held-out sets, one probe, the arms and a scoring rule, declared before
any run is read (Table~\ref{tab:output-protocol}). The 019 window rule stands: budget and
window declared with the launch (500 epochs read over epochs 400 to 499 is the precedent;
its 1{,}000-epoch runs scored lower over 900 to 999 than over 400 to 499 in 7 of 8), a
cell read by its escape fraction first and its window mean second, no maximum over epochs.
Two rules are added from 031 and 035: the validation fold monitors the escape and never
selects the epoch (031 found epoch selection on validation null, $-0.009$), and the test
fold is read once per round. Spearman is reported beside Pearson everywhere, and every
calibration slope is named by its population (the whole fold and the predicted top
decile), since 010 measured 0.88 on a whole test split against 1.59 inside the predicted
top 100.

\notesec{The fold file}
\file{conf/splits/global\_split\_v1.json}, written by \file{make\_global\_split\_043.py}
with \texttt{default\_rng(0)} from the raw store's index and validated by the pydantic
record \texttt{GlobalSplit043} (version, seed, store sha256, test fold 0, validation fold
1, the pinned training genes, and one fold id per key in four key spaces). Deletion genes:
every gene deleted in Kemmeren, Hu, Jackson, Nadal-Ribelles, Messner, Zelezniak or Ohya,
the 82 Sameith singles pinned to the training folds (P2), the rest in ten folds by a draw
stratified on the gene's dataset-membership pattern, so each dataset holds out about a
tenth of its genes (Table~\ref{tab:plan}). A deletion record trains only if none of its
genes is in fold 0 or 1. Isolates: the 979 strain names of Caudal and Muenzner (759
shared, 55 with turnover) in five folds stratified by membership and turnover. Segregants:
five folds within each cross, de novo cells as records of their own. Promoters: five
folds over distinct Vaishnav 80-mers. Ten folds for genes and five for the rest because a
held-out gene leaves every deletion dataset at once and Kemmeren at 1{,}400 eligible genes
cannot spare 40\% to test and validation. The dataset module answers
\texttt{role(sample)} from the file; a per-dataset-alone run is the joint configuration
with one dataset listed, so alone and joint read the same held-out records. Every run
logs the file's version and store hash and refuses a mismatch. A second, clade-disjoint
isolate file is the hard split once the Peter 2018 clade is on the isolate descriptor
(owner question 6); it sits beside the random file and does not replace it.

\notesec{Split A, deletion genes}
Fold 0 of the gene folds, scored on every deletion dataset that measures the gene. The
primary statistic is the median per-gene Pearson across the held-out strains; the
per-strain Pearson across genes is reported centered on the training-mean profile, with
the raw value and the Kemmeren different-mutant floor of 0.284 beside it (job 5893;
Jackson's different-genotype constructions agree at 0.095). The bars are the ladder of
Sec.~\ref{sec:output-linear} on the same genes; the ceiling is Kemmeren's own-culture
0.729 (test-retest) and 0.653 (decomposition); the 019 by-strain reading $0.180 \pm
0.006$ is a reference and not a paired bar. A held-out gene still appears in the isolates
and segregants as a sequence variant, an absence or an inherited block; those are not
deletions and are not held out. Jackson is scored only inside the joint arm, never alone.

\notesec{Split B, every Sameith double}
All 72 Sameith 2015 double deletions are held out; their 82 constituent singles are in
training as Kemmeren profiles (Sameith Sm is excluded outright, the same arrays). Each
double is scored by the centered per-strain Pearson against its measured profile and
paired against the four rungs of Sec.~\ref{sec:output-linear}, the additive null being the
paper's own expected value (``the deviation between the expression change observed in the
double mutant $M_{x\Delta y\Delta i}$ and the expected expression change, given each single
mutant $M_{x\Delta i} + M_{y\Delta i}$'', paper.md line 39); the same on the genes where
$|M_{xy,i} - (M_{x,i} + M_{y,i})| > \log_2 1.5$, where the additive null is wrong by
construction. Sameith ran ``four times from two independent cultures'' per mutant
(paper.md line 33) and released one profile, so no double has a replicate; the Kemmeren
0.729 is the bound for the same arrays.

\notesec{Split C, isolates}
Fold 0 of the isolate folds, out of Caudal, Muenzner protein and turnover together. The
primary statistic is the median per-protein Pearson across the held-out isolates over
1{,}540 proteins; the per-isolate Pearson is reported centered. GBLUP on the same isolates
is the bar; Caudal against Muenzner is the reference for another readout of the same
strain; the turnover read is the fold average over all five folds, the only stable read at
55 isolates. The random isolate fold is the easy split, since relatives of a held-out
strain sit in training, and GBLUP will look strong on it.

\notesec{Splits D and E, segregants and promoters}
Fold 0 within each cross, scored by the median per-gene Pearson across held-out segregants
against the marker ridge; fold 0 of the promoters, scored by Pearson and Spearman across
held-out promoters against the $k$-mer ridge.

\notesec{The transfer statistic and the rule}
For dataset $d$ and initialization seed $s$, $T_d(s) = S_d(\mathrm{joint}, s) -
S_d(\mathrm{alone}, s)$ on the fold-0 records of $d$, with $S_d$ the primary statistic.
Alone and joint are matched on optimizer steps, not epochs, since an alone run sees fewer
records per epoch. A gain, transfer or arm difference is called when its mean is at least
0.02 on the primary statistic, at least 5 of 6 paired seeds are up, and the 95\% interval
resampled over held-out strains (genes for per-gene statistics) excludes zero: 0.02 is
three seed standard deviations on either readout (019 expression 0.006 over four runs,
proteome 0.008 over twelve), 5 of 6 is the 031 campaign rule at six seeds (9 of 12 at
twelve), and the interval guards against six seeds agreeing on the sign of a 0.005 effect.
The rule is committed as \file{conf/decision\_rule.yaml} before the first GPU run. Six
seeds are six initializations on the pinned fold 0; fold 1 as test with fold 0 as
validation is the second split seed when the budget allows, and the rule then reads 9 of
12. One seed is never read.

\notesec{The morphology probe}
Ohya 2005 enters no training. For each of its 4{,}678 genotypes the frozen model produces
$h$, $z_S$ and the mean over genes $\bar H$; a ridge from each, from their concatenation
and from the frozen token $x_g$ to the 116 moving CalMorph features is fit 5-fold over
genotypes with the penalty chosen on an inner split, inputs and targets $z$-scored and
clipped at 8, and scored as the out-of-fold Pearson per feature across held-out genotypes,
median over the 116 (the construction of Table~\ref{tab:morph}). Two reads: raw, and
growth-residualized, with the Costanzo 2016 single-mutant fitness of the genotype
regressed out of every feature on the training folds and appended as a covariate, because
the chemogenomic growth matrices predict morphology nearly as well as the proteome
(Hoepfner 0.263, Hillenmeyer 0.246, Messner 0.282, three growth panels 0.310; 019 screen,
job 3919). The bars, in order: the 019 genotype-only model read through its own trained
head, $0.149 \pm 0.022$; the sequence-embedding ridge 0.08 to 0.14
(\texttt{11-inductive.tex}); the Kemmeren-expression ridge 0.348 on the 1{,}437 shared
genotypes, the Messner ridge 0.278 on 4{,}279 and the Hu ridge 0.235 on 259
(Table~\ref{tab:morph}, job 5892), transductive and each also paired on its own strains;
the residualized bars recomputed in the same script; and the ceiling 0.847 on the 116 or
0.611 over the 281. The learned state carries morphology beyond growth when the
residualized median clears the residualized Kemmeren ridge with the strain interval clear
of it; a raw median near 0.149 says the probe is the trained head by another route. The
Ohya genotypes whose gene is in fold 0 (about 468) are the strictly inductive subgroup,
unseen on every readout. Hypothesis (untested): $z_S$ carries the most, since the 031
audit placed the score in the deleted gene's own row and $z_S$ is the readout 019 trained.

\notesec{Arms}
Main: every training dataset, heads per level, tokens at the head with the per-dataset
bias, HL-Gauss. Data arms in a $2 \times 2$: with and without the natural-genome datasets
(Caudal, Muenzner protein and turnover, Boocock, N'Guessan), with and without Vaishnav
(once its window exists); the arm without Hu, Jackson and Nadal-Ribelles, which can only
lower the Kemmeren score if their signal does not transfer (owner question 5 names the
headline control). Loss arms: pinball; one MSE cell. Token arms: the attended layer
(arm 1); the flat type id; no token. The six-heads-by-type control; the record-count
weighting arm; the transductive identity-table ceiling; and the alone runs, one per
training dataset. Every arm is scored on splits A and B and the probe, C, D and E in the
arms that train those datasets; six initializations per cell on fold 0. Main plus these
arms at six seeds is on the order of 70 runs of 500 epochs before the alone runs, so the
linear ladder cuts any arm whose lower-order bar already answers it, and the owner ranks
the rest.

\subsection{Owner decisions\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:output-decisions}

Each item names the main option first; Table~\ref{tab:decisions} carries the same rows
with their measured facts.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Heads.} One head per readout level, shared across readout types and
conditioned by the type token, on targets standardized per dataset with a per-dataset
bias ($c_d$ on the logits, $b_d$ under pinball or MSE). Control arm: six heads by type.
\item \textbf{Experiment-type token.} Ontology path with the modeling-side grouping
(expression, protein, promoter, fitness, interaction, metabolite, morphology); the flat id
as the ablation.
\item \textbf{Where the tokens enter.} At the head (main); type and environment attended in
one layer after the operator (arm 1); no token (ablation). The dataset never enters the
trunk.
\item \textbf{Loss.} HL-Gauss bins ($K = 32$ over $[-4, 4]$ in $z$ units, $\sigma$ 0.75 bin
widths, expectation as the point); the 19-quantile pinball as arm 1; one MSE cell as the
reference.
\item \textbf{Dataset weighting.} Equal per dataset; agreement weighting dropped; record
count as an arm.
\item \textbf{Nadal-Ribelles sentinels.} $|\log_2 \mathrm{FC}| > 20$ set to missing before
standardization (20.2\% of cells).
\item \textbf{Jackson target.} The effect against the HO control of the same environment;
Jackson not scored alone.
\item \textbf{Split A leakage rule.} The held-out gene out of every deletion dataset, from
the fold file.
\item \textbf{Split C.} Fold 0 of the isolate folds, about 196 isolates with about 159
Muenzner and 11 turnover; random over isolates now, a clade-disjoint second file when the
clade is on the descriptor.
\item \textbf{Budget, window and selection.} Declared with the launch; alone and joint
matched on optimizer steps; the validation fold never selects the epoch; the test fold read
once.
\item \textbf{The fold file, the rule, the ladder, the centered statistic, the transfer
statistic, the probe control and the reporting} are rows S1 to S7 of
Table~\ref{tab:decisions}.
\end{enumerate}
