\section{Limits\secstatus{todo}}

\notesec{The recomputed p-values are not the sources' p-values}
Section~\ref{sec:confidence} is a ranking comparison. The normal test on the double's
own colony SD orders the source p-values at $\rho = 0.984$ but calls 2.5 times as many
interactions, because the source null pools an array-strain and a query-strain
variance from control screens that were never released. No recomputation from the
build or from the released tables can recover those; the statement is that the
source p is reproducible in rank, not in value.

\notesec{The measured-mean restoration assumes exactly one 0 in the mean}
The $k/(k-1)$ correction in Section~\ref{sec:hazards} assumes a tainted single merged
exactly one essentiality entry of value 0 with $k-1$ measured entries. The
\texttt{num\_duplicates} field gives $k$ and the source list confirms one essentiality
entry for every tainted single, but the individual measured values are gone after the
merge, so the restored mean is exact only if the converter wrote exactly 0. It did
(\texttt{CompositeFitnessConverter}), and the two checked cases (YER048W-A, stored
0.452 from 0.904 and 0; YDR292C, stored 0.630 over $k = 7$) agree.

\notesec{The within-screen trigenic control matches control queries by array strain, not by batch}
Section~\ref{sec:strength} matches a trigenic row to its two single-mutant control
query rows on the array strain identifier and the control gene. The supplementary
methods say those controls were screened in the same batch as the double-mutant query,
but the released tables carry no batch field, only the query strain identifier
(\texttt{tm} number), which differs between a double-mutant query and its controls. A
gene was screened against the same array strain more than once in 2.8 percent of the
2018 matches and 7.6 percent of the 2020 matches, and those are averaged. The residual
this leaves is small, $r = 0.985$ and a median absolute residual of 0.0016, so the
matching is not what limits that control.

\notesec{The trigenic score reproduces itself at 0.59, and two published figures differ}
Two numbers are in circulation for the replicate reproducibility of the adjusted
trigenic score, both from the Kuzmin 2018 screens. The supplementary text reports
correlations between independent replicates of 0.90 to 0.91 for double-mutant and raw
triple-mutant scores and 0.74 to 0.81 for the adjusted trigenic score
(\texttt{si/si1.md} line 171), but the panel those come from plots only interactions
called significant at $p < 0.05$. That p-value is a normal test of the score against a
variance propagated from the query and array fitness estimates, so conditioning on it
keeps the scores that are large relative to their own measurement error and drops the
rest, which raises a correlation computed within the subset. Over the whole
distribution the number is lower: Dango (\texttt{zhangDANGOPredictingHigherorder2020})
recomputed it on all 91{,}050 triples of the Kuzmin 2018 diagnostic-array screen and
reports ``The Pearson correlation between the trigenic interaction scores of two
individual replicates is around 0.59, which is much lower than the Pearson correlation
between the digenic interaction score of two replicates from the same data source
(0.88)''.

The 0.59 is the honest figure for an unselected pool such as S3, and it is the right
one to read the recompute against, with two qualifications. It is the correlation
between two single replicates, so it is what a replicate oracle achieves rather than a
mathematical ceiling; a model predicting the denoised value and scored against a noisy
one is limited instead by the square root of the target's reliability, which for a
score combining two replicates of individual reliability 0.59 is about 0.86. And it is
measured on one screen's diagnostic array, not on the pooled S3 or 029 triples. With
those held in mind, the Kuzmin-first policy's $r = 0.517$ on the 2018 screen
(Section~\ref{sec:deletion-query}) sits just under the value two repetitions of the
experiment attain against each other, so the identity recomputed from the database is
close to as consistent with the released score as the measurement is with itself.

\notesec{On the 030 build the same triple agrees with itself at Spearman 0.23 to 0.77, depending on what was repeated}
The published figures are Pearson values on one screen. The 030 build, which merges
nothing, lets the reproducibility be measured on the pool itself and in rank: 12{,}914
of the 376{,}732 S3 triples carry two or more interaction entries, and each such pair
is one re-measurement of the same gene triple
(\texttt{triple\_noise\_ceiling\_030.py}, one random pair per triple, exact duplicate
rows dropped, interval by bootstrap over triples). Table~\ref{tab:triple-reproducibility}
groups the pairs by what differs between the two entries, because the classes answer
different questions.

The large class is 11{,}013 Kuzmin 2020 triples whose identical strain combination is
scored twice within that study. Kuzmin 2020 released two trigenic tables, the
diagnostic-array screen of Table S1 and the pilot genome-wide screens of Table S3, and
the 030 recompute (\texttt{closure\_recompute\_030.py}) found 11{,}443 trigenic groups
whose array single fitness differs between them; the count and the fact that the strain
identifiers are the same on both entries point to those two tables as the source of the
pairs, an inference rather than a field the loader carries. Between the two screens the
same strains agree at $r = 0.44$ and $\rho = 0.23$. The Pearson is carried by the tail
of strong negative interactions; the bulk of the distribution near zero does not rank
reproducibly. The 483 triples measured in both years agree far better, at 0.89 and 0.77,
and those are 2018 query strains re-screened in 2020 on the same diagnostic array, so
the array format and protocol match in a way the two 2020 tables do not. Hypothesis
(untested): the array format, not the year, is the larger source of disagreement.

A second route was tried and is not trusted. The stored one-sided p-value, read as a
normal test of the score against its propagated error, gives a per-row standard error
of $|\tau| / z(p)$ with a median of 0.050 on 2018 against a score standard deviation of
0.054, which implies two replicates of the 2018 screen would correlate at 0.23. Dango
measured 0.59. The gap is a factor of two in noise variance, and Section~\ref{sec:confidence}
already found the trigenic p cannot be reproduced from the stored fitness standard
deviations, so the error model behind that p is not the one assumed. The re-measured
pairs are the figure to use.

Two cautions carry over from the previous paragraph. Every number in the table is
agreement between two measurements, so it is what a replicate oracle achieves, and a
predictor of the denoised score is bounded instead by the square root of the released
score's reliability. And the classes are not random samples of S3: the 2020 pairs are
the triples the pilot screens happened to cover, and the cross-year pairs are the
queries Kuzmin 2020 chose to repeat.

\notesec{Four colonies is a documented representative, not a per-record count}
The null standard deviation $\sigma_{ab}/\sqrt{4}$ uses the colony count the loaders
record (Costanzo 2016: ``4 replicate colonies per double mutant''; the Kuzmin loader
back-solved $n = 4$ against the reported p-values). Only 41{,}370 of the closure
doubles carry an explicit \texttt{n\_samples}; the rest use the same value. A
different $n$ rescales every recomputed p monotonically and changes no ranking in
Table~\ref{tab:confidence}.

\notesec{Nothing here reads a training run}
Every number is a property of a pool. The three S3 seeds
(IGB \texttt{mmli} 2409033, 2409031, 2409032) were running or queued when this was
written and are read out elsewhere.
