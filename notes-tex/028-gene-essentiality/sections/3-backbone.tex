\section{The backbone: splits, labels, weight decay and the shape of the curve\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:backbone}

Every cell in this section is the pooled backbone at width 12 with one transformer block
and a one-layer preprocessor (16,874 parameters), trained for 80 epochs at three
initialization seeds with the split pinned. Round 1 (jobs 2149 to 2181) moves one lever at
a time away from the cell that trains on the released-label split file
(\texttt{ess\_fcl\_val0.2\_s0}, 629 training genes, 158 validation genes):
the old 75-gene validation draw, the genome-wide labels, weight decay at 0, $10^{-2}$ and
$10^{-1}$, batch 32, learning rate at $3 \times 10^{-4}$ and $3 \times 10^{-3}$, the class
weight $772/126$, and graph regularization off. Table~\ref{tab:w12} carries every number;
Figure~\ref{fig:w12} the curves.

\subsection{The curve was noisy because the validation set was small\secstatus{todo}}

The median epoch-to-epoch step of the validation AUROC is 0.0056 on the 75-gene draw,
0.0026 on the 158-gene split and 0.0020 on the 1,093-gene genome-wide split
(Figure~\ref{fig:w12}b). Two of the three width-24 cells of the previous protocol selected
epochs 4 and 5 on validation peaks of 0.82 and 0.86 and scored 0.74 on the held-out genes;
no cell on a split file selects before epoch 50. The old protocol's curve reads near 0.8 on
its own 75 genes and the split cells read 0.65 to 0.72 on theirs, so the levels are not
comparable, only the steps are.

\subsection{The split file costs training genes, and the cells are under-trained\secstatus{todo}}

The split-file cell scores 0.725 against the old protocol's 0.759 on the same held-out
genes. It trains on 629 labeled genes where the old draw trained on roughly 740, and every
one of its seeds selected epoch 66 to 77 of 80 with the validation curve still rising
(Figure~\ref{fig:w12}a). At batch 128 an epoch is five optimizer steps, so 80 epochs is 400
updates. The two levers that move all three seeds in the same direction are both more
optimization: batch 32, four times the steps per epoch, lifts the seeds by 0.020, 0.048 and
0.051 and collapses their spread to 0.005; the class weight, which steepens the curve,
lifts them by 0.019, 0.051 and 0.033. Round 3 combines the two and raises the epoch budget.

\subsection{Weight decay and graph regularization do nothing here\secstatus{todo}}

Weight decay at 0, $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-1}$ reproduces the base cell to the third decimal on
every seed, and so does removing graph regularization. At 400 updates with learning rate
$10^{-3}$, a decay of $10^{-1}$ shrinks a weight by four percent, so it is not a lever at
this budget. Graph regularization is a different case: its configuration pins the nine
gene graphs to attention heads 0 to 8 of the \emph{second} transformer block, so on a
one-block cell the term is never evaluated, and the arm with it off reproduces the arm
with it on because both are the same cell. The term was inert on every one-block cell in
this document; a two-block cell with four heads indexes a head it does not have and
fails (round 8, job 2261), so the two-block cells run with it off.
The learning rate at $3 \times 10^{-4}$ and $3 \times 10^{-3}$ is worse than $10^{-3}$ on
the seed mean, with seed spreads of 0.09 and 0.04 that put the pooled numbers inside the
base interval.

\subsection{More labels from the genome make the held-out ranking worse\secstatus{todo}}

The genome-wide file adds SGD's essential list and the viable single-mutant fitness records
as nonessential, 4,368 training genes against 629, and scores 0.704 against 0.725, three of
three seeds down. Its validation AUROC on its own 1,093 genes is 0.84 while the released
held-out genes read 0.70, the widest validation-to-held-out gap of any arm. The
viable-fitness negatives are a different population from the released split's nonessential
genes, and a model that ranks them well does not rank the released genes better.
Hypothesis, untested: the released split's nonessential class is enriched for genes with a
measurable fitness defect, which the genome-wide negatives are not.

\subsection{Gradient norms\secstatus{todo}}

On the split-file cells the preprocessor's gradient norm starts near $10^{-2}$ and rises
about thirty-fold over 80 epochs while the head's stays flat near 0.4
(Figure~\ref{fig:w12}d); the clip at 10 never binds. The rise tracks the validation curve's
climb, which is the same under-training reading from the gradient side.

\subsection{Round 3: the two levers combined, a longer budget, and the fold ensemble\secstatus{todo}}

Round 3 (jobs 2209 and 2224 to 2236) combines the two round-1 levers that moved three of
three seeds, batch 32 and the class weight, at 80 and at 200 epochs, runs batch 32 alone at
200 epochs as the class weight's control, and trains five fold models on the
cross-validation files, which partition the 787 labeled non-test genes five ways with the
held-out genes fixed; their logits are averaged on the held-out genes, the protocol behind
the published Flux Cone Learning number (Table~\ref{tab:w14}, Figure~\ref{fig:w14}). With
the loader workers removed a 200-epoch cell takes 11 minutes.

read was fixed first: the transformer gains the ladder's complement if its seed mean moves
from 0.774 toward 0.82 on three of three seeds and the fold ensemble from 0.795 toward the
ladder's 0.81 to 0.83.
