\section{Physically constrained stores on the strain, and the store read without the backbone\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:physics}

The module so far carries mass balance and nothing else: one parsimonious vector per
deletion. On the essentiality endpoint the store ladder (the essentiality document's
round 113) built the same vector under enzyme ceilings, sampled turnover, GECKO pools
and a minimization of metabolic adjustment, and none moved the score beyond the seed
spread. None of those programs had been run on the target model, the cassette strain
with its markers deleted on YPD, which covers every GEM gene rather than the labeled
ones. On 2026-09-23 they were, on CPU beside the packs, with reads that need no card.

The second law as fixed directions admits no growth. The direction rule of the
essentiality study's round 114 (a reaction whose standard energy on the union-external
route is at least $T$ below zero loses its reverse part, at least $T$ above it its
forward part; unpriced reactions, the cassette's included, keep the GEM box) leaves the
parent strain infeasible at both thresholds, as it had on the essentiality model. On the
cassette strain the 10 kJ/mol rule fixes 1,754 directions forward and 1,068 reverse, and
97 of them run against the parsimonious wild-type flux; at 30 kJ/mol it fixes 1,071 and
905 with 69 conflicts. No store is written for an infeasible parent (a store of zero rows
would be read as every deletion lethal), and the outcome is recorded
(\texttt{results/target\_physics\_store\_betaxanthin\_sign10.json}, \texttt{\_sign30}).
Standard energies at 1 M cannot be imposed as directions on this model; a
thermodynamically consistent program needs the concentrations as variables, which is
what the driving-force store below does with a linear program.

The stores that do exist. The linear MOMA store on the cassette strain (1,155 genes,
27 s on eight processes) has 107 lethal deletions against the parsimonious store's 93,
moves the growth ratio by more than 0.01 on 59 genes, and moves the $\log(1+|v|)$ vector
on 1,056 genes with a median relative distance of 0.39 to the parsimonious deletion
vector. The parsimonious store rebuilt on the HiGHS path keeps the 93 lethal deletions,
moves 17 ratios, and sits at a median distance of 0.015 from the GLPK build. The
max-min driving-force store (\texttt{target\_driving\_force\_store.py}) solves, per
deletion, for the metabolite concentrations between 1 $\mu$M and 10 mM that maximize the
smallest driving force over the reactions the parsimonious flux carries, with transport
and proton-translocating reactions unconstrained and, in the capped variant, reactions
priced above 100 kJ/mol set aside; the wild type's max-min driving force is $-1.16$
kJ/mol over 172 constrained reactions with 32 binding, 1,062 of 1,062 viable deletions
have a negative optimum, and 451 change the concentration vector. The sampled store
(\texttt{target\_sampled\_flux\_store.py}) draws six OptGP samples of each deletion's
cone at 99 percent of its optimum, 1,155 deletions in 6 h on twelve processes, and keeps
the mean over draws and, in a second store, the per-reaction spread.

Why the MOMA store differs so much from the parsimonious one: the GLPK build is
degenerate (Table~\ref{tab:store-degeneracy}, from
\texttt{store\_degeneracy.py}). Among the 1,031 deletions that leave growth unchanged,
the GLPK store's vector differs from its own wild type's for 1,025, at a median relative
distance of 0.38 in the encoding the affine reads; the HiGHS rebuild differs for 428
(median 0.0, ninetieth percentile 0.39)
and the MOMA store, anchored to one wild-type vector, for 18 (the prototroph's stores
are in the sibling document). So the parsimonious
optimum has alternative vertices for a large share of no-effect deletions under either
solver, and the GLPK path lands on a different one almost every time. Hypothesis
(untested): the vertex noise is what erased the growth block's signal in round 125, and
round 139 is the transformer read of the same store without it.

The store read without the backbone (Table~\ref{tab:store-baselines};
\texttt{target\_store\_baselines.py}, the 027 ladder's split, standardization and
selection, with a random forest added, the Flux Cone Learning form on one point per
deletion). Two things frame the numbers. The screen is genome-wide and the stores cover
the 1,155 GEM genes, so 3,255 of the 3,494 betaxanthin training genotypes read the
wild-type row and every store baseline learns from about 240 in-model deletions, while
the pinned test set is inside the model (628 of 629). And a gene's row is one vector
with no graph, no sequence and no pathway. Two of the ladder's reads were fragile to
this degeneracy and are made exact: a nearest-neighbor query on the wild-type row has
about two thousand identical training rows, so the $k$ neighbors taken by sort order
were an arbitrary subset that changed between runs, and every training row tied with
the $k$-th similarity is now averaged in; predictions within $10^{-9}$ count as one
rank; and the validation criterion is rounded to $10^{-6}$ before the grid is chosen.
Two consecutive runs then agree to every printed digit. On betaxanthin the GLPK growth
block carries nothing (ridge $-0.006$, kNN $-0.004$, forest $+0.009$); the whole GLPK
vector, the one round 117 read, carries 0.097 by kNN; the HiGHS rebuild of the growth
program carries 0.195 by kNN with a top-50 enrichment of 3.39, the highest rank of any
flux-side read on this split (the best single transformer arm is 0.121, the best six-seed
ensemble 0.153, the cosine kNN over ProtT5 0.145, reproduced here at 0.144); the MOMA
store 0.135 by ridge; the six flux statistics of the parsimonious build 0.145 by kNN;
the capped driving-force concentrations 0.152 by
ridge with a top-50 of 3.39, the metabolite side carrying rank linearly. The mean of
six samples of each deletion's flux cone at 99 percent of optimum carries 0.007 by
ridge, 0.064 by kNN and 0.102 by forest, below the parsimonious point it is sampled
around, as the sampled stores sat on essentiality (the essentiality document's round 87). The forest is not the
best model on any flux representation, so the Flux Cone Learning form does not rescue a
point representation. Rank and call separate as they did in every transformer round:
ProtT5 gives the best top-50 (4.59 alone, 4.99 with the GLPK store under a forest), the
clean flux store the best rank.

The region, not its point. The max-min program returns one concentration vector, but
the second law does not single it out: every log-concentration vector within the bounds
whose carried priced reactions all run downhill by at least the optimum is as
consistent, and that set is a polytope in $\ln c$.
\texttt{target\_concentration\_polytope\_store.py} samples it per deletion, the region
slackened by 1 kJ/mol below $B^*$ so it has volume where several reactions bind, by
coordinate hit-and-run from the point program's solution (a random direction stalls when
the start sits on the box, as the wild type's does; one metabolite per step does not),
20,000 burn-in steps, six draws 2,000 steps apart, the metabolites in no constrained
reaction uniform in the bounds. The chain is seeded from a hash of the polytope rather
than the row, so two deletions with the same region read the same row; seeded by row,
every one of the 1,062 viable rows differed from the wild type's, a sampling-noise
degeneracy of the GLPK kind, and under the polytope hash 455 do, beside the point
store's 451. The wild type's region constrains 234 of the 2,803 variable metabolites,
and over those the median spread of $\ln c$ across draws is 2.18 against 2.63 for a
metabolite the constraints never touch (uniform over four decades), so at this cap the
second law narrows a typical constrained metabolite by little and the information sits
in the few it pins; the mean over draws sits a median 0.12 from the point in relative
distance. Two stores, the mean concentration over draws with the point store's
statistics and the count of constrained metabolites, and that mean with the per-metabolite
spread of $\ln c$ appended. On the ladder the polytope mean reads 0.136 by ridge, 0.127
by kNN and 0.126 by forest, the one flux-side representation that every model reads above
0.12 (the point gives ridge 0.152 but kNN 0.072 and forest 0.098; the HiGHS flux vector
gives kNN 0.195 but forest 0.117), and the spread store 0.096, 0.138 and 0.128, so the
width of the region carries rank the mean does not under nearest neighbors. Round 147
(w164) is the transformer read of the polytope mean, paired against the point.

The annotation-only family, on the same split (Table~\ref{tab:go-models};
\texttt{target\_go\_models.py}). The essentiality study read DCell's ontology hierarchy
and a graph convolution over the GO multi-hot beside the transformer; neither had a
target head, so both are given one here on the ladder's split: DCell's hierarchy in the
essentiality script's shared-input form with a linear head on the root's state and
auxiliary regressions, and a perceptron with one hidden layer of 256 over the gene's
propagated GO multi-hot (2,341 of 2,366 columns with training support), which is what
the GO graph convolution reduces to on one deletion per instance; the same perceptron
over the HiGHS parsimonious store row, over ProtT5 and over their concatenations
separates the annotation, the sequence and the flux families on one model. Full batch,
Adam at $10^{-3}$, a masked squared error on standardized targets, 200 epochs, the
epoch selected on validation per-feature Pearson, six seeds. The GO perceptron reads
0.200 (sd 0.002 over seeds) with a top-50 enrichment of 4.79, selected at epoch 21 or
22 on every seed and trained in three seconds a seed: above every transformer arm (the
best single 0.121, the best six-seed ensemble 0.153), above the HiGHS store's nearest
neighbors (0.195) and with a call at the best transformer arms' level. The store row
through the same perceptron reads 0.119 with a top-50 of 2.00, ProtT5 0.103 and 4.13,
so the two inputs the transformer arms read, ProtT5 with the pathway multi-hot, are
the weaker two of the three families on this target and the pathway multi-hot is a
weaker annotation than propagated GO. Concatenation adds no rank: GO with the store
0.198 (sd 0.041), GO with ProtT5 0.173 with the best calls (4.99 single, 5.39
ensembled), all three 0.175 and 5.03. DCell's hierarchy over the same columns reads
0.049 (sd 0.043), selected at epochs 8 to 13, under the perceptron on every seed: the
one-subsystem-per-term wiring costs three quarters of what the flat annotation carries.
Two things bound the reading. GO annotation is curated in part from deletion
phenotypes, respiratory function among them, and the screen's top hits are respiratory
(the 027 read), so an annotation input can carry the target through the literature the
target itself produced; the split holds out genes, not annotations. And the transformer
arms were never given this input: the annotation the backbone reads is the pathway
multi-hot, and rounds 150 and 151 (w167, w168) put the GO multi-hot in the affine
slot, alone and appended to the HiGHS store, over every annotated gene, the interface
every other input has entered through. The same two inputs read by the ladder's three
models (Table~\ref{tab:store-baselines}) fix what the transformer adds to them: the GO
multi-hot reads 0.076 by ridge, 0.179 by nearest neighbors and 0.187 by forest with a
top-50 enrichment of 5.39, the highest call of any genotype-side read on this split; GO with the
HiGHS vector appended reads 0.123, 0.198 and 0.204 with a top-50 of 4.79. The forest
over the 6,539 columns round 151's affine reads sits 0.008 under that round's 0.211,
the size of the round's own seed spread, with the higher call, so on this input the
transformer's rank is what a backbone-free model of the same columns reaches, and
averaging its six seeds adds 0.001.

The measured side enters the same ladder. The strain's own M\"ulleder amino-acid pool,
nineteen concentrations in mM as an affine store over the 4,678 strains the screen
measured (a strain it did not measure reads the loader's reference, the population
robust mean per amino acid), reads 0.149 by ridge, 0.285 by nearest neighbors and 0.280
by forest, with top-50 enrichments of 3.99, 4.19 and 4.79: nineteen columns carry more
rank than any genotype-side representation and more than any transformer arm (0.216,
round 171). Appended to GO the pool reads 0.102, 0.174 and 0.264 with a forest call of
5.99; appended to GO with the HiGHS vector, 0.156, 0.195 and 0.247 with a forest call
of 6.19, the highest call of any read on this split. The ridge and the nearest-neighbor
reads fall when the annotation's 2,362 columns join the nineteen while the forest,
which selects columns, keeps 0.26 and gains the call; a plausible reason, unverified,
is that the two distance-based models weigh every standardized column alike, so the
pool is then a hundredth of the input. This read is phenotype to phenotype: the pool is
a measurement of the same strain in a different screen, on minimal medium, not the
strain's sequence, and the split by gene holds because the pool row is never the
betaxanthin target. What it fixes is that the amino-acid data carries betaxanthin
signal beyond the gene's identity, at a rank above the sequence, the annotation and
every flux store; the transformer's affine reading the same columns, alone with GO and
beside the HiGHS vector, is queued (rounds 175, 176).
