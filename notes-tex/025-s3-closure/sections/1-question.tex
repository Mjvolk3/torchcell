\section{The question\secstatus{todo}}

A genetic interaction score is not a measurement. It is a difference between a
measured fitness and a fitness expected from lower-order measurements, and the
expectation is a product. For two deletions $a$ and $b$ with single-mutant fitness
$f_a$ and $f_b$ and double-mutant fitness $f_{ab}$,
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{ab} = f_{ab} - f_a f_b ,
  \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}
and for three, with the three doubles already scored,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{abc} = f_{abc} - f_a f_b f_c - \varepsilon_{ab} f_c - \varepsilon_{ac} f_b
             - \varepsilon_{bc} f_a .
  \label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
Both equations are stated verbatim in the supplementary text of Kuzmin 2018 and
Kuzmin 2020 (mirror keys \texttt{kuzminSystematicAnalysisComplex2018},
\texttt{kuzminExploringWholegenomeDuplicate2020}, \texttt{si/si1.md} lines 84 to 91
and 64 to 67), and Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} in Costanzo 2016 and Baryshnikova 2010. Substituting
Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} into Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} gives the form used here,
$\tau_{abc} = f_{abc} - f_{ab} f_c - f_{ac} f_b - f_{bc} f_a + 2 f_a f_b f_c$.

The S3 closure pool of the 025 build was assembled so that a model trained on it sees
every term of Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} for every triple it is asked to score: all 5{,}694
single-deletion records in the build, the 739{,}219 doubles whose gene pair lies
inside some measured triple, and all 376{,}732 triples. The training arm
(\texttt{cgt\_s3\_r\_kl\_fit\_031}, three seeds on IGB \texttt{mmli}) supervises
fitness and interaction jointly, with the 010 random split pinned over the triples.
That design carries an assumption worth testing before the runs finish: that the
fitness values in the pool and the interaction values in the pool are consistent with
each other under the equations that define the latter.

Two questions follow, and the document answers both against the source tables the
build was assembled from.

\notesec{Strength}
Does the fitness stored in the build reproduce the interaction stored in the build?
Eq.~\ref{eq:eps} on every closure double and Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} on every triple, the
recomputed score against the stored one, and the same recomputation on the raw source
rows as a control for the formula.

\notesec{Confidence}
Does anything stored in the build reproduce the p-value stored in the build? Each
interaction record carries a p-value from its source screen, or, when the build merged
several screens of the same genotype, a p-value the build computed itself. The
recomputation uses the stored standard deviations under the normal model the sources
describe, and for merged records compares the stored p against the p-values the
sources reported for the same genotype.

Three earlier readings frame the answer. The recapitulation of 2026-09-04
(\texttt{recapitulate\_tmi\_from\_fitness.py}) put the trigenic identity on the whole
build at $r = 0.23$ and concluded that noise accumulation, not a sign or scale error,
was the cause. The 025 loaders' provenance work established that the released
combined-mutant standard deviation is a colony sample SD over four colonies and that a
two-sided normal test on it ranks the reported p-values at $\rho = 0.985$. And the
additive-baselines report established that a per-gene additive ridge reaches
$r = 0.40$ on these labels without any fitness at all. What is new here is the
decomposition: which step of the join costs what, measured on the pool a model is
about to be trained on.
