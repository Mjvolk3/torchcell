\section{Where these tables enter the model\secstatus{todo}}

Figure~\ref{fig:cgt-module} places the flux module inside the cell graph transformer. The
module is neither of the two instrument classes the architecture previously had: a Type I
instrument maps a representation to a representation and a Type II instrument maps a
representation to a measured output, while the flux module maps a representation to a
\emph{flux vector}, a latent physical state from which phenotypes are then read. It sits
between them.

Three of its inputs are parameter tables rather than learned weights, and those three are
what this document builds. The $k_{cat}$ table sets the upper bound of the box that the
flux is parameterized inside, so it fixes what fluxes are reachable at all. The formation
energies set the reaction potential whose sign the second-law penalty acts on, which is
what makes that penalty a physical statement rather than a regularizer. The $K_M$ table is
built and not yet enabled, for the reason Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives.

\subsection{Why these two tables, in two equations\secstatus{todo}}

Each table earns its place by removing a specific failure the stoichiometry alone cannot.

\paragraph{$k_{cat}$ is what lets a genotype change a flux.} The capacity bound is
\begin{equation}
  |\nu_j| \;\le\; k_{cat,j}\,E_{g(j)},
  \qquad
  E_{g(j)} = f\big(H_{\mathrm{pert}}\big),
  \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
with $E$ the enzyme abundance folded up from the gene representation. Without it the only
limits on $\nu_j$ are yeast-GEM's own bounds, and those carry no capacity at all:
\textbf{4{,}129 of 4{,}131 reactions sit at $\pm1000$ or $0$}, which encodes direction and
on-off and nothing else. A gene deletion would then have no route to change the
\emph{magnitude} of any flux. Equation~\eqref{eq:capacity} is the unit conversion from an
amount of enzyme to a ceiling on rate, and it is the only term through which the genotype
reaches the network's capacity.

\paragraph{$\Delta_r G$ is what forbids loops without an integer variable.} The second-law
constraint is
\begin{equation}
  \nu_j\,\Delta_r G_j \;\le\; 0,
  \qquad
  \Delta_r G_j = \Delta_r G'^{\circ}_j + RT\!\!\sum_{i \neq \mathrm{H}^+}\!\! S_{ij}\ln c_i,
  \label{eq:secondlaw}
\end{equation}
where the tabulated formation energies supply the first term. Because $\Delta_r G$ is a
difference of potentials it sums to zero around any cycle, so no cycle can run downhill at
every step, and Equation~\eqref{eq:secondlaw} therefore forbids internal loops. That
matters because capacity does not: requiring growth at 90 percent of optimum narrows 2{,}316
reactions, 56.1 percent of the network, and leaves \textbf{427 reactions still free to take
any value in $\pm1000$}, all of them internal loops. Only the thermodynamic term removes
those, and it does so with no binary variable, which is what a loopless flux balance program
needs one for.

The heterologous cassette enters as a typed perturbation on the genome-scale model, which
is what puts a betaxanthin pathway and a gene deletion inside the same object rather than
in two parallel code paths.
