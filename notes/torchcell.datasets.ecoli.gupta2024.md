## 2026.10.07 - Row 43: Gupta 2024 global protein turnover, the first consumer of ProteinTurnoverPhenotype

`ProteinTurnoverPhenotype` was added to the schema for this record type and nothing used
it. `ProteinTurnoverGupta2024Dataset` is its first consumer: 13 records, one per released
growth condition, keyed on MG1655 b-numbers.

- loader: `torchcell/datasets/ecoli/gupta2024.py`
- tests: `tests/torchcell/datasets/ecoli/test_gupta2024.py`
- raw mirror: `$DATA_ROOT/torchcell-raw/guptaGlobalProteinTurnover2024/`
- dev store: `$DATA_ROOT/data/torchcell/protein_turnover_gupta2024/`

### The released quantity, verbatim

The released per-protein number is a **total half-life in hours**, not a rate. The
Supplementary Data file descriptions (`si/si3.md`, sha256
`87a1760129b2feb312a009dbe1486e3fbbc75fe6fcdbe0711b52576bf28ddc14`) say:

> Description: Half-lives for 3262 proteins across 13 growth conditions

and the consumed workbook column is headed, per condition,

> Average of half-lives (hrs) in C-lim chemostat, doubling time 6 hrs

The quantity behind the half-life is the **total** turnover rate, which is the single
free parameter the model fits. `paper.md` (sha256
`1be6b9d958235a80ce2421c552299dd6bf2371cccfed28a24bd813a30d3d35a2`), Results:

> (total turnover rate) is obtained by fitting this model to the experimentally measured signal of peptides.

and the Supplementary Note (`si/si1.md`, sha256
`5f913f0b846865ad0bcac57cfe7a9f1895d04dd2ec7b16db11bf28be10d1062a`), under "Protein
half-life fitting", fits "a free parameter $\mathsf { k } _ { \mathsf { D } } + \mathsf
{ D } )$" and states the identity that converts the two:

> Since $\mathsf { k } _ { \mathsf { t o t a l } } = \ln 2 / \mathrm { T } _ { 1 / 2 }$

**Unit.** Half-life in hours (the column header says `hrs`), so the derived rate is per
hour. The record stores both: `half_life` is the released cell verbatim and
`degradation_rate` is `ln(2) / half_life`, which the verifier re-derives at L3 (largest
`|ln2/T - k|` over 33,187 stored values: `0.000e+00`).

**What `degradation_rate` is NOT.** It is the TOTAL rate, active degradation plus
dilution, and `measurement_type` says so and names the reactor and the doubling time:
`n15_ammonium_tmtproc_total_turnover_rate_per_hour_chemostat_doubling_6h`. The ACTIVE
rate `k_D = k_total - D` is not stored, because it is negative wherever the fitted total
half-life exceeds the doubling time, which this release is full of (a stable protein), and
`ProteinTurnoverPhenotype` requires a non-negative rate. Clipping at zero would
manufacture a value the fit did not give.

### The uncertainty, and why no published standard error exists

`n_samples` is **2 biological replicates per condition** ("each with two biological
replicates"), and a protein quantified in only one of a condition's two replicate columns
has `n_replicates = 1` in that record.

The **released** dispersion is a per-REPLICATE 95% confidence interval on the fitted rate
(Supplementary Data 7), built from `curve_fit`'s parameter variance times a t quantile at
`dof = i x 8 - 1`, with `i` the protein's peptide count. `i` is not released, so the
interval does not invert back to the standard deviation it came from, and there is no
published standard error of the per-condition MEAN.

`degradation_rate_se` is therefore the paper's **own** n = 2 dispersion, applied on the
rate scale (the scale the stored label is on): `sd / sqrt(n)`, which for n = 2 is half the
difference of the two replicate rates. It is `nan` for a key with one replicate or with a
ceiling-flagged replicate, the landed convention for "this key has no SE" (the validator
admits NaN in the companion maps).

Two honest caveats, both measured rather than asserted:

- The point estimate and the SE do not share a centering. `degradation_rate` is `ln 2`
  over the authors' arithmetic mean half-life (the harmonic-mean rate), while the SE is
  the spread of the two rates around their arithmetic mean. Over the 28,610 released
  two-replicate cells the two rate estimators differ by a **median 0.0023 relative,
  0.053 at the 95th percentile**, with one pathological cell at 1.0.
- The released intervals are symmetric on the RATE, not on the half-life, which is the
  independent confirmation that the fitted parameter is the rate: of the 59,727
  non-ceiling released cells, 59,621 have `(ln2/lo + ln2/hi)/2` within 0.2% of
  `ln2/T`, 22 are off by a median 0.0034 relative (printing is rounded to three
  decimals), 84 have a negative upper endpoint on the half-life scale (the rate CI's
  lower bound crossed zero) and 7 read `[0.0 0.0]`.

### Ceiling cells are kept, and the class cannot say they are censored

2,084 of the 61,811 released replicate cells carry a trailing `*`. The two workbooks agree
on exactly which: Supplementary Data 1's `*` and Supplementary Data 7's `Undetermined`
match on **all 61,811 cells, 0 disagreements in either direction**, which the loader
asserts as a cross-source oracle. The measured ceiling is one value per doubling time:

| doubling time | ceiling (h) | flagged cells |
|---|---|---|
| 42 min (batch) | 2.0 | 179 |
| 3 h | 4.0 | 260 |
| 6 h | 8.0 | 174 / 64 / 45 / 225 / 196 / 168 / 264 / 64 (C, P, N, clpP, lon, hslV, triple, smpB) |
| 12 h | 16.0 | 170 / 168 / 107 (C, P, N) |

They are **kept**, because the authors keep them: every "Average of half-lives" cell is the
arithmetic mean of its available replicates including the flagged ones (0 mismatches over
28,610 two-replicate cells), and Table 1's per-condition protein counts include them.
Dropping them would delete the stable half of the proteome and break that oracle. What is
lost is the per-protein censoring FLAG, for which `ProteinTurnoverPhenotype` has no field;
every flagged cell is written to `preprocess/ceiling_cells.csv` with its condition,
replicate number and that condition's ceiling.

### Identifier route: two layers, both measured

The release keys on UniProt entries (`sp|A5A614|YCIZ_ECOLI`) plus a gene-name column,
neither of which is a locus tag. Both layers are DERIVED and are recorded in
`preprocess/identifier_route.csv` with the stored key per released row.

| layer | what it reads | rows it resolves |
|---|---|---|
| 1. UniProt `db_xref` of the pinned assembly | `/db_xref="UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot:<acc>"` on the GenBank CDS features of `ecoli_K12_MG1655_ASM584v2` (4,275 accessions carried, none claimed by two loci) | 3,225 |
| 2. released gene name through `reconcile_locus_tags` | the genome's symbol and synonym layers | 35 |
| unresolved | the two alternative-isoform rows | 2 |

Total 3,260 of 3,262 (0.99939) to unique b-numbers, with no two rows claiming one locus,
so `MIN_RESOLVED_FRACTION = 0.998`. Layer 1 takes precedence, and the one row where the
two layers DISAGREE shows why: `sp|P0A6E9|BIOD2_ECOLI` carries the gene name `bioD`, whose
symbol resolves to `b0778` (bioD1), while the accession resolves to `b1593` (bioD2). The
UniProt entry name is BIOD2 and the table carries `bioD1` as its own separate row, so the
symbol column is the imprecise one. A symbol-only route was measured for comparison:
3,244 of 3,262 (3,100 by symbol, 145 by synonym), with 15 retired names, `ade` ambiguous
across six loci, and that one misassignment.

`DerivedIdentifierRoute` has no member for a UniProt `db_xref` route, and a dict-keyed
phenotype carries no `identifier_mapping`, so the per-key route lives in the build
accounting. The DELETION perturbations, which the paper releases as gene symbols, DO carry
`identifier_mapping` with `route="gene_symbol"`.

### Genotype and environment

Conditions 1-8 are the unperturbed strain, so those records carry
`Genotype(perturbations=[])`: the axis that varies is the environment, and NCM3722 itself
is the reference's background rather than an edit of it. Conditions 9-13 carry one
`BacterialDeletionPerturbation` per deleted gene (three for the triple knockout), on
b-numbers resolved from the released symbols: clpP, lon, hslV, smpB. The three Keio-derived
alleles record `collection="Keio collection"`; smpB and the gift triple knockout do not.

The strain is NCM3722 and the assembly pin is MG1655, which is a DERIVED choice stated as
such. No NCM3722 assembly is in the genomes tier, and the paper states no genotype for
NCM3722 against any sequenced K-12, so it is carried as a `BacterialStrainBackground` on
MG1655 with `ProvenanceGap`s on `genotype_statement` and `construction`. The pin is MG1655
because that is the identifier space the release is written in: all 3,262 UniProt entry
names end in `_ECOLI`, UniProt's K-12 mnemonic, and 3,260 reach a b-number of
`ecoli_K12_MG1655_ASM584v2`.

Four media are built in the loader (NOT added to `MEDIA_LIBRARY`, which is a value
surface): `MOPS_MINIMAL` plus the stated glucose, with the limiting component replaced.
`MOPS_MINIMAL` already carries 9.5 mM ammonium chloride and 1.32 mM dipotassium hydrogen
phosphate, which are exactly this paper's non-limiting amounts.

| medium | glucose (% w/v) | NH4Cl (mM) | K2HPO4 (mM) |
|---|---|---|---|
| no limitation | 0.4 | 9.5 | 1.32 |
| carbon-limiting | 0.08 | 9.5 | 1.32 |
| nitrogen-limiting | 0.4 | 1.9 | 1.32 |
| phosphorus-limiting | 0.4 | 9.5 | 0.132 |

`duration_hours` is the labeling window: the LAST sampling time of that doubling time's
Methods Table 2 series, in hours (2.917, 12.15, 26.867, 36.1 for 42 min, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h).
It is what separates the same medium read at three dilution rates. The chemostat
conditions also carry `EnvironmentPhysicalPerturbation(factor=pH, magnitude=7.2)`; the
batch condition does not, because its pH is not stated.

`Environment` has no slot for a chemostat dilution rate or doubling time, so the doubling
time is named on `measurement_type` instead, where it belongs anyway: the stored rate
includes the dilution term.

The `phenotype_reference` is the wild-type N-limited 6 h condition, which is the comparator
the paper reads every knockout against.

### Verification

`torchcell/verification/` has no protein-turnover family verifier: `verify_protein_dataset`
reads `phenotype["protein_abundance"]` and cannot see a rate. So `gupta2024.verify_build`
composes the gate from the shared primitives in `torchcell.verification.levels` rather than
forcing the records through an unrelated family. All levels pass on the dev store:

Records come back from the LMDB in lexicographic key order (0, 1, 10, 11, 12, 2, ...), so
L1 matches each record to its condition by `(measurement_type, medium name, deleted
symbols)`, which is unique across the 13. Positional matching compared the wrong pairs and
failed, which is how the ordering was found.

### Did ProteinTurnoverPhenotype suffice

Yes for the label, with two representational gaps that cost released information and are
raised rather than worked around (the schema was not touched):

1. **No interval field.** The released uncertainty is a per-replicate 95% confidence
   interval, and the class has only `degradation_rate_se`. The intervals are consumed as
   the censoring oracle and are not stored. `UncertaintyType.ci95` exists and documents
   `SE = hw / 1.96`, but the class has no `uncertainty_type` field to declare it with, and
   the exact t quantile is unrecoverable, so relabeling a half-width as an SE was refused.
2. **No per-protein censoring flag.** 2,082 stored cells are right-censored at a known
   ceiling and the record cannot say which. They live in `preprocess/ceiling_cells.csv`.

A third, outside this class: `Environment` cannot express a chemostat dilution rate, which
every continuous-culture dataset will want.
