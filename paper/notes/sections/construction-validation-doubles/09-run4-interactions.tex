\section{Digenic interaction versus Costanzo --- measured, and not yet usable}
\label{sec:run4-eps}

The design supports the comparison cleanly. Both constituent singles of every double sit on
the same plate, so

\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{ab} \;=\; f_{ab} \;-\; f_a f_b
  \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}

is formed entirely within a plate --- no cross-plate normalization enters the interaction
term, and the three plates give three independent replicates of the same quantity, which is
what the across-plate bootstrap turns into an error bar.

Run against the published Costanzo $\varepsilon$ for the same pairs, the answer is
\textbf{$r=-0.245$ ($p=0.44$, $n=12$)}: no agreement, and the sign is wrong.

\subsection{Our own significance --- and which error term you use decides everything}

Section~\ref{sec:whyfixable} argued that a big-but-insignificant interaction is fixable
because the error bar is set by how many colonies we plate, and that is our choice. This
round is the first test of that argument, and the answer is more interesting than expected:
\textbf{colony count was never the binding constraint.}

Two error terms can be put on $\varepsilon$, and the table reports both.

\notesec{Within-plate} $\varepsilon$ is a function of three strain means measured on the
same plate, so by the delta method
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{var}(\varepsilon) \;=\;
  \operatorname{var}(f_{ab}) \;+\; f_b^{2}\operatorname{var}(f_a)
  \;+\; f_a^{2}\operatorname{var}(f_b),
\end{equation}
each variance being $(\text{colony SD}/\sqrt{n})^{2}$. This is the term Costanzo's own
$p$-value is built from, and the one that 14 colonies per plate rather than 4 was supposed to
shrink. It averages \textbf{0.058} across the thirteen doubles.

\notesec{Across-plate} The scatter of the three per-plate $\varepsilon$ values, which carries
the batch effect the within-plate term cannot see. It is \textbf{larger than the within-plate
term for 12 of 13 doubles}, by a median factor of \textbf{2.4} and up to \textbf{8.5}.

That gap is the finding. Judged on colony scatter alone, \textbf{11 of 13} doubles would
clear $p<0.05$ (median $|\varepsilon|/\mathrm{SE} = 5.9$). Judged honestly, on three
independent plate-level estimates, \textbf{2 of 13} do. Plating deeper does buy precision
within a plate --- it just buys precision on a quantity whose plate-to-plate reproducibility
is the actual limit. The lever for this assay is \emph{more plates}, not more colonies per
plate, which is the opposite of what Section~\ref{sec:whyfixable} assumed.

\notesec{Read the \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p} column with the caveat below} The test is a
two-sided $t$-test on three plate-level estimates, $df=2$ --- weak by construction, so a
non-significant call here is poor evidence of absence. And more importantly: with the WT
shift of Section~\ref{sec:wtshift} unresolved, this $p$ measures how \emph{reproducibly} we
recover a biased $\varepsilon$, not whether that $\varepsilon$ is right. Precision is not
accuracy. The two doubles that do clear $p<0.05$ (YER079W$+$YLR312C-B, YLL012W$+$YPL046C) are
\emph{not} the two Costanzo calls significant, and neither should be reported as a detected
interaction.

\subsection{Where the inflation comes from}
\label{sec:wtshift}

The singles are on the same scale as run 3's --- just shifted. Comparing our two rounds
directly, the eleven strains common to both correlate at \textbf{$r=0.685$ ($p=0.020$)}, but
run 4 sits a near-uniform \textbf{$1.26\times$} above run 3 (median ratio; dividing it out
leaves a mean absolute difference of 0.111). In run 3 every single scored \emph{below} WT
(0.67--0.92); in run 4 most score above it.

\notesec{Two correlations, not one} Two similar numbers appear in this document and they are
\emph{different comparisons} on the same eleven strains --- worth separating, because read
together they look like one statistic quoted twice:

\notetab{%
\begin{tabular}{llrr}
\toprule
comparison & against & $r$ & $p$ \\
\midrule
run 4 singles vs run 3 singles & our own earlier round & 0.685 & 0.020 \\
run 4 singles vs Costanzo SMF & the published reference & 0.706 & 0.015 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}{The two single-mutant correlations reported for run 4. Both are $n=11$ over the same
strains; they differ in what run 4 is compared \emph{to}. Neither is a restatement of the
other.}

This matters for $\varepsilon$ and not for the correlations because \textbf{$\varepsilon$ is
not scale-invariant}. Under a uniform inflation $f \to k f$,

\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon' \;=\; k f_{ab} - k^{2} f_a f_b,
\end{equation}

so the expectation term grows quadratically while the observed term grows linearly, driving
$\varepsilon$ negative in proportion to $f_a f_b$ --- exactly the $-0.926$ correlation
observed. A Pearson correlation, by contrast, is invariant to $k$, which is why the singles
still reproduce the published values at $r=0.706$ while every interaction is corrupted.

   pairs marked. Every point sits below zero and the published spread is not recovered. This
   panel is published as the record of a diagnosed artifact, not as a measurement of
   epistasis.}

\subsection{What is not yet known}

\textbf{Why the WT reference moved is untested.} At the colony level the WT median is
\emph{higher} than the mutant median on all three plates (ratio 0.90--0.94), so this is not
simply sick or contaminated WT wells. Run 3 named its wild type \texttt{BY4741} explicitly;
run 4's strain list gives it as \verb|'-|. Whether that is a different stock, a different
plating density, or something else has not been checked, and no correction should be applied
before it is --- rescaling to make the numbers look right would bury the question.

\subsection{Diagnostic: it is not the wild-type wells}
\label{sec:wtdiag}

\textbf{This subsection reports a diagnostic, not a correction.} Re-scoring against the plate
median rather than the WT wells does not make the numbers right --- it makes them differently
normalized, and the plate median is a poor stand-in for a wild type here because 25 of the 26
strains are mutants and 13 of those are doubles that genuinely grow less, so it compresses the
very range the assay exists to measure. It was run to answer one narrow question: is the shift
specific to the WT \emph{wells}, or a property of the whole round?
(\path{experiments/W019-echo-crispr-array/scripts/run4_wt_reference_diagnostic.py}.)

\textbf{The answer is no.} Every diagnostic is unchanged or marginally worse under the plate
median. The inflation is not an artifact of the wild-type wells, so the hypothesis in the
previous subsection --- a different WT stock or plating density --- is not supported and
should not be pursued as though it were.

What the swap does clarify is where the asymmetry actually sits. It is not that the reference
is low; it is that \textbf{the doubles are low relative to the singles}, and no choice of
denominator changes that, because a common factor cancels from the ratio $f_{ab}/(f_a f_b)$
only when it applies to all three equally. Here the singles sit near 1.2 and the doubles near
0.8 against either reference.

\notesec{Hypothesis, untested} A double carries two selection cassettes where a single carries
one. If the second marker costs growth independently of any genetic interaction, every double
is depressed by a roughly constant factor and $\varepsilon$ absorbs it --- which would produce
exactly this signature, and would not be epistasis. Distinguishing that from real biology
needs a marker-only control (a double-cassette strain with no second gene deleted), which this
round does not contain. Nothing here tests it.

\subsection{What is not yet known}

The WT-wells explanation is now \emph{tested and rejected} (Section~\ref{sec:wtdiag}). What
replaces it is a broader question about the round: why the doubles sit low relative to the
singles under any normalization. The marker-load hypothesis above is one candidate and is
untested; so is the possibility that this is partly real biology at 48\,h 12\,min on this medium.

Distinguishing them belongs at the bench rather than in the analysis, and the cheapest
discriminating control is a \textbf{marker-only strain} --- two cassettes, one gene deleted ---
which would separate a constant construction cost from genuine epistasis. Until something of
that kind exists, no $\varepsilon$ from this round should be reported as an interaction,
and no rescaling should be applied to make the numbers look reasonable: every correction
available here is cosmetic and would bury the question.
