\section{The readouts: four rounds, one large effect\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:readouts}

Three rounds returned within two days of each other on 2026-09-08, the $2^4$ factorial on
Delta, the mechanism round on IGB (both sized by the arithmetic of
\cref{sec:strand-arithmetic}) and the metric-aligned objective round, which trains on
$1 - r$ directly (\cref{sec:findings-pearson}); the ranking round that replaced the dead
half of the metric-aligned one is a day old at this read. Every score below is
\file{roll_max}, the maximum of a centered 5-epoch rolling mean of
\file{val/expression/pearson_per_feature}, read from the full per-epoch history, and every
contrast is at a MATCHED budget computed as the smallest final epoch across the runs being
compared. The scoring rule is an upward-biased order statistic; it is the same rule on every
side of every contrast, so the bias cancels in a difference and not in an absolute number.
Runs still training are read at the epoch they had reached, stated with each number, and
W\&B's \texttt{finished} state is not evidence that a run ended: these runs train offline
on IGB and every sync from the login node stamps the snapshot \texttt{finished}. Whether a
job is alive is read from \file{squeue}.

\pfstep{Embedding content is worth $0.07$, three times the replicate spread} The ProtT5
cells average $0.129$ against $0.061$ for random vectors of the same width, and no cell of
one level overlaps any cell of the other (\cref{fig:readouts-factorial}a). The contrast was
built as a content test at matched width 1{,}024, so it says the content matters and says
nothing about \file{calm}, the incumbent's embedding, which is not a level of the grid. The
one healthy draw of the incumbent-with-ProtT5 cell scores $0.141$ against the incumbent's
$0.161 \pm 0.010$ at the same budget, one draw against eight, and that is not a resolved gap
in either direction.

\pfstep{Weight decay is a measured null; the readout row is retracted} The weight-decay
effect is under $0.008$ against a resolution of about $0.02$ at two standard errors. The
readout row is a null by construction, not a measurement: \file{linear_readout} was
declared in the config and carried in every run's recorded configuration, but the model
never passed it to the head, so both levels trained the two-layer head. The parameter
counts logged to W\&B are identical across the two levels, $1{,}447{,}309$ under the deep
trunk and $682{,}988$ under the shallow one, which is the tell. The option was wired on
2026-09-10 with a regression test, and linear against two-layer readout is unmeasured until
the v12 readout round (\cref{sec:next}). The trunk is a small effect in favor of the deeper
one, $-0.012$ over all runs and $-0.018$ once the stuck run is dropped, and it is sharper
under ProtT5 (the interaction). Of the three factors that were in fact varied, one acts.

\pfstep{One run in 32 never learned} Cell 1 seed 0 (\texttt{te8272kk}: ProtT5, $L{=}6$,
two-layer readout, weight decay $10^{-8}$) sits at $0.019$ at its best and $-0.007$ at
epoch 1{,}399 with \file{nmse} $1.010$, while its seed-1 twin is the grid's best single run
at $0.169$. That pair alone carries most of the pooled variance, which is why both readings
are given. Its cause is not measured.

\pfstep{The pair term alone is a null} Its two paired differences disagree in sign and
average $+0.008$. The per-gene readout arms average $+0.027$, and the combined arm is
positive at both seeds, but the round was sized to resolve about $0.06$ at two pairs
(\cref{tab:findings-power}), so this is a direction and not a result. What every curve does
show is the dynamics: the per-gene arms reach their peak between epochs 1{,}200 and 2{,}600
and give part of it back (\texttt{R\_pergene} ends at $0.137$ and $0.170$ against peaks of
$0.189$ and $0.224$), while the reference and basis arms are flat or still rising at 8{,}500.
Whether the give-back is overfitting of the extra per-gene parameters is a hypothesis and
is not measured.

\pfstep{A tagging defect, found in the readout} The arm script carried a wave-4b
\texttt{R\_ref} branch ahead of the wave-5 one; a shell \texttt{case} takes its first
match, so every reference run of this round is tagged \file{stage-wave4b}. The readout
selects by arm and config tag, and the shadowing branch is removed.

\pfstep{Five of six batch-32 runs are on the floor, and the loss does not see it} The
three pure-Pearson runs peak between $0.13$ and $0.145$ at epochs 150 to 240 and then fall,
their predicted spread dropping from about $0.3$ to $10^{-7}$ over the same epochs
(\cref{fig:readouts-pearson}b): the network's output goes constant per gene. Their
validation loss holds near $0.56$ throughout, because the loss drops a constant column as
invalid and averages over the columns still varying, so it reads $1 - 0.44$ on a handful of
genes while the metric reads $0$ on all of them. The failure mode the design named is the
one that occurred, with the difference that the loss never reads its connected zero. The
squared-error anchor at unit weight did not prevent it: \file{pearson_mse} collapsed by
epoch 14 at one seed and 360 at another, and its surviving seed reads \file{roll_max}
$0.194$ at epoch 6{,}576 with a last value of $0.172$, inside the long-budget arm band and
still training.

\pfstep{The batch-64 runs survive and sit on the band, not above it} At 9{,}114 and
9{,}118 epochs, with spread ratios of $0.89$ and $0.90$, they read \file{roll_max} $0.194$
and $0.186$ against the incumbent's $0.1965 \pm 0.0222$ at 9{,}900, peaked near epochs
1{,}900 to 2{,}400 and sit at $0.148$ and $0.160$ now, $0.03$ to $0.05$ below their
peaks with under 800 epochs to run. Batch size and solo placement are confounded in that
arm, so why it survives is not measured; the in-batch correlation being taken over twice
as many strains is a hypothesis. Nothing in the round beats the quantile head at any
budget read so far. The question it was launched to answer, whether the metric's late
rise is generalization gap or objective disagreement, is not answered by two survivors
that peaked by epoch 2{,}400: on the Pearson objective the late rise did not happen, and
whether that is the objective or the batch size is the confound above.

\pfstep{The anchor kills it, the pure loss does not} With the squared error at unit
weight both seeds went to the per-gene mean by epoch 160 and stayed there; the MSE
gradient is about $50\times$ the ranking gradient at initialization and reaches its own
optimum, the constant, which the ranking term's $\log(n!)/n$ floor does not pull it off.
That is the mirror image of the Pearson round, where the anchor was what kept the one
surviving batch-32 run alive. The pure ranking loss has not collapsed at any of its four
runs, and its spread ratio climbs, from under $0.05$ before epoch 200 to $0.80$ at 1{,}959,
as a shift-invariant objective that rewards larger score gaps should make it; the
leaderboard's \file{nmse} of $74.7$ for these runs (\cref{tab:findings-head}) is that
free location, not a failure to order.

\pfstep{Behind the band at matched epochs, by $0.01$ to $0.04$} At epochs $\le 2{,}000$
the batch-32 pair reads $0.168$ and $0.150$ against the long-budget arms' $0.1745 \pm
0.0151$, one seed inside the band and one below it; the batch-64 pair at 2{,}400 reads
$0.144$ and $0.150$ against $0.1821 \pm 0.0175$ at 2{,}800, both below. Every curve is
still rising and none has peaked, so these are epochs reached and not results. On the
quantity ListMLE targets, the per-feature Spearman, the batch-32 seed-0 run reads
$0.173$ at 1{,}920 epochs where the surviving Pearson-objective runs read $0.171$ to
$0.178$ at 6{,}500 to 9{,}100; whether that is a real ordering advantage or a matched-epoch
artifact is answerable when the round ends. Hypothesis (untested): the ranking gradient is
small in absolute terms, norm $0.3$ at initialization, and the learning rate pinned at
$3 \times 10^{-4}$ for the pinball loss is low for it, which would show as the slow start
of \cref{fig:readouts-listmle}a.

\subsection{Packed five-day runs die of host memory\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:readouts-oom}

Four of four packed tasks that ran past three and a half days ended \file{OUT_OF_MEMORY}
(\texttt{2369697} 0--1, \texttt{2371531} 2--3), at \file{MaxRSS} 61.4 and 61.1~GB. The
Pearson-round packed tasks read 21.3~GB at 1.9 days and the solo batch-64 tasks 17.2~GB.
Hypothesis (untested): resident memory grows roughly linearly through a run, in which case
the packed Pearson tasks reach the 60~GB line near day five, at the edge of their wall. What
grows is not identified; the \file{file_system} sharing strategy introduced in
\texttt{f1bfa95b} to stop the file-descriptor exhaustion is the obvious suspect and is
unverified. The memory of a running job cannot be raised, and the two rounds that finished
lost one run per task to it rather than the whole task.

genotype. \textbf{d}, split-half inside one batch with the wild-type cells split too, so
the two profiles share no cell. \textbf{e}, gene co-variation: within one panel the
correlation of every gene pair across that panel's strains, then the two panels' upper
triangles compared, which needs no shared strain and admits the Caudal isolates.
\textbf{f}, the paper's own comparison: DE-gene counts per genotype (published) and the
profile correlation its script computes (not published). Generated by
\file{comparison_designs.py}.}
\label{fig:comparison-designs}
\end{figure}

\pfstep{The stored values are on another scale, and one cell in five is a sentinel} The
microarray $\log_2$ ratios have standard deviation $0.23$ and $0.18$; the stored
Nadal-Ribelles values have $10.1$ as stored and $0.89$ after the correction below
(\cref{fig:cross-study}a, b). They are scanpy \file{logfoldchanges} from a Wilcoxon
rank-sum call, computed as $\log_2$ of the ratio of two expm1-transformed means with a
$10^{-9}$ pseudocount, which returns $\pm 23$ to $\pm 33$ whenever one group's mean is
zero: $20.2\,\%$ of the stored cells carry such a value and $22.6\,\%$ of genes carry it
in more than half of their records. The Wilcoxon test is the paper's DE test; the fold
change beside it is a separate quantity, and the paper used it only above an
independent filter at mean normalized counts of $1.27$ and a $p$ threshold, whereas the
loader stored it for every gene with a nonzero count. Those cells are treated as absent
here. Even after that, the per-strain spread of a Nadal-Ribelles profile is bimodal, with
a mode at sd $0.86$ against the microarrays' $0.19$ and $0.15$ (\cref{fig:cross-study}c),
so the remaining values are still not on the microarray scale.

\pfstep{On the shared deletions the two panels almost do not agree} Over 914 shared
deletions the per-strain profile correlation with Kemmeren has median $0.003$, against
$0.744$ for Kemmeren with Sameith over 82; the per-reporter test-retest median is $0.030$
against $0.620$; the OLS slope of Kemmeren on Nadal-Ribelles over $4.06$ million shared
cells is $0.006$ against $0.82$ on Sameith (\cref{fig:cross-study}d--f). On the
reporters where Kemmeren's effect exceeds one $\log_2$ unit the agreement is weak but
above chance, median $0.10$ with signs agreeing $60\,\%$ of the time (chance $50\,\%$,
the microarray pair $93\,\%$). The gene mapping is right: the cys3 deletion carries MET14,
MET3 and MET6 up by $3$ to $3.7$ and CYS3 itself down, the induction that deletion is
known for. The paper's only comparison to Kemmeren is a count of genes at $p < 0.05$ over
837 shared genotypes; it reports no profile correlation.

\pfstep{What the agreement tracks} It rises with the strain's effect size in Kemmeren
(Spearman $0.37$ over 914 strains) and with the reporter's variance across strains
($0.29$ over 5{,}405 reporters), and it does not track the number of cells behind the
pseudobulk at all (Spearman $0.02$; median 92 cells per genotype)
(\cref{fig:cross-study}g--i). A pseudobulk that was merely undersampled would agree
better where more cells were pooled; this one does not.

\pfstep{Recomputing the fold change from the cells does not recover agreement} The
single-cell object (\file{seus_split.RData}, Zenodo 14062629, now in the raw mirror) holds
326{,}438 control cells over 3{,}506 genotype labels with raw UMI counts, so the statistic
can be replaced. Three were computed per genotype against the 500 pooled wild-type cells:
a bulk-like pseudobulk ratio, summed UMI to counts per million,
$\log_2((\mathrm{cpm}_g + 1)/(\mathrm{cpm}_{wt} + 1))$; Seurat's \file{avg_log2FC}; and
scanpy's own formula, which reproduces the stored sentinel rate ($22.6\,\%$ against
$20.2\,\%$, cell-by-cell sentinel status agreeing $98\,\%$) and confirms what was
stored. Against Kemmeren over 1{,}002 shared deletions the per-strain median is $0.013$,
$0.008$ and $0.011$ for the three, the per-reporter median $0.019$, $0.020$ and $0.012$,
and the slope $0.002$, $0.010$ and $0.000$ (\cref{fig:cross-study-recomputed}a--e); none
tracks cell count. The statistic was not the cause.
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/cross_study_recomputed.json}

\pfstep{The conditions differ in the medium, and the pooling is brief} Kemmeren and
Sameith grew each strain alone in SC with $2\,\%$ glucose at 30\,$^\circ$C in 24-well
plates to early mid-log at OD$_{600}$ 0.6, two independent cultures each hybridized in
dye-swap against a common wild-type reference RNA, with same-day wild-type cultures and a
pool of 200 wild-type profiles as the statistical reference, BY4742 MAT$\alpha$ (Sameith
follows the same procedure by citation and adds no numbers of its own). Nadal-Ribelles
recovered the URA3-marked collection on URA$^-$ at 25\,$^\circ$C for 48 h to saturation,
diluted each strain 1:40 into YPD in 96-well plates for 6 h to OD$_{660}$ 0.6 to 0.8
(temperature not stated), pooled the plates into one flask, applied the 15-minute
control or NaCl treatment to the pool, and fixed it in methanol; the pooled window is
the treatment, not the growth. Cells were captured on Singleron GEXSCOPE microwell
cartridges, 14 for the control condition, and the reference is 500 wild-type cells from
seven barcoded wild-type strains in the same run. Kemmeren's own SC-against-YPD
comparison of wild type found 128 genes changed at 1.7-fold, so the baseline differs, and
a deletion's response can differ with it. Hypothesis, unmeasured here: the medium lowers
cross-study agreement below the $0.62$ same-platform reference. Whether it takes it to
zero is what the within-study replication and the cell-level measurements below address,
and those do not depend on Kemmeren.

\pfstep{Read-through from the barcode cassette is not what puts counts on the deleted
gene} The redesigned cassette shortens the heterologous terminator from 262 to 43 nt so
that the pTEF1-driven URA3-barcode transcript ends at the deleted gene's own terminator,
and 3$'$-end capture reads that transcript at the deleted locus. If that were the source
of the deleted gene's counts in its own cells, those counts would sit at one pTEF1 level
whatever wild type expresses there. Measured over 2{,}724 genotypes with at least 20
cells, the fraction of a genotype's cells detecting its deleted gene tracks the
wild-type fraction all the way down (Spearman $0.64$) at about $0.6\times$ in every bin,
and for the 1{,}586 deleted genes wild type itself detects in under $5\,\%$ of cells the
genotype's own cells detect it in a median $2.9\,\%$. The counts at the deleted locus
scale with native expression; the cassette does not supply them.
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/nadal_readthrough_and_signature_tests.json}

\pfstep{Where a response is detectable, about half the labeled cells carry it; for most
genotypes none is detectable} Two single-cell tests that never use the deleted gene. For
the 240 shared deletions with at least 20 Kemmeren reporters beyond one $\log_2$ unit,
every cell of the genotype and every wild-type cell was scored on Kemmeren's signature
(mean over up reporters minus mean over down reporters). The score separates the
genotype's cells from wild type at a median AUROC of $0.53$ (IQR $0.50$ to $0.61$), 8 of
240 above $0.8$; a median $7.5\,\%$ of a genotype's cells sit above the wild-type 95th
percentile, against $5\,\%$ by chance. The 8 that separate are the strong regulators,
\textit{rfx1}, \textit{rim101}, \textit{skn7}, \textit{sam1}, \textit{ada2},
\textit{snf5}, \textit{swi3}, YGR122W, and in those $33$ to $68\,\%$ of the labeled
cells sit above the wild-type 95th percentile, the rest inside the wild-type range. The
second test asks for any reproducible signal: each genotype's cells were split in half,
a 50-gene signature derived from one half against half the wild-type cells, and the
other half scored against the held-out wild-type half. Over 207 genotypes with at least
40 cells the median AUROC is $0.52$ (IQR $0.49$ to $0.57$) against a null of $0.49$
(wild-type cells playing a genotype), with 18 above $0.65$ and 6 above $0.8$; the two
tests agree on which genotypes separate (Spearman $0.24$).
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/scripts/nadal_signature_mixture.R,
nadal_split_half_signal.R}

The excess-zero purity estimate (median $0.36$ to $0.40$ from the deleted gene's own
counts; \cref{fig:cross-study-recomputed}f) and the signature fractions above say the
same thing for the genotypes with a strong response: roughly half of the cells under a
label are that genotype. For the majority of genotypes the labeled cells carry no
reproducible signal at this depth (a median of 92 cells at 215 to 7{,}713 UMI each), and
whether that is the same impurity, a response too small for the depth, or a response
absent in YPD is not separable here. Batch composition is not the cause; 3{,}307 of the
3{,}506 genotypes span three or more of the 14 batches and the wild-type cells span all
of them. What produces the impurity in the strong-response genotypes, the
clone-and-genotype barcode consensus (a cell called by only one of the two libraries
keeps that call), ambient barcode reads in the microwell format, or strain identity in
the library, is not measured here.

\pfstep{The deleted gene cannot be read off its own profile} A deletion removes its own
transcript, so in a clean knockout profile the deleted gene sits at the bottom. In
Kemmeren it is the single most reduced gene in $59.5\,\%$ of 1{,}479 strains and in the
bottom $1\,\%$ in $93.4\,\%$, with a median own value of $-2.48$. In Nadal-Ribelles it
is at rank 1 in $0.1$ to $0.8\,\%$ of genotypes and in the bottom $1\,\%$ in $2.0$ to
$8.2\,\%$ under the three statistics, with a median rank at the 35th to 47th percentile
of the profile (chance is the 50th) and a median own value of $-0.02$ to $-0.41$
(\cref{fig:identify-deletion}). The rate does not rise with cells per genotype
($2.0\,\%$ under 30 cells, $0\,\%$ above 300). The deleted gene is not identifiable
from a Nadal-Ribelles profile, and read-through from the cassette is not the reason (above).
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/nadal_identify_deletion.json}

\pfstep{The panel does not replicate itself} The microarray pair has a test-retest of
$0.74$ per strain; the single-cell panel had none, and without one a cross-study zero
cannot be read. The same genotype's cells fall in several of the 14 control cartridges,
so its pseudobulk in one batch against that batch's wild-type cells can be compared with
its pseudobulk in another (\cref{fig:replication-coverage}a). Over 17{,}554 pairs of
batches of one genotype with at least 15 cells in each, the median Pearson is $0.043$;
two different genotypes across the same two batches, cell counts matched, give $0.022$;
$1.8\,\%$ of same-genotype pairs exceed $0.2$ against $0.2\,\%$ of the null. The value
does not rise with cells ($0.042$ at 15 to 30 cells, $0.051$ above 120). With the pooled
wild type as reference both become $0.11$: the batch effect (wild type against wild
type between batches, sd $0.75$ $\log_2$, $16\,\%$ of genes beyond 2-fold) enters every
profile as a common component, and a per-batch reference removes it. Splitting one
genotype's cells within one batch in half, with the wild-type cells split too so no
reference is shared, gives $0.080$ against an other-genotype null of $0.051$ over 786
genotype-batches with at least 30 cells (\cref{fig:replication-coverage}b); with a
shared reference the same split reads $0.37$, which is the reference's sampling noise. A
Nadal-Ribelles genotype profile reproduces itself by $0.02$ to $0.03$ above a different
genotype, and $0.04$ within the study is what a zero against Kemmeren predicts.
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/scripts/nadal_batch_replication.R}

\pfstep{Coverage and depth} A Kemmeren profile carries $6{,}182$ reporters with a ratio
and a $p$ value; Kemmeren calls $52\,\%$ of its 1{,}484 mutants responsive (at least
four genes at 1.7-fold and $p < 0.05$ after excluding 58 wild-type-variable genes; the
paper's figure is $47\,\%$), with a median of 4 changed genes. A Nadal-Ribelles cell has
a median 934 UMI over 492 genes, and a genotype's own cells put five or more UMI on a
median $2{,}826$ of the object's $6{,}951$ genes (120 under 10 cells, $2{,}558$ at 30 to
100, $4{,}810$ above 300); the pseudobulk reports $4{,}601$ because the 500 wild-type
cells satisfy the summed-UMI rule on their own (\cref{fig:replication-coverage}c, d).
The paper's DEG count per genotype (median 10) is a cell-count artifact: it falls with
cells at Spearman $-0.67$, because a genotype with few cells has more genes at a zero
mean and each is a $\pm 23$ sentinel that passes $|\log\mathrm{FC}| \ge 1$.
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/nadal_replication_coverage.json}

\pfstep{What the paper itself computed against the deleteome} Its released script
computes, per genotype with more than five cells, the Spearman between the single-cell
fold change and Kemmeren's $M$ over the shared genes, and the two studies' changed-gene
counts; it plots only the counts (its Supplementary Fig.\ 1i) and the text calls that
``a consistent correlation'' without a coefficient. Rerun from the released objects
over 874 genotypes: the count-against-count Spearman is $0.23$ ($0.26$ with cells
partialled out), and the profile correlation it computed and did not show has median
$0.013$, IQR $-0.006$ to $0.037$, one genotype above $0.2$
(\cref{fig:replication-coverage}e, f). The paper's own number is the number measured
here.
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/scripts/nadal_paper_deleteome_comparison.R}

\pfstep{Coarser structure: what the two panels share is small and on the growth axis}
Six tests on the 1{,}002 shared deletions, each with Kemmeren against Sameith as the
same-platform reference (\cref{fig:cross-study-structure}). Which genes vary: the
reporter's sd across the shared strains agrees between Kemmeren and Sameith at Spearman
$0.88$ and between Kemmeren and Nadal-Ribelles at $-0.19$, and the Nadal-Ribelles sd
tracks depth ($-0.50$ with wild-type counts per million); it is sampling noise. Which
strains respond: Kemmeren's changed-gene count against the genotype's cross-batch
replication in Nadal-Ribelles, Spearman $0.20$ over 260 ($p = 0.001$), small and real.
Strain similarity: the strain-by-strain correlation matrices agree between Kemmeren and
Sameith at $0.52$ (the top $1\,\%$ Kemmeren pairs rank at AUROC $0.73$ in Sameith) and
between Kemmeren and Nadal-Ribelles at $0.03$ (AUROC $0.50$). Function within one study,
with no second study involved: deletions annotated to one GO term of 5 to 100 genes are
more similar than other pairs at AUROC $0.55$ (cellular component) and $0.53$ (process)
over Kemmeren's responsive strains, $0.50$ and $0.48$ over all its strains, and $0.51$
to $0.53$ in Nadal-Ribelles ($p = 4 \times 10^{-10}$ for the stored values); a function
signal of the size Kemmeren shows with its nonresponders included. The ribosomal protein
module (159 genes, the slow-growth axis): mean $\log_2$ per strain agrees between
Kemmeren and Sameith at $0.54$, between Kemmeren and Nadal-Ribelles at $0.05$; against
the authors' own per-cell iESR score averaged per genotype it is $-0.11$ ($p = 6 \times
10^{-4}$, the expected sign) and against their ribosomal read fraction $+0.09$. The best
case, deletions Kemmeren calls responsive whose Nadal-Ribelles profile replicates across
batches at $r \ge 0.1$: 14 strains, median cross-study $r$ $0.078$, one above $0.2$
(\textit{nmd2}, $0.22$).
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/cross_study_structure.json}

As released, the panel cannot be pooled with the microarrays as a knockout-expression
target, and the reason is now measured rather than inferred: a genotype's profile does
not reproduce itself within the study ($0.04$ across batches against $0.02$ for a
different genotype), so it cannot reproduce Kemmeren's. What survives is small and
sits on the growth axis and on function at the level Kemmeren shows with its
nonresponders included. The paper's own profile correlation against the deleteome, which
it computed and did not show, is $0.013$. Where the labeled cells are half another
genotype, a per-cell filter on the deleted gene's own counts (the cells at zero) or a
re-assignment from the raw barcode reads would be needed, and the released object
carries only the consensus label. Its per-genotype dispersion, a single-cell
heterogeneity readout the loader already carries, is a different target and is not
affected in the same way.

\subsection{The knockout proteome against three expression panels\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:readouts-proteome}

Messner et al.\ 2023 (doi:10.1016/j.cell.2023.03.026) measured 1{,}830 proteins over
4{,}549 deletion ORFs of the same collection by SWATH mass spectrometry in synthetic
minimal medium, one measurement per strain against a 388-replicate HIS3 reference. An
exploratory pass in July (\file{proteome_expression_eda.py}, served graph) set it
beside Kemmeren strain by strain: per gene across the 1{,}350 shared deletions a median
Pearson of $0.08$ (IQR $0.04$ to $0.14$, $1.7\,\%$ of genes above $0.3$), per strain
across the 1{,}832 shared genes $0.04$ (IQR $-0.02$ to $0.12$), and a ridge map from
either modality to the other recovering $2$ to $3.5\,\%$ of held-out variance, with the
medium (SM against SC) named as a confound that attenuates every number. The question
here is whether that weak strain-level agreement is what a knockout proteome and a
knockout transcriptome share, or whether the two agree at another level, and how the
single-cell panel and the natural-isolate panel of Caudal et al.\ 2024 (943 isolates,
RNA-seq) sit against both.

\pfstep{Strain by strain} Protein against mRNA $\log_2$ ratio for the same deletion,
z-scored per gene across strains, over the proteins measured in both: with Kemmeren
median $0.036$ per deletion over 1{,}350 and $0.075$ per protein across deletions, the
July numbers reproduced from the LMDBs; with Nadal-Ribelles A $0.011$ and $0.012$ over
2{,}038 shared deletions (\cref{fig:proteome-covariation}a, b). Caudal shares no strain
with a deletion panel, so it has no row here.

\pfstep{Gene by gene} The co-variation of two genes across a panel's strains, one
gene-by-gene correlation matrix per panel on the Messner proteins, and the Spearman
between two panels' upper triangles (\cref{fig:comparison-designs}e): the proteome
against the Caudal isolates $0.36$ over 1{,}777 genes, with the top $1\,\%$ proteome
pairs having a median mRNA $r$ of $0.48$ against $0.07$ for the rest; against Kemmeren
$0.31$; Kemmeren against Caudal $0.48$. Against Nadal-Ribelles A the three read $0.05$,
$0.11$ and $0.08$ (\cref{fig:proteome-covariation}d--f). The abundance level itself
agrees as expected, protein in the HIS3 reference against mean expression over the
isolates at Spearman $0.68$ over 1{,}823 genes (\cref{fig:proteome-covariation}c).
\src{experiments/028-knockout-expression/results/proteome_expression_covariation.json}

Three panels that differ in platform, perturbation type and medium agree pairwise on
which genes co-vary, at $0.31$ to $0.48$, while their strain-level agreement is $0.04$
or less; what a deletion does to a transcript and to its protein is largely different,
but the gene modules that move together are the same modules. The single-cell panel
agrees with none of the three at either level. For the campaign this is the reason to
carry the proteome as a target of its own rather than as a proxy for expression, and to
leave the single-cell panel out of both.
