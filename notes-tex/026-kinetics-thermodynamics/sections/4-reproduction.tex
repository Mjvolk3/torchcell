\section{Reproducing Wu et al.'s Fig.~3\secstatus{todo}}

\subsection{What the original figure claims\secstatus{todo}}

Wu et al.~\cite{wuSystematicallyExploringYeast2026} build Yeast-MetaTwin, a comprehensive
metabolic network for \emph{S.~cerevisiae} obtained by expanding reaction rules over the
yeast metabolome, and ask how the resulting \emph{underground} reactions, the promiscuous
side activities of enzymes named for a different
substrate~\cite{demouraferreiraProteinconstrainedModelsPinpoints2025}, differ kinetically
from the \emph{core} network already in
Yeast9~\cite{zhangYeast9ConsensusGenomescale2024}. Their Fig.~3a--h answers it with eight empirical
cumulative distributions, one per predictor and parameter: $k_{cat}$ from DLKcat, UniKP,
EITLEM-Kinetics, TurNuP and DeepEnzyme, then $K_M$ from Boost\_KM, UniKP and
EITLEM-Kinetics. Their conclusion is that underground metabolism is separated from known
metabolism by $K_M$ rather than by $k_{cat}$.

The construction is taken from their own notebook (Figure~\ref{fig:kinetics-fig3}). Their
split is on
whether a reaction identifier contains \texttt{rxn}, which is how their tables encode a
predicted rather than a curated reaction.

\subsection{The per-pair predictions behind Fig.~3 were not released\secstatus{todo}}

The authors' own released predictions, split core against underground, are not available.

Their notebook reads its tables from \texttt{Results/kcat\_km\_predict/}, naming
\texttt{yeast8U\_kcat\_predict.csv} for DLKcat, \texttt{yeast8U\_unikp.csv} for UniKP on
both parameters, \texttt{yeast8U\_kcat\_result\_TurNuP.csv} for TurNuP and
\texttt{yeast8U\_km\_km\_predict.csv} for Boost\_KM. That directory is in neither released
artifact. The GitHub repository ships \texttt{Results/} with nine subdirectories, none of
them \texttt{kcat\_km\_predict}, and its \texttt{source\_data} holds only
\texttt{fig2-a}, \texttt{fig3-g}, \texttt{fig3-j}, \texttt{fig4-b}, \texttt{fig5-b},
\texttt{figs11} and \texttt{figS8}; the file named \texttt{fig3-g.csv} contains EC-number
counts rather than a $K_M$ distribution. The 6.6 GB Zenodo archive
(10.5281/zenodo.13911783) contains exactly three top-level directories, \texttt{Data},
\texttt{Data\_retrosynthesis} and \texttt{esm}, and of its 1{,}021{,}022 entries
\textbf{zero} match \texttt{kcat}, \texttt{unikp}, \texttt{turnup}, \texttt{eitlem} or
\texttt{deepenzyme}.

The Data Availability statement says data for reproducing all figures is available on GitHub
and Zenodo. The \textbf{underground arm cannot be drawn}, since generating the Yeast-MetaTwin
reaction set is a separate retrobiosynthesis pipeline this work does not rebuild;
Figure~\ref{fig:kinetics-fig3} re-derives the curves by running the same published models
over the same organism from hash-pinned mirrors.

\subsection{The log base is not a convention\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:logbase}

\textbf{Two of these models exponentiate base 2 and four base 10}, and
reading either wrong produces turnover numbers that look entirely credible: DLKcat
exponentiates with \texttt{math.pow(2, \ldots)}, and reading that as base 10 inflates every
turnover number by a power of roughly 3.3.

That matters for how Fig.~3 should be read. A shift between two curves within one panel is
interpreted as a property of underground metabolism, while the spread \emph{between} panels,
on the same pairs, is a factor of three to five, so an effect smaller than that sits inside
the disagreement between the instruments measuring it.

TurNuP~\cite{krollTurnoverNumberPredictions2023} and CatPred, at 10.8 and 9.9 s$^{-1}$,
are the two exceptions to the median agreement; TurNuP's and its coverage is
55.2 percent rather than 95. Both follow from the same property: it consumes the
\emph{whole reaction}, and the authors' code invalidates a reaction as soon as one
participant lacks a structure. That excludes 1{,}147 of 2{,}540 reactions, almost all
acyl-chain-resolved lipids.

\textbf{Class-plausible is not the same as right, and the two parameters separate on it.}
Every $k_{cat}$ predictor beats the EC-median lookup, and for $K_M$ only CatPred does by a
margin (Figures~\ref{fig:plausibility-kcat}a and \ref{fig:plausibility-km}a), so on these
pairs the four incumbent $K_M$ predictors add nothing measurable to knowing the enzyme's
function and the eighth does. That gain is not held out: 44 of the 58 pairs are in CatPred's
own training table.

\textbf{The consequence for the flux layer.} An enzyme-constrained bound $v_j \le
k_{cat,j} E_j$ inherits panel a as its error bar, so a single predictor's table should not
be treated as the capacity constraint; the registry of Section~\ref{sec:mirrors} exists for
that reason, since predictor choice is a hyperparameter with a measured effect size.

\subsection{Accuracy against measured values, and which database can supply them\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:accuracy}

UniProt accession, a substrate, an assay temperature and a pH. Restricting to wild-type
enzymes assayed between 20 and 40\,\textdegree C and joining on (accession, substrate) gives

\textbf{The ranking is not the agreement ranking.} EZKinetics leads on rank accuracy, UniKP
is second, and EITLEM leads on magnitude while placing fourth on rank; DeepEnzyme is last on
both by a clear margin while sitting mid-pack on agreement. So the pairwise-agreement matrix
in panel c cannot be read as a quality ordering. On $K_M$
EZKinetics leads on rank as well, by a smaller margin over Boost\_KM, and its bias of
$-0.05$ orders of magnitude is the smallest of any predictor on either parameter apart from EITLEM's.

\textbf{The leakage caveat is real and is not a reason to discard the numbers.} Every one of
these predictors trained on BRENDA or SABIO-RK, from which the Open Enzyme Database is
itself assembled, so these values are an \textbf{upper bound} on held-out accuracy. A
predictor that cannot reproduce values it may have trained on has a defect no test set
forgives, and the ranking between predictors is more robust than any single value because
the leakage is common to all.

\textbf{The measurable subset is not the easy tail, which was worth checking rather than
assuming.} Accuracy can only be scored on the best-studied enzymes, so the worry is that
predictors agree there and the accuracy does not transfer. The across-predictor spread on
the 58 pairs is \textbf{1.50 orders of magnitude} against \textbf{1.45} over all 3{,}891
pairs, so the scored subset is if anything harder than the model as a whole and the
upper-bound caveat stands on leakage alone.

\paragraph{Why the Enzyme Engineering Database supplies no comparison.}
EnzEngDB~\cite{longEnzymeEngineeringDatabase2026} was read in full and queried read-only on
2026-09-02. It flattens every assay output into one untyped \texttt{fitness\_value} column
by explicit design, so it holds \textbf{no $k_{cat}$ and no $K_M$}; it has no EC field and no
organism field; and it contains \textbf{zero} occurrences of \texttt{yeast},
\texttt{cerevisiae} or \texttt{Saccharomyces} across the paper, all 140 public experiment
records and all 1{,}342 analysis rows. Its six scaffolds are bacterial and archaeal, and
93 percent of its measurements are whole-cell rather than purified enzyme. Overlap with the
3{,}728 yeast-GEM catalytic units is \textbf{zero}, drawn at zero in
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}d. It remains worth mirroring on a different axis, as a
sequence-level genotype-to-phenotype corpus of designed variants.

\subsection{What that correlation is made of\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:ec-class}

A Pearson of 0.90 across a quantity spanning six orders of magnitude is not self-evidently
good. If most of that range separates enzyme \emph{classes}, a predictor that has learned
only which reaction an enzyme catalyzes earns a high correlation while saying nothing about
what \emph{this} enzyme's turnover number is, which is what a capacity bound reads.
Separating the two cases needs enzymes of the same function, and yeast cannot supply them:
its 90 EC4 classes include only four with three or more distinct enzymes. The whole Open Enzyme Database can. It was mirrored under the same provenance
rules as the yeast slice, 35{,}753 records at \texttt{sha256}
\texttt{b8d1e652\dots}, of which 22{,}975 are wild-type. Every wild-type row carries a
$k_{cat}$ \emph{and} a $K_M$, so the two parameters below are measured on one set of rows
rather than on two independent samples.

\textbf{Enzymes doing the same job differ by far more than the measurement can explain.}
The median EC4 class spans 1.77 orders of magnitude against a measurement floor of 0.12, a
factor of 15 (Table~\ref{tab:scales}). So
the within-class spread is biology rather than noise, and it is the floor for any predictor
that is really conditioning on function. Three quarters of classes have a standard
deviation above 0.5 orders of magnitude and 79 percent span more than a full order of magnitude.

\textbf{Knowing the exact EC number explains under half the variance.} The one-way
intraclass correlation rises from 0.04 at EC1 to \textbf{0.46} at the full four-digit
number for $k_{cat}$ (0.58 for $K_M$). The intraclass correlation is quoted rather than the
$R^2$, which reaches 0.59 at EC4, because $R^2$ rises as classes get smaller whether or not
the class means genuinely differ, and at EC4 the median class holds four enzymes.

\textbf{The correlation is mostly the class.} Every predictor loses most of it when the
between-class range is removed. Read with the lookup-table result, these models rank enzyme \emph{functions} much better
than they rank enzymes \emph{within} a function, and it is the second that an
enzyme-constrained bound needs, since a flux model already knows which reaction it is
parameterizing. What is established is the size of the within-class signal rather than an ordering on it.

\subsection{EZKinetics, the seventh predictor and the only condition-aware one\secstatus{todo}}

EZKinetics (Le Yuan, Zhengyi Zhang and Huimin Zhao) was added after the five above. It is
\textbf{unpublished}, with the manuscript under review, so it has no DOI and is pinned by
repository and commit (\texttt{le-yuan/EZKinetics}, MIT since 2026-04-16, revision
\texttt{bf7a897}) rather than cited. It is also the only predictor here that ships its
weights inside the repository rather than on figshare, Zenodo or Hugging Face.

\textbf{Which of its 36 models to run is not a matter of taste.} The repository holds three
parameters crossed with four protein encoders and three substrate encoders, and any pair
produces numbers. \texttt{inference/predict.py} fixes the combination, and the shipped
scalers confirm it independently through their feature widths: $k_{cat}$ uses ESM2\_650M
with ChemBERTa-77M-MTR, $1280 + 384 + 2 = 1666$, and $K_M$ uses ESM2\_3B, $2560 + 384 + 2 =
2946$. The two extra features are pH and temperature. Output is base 10, the same convention
as UniKP.

\textbf{The reproduction gate is on the median, and that is a deliberate choice.} A
max-based gate would be the wrong statistic: reproduction here is not bit-exact and cannot
be, since our ESM2 forward pass runs on different hardware and a different torch build. The
residual survives setting the protein batch size to 1, so it is not attention over padding;
beyond that the cause is not established and no explanation is offered. What is established
is the scale, and 0.03 orders of magnitude at the 90th percentile against an across-predictor
disagreement of 1.28 orders of magnitude cannot move any conclusion here.

\textbf{Conditioning is worth a median 0.046 orders of magnitude, and we cannot use it}, as
much as 0.77 on a single row. yeast-GEM carries no per-pair assay pH or temperature, so
\textbf{the production run is necessarily condition-blind}, at 7.0 and 25.0\,\textdegree C,
which keeps EZKinetics comparable with the other condition-blind predictors and means its
Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} accuracy is measured with one input held at a default. It leads both
rankings anyway. The $K_M$ table was built the same way, with the ESM2\_3B encoder its
model expects, after the same gate passed at a median of 0.00003 orders of magnitude.

Coverage is 3{,}552 of 3{,}728 catalytic units, the same substrate-structure ceiling
DLKcat, UniKP and EITLEM hit; median predicted turnover is 4.62\,s$^{-1}$, inside the 3.98
to 4.17 band the other sequence-plus-SMILES predictors occupy.
