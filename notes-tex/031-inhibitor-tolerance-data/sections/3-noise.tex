\section{Replication, error, and the ceiling on prediction\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:noise}

Two views of noise are available for every condition, and where they can be joined
they check each other (\file{replicate\_noise\_and\_ceilings.py}).

\notesec{The served view}
The served record carries the replicate count and, where the source released it, a
sample SD from which the SE is derived. Per condition the reliability index
$\mathrm{rel} = 1 - \overline{\mathrm{SE}^2}/\mathrm{Var}(\text{response})$ over genes
is the share of the across-gene spread that replicate noise cannot produce. Under the
usual measurement model, response $=$ truth $+$ noise with the noise independent of the
truth, $\sqrt{\mathrm{rel}}$ is the ceiling on the Pearson correlation between a
measurement and the noise-free truth, and $\mathrm{rel}$ itself is the ceiling on the
correlation between two independent measurements of the same condition, which is what a
held-out replicate, or a second dataset, can reach.

\notesec{The empirical view}
The raw matrices the loaders consumed hold the replicates the served SD summarizes:
Vanacloig's three batches, each a log2 CPM ratio against its own batch's control columns
(the loader's quantity), and Hillenmeyer's replicate arrays of one condition label.
Pairwise Spearman between replicates over genes, and the Jaccard of their bottom-5\% hit
sets, are the model-free reliability of a condition and of its hits. Raw labels are
mapped to served compound names with the loaders' own resolver, so the two views join.

\notesec{What the two views say}
Vanacloig's three batches agree at a median Spearman of 0.33 and its hit sets at a
Jaccard of 0.24 (Table~\ref{tab:noise}, Figure~\ref{fig:noise}b,c). Its served index
tracks the raw agreement at a rank correlation of 0.87 (Figure~\ref{fig:index}d), so
the served SE is an honest per-compound noise estimate for this dataset. For Hillenmeyer
it is not: the served index has a median of 0.92 for HOM and 0.84 for HET while the
raw replicate arrays agree at 0.39 and 0.26, and the two views correlate at only 0.40
and 0.43. Plausible reason, unverified: the served SD exists only for the third of
records with more than one array and describes array-to-array scatter within one
control set, while the raw replicates of a label span control sets and scanners. For
Hillenmeyer the raw replicate agreement is the ceiling to quote.

\notesec{The ceiling per Vanacloig compound}
Figure~\ref{fig:ceilings} and Table~\ref{tab:vconditions} give every Vanacloig compound
its ceiling. Methyl methanesulfonate, 2,2'-bipyridine, crystal violet, ferulic acid and
methylglyoxal sit above 0.92. Isobutanol is at 0.89, furfural at 0.88, ethanol at 0.91.
Six compounds have an index below 0.1, and three of those (sodium glyoxylate, sodium
butyrate, 5-hydroxymethylfurfural) are at or below zero: at the doses used, their
replicate scatter is as large as the spread between genes, and there is nothing for a
model to predict. 5-hydroxymethylfurfural is the one loss that matters for the
hydrolysate question.
