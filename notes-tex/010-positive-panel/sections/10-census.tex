\section{The whole corpus, and what the demonstration should be\secstatus{todo}}

One screen cannot say whether the monotone ladder is rare everywhere or
concentrated in queries nobody has walked. The Kuzmin trigenic corpus answers
that from measurements alone, at a cost of minutes against the GPU-days a new
inference space would take, so it comes first.

\subsection{The census\secstatus{todo}}

329{,}462 trigenic records with a deletion array, across 421 query doubles.
286{,}002 have a complete four-rung lattice once all three singles and all three
doubles resolve, which is 1{,}716{,}012 orderings.

The ladder exists and is common enough to build on. What it does not do is
carry an interaction. Across 286{,}002 measured triples, not one both climbs
monotonically and clears the strong positive tier, and only 27 clear the ordinary
call. The median $\tau$ of a monotone triple is $+0.0071$ against $-0.0089$ for
the corpus, a difference with no biological content.

What the ladders do carry is a beneficial query double. 83.9 percent of monotone
triples sit under a query double fitter than wild type, against 23.4 percent of
all triples, and the median query fitness is 0.992 for a hosting query against
0.859 for one that hosts nothing. So an ordering that never steps back is mostly
a report that the first two deletions were already good, which is the greedy
result, not an epistatic one.

\subsection{The demonstration is the valley, not the ladder\secstatus{todo}}

A monotone route is exactly what a greedy stack-and-keep search can find, because
it never has to accept a loss. Reading the same census through that lens inverts
the result.

Nearly three quarters of the fitness wins in the corpus are invisible to a search
that accepts only improvements. That is the claim worth demonstrating, and it is
the one a predictor is needed for: the endpoint has to be predicted, because no
sequence of local decisions arrives at it.

The barrier is usually shallow rather than deep. On the best available route the
median dip sits 0.004 \emph{above} wild type and the 90th percentile only 0.033
below it, so the obstacle is a step backwards, not a lethal intermediate. That
makes these strains cheap to build and it removes the objection that valley
crossing needs a large tolerance for loss. It also means the demonstration must
be stated as non-monotonicity rather than as a deep fitness valley.

Both figures in this section come from measurements. Nothing in them depends on
the transformer, which is what makes them a specification for it rather than a
result of it.
