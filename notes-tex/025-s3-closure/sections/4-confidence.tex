\section{Confidence: the p-value against what the build holds\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:confidence}

\notesec{What a p-value in the build is}
Two kinds of p-value sit under the same field. A record kept from one screen carries
the source's p-value unchanged: for every one of the 41{,}392 single-screen closure
doubles and 363{,}818 single-screen triples, the stored p equals the source row's p
to the last digit. A record merged from $k$ screens carries a p-value the build
computed, a two-sided $t$-test of the $k$ merged scores against zero with $k-1$
degrees of freedom. Every source p-value that went into that record is gone.

The sources' own p-values are not recomputable from anything they released. All four
papers describe the null as a pooled variance: Baryshnikova 2010 combines an
array-strain term from wild-type control screens with a query-strain term pooled
across the screen (\texttt{si/si1.md} lines 262 to 288), and Kuzmin estimates the
array term from 91 wild-type control screens (line 63). The released tables carry only
the combined mutant's own colony standard deviation. The one formula written out
explicitly, for Kuzmin 2020's query-query interactions (\texttt{si/si1.md} line 52),
is a one-sided normal test of the score against the propagated variance of the
expected product. What can be tested is whether that normal model, on the standard
deviations the build stores, ranks the stored p-values.

\notesec{A single-screen digenic p is the double's own colony SD}
On the 41{,}394 single-screen closure doubles the normal test on
$\sigma_{ab}/\sqrt{4}$ ranks the source p at $\rho = 0.984$
(Figure~\ref{fig:confidence}a), the same ordering the loader provenance found on the
Kuzmin raw table. Its calls contain 606 of the source's 613 at the intermediate
threshold. The test is not the source's test: it calls 1{,}505 doubles, 2.5 times the
source, because the source's pooled null is wider than one double's four colonies.
Adding the singles' stored standard deviations to the null lowers the ranking to
$\rho = 0.813$, so the stored single-mutant SD is not the quantity the source
propagated. Applying the same test to the recomputed score instead of the stored one
calls 13{,}705 doubles, 22 times the source, with 418 in common: the recomputed score
carries the join's error but the test does not know it.

\notesec{A trigenic p is reproducible from nothing in the build}
On the 339{,}616 single-screen triples the same test on the triple's own SD ranks the
source p at $\rho = 0.199$ (Figure~\ref{fig:confidence}b); propagating every term of
Eq.~\ref{eq:tau} through the stored SDs gives $\rho = 0.097$. The trigenic null
variance is dominated by the two digenic control-screen terms, whose variance was
estimated from the same-batch control screens and not released, so the triple's
colony SD carries almost none of it. The test on the stored $\tau$ recalls 0.91 of the
source calls but makes 5.2 times as many; on the recomputed $\tau$ it agrees with the
source on 2{,}323 of its 46{,}957 calls.

\notesec{A merged p is a different statistic}
On the 697{,}825 merged closure doubles the stored $t$-test p correlates with the
median source p at $\rho = 0.053$ (Figure~\ref{fig:confidence}c), and with the normal
test on the merged double's SD at $\rho = 0.686$. The $t$-test measures agreement
among the $k$ duplicate scores, not the score against measurement noise, and with one
or two degrees of freedom it is both too lenient and too strict: 5.4 percent of merged
doubles are stored as $p < 0.05$ against 2.0 percent by the source median, while
1{,}754 doubles significant in every one of their sources are stored as not
significant. At the intermediate threshold the stored fields call 3{,}169 merged
doubles and the source medians 5{,}719, with 2{,}125 in common. Among the 12{,}914
merged triples, 505 are significant in every source and 406 of those are stored as
not significant.
