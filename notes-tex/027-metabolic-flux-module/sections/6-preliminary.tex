\section{The grid on Delta\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:preliminary}

\subsection{What produced the 144 cells\secstatus{todo}}

Job array \texttt{21813442\_[1-8]} plus its replacement task \texttt{21814993\_0} on
\texttt{bfjt-delta-gpu}, partition \texttt{gpuA40x4}: nine nodes, four A40 per node,
\textbf{two runs per GPU}, so 72 workers, each owning two cells and its own Optuna database.
Six arms times 24 seeds is 144 cells, sharded by whole seed so that a wall-clock kill leaves
every arm at the same seed count. Per-worker databases are what avoid the race that had
several workers claiming one trial from a shared file and silently turning four cells into
duplicates.

Two earlier attempts are part of the record. Array \texttt{21797666} on \texttt{bbub}, laid
out as twelve seeds per arm, ran every worker into the embeddings coupling of
Section~\ref{sec:couplings} (\texttt{No gene embeddings available}) and completed seven
tasks of six and a half hours each with zero epochs trained. Task 0 of \texttt{21813442}
lost seven of its eight workers in the first minutes to
\texttt{FileNotFoundError: data/sgd/genome/data.db}, which is the genome-rebuild race: the
dataset constructor defaults to \texttt{overwrite=True}, so the first worker to open the
genome deletes the file the other seven are reading. The fix is \texttt{overwrite=False},
which the older experiments already pass; tasks 1 to 8 read the corrected script at start
and task 0 was canceled and resubmitted with fresh Optuna databases, since a canceled
worker leaves its trial in \texttt{RUNNING} and a reused database would have dropped a cell
without saying so.

The primary endpoint is Spearman on the pinned Merzbacher 639-gene test split at an epoch
selected on validation, so the selection set and the reporting set are disjoint. The
decision rule is the difference of differences in Section~\ref{sec:arms}.

\subsection{Three faults found and fixed before submitting\secstatus{todo}}

\textbf{The Open Enzyme Database mirror was absent from Delta.}
\texttt{resolve\_kcat\_table} catches the resulting \texttt{FileNotFoundError} and returns
an empty record list, which replaces every measured $k_{cat}$ with the organism default. The
enzyme-constrained arm would have run with no enzyme constraints and reported a plausible
number. The mirror is 516\,kB and was synced; the coverage gate exists to abort exactly this
case, and it can only fire if there is a mirror to measure.

\textbf{\texttt{WANDB\_ENTITY} was unset}, so runs would have landed under whatever default
entity the node carried while 026's are under \texttt{zhao-group}, leaving the arms
unjoinable at analysis time.

\textbf{\texttt{OMP\_NUM\_THREADS} was unset}, so eight workers on a 32-CPU allocation of a
64-core node would each have defaulted to the node's core count.

\subsection{Three couplings the backbone change forced\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:couplings}

Moving 027 onto the strong backbone broke three things, each found by running a smoke test
rather than by reading the code, and each silent in a different way.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \item The perturbation head defaults to eight attention heads and 90 is not divisible by
    eight, so its cross-attention refuses to build. 019 uses six.
  \item The model reads \texttt{cell\_graph["gene"].x} only when it is told it has
    precomputed embeddings. With the learnable table disabled and \texttt{node\_embeddings}
    unset it finds neither and raises. It also indexes the embedding dataset to infer its
    width, so it needs the objects rather than their names.
  \item Dropping 026's \texttt{build\_dataset} dropped its module globals with it.
\end{enumerate}

A two-epoch local smoke run on the corrected backbone moved loss 1.0586 to 1.0088 and
validation Pearson $-0.0214$ to $+0.1055$, and computed the pinned test Spearman at
$+0.0261$ on $n = 629$. Two epochs is not a result. What it establishes is that the
nine-graph \texttt{prot\_T5} backbone builds, trains and scores end to end.

\subsection{The prespecified decision, on the full grid\secstatus{todo}}

All eight array tasks of \texttt{21813442} completed on \texttt{bfjt-delta-gpu}, six to six
and a half hours each, 64 workers, \textbf{no worker lost}, and the replacement task
\texttt{21814993} completed in 6:30 with its own eight. 144 cells were written, giving

the 10 the noise floor requires.

\textbf{Verdict: the metabolic module is not shown to improve betaxanthin prediction.}

Every one of the 144 cells is a W\&B run with its per-epoch curves, and
Table~\ref{tab:wandb-runs-027} links each by seed and arm, so any number above can be
followed to the training it came from. The project is
\href{https://wandb.ai/zhao-group/torchcell_027_bxfx}{\texttt{zhao-group/torchcell\_027\_bxfx}}.

\paragraph{The D1 interval moved twice, and the sequence is worth recording.} At the first
eight balanced seeds it was $[-0.1172, -0.0237]$, excluding zero. At 20 seeds it was
$[-0.0787, +0.0026]$, including zero, and the claim that the module was \emph{measurably
worse} was withdrawn on that basis. At the full 24 it is $[-0.0724, -0.0031]$ and excludes
zero again, by 0.0031. \textbf{The prespecified sample size was 24, so that is the result;
the two earlier readings were interim looks at a design that had not finished.} The verdict
was FAIL at every one of the three, which is the part that did not depend on where the
interval landed. The honest summary of the magnitude is that the anchored module is worse
than no module by an amount the full design can just resolve from zero, and anyone reading
$-0.0031$ as a comfortable margin has read it wrong.

\paragraph{The permuted arms are why D1 is not just the raw gap.} With the training labels
destroyed the same contrast is $-0.0093 \pm 0.0124$. So roughly a fifth of the raw $-0.0465$
gap is reproduced by an architecture that cannot have learned anything, which is exactly the
confound D1 exists to subtract and exactly what a comparison against zero would have
credited to biology.

\paragraph{Stoichiometry alone is the one arm that does not hurt.} \texttt{flux\_off}, which
keeps the network and the box but switches off the energies, is the top measured arm at
$+0.0388$ and sits level with the published Flux Cone Learning baseline at $+0.0391$, a
difference far inside one standard error and not a claim of either direction. Its contrast
against no module at all is $+0.0118 \pm 0.0144$, indistinguishable from zero.
\textbf{The cost is specifically in the thermodynamic anchor}: \texttt{flux\_anchored} minus
\texttt{flux\_free} is $-0.0490 \pm 0.0135$, so learning the potential beats reading the
tabulated one.

\subsection{Correction: every cell is a constant predictor\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:collapse}

The numbers above were read as a comparison of trained models. An audit of the prediction
dumps the trainer writes at the selected epoch, \texttt{audit\_prediction\_collapse.py},
says they are not. \textbf{Every one of the 144 cells outputs a near-constant across the
905 held-out genotypes.} The largest prediction spread in any cell is 0.8 percent of the
target spread, and the median across cells is under $10^{-5}$ of it. Pearson and Spearman
are scale-free, so a correlation can still be computed from the residual jitter, and that
is what every entry in Tables~\ref{tab:armresults} and \ref{tab:decision} is: the
correlation of jitter five orders of magnitude below the signal with the truth.

\input{tables/prediction_collapse.tex}

The two families of collapse are different, and the difference explains the arm ordering
without any biology. The no-module arms sit at the target mean, which is the loss-optimal
constant under CRPS when the model has found no signal; their training loss reads 1.00 from
epoch 10 onward, the standardized variance, and never moves again. The flux arms sit at
constants as far as $-7$ from the mean, so they have not even fit the intercept: their
training loss is still falling at epoch 199 (12 percent over the last 50 epochs for
\texttt{flux\_anchored}), driven by the penalty terms rather than the data term. A model
whose output is a wrong constant scores below a model whose output is the right constant
on any correlation computed from jitter, and that is the whole of the ``cost of the
thermodynamic anchor'' reported above.

\textbf{What survives.} The verdict that the module is \emph{not shown} to improve
betaxanthin prediction stands, because nothing in the grid predicts betaxanthin. The
stronger reading, that the anchored module is measurably worse and that the cost sits in
the tabulated energies, is withdrawn: a comparison between two collapsed models says
nothing about either. The D1 interval excluding zero by 0.0031 is a property of the jitter.

\textbf{Where the defect must be.} The same nine-graph \texttt{prot\_T5} backbone reached
validation Pearson 0.434 on this target under the 019 Lightning trainer (job 1344,
Table~\ref{tab:families}), so the target is learnable and the architecture can learn it.
The 027 loop in \texttt{train\_bx.py} is a hand-written trainer sharing none of that code
path. Hypothesis, untested: the pooled arm's immediate settle at the mean, within ten
epochs and at every seed, points at the loss or the inputs rather than at optimization
noise, and the first things to check are whether the perturbation actually reaches the
model in this loop, whether the CRPS head's $\log\sigma$ is absorbing the fit, and the
standardization of the masked pooled target. The trainer also logs no prediction spread,
which is the one diagnostic that would have caught this at epoch 10 rather than after
ten node-days; \texttt{pred\_sd\_ratio} goes next to \texttt{val\_loss} in the next
version.

\paragraph{What this does and does not settle.} Twenty-four seeds, one phenotype, and a
trainer that did not learn in any arm. \textbf{None of the parameter tables built in this
work was wired in}, and after the correction above the grid tests neither the module's
structure nor its parameters. The order of work is now fixed: first a trainer that
reproduces the 0.434 on the no-module arm, then the module arms on that trainer, and only
then the merged $\Delta_f G$ table and a predicted $k_{cat}$ table.

\subsection{The plateau, and the recipe that leaves it\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:plateau}

The constants are not a defect in the loop. Twenty-nine single-GPU runs on GilaHyper on
2026-09-09 (slurm jobs 1610 to 1638, one script and one log per run under
\texttt{experiments/027-betaxanthin-metabolic/}) show that every betaxanthin run, on every
trainer and every commit since July, starts on the same mean plateau: a validation
prediction spread near $10^{-4}$ of the target spread and a training loss of exactly the
standardized variance. What varies is when a run leaves it, and that is set by the recipe.
The 019 trainer on today's tree with the July recipe, betaxanthin alone, a Gaussian CRPS head
and the graph regularizer at $6.5\times10^{-5}$ over two layers, has a spread ratio of 0.003
at epoch 16, 0.12 at epoch 23 and 0.42 at epoch 28, with validation Pearson 0.20, 0.26, 0.32
and 0.39 at epochs 23, 24, 27 and 28 and 0.25 to 0.40 through epoch 34 (job 1635). Five
commits from 2026-07-26 to 07-31 re-run the same way escape at epochs 21 to 24 (jobs 1623,
1626, 1630, 1632). The August 020 grid, with hard attention masking at six layers and no
regularizer, escaped at epochs 117 to 157 in its W\&B histories, which is a slower recipe
rather than a slower code base.

The grid above ran neither element. Its loop sliced $\log\sigma$ off the CRPS head before
the loss, so the head was scored by MSE, and it never passed the regularizer to the model,
so \texttt{graph\_reg\_loss} was zero; under that recipe 200 epochs were not enough for any
of the 144 cells. Both are now switches on \texttt{train\_bx.py}, which also logs the spread
ratio every epoch; under CRPS plus regularizer its own pooled cell leaves the plateau on
the same path as the 019 trainer at a slower clock: spread ratio 0.021 at epoch 16, 0.12 at
epochs 26 to 28, 0.30 to 0.40 at epochs 32 to 35, with validation Pearson 0.15 to 0.28 over
epochs 27 to 35 (job 1634). A ten-epoch collapse abort, which the first diagnostics ran
with, would have killed every one of these runs; the abort's patience now defaults to 200.
The regularizer alone had not lifted the spread by epoch 21 (job 1638), where the combined
recipe was already at 0.025, so CRPS is doing at least part of the work. The module arms
under the combined recipe, \texttt{flux\_off} and the new \texttt{flux\_capacity} arm with a
predicted per-gene enzyme abundance in the capacity bound (jobs 1636, 1637), were still on
the plateau at epoch 31 with a spread ratio under $10^{-3}$ and their constraint terms
dominating the loss at 3 to 6 against a data term near 0.5. The flux arms escape later than
the pooled arm, if they escape at this budget at all, and that is the next measurement: the
same recipe, a longer budget, and the spread ratio read before any correlation.
