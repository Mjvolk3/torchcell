\section{Molecular encoders and their coverage\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:encoders}

A compound enters a model as a vector, and the choice of encoder is a modeling
decision that should be made on measured coverage and measured behavior rather than
on a reading list. Twelve small-molecule encoders now live in
\file{torchcell/molecule/}: a \texttt{MoleculeEncoder} turns a list of SMILES into one
fixed-width float32 vector per molecule, raises on a SMILES it cannot parse rather than
returning zeros, and is deterministic across calls. Four are RDKit fingerprints and
descriptors with no learned weights (count and bit ECFP4 at 2{,}048, feature-class
FCFP4, MACCS keys, and the 217 RDKit 2D descriptors); one is Mol2Vec, a skip-gram over
Morgan substructure identifiers; four are SMILES transformers (the two ChemBERTa-2
heads, MoLFormer-XL, a ZINC RoBERTa); one is Uni-Mol, which embeds an ETKDG conformer;
and one is MolE, the contrastively pretrained graph network built for antimicrobial
activity. Weights that are not on the Hugging Face hub (Mol2Vec, MolE) sit under
\file{\$DATA\_ROOT} with a pydantic manifest recording the source URL, the retrieval
command and the sha256, re-hashed on every load. The registry and its tests are
described in the dendron note \texttt{torchcell.molecule.encoders}.

Reaction-aware encoders were left out on purpose. A reaction-hypergraph embedding places
a metabolite by the reactions it takes part in, which is the right context for a
Yeast9 metabolite and no context at all for an inhibitor or a drug whose fate in the
cell is not in the model. When the medium components enter the environment encoding
(Section~\ref{sec:next}) the metabolites among them can carry a reaction branch; the
inhibitors cannot.

\notesec{Coverage}
Every one of the 343 compounds has a SMILES in the pinned identity table, and eleven of
the twelve encoders embed all of them (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}). Uni-Mol embeds 332:
its wrapper library substitutes flat or all-zero coordinates when a conformer cannot be
generated (metal dihalides, isolated ions, nitric oxide, and bleomycin at 180 atoms) and
would have embedded eleven Hillenmeyer compounds from nothing. The encoder's
\texttt{check} method now reruns the conformer step and raises on either signature, so
those eleven are counted as failures rather than served as vectors. None of them is a
Vanacloig compound. One dosed Hillenmeyer name, tunicamycin, is a homologue mixture
with no InChIKey and is counted as not a compound.

\notesec{Cost}
The fingerprints, Mol2Vec and MolE embed the union in well under a second; the SMILES
transformers in under a second; the RDKit descriptors in two seconds; Uni-Mol in fifty
because of conformer generation. At the scale of these datasets no encoder is
expensive, and the choice can be made on what the vectors do.
