\section{Round 2: the arms that decide it\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:round2}

Nothing in this section is measured. It is the design of the next Delta round, written
before submission so the figures it feeds are fixed in advance, as the first round's
mock-up was (Figure~\ref{fig:mockup}). Every arm is the same protocol as round 1,
the control config with the named keys changed, three seeds, on Delta
\texttt{gpuA40x4}, so every new column is read against the columns already in hand.
Table~\ref{tab:round2} is generated from
\file{experiments/025-solid-growth/scripts/graph_reg_round2_plan.py}, which is also
where the launcher will read the arm list from once the round is approved.

\notesec{What round 1 left open, and the arm that closes each}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Biology or conditioning.} The random-graph control ran at $\lambda =
  10^{-3}$, where the biological prior adds 0.005 to 0.013 and two seeds cannot resolve
  it. At $\lambda = 1$ the biological prior adds 0.021 to 0.023 on every seed. Three
  seeds of the rewired graphs at $\lambda = 1$ decide whether that is the graphs or the
  penalty; three more at $10^{-1}$ cover the loss-selected optimum.
\item \emph{The top of the ladder.} Accuracy at epoch 29 rises to $\lambda = 1$ with no
  turnover inside the ladder, while contraction and divergence are flat from $10^{-2}$.
  $\lambda = 10$ and $100$ find the turnover, or show there is none in reach.
\item \emph{Direction.} The prior's target is directed, $A[\text{source}, \text{target}]$
  row-normalized; the mask builder symmetrizes it. So the
  mask-versus-prior gap confounds soft-versus-hard with directed-versus-undirected on
  the regulatory and TFLink heads. Two arms separate them: the prior at $\lambda = 1$
  toward symmetrized targets, and a directed mask (the transpose write removed, which
  leaves the seven symmetric graphs unchanged). If direction matters, the symmetric prior
  falls toward the mask and the directed mask rises toward the prior. A two-head variant,
  one head on $A$ and one on $A^{\mathsf{T}}$ for each directed graph, is the follow-up
  if the directed mask helps, not part of this round.
\item \emph{Reach.} A head under the mask can only attend one hop. A mask whose support
  is $A \cup A^{2}$ (self-loops kept) lets a head reach two hops inside one layer, which
  the prior never forces and the free model can only learn.
\item \emph{Placement.} The encoder has eight layers and the graphs enter layer 1 only,
  an $[S, M, S, \ldots]$ pattern. The round adds the mask on layers 1 to 2, 1 to 4 and
  all eight, and the prior at $\lambda = 1$ on layers 1 to 2 and 1 to 4. The mask costs
  nothing in memory; each regularized layer of the prior materializes its attention, so
  four layers is about four times the 6 GB of one and eight would return to the 41 GB
  of the old path, which is why the prior stops at four.
\item \emph{Budget.} No penalty peaks at epoch 16 and declines; $\lambda = 1$ peaks at
  23 to 26 and was still at its high at epoch 29. Sixty epochs of no penalty, the mask
  and $\lambda = 1$ show whether the strong prior keeps rising or only declines later,
  and whether the fixed-budget gap grows or closes. These need the 48 h clock.
\item \emph{Completion.} Seed 2 of $\lambda = 10^{-5}$ never reached W\&B (Delta job
  22055161); one run fills it. The third random-graph seed at $10^{-3}$ finishes on its
  own.
\end{enumerate}

\notesec{Three code changes, all small}
The symmetric prior is one flag in the adjacency normalizer: write
$A[j, i]$ beside $A[i, j]$ for every edge before row normalization, so the directed
graphs get the same treatment the mask gives them today. The directed mask is the removal
of one line, the transpose write in the mask builder, behind a flag that
defaults to today's behavior so no existing config changes meaning. The two-hop mask
builds its support as $A \lor (A A > 0)$ per head at construction, a dense
$6{,}608^{2}$ boolean per graph computed once. Multi-layer placement needs no code: the
mask takes a list of layers and the prior's \texttt{graph\_reg\_layer} already accepts a
list. Each change ships with a test that composes the config and checks the mask or the
target against a hand-built four-gene graph.

\notesec{What is not in the round, and why}
A two-hop prior (the KL toward a normalized $A + A^{2}$) waits for the two-hop mask's
result, since the normalization of $A^{2}$ is a design choice the mask does not have to
make. The 010 checkpoints and the cosine-schedule runs stay out; their schedule and
normalizer differ, and the constant-rate protocol now has its own anchors. Seeds beyond
three are not added: at three seeds the epoch-29 reading already separates the arms that
differ by 0.01 or more, and the readings that do not separate (max over epochs, the
loss-selected epoch) are limited by epoch-to-epoch noise of 0.005 to 0.010 within a run,
which more seeds do not remove.
