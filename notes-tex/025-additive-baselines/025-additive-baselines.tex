\title{\vspace{-1.5em}Additive Baselines on the 025 Build\\
  {\large The trigenic ladder under a random and a query-pair-disjoint split,
   what each model sees, and what the graphs did}}
\author{Michael Volk}
\date{\today}

\maketitle
\thispagestyle{empty}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A model that claims to learn genetic interaction has to beat a null that cannot
represent interaction. Five such nulls, and a nonlinear baseline on the same
features that can, are fit on exactly the 376{,}732 trigenic records and the two
splits the 025 transformer arms train on. Under the published random-over-records
split the per-gene additive ridge reaches 0.400 test Pearson, a model that sees
only which Kuzmin query double a triple carries reaches 0.390, and the transformer
checkpoints reach 0.438 to 0.455. Reading the model code establishes what that
comparison is between: the transformer's encoder never sees the strain, the nine
interaction graphs enter training through a penalty on layer-1 attention rather
than through the function that maps a checkpoint to a prediction, and the
strain-dependent computation is a set function over the three perturbed gene
identities, the same input and the same hypothesis-class shape the nulls have. The
penalty was not incidental: it carried 99.99 percent of the 010 training loss, the
regularized heads recover their graphs at 0.73 to 1.00 recall, and a matched pair
of runs without it fails to train. Rebuilding the forward pass reproduces the
recorded metrics to the fourth decimal and shows the transformer is not a relabeled
additive model, correlating with it at 0.73 to 0.76 rather than the 0.999 reported
for MULTI-evolve, with a gain of $+0.038$ to $+0.055$ Pearson whose bootstrap
intervals exclude zero.

The result that changes what the metric means is the split. Holding out whole query
doubles collapses the additive ridge to 0.185 on the one disjoint split the
transformer trains on, and to $0.127 \pm 0.033$ over five disjoint folds, and drops
the nonlinear baseline below the additive one. Four transformer runs have trained
on the disjoint split, one in the 010 configuration and three with a fixed sequence
embedding and a constant learning rate. Every one peaks on validation between epoch
2 and epoch 7, at 0.199 to 0.270 against an additive validation null of 0.150, and
every one falls back afterward; the 010 configuration ends below the null at 0.131.
Its validation-selected checkpoint, scored on the 46 held-out test screens, reaches
0.139 against the additive ridge's 0.185, a paired gap of $-0.046$ with a bootstrap
interval of $[-0.056, -0.036]$, and is level with the nonlinear baseline. Under the
prespecified criteria the transformer in the 010 configuration is not shown to beat
the additive null on held-out screens, and the one scored checkpoint is below it;
the seed replicates that would make this a statement about the model rather than
about one run have not been run. Every run is in a registry linked to its W\&B
page, and three earlier claims are corrected in place.
\end{abstract}
