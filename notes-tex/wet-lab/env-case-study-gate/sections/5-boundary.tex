\section{The boundary readout, and what the first model arms say\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:boundary}

For several inhibitors the useful prediction is the shape in dose space inside which the
host still grows, the line between alive and dead at each concentration. The
fixed-dose combination plate gives one point per combination, so the boundary lives in
the three isobole grids and in the single-agent curves that fix where it meets the axes.
Bliss multiplies surviving fractions, two independent hits; Loewe adds doses as
fractions of each compound's own dose for the same effect, one shared capacity. With the
steep single-agent curves of this host the two fronts sit far apart, so which one a pair
follows is a large, visible difference, and it is scored here directly: a rule's alive
region is the interior cells where its prediction clears the grid's growth threshold,
compared with the observed alive region by the overlap of the two sets (the Jaccard
index), the cells wrong in each direction, and the growth edge, the highest dose called
alive in each column, in grid steps.

\pfstep{Post-processing the observed region} A dose-response boundary is monotone: more
inhibitor never rescues growth. A grown cell sitting above a dead one is therefore a call
error, and the observed region is closed downward before it is scored (a cell stays alive
only if every cell with lower doses on both axes is alive). The furfural and formic acid
grids are already monotone. The 5-HMF grid, flagged since the processor round for its
growth islands, loses \hydbndexTwoEightislands{} interior cells to the closure, going
from \hydbndexTwoEightaliveRaw{} alive cells to \hydbndexTwoEightaliveMonotone{}; the
figure marks each dropped cell with a cross. Any predicted surface gets the same closure
before it is scored as a boundary.

\pfstep{What the figure now claims} The boundary is model-free and it is not one rule:
Loewe for the acids, Bliss for the short acids with an aldehyde. The rule choice is the
quantity a learned model would have to supply, and the leave-one-pair-out test is the
bar it has to clear. That replaces "Loewe wins overall" as the statement of the bottom
row.

\pfstep{The first model arms} The mixture trainer of this experiment ran its first
round: the environment-encoder recipe of the corrected-store campaign with one compound
token per inhibitor, a log-molar dose modulation (FiLM) that can be switched off, and a
host head read at the mean over genes, fitted on the public dose anchors alone or on the
anchors with the titration run, five compound-cold folds of one fold seed with three
seeds each. The combination plate and the isobole grids are never trained on.
\hydarmsscored{} arms have score files here; two more, the dose-off pair, finished on
Delta and await an rsync, and three are running there. Every arm sits below the
model-free rules on every wet-lab readout.

\pfstep{What the arms say} The dose-aware joint arm fits the titration wells it trains on
and then predicts the combinations at chance; the anchors-only arm, the zero-shot host,
is below chance and ranks the grown combinations the wrong way. Switching the dose
modulation off raises the growth AUROC in both host regimes (the Delta pair, recorded in
the experiment note until its files arrive), which is the opposite of what a dose-aware
model is for. Every arm's grid residual is negative: the arms predict more growth in the
interior cells than was seen, and the dose-aware arms predict the most. Hypothesis
(untested): the host head composes inhibitors near-additively or less, so each added
inhibitor lowers predicted fitness by less than it lowers observed fitness, and the
sharper the single-agent fit the larger that gap. On the public side every arm reads
within a few hundredths of the earlier campaign's encoder and below ridge, so the wet-lab
failure is not a model that was working elsewhere.

\pfstep{Where a learned model can still earn the panel} The rules need the single-agent
curves. The regimes in which they have no prediction, or one they cannot improve with
data, are the model's territory, and each needs its own baseline. Zero-shot singles: no
Bioscreen well in training, the six titration curves predicted from public screens; the
honest readout is the pooled ranking over all titration wells, since within one compound
fitness falls with dose and any dose-aware model scores near perfectly there; on file the
anchors arm reads weakly and the dose-off anchors arm reads nothing. One inhibitor
unknown: five curves and the anchors known, the sixth withheld, the model predicts its
curve and every combination that contains it; the baseline is the hybrid, the model's
curve fitted as a Hill and fed to Loewe. Singles and pairs known, higher orders predicted:
the fifteen pairs become training data and the forty-eight higher-order combinations the
test; the baseline is a rule with one interaction parameter fitted on the pairs. Leave one
pair out: the same, with the fifteenth pair as the test and the rule choice as the
quantity learned.

\pfstep{Open decisions} First, whether the boundary readout, the overlap
of alive regions and the edge error under the monotone closure, replaces growth AUROC as
the primary wet-lab statistic of the bottom row; this document already scores both.
Second, whether the fifteen pairs may become training data in the two regimes that need
them; that breaks the pre-registered rule that the combination plate is never trained on,
for those arms only, and the note would say so. Third, whether the bottom row ships with
the model beside the rules, as the arm table shows it, or with the rules alone until an
arm clears one of the bars. The first two are open; the third follows from whatever the
queued rounds return.

\pfstep{Round three, read over both machines} The screen that followed round one
(\cref{fig:sifour}) was read on 2026.10.12 with every arm scored on Delta and most of them
on GilaHyper as well. Three readings. The same config at the same seed differs between the
machines by a median of \hydrThreemachinesmedianAbsDiff{} and by up to
\hydrThreemachinesmaxAbsDiff{} on the public score, so no single-seed difference in the
screen is a finding. Over every run so far the one-, two- and three-source arms read
\hydrThreeonegenemean{}, \hydrThreetwogenemean{} and \hydrThreethreegenemean{} on the
public side and \hydrThreeonegrowthmean{}, \hydrThreetwogrowthmean{} and
\hydrThreethreegrowthmean{} on the growth call: adding the public screens does not lift
either readout, and the arms predict a cold compound on those screens at
\hydrThreesrchoepfnercgt{} (Hoepfner) and \hydrThreesrchillenmeyercgt{} (Hillenmeyer)
against the chemistry-feature learners' \hydrThreesrchoepfnerridge{} and
\hydrThreesrchillenmeyerridge{}. The dose modulation learns a held-out public dose
(\hydrThreesrchoepfnerdose{}) and lowers the growth call on
\hydrThreedosepairsfilmLower{} of \hydrThreedosepairs{} paired comparisons, which is round
one's reading again under a protocol that selects on a held-out signal. Round four, six
seeds on five folds of the three source-set arms, is what will turn the first reading
into a measured null or a difference.

\pfstep{Rounds seven to ten, read over both machines} Round four returned the measured
null: the three source-set arms at six seeds sit inside one spread on both readouts
(\cref{fig:sifour}). Rounds seven to nine changed what a record is. Under the cell
readout every record is a cell, the host the record with the empty genotype read through
the shared head, and the public score did not move; what moved the private readout was
the composition. With the compound tokens of a medium averaged, every arm read one
fitness at one, two and three inhibitors (growth AUROC 0.48 to 0.57 over six runs);
summed, the medium read fitter than its singles (0.27); read one inhibitor at a time and
composed as Bliss independence, the same model orders the combinations at
\hydmodelAuroc{} fitted over \hydmodelRuns{} runs and \hydrOneZerozsOnegrowthmean{}
zero-shot, and its predicted fitness falls with the inhibitor count. Round ten
(\cref{fig:sieight}) put three seeds on the questions. Sources: no public screen lifts
the target, and the four-source arm is the lowest (\hydrOneZeroonegenemean{} to
\hydrOneZerofourgenemean{}); the fitted wet-lab readout does not move with sources; the
zero-shot readout does, \hydrOneZerozsOnegrowthmean{}, \hydrOneZerozsTwogrowthmean{} and
\hydrOneZerozsThreegrowthmean{} on one, two and three sources, three runs each and the
steps inside the spread. Dose: FiLM on the tokens reads the titration
(\hydrOneZerodosefilmtitration{}) and costs the host (\hydrOneZerodosefilmauroc{}
against \hydrOneZerodoseoffauroc{} with no channel), its public cost of the single-seed
rounds being seed noise; the dose offset reads the titration at
\hydrOneZerodosedofftitration{} at no public cost and composes at
\hydrOneZerodosedoffauroc{}; the titration fine-tune with FiLM composes at
\hydrOneZerodoseftauroc{}.

What the public screens themselves can be predicted at is a narrower statement than it
may read. The held-out compound score is the only thing measured on them: a compound the
model never saw, its predicted profile across the HOP strains against the measured one,
\hydrOneZerosrchoepfnercgt{} over the round-ten arms against ridge's
\hydrOneZerosrchoepfnerridge{}, and across the Hillenmeyer HET strains
\hydrOneZerosrchillenmeyercgt{} against \hydrOneZerosrchillenmeyerridge{}, the HET
training loss rising rather than falling in every round. A seen compound at a held-out
dose is a different question and is predicted (\hydrOneZerosrchoepfnerdose{}). A held-out
strain with every compound seen, the gene-cold split, has been run on Vanacloig alone,
where it read near zero, and not on HOP or HET, so whether a new HOP or HET strain's
response can be predicted is unmeasured rather than negative; the honest experiment is a
gene-cold split on each screen scored as hit recovery over the strains called sick rather
than a rank over every strain. Hoepfner's HIP arm is not in the pool at all, only its HOP
arm and Hillenmeyer's HET arm. Hypothesis (untested): haploinsufficiency profiles are
sparse, so a rank over all strains is a poor target for HET and a hit-level call would
read differently.
