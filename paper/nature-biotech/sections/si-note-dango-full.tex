\suppnote{note:dango-full}{DANGO on the full trigenic dataset of the CGT experiment\secstatus{todo}}

The DANGO value in Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d, Pearson $r = 0.367$, is the mean of the three
highest of five DANGO runs trained with STRING v9.1 graphs on the trigenic dataset of the
Cell Graph Transformer (CGT) experiment; \supptab{tab:dango-full-runs} lists every run,
including nine with STRING v11.0 and v12.0 graphs, and \suppfig{fig:dango-full} their
training curves. Two facts qualify the comparison: the DANGO and CGT runs were trained on
two different builds of the trigenic dataset, and every DANGO run on this dataset plateaus
at $r = 0.354$ to $0.368$, well below the $0.42$ the same implementation reaches on the
Kuzmin 2018 screen alone (\suppnoteref{note:dango-repro}).

\notesec{Dataset builds} The DANGO and DCell baselines of Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d were trained on
the experiment-006 build: the Kuzmin 2018 trigenic
interactions~\cite{kuzminSystematicAnalysisComplex2018a} with any perturbation type plus the
Kuzmin 2020 trigenic interactions restricted to deletions, 332,313 records. The CGT
replicates were trained on the experiment-010 build, every perturbation type from both
screens, 376,732 records. Both are split into training, validation, and test (nominally
80/10/10) by the same seeded splitter, stratified on phenotype label and perturbation count,
so the two validation splits differ in membership. No DANGO or DCell run on the
experiment-010 build exists; the like-for-like baseline requires that run.

\notesec{Training and result} DANGO is the implementation of \suppnoteref{note:dango-repro},
trained as \supptab{tab:dango-full-runs} records; the split seed is fixed and the
initialization is not, so runs of one configuration are replicates over initialization and
DDP nondeterminism. Read from the code, not measured in training: the v9.1-keyed weight
lookup of \suppnoteref{note:dango-repro} is the same script here, so the v11.0 and v12.0 runs
trained with $\lambda_k=1$ for every channel. Scored as the best validation Pearson $r$
(maximum over epochs), the five v9.1 runs reach $0.3664 \pm 0.0004$ (mean $\pm$ SEM), the
four v11.0 runs $0.3557 \pm 0.0012$, and the five v12.0 runs $0.3581 \pm 0.0010$
(\suppfig{fig:dango-full}b). The three runs behind Fig.~\ref{fig:ggi}d are the v9.1 runs
\texttt{014mprap}, \texttt{x3savllr}, and \texttt{6q08iign}; the two launched later reach
0.3658 and 0.3652, so the mean over all five sits 0.0006 below the reported 0.3670. The
three CGT replicates reach $0.4537 \pm 0.0043$ at epochs 24 to 25.

\notesec{Release and dataset effects} Every DANGO run reaches $r \ge 0.35$ within 16 to 23
epochs with v9.1 and 25 to 30 with v11.0 or v12.0 (\suppfig{fig:dango-full}a,\,c); what the
release changes is the plateau, a later and lower maximum rather than a slower initial rise.
Against the Kuzmin 2018 screen alone (\suppfig{fig:dango-full}d;
\supptab{tab:dango-string-versions}), the only measured data-size comparison for DANGO, the
two builds are split separately, so no like-for-like split can be claimed; dataset size,
split membership, and the addition of the Kuzmin 2020 screen change at once, and the drop
from $0.42$ to $0.36$ is not attributed to any one of them here. Hypothesis (untested): the
Kuzmin 2020 triples, screened around duplicated gene pairs, are the harder part of the
union, given that the CGT scores $0.396$ on the deletion-only experiment-009 build and
$0.454$ on the experiment-010 build. Whether DANGO trained on the experiment-010 build would
close any of the gap to the CGT is likewise not measured.

\notesec{Prediction and channel weights} Rescored from the saved weights on the
experiment-006 splits, the minimum-validation-MSE checkpoints of \texttt{014mprap} (v9.1,
epoch 81) and \texttt{9jpfy547} (v12.0, epoch 53) reproduce their logged validation Pearson
$r$, 0.3672 and 0.3543, to within $4\times10^{-5}$. The v9.1 checkpoint's validation
predictions are compressed toward zero, SD 0.023 against 0.061 for the measured $\tau$, and none
exceeds 0.064 (\suppfig{fig:dango-full}e). The meta-embedding weights depend on the release
(\suppfig{fig:dango-full}f): with v9.1 the experimental channel takes a mean weight of 0.277
against the uniform 0.167 and fusion the least, 0.125; with v12.0 fusion takes 0.261,
co-expression 0.187, and experimental 0.161. Restricted to the 4,036 genes perturbed in the
dataset, no channel mean moves by more than 0.011.

\notesec{What the ReZero gate carries} Each of DANGO's two Hyper-SAGNN attention layers returns
$x+\beta_l\,O_l(\mathrm{attn}(x))$, where $x$ is the layer input, $O_l$ the output projection,
and $\beta_l$ a learned scalar gate (the ReZero parameterization, which starts the gate at zero;
read from the code, this implementation starts it at 0.01). In the
\texttt{014mprap} minimum-MSE checkpoint $\beta_1=0.0732$ and $\beta_2=0.0649$, yet the
residual branch is not small: over the validation records its norm averages 0.415 and 0.334
times the norm of the layer input (Frobenius norms of each record's $3\times64$ block), so the
attention output before scaling is about 5.7 times its input, and a $\beta$ of 0.07 is not a
shut branch here. Setting both $\beta_l$ to zero at inference leaves the prediction as the mean
of three per-gene scores with no term that depends on two genes jointly (verified against
singleton scoring to $10^{-7}$) and drops validation $r$ from 0.3672 to 0.0791. The same
ablation takes the epoch-999 checkpoint of that run ($\beta_l=0.171,\,0.201$; ratios 0.672,
0.618) from 0.2973 to $-0.0283$, and the v12.0 checkpoint \texttt{9jpfy547} ($\beta_l=0.0921,
\,0.0745$; ratios 0.393, 0.324) only from 0.3543 to 0.3370, its $\beta=0$ predictions
correlating 0.963 with its full ones. This is an inference-time ablation of models trained
with the branch: it measures how much of each trained model's validation correlation passes
through the attention branch, not whether the gate helps training, which a model trained
without the gate answers (next paragraph).

\notesec{Ablation of the reconstruction loss and the ReZero gate} Three arms of a two-by-two
ablation of the v9.1 configuration were each trained once, for 150 epochs at batch 64 on one
GPU with seed 42 (\supptab{tab:dango-ablation-arms}, \suppfig{fig:dango-ablation}); the fourth
arm, with both changes, has not been run. The baseline (\texttt{qy17re1f}) is the v9.1
configuration of \supptab{tab:dango-full-runs}. The arm without the reconstruction loss
(\texttt{s2bpfsqr}) sets $\alpha_e=0$ from the first step, so the reconstruction loss is
computed and logged but sends no gradient and the model trains on the interaction loss alone.
It removes an auxiliary loss trained jointly with the interaction loss and does not test the
staged pretraining of the DANGO paper, which TorchCell folds into one run
(\suppnoteref{note:dango-repro}). The arm without the gate (\texttt{wlszxkke}) replaces
$x+\beta_l\,O_l(\mathrm{attn}(x))$ by the plain residual $x+O_l(\mathrm{attn}(x))$ in both
layers, with no $\beta_l$. One seed per arm cannot resolve a difference the size of the spread
of the five v9.1 runs, 0.3652 to 0.3676 in best validation $r$, and those runs took two to four
times fewer optimizer steps per epoch, so their spread is a scale, not a matched control.

Without the reconstruction loss, validation $r$ reaches 0.35 as early as the baseline (epoch 12
against 13), although the baseline trains on the reconstruction loss alone in epoch 0 and
starts at $-0.043$ where this arm starts at 0.240. It then stays near 0.35 (0.351 at epochs 50
and 100, against 0.364 for the baseline) and reaches 0.36 only at epoch 126, against 33 for the
baseline and 39 to 66 for the five earlier runs: 7.5 h of training against 3.0 h, although its
epochs take 212 s against 311 s. Its best, 0.3611 at epoch 137, is 0.004 below the baseline's
0.3654 and below all five earlier runs; at the minimum-validation-MSE checkpoint its test $r$ is
0.3705 against 0.3711. Its validation reconstruction loss, never trained, rises from 0.0047 to
0.0062 while the baseline's falls from 0.0019 to 0.0010 (\suppfig{fig:dango-ablation}c). On
this seed the reconstruction loss gives no head start to $r=0.35$ and no gain in test $r$ at the
kept checkpoint, but brings the model to its plateau about 90 epochs sooner; whether that holds
over seeds is not measured.

Without the gate, validation $r$ reaches 0.35 at epoch 6 and 0.36 at
epoch 17, against 13 and 33 for the baseline, at the same cost per epoch (309 s against 311 s);
its best, 0.3701 at epoch 70, is 0.0047 above the baseline and 0.0025 above the five earlier
runs, and its test $r$ at the kept checkpoint is 0.3784 against 0.3711. Removing the gate
therefore leaves the score unchanged or higher on this seed. The value a gate trains to is not a
readout of the branch's contribution: the baseline's gates end at $\beta_1=0.071$ and
$\beta_2=0.072$, the inference ablation above shows that a branch under such a gate carries
nearly all of the v9.1 correlation, and this arm shows that the model does not need the gate to
use the branch. Hypothesis (untested): a gate initialized at 0.01 scales down the attention
branch's gradient early in training, which would slow the rise that the plain residual avoids.
