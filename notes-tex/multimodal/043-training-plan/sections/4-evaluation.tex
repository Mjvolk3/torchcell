\section{Evaluation\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Split, window and seeds\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:evalsplit}

Both stages use \file{conf/splits/global_split_v1.json} (\dsec{5.1}): fold 0 test, fold 1
validation, the rest training, and the prepare stage drops fold 0 so its labels are never
loaded during development. Each run reads its registered window, the last 20\% of its own
epochs (\deq{28}): epochs 41 to 50 of a 50-epoch stage-1 run, epochs 169 to 210 of the main
Kemmeren fine-tune. Validation is logged every evaluation and never selects an epoch. Seeds
pair across stages: the stage-2 runs of seed $j$ start from the stage-1 run of seed $j$, and
both of their from-scratch controls take seed $j$.

\subsection{The decision rule\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:rule}

An arm is called better than its named control when the mean paired gain in the primary
statistic's window mean is at least 0.02, the gain is positive in at least 5 of 6 paired
seeds, and the 95\% interval of the gain, resampled over held-out strains (over genes for a
per-gene statistic, over doubles for the doubles), excludes zero (\dsec{5.5}). The control is
named before the first read: the stage-1 reference for every stage-1 contrast,
$\mathrm{alone}(k_{\mathrm{tot}})$ and $\mathrm{alone}(k)$ for a fine-tune. The rule is
committed as \file{conf/decision_rule.yaml} before the Polaris launch; at commit
\texttt{f1cf262d2} it stands in the primer and the design review only. Eleven stage-1
contrasts are each called under the rule, which does not adjust for their number; the
single read of fold 0 at the end of the campaign is the check on the calls that survive.

\subsection{Per-dataset statistics, with the fold-1 ladder beside fold 0\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:perdataset}

Every stage-1 run reports each dataset's statistic of \dsec{5.2} on fold 1, against the
linear ladder of \file{results/linear_evals_043.json} on the same fold
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The ladder was first reported on fold 0; its fold-1 values train on
fold 0 as well but score the same held-out units as the runs, so fold 1 is the column a run is
read against during development and fold 0 the column it is read against once at the end.

\subsection{The Ohya probe\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:ohya}

Ohya 2005 enters no training. For each of its 4{,}678 genotypes the frozen model produces the
perturbed gene state, the mark summary and the mean perturbed state; ridge regressions from
each and from their concatenation predict the 116 moving CalMorph features, 5-fold over
genotypes, raw and residualized on growth (primer S6). The statistic is the median over the
116 features of the out-of-fold Pearson correlation, with the subgroup of genotypes whose gene
is in fold 0 (459) reported as the strictly inductive read. The bar is the same ridge on the
frozen composite token: 0.121 raw, 0.083 residualized, 0.137 on the fold-0-gene subgroup; the
019 trained head read $0.149 \pm 0.022$. The probe is not wired into the trainer; it runs as a
CPU job on the window-end checkpoints of every stage-1 and stage-2 run, so it costs no card
time and reads how much morphology the representation carries before and after fine-tuning.

\subsection{The route consistency readout\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:routeread}

For each seed of the route-B arm and the stage-1 reference of the same seed,
Eq.~\ref{eq:routecons} is computed on the fold-1 isolates of Caudal and of Muenzner protein and
reported as three medians: $\rho_b$, $r^{A}_b$ and $r^{B}_b$. The readout decides nothing alone;
it qualifies the route contrast, since a route-B gain with a low $\rho_b$ is a different function
of the genotype rather than a better reading of the same one.
