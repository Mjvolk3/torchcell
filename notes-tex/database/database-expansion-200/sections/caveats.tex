\subsection{Two rows are not untouched candidates\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:status}

Checking a name against the served list is not sufficient. A dataset can carry a loader, a
failed retrieval attempt, or an already-ingested deposit under a different paper's name.

\notesec{Lee 2014, row 47, is blocked rather than available} An earlier ingestion attempt
is recorded as awaiting author matrices, which means the Science supplementary tables and
the Nislow and Giaever lab portal did not yield per-strain values. Its rank is still
earned: 3,356 compounds against the full heterozygous and homozygous collections really is
the largest chemical-genetic matrix in existence, and it really is on the YKO genotype axis
the served set already uses. What changes is the nature of the work. Row 47 is an author
request, not a loader, and the $1.3\times10^7$ instances it contributes to the totals in
Sec.~\ref{sec:summary} should not be counted as reachable until that request succeeds.

\notesec{Turco 2023, row 60, already has a loader}
\file{torchcell/datasets/scerevisiae/yeastphenome.py} consumes the curated YeastPhenome
release and retains 49 growth screens, so this row is in flight rather than net new. It is
not in the served set, which is why it appears here at all. Its de-duplication problem is
partly solved already, since the loader excludes the primaries served directly, along with
heterozygous-diploid and expression screens. Lee 2014 is not among its 49 screens, so
ingesting YeastPhenome does not backfill row 47.

\subsection{Overlap is checked by script, and the check is not yet strong\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:overlap}

Rows that are already held have been caught by inspection before and by script once, so
the check is \file{experiments/database/scripts/check_candidate_overlap.py}, run against
the candidate JSON. It applies three keys of increasing strength: the candidate DOI cited
in a served loader, an accession token shared with one, and the candidate PMID appearing in
an aggregator's source-PMID column.

Against the 55 served loaders it reports one overlap, Turco 2023, which
Sec.~\ref{sec:status} already records as a loader in flight, and no overlap with SynLethDB
across 3,658 source PMIDs spanning both the lethality and rescue layers. The honest limit
is coverage. The script resolves a PMID for 124 of 154 rows and finds a parseable
accession in 11. Accession is the strongest key, so it is running on about one row in
fourteen. Recording each row's deposit identifier is the single highest-value edit
available to this list.

\subsection{Confidence, and the candidate gate\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:confidence}

Rows carry a confidence value in the generated JSON. \emph{sourced} means the figures trace
to something read in a pass or to the sourced triage note, and covers 65 of the 154 rows,
34 of the sixty printed. \emph{recall} means the row was written from domain knowledge,
and covers 89 of the 154, 26 of the sixty. For a recall row the dataset is real and the
description is sound, but the citation details, the exact counts and the accession all
need confirming before a loader is written.

The new first band sharpens that warning rather than softening it. Six of the eight state
rows are recall rows, Albert 2018 among them, and none of the eight has a deposit
identifier fetched: Albert 2018's GEO series, Jakobson 2025's accession, the PRIDE
identifiers of Teyssonniere 2024 and Grossbach 2022, Smith 2008's GEO series and
McManus 2014's are all marked unconfirmed in Table~\ref{tab:sources}. Treat the top of the
table as a claim to verify first, not a queue to start work from; the candidate gate is
where that verification is recorded.

Table~\ref{tab:verdicts} in Sec.~\ref{sec:full} gives each row's verdict from the gate's
verdict store. Eight of the 154 rows have been gated: one admissible, one admissible with
typed gaps, three blocked on provision and three blocked on a wall. None of the eight
state rows has a verdict yet, so gating them is the first step of the next build week.

\section{Before a loader is written\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:caveats}

Every figure in Table~\ref{tab:final} is an axis count, not a verified record count,
and several are weaker than that. Three qualifications apply, in decreasing order of how
much damage they would do if ignored.

\notesec{Instance counts marked $\dagger$ are products, not counts} A screen reported as
4,800 strains against 3 conditions is entered as 14,400 instances. Real screens drop
strains per condition, so the released matrix is smaller, sometimes by a third. Thirteen of
the sixty rows carry the mark.

\notesec{Measurements assume a dense vector} A proteome row is counted as instances times
the number of proteins quantified, but a label-free proteome quantifies a different subset
of proteins in each sample. The served Messner 2023 ranges from 1,441 to 1,849 proteins per
record, and a candidate proteome will be sparser than its headline depth in the same way.

\notesec{Estimates marked $\ddagger$ are order-of-magnitude} Twenty-one of the sixty rows
have an instance count that is an estimate rather than a reported figure or a product of
reported axes, and among the eight state rows Foss 2007 has none at all.
