\section{State on the day the sheets were drawn\secstatus{todo}}
\label{sec:state}

\pfstep{What had finished} The processor round of this experiment: the four
pre-registered claims scored, which is every panel marked on file above. The ladder of
the corrected-store campaign, giving main \textbf{d} and SI-1 \textbf{a}. The learning
curve of the earlier campaign, giving main \textbf{f} in its ridge arm. The pooled table
and its axes, giving main \textbf{b}.

\pfstep{What finished while the sheets were drawn} The second factorized round of the
corrected-store campaign, submitted in the small hours of the same day on two GilaHyper
cards: one job for the bilinear control, one for the environment encoder, each fifteen
configurations of five compound-cold folds on three fold seeds, three seeds per fold. Both
completed, giving main \textbf{e} in full: the control's three-seed ensemble at
\hydbilinearMedian{} and the encoder's at \hydenvencMedian{} over
\hydbilinearEvaluations{} compound-evaluations, against nested ridge at
\hydladderRidge{}, with the encoder \hydpairedMedian{} against its own control when the
two are paired on compound and fold. Neither head reaches the fingerprint ridge on this
store, which reverses the earlier campaign's reading and is the result the two Delta
rounds below are meant to explain or confirm. The sheet re-reads the directory every time
it is drawn, so a later round replaces these values rather than adding to them.

\pfstep{What had not started} The first mixture round, which is every panel of the bottom
row in its model arm. The trainer and its configuration groups are owned by the
implementation thread and are not part of this document.

\pfstep{What to expect, labeled} Hypothesis (untested): the environment encoder ties
nested ridge on the corrected store, as it did on the earlier store, so main \textbf{e}
closes with the two within a hundredth of each other and the figure's public-benchmark
row says that architecture is not where the gain is. Hypothesis (untested): the encoder's
compound-cold profiles for the two inhibitors in store read no better than ridge, so main
\textbf{j} keeps its shape and the predicted profiles stay generic. Hypothesis
(untested): a dose-aware host head fitted on the titration run beats the highest single
agent on growth, which reads \hydhsaAuroc{}, and does not beat Loewe, which reads
\hydloeweAuroc{}, because Loewe already uses the fitted single-agent curves and the model
has no information about this host that those curves do not carry. If that last one holds
the honest figure is the model-free one, and the bottom row says so in the caption rather
than in a footnote.

\pfstep{What would change the figure's claim} A model arm that beats
\hydloeweAuroc{} on growth, or \hydblissSpearman{} on fitness over the combinations that
grew, moves the bottom row from a validation of arithmetic to a result about transfer. A
model arm that does neither still earns the row, because the comparison is the point: the
rules are computed from six titrations on the same strain, and a model trained on
\hydnPoolCompounds{} public compounds that cannot beat them is the measurement the
section needs to report.

\pfstep{The same day, evening} The first mixture round ran: two arms on the GilaHyper
cards named for this work, two on Delta, two more on GilaHyper by the evening, and the
rest on Delta overnight; \cref{tab:arms} holds the arms with files here. The third
hypothesis above held in its pessimistic half: no arm beats Loewe on growth or Bliss on
fitness, and the dose-aware arms do not beat the highest single agent either. The
readout was then reframed around the alive and dead boundary (\cref{sec:boundary}),
which is model-free and splits by pair, and the leave-one-pair-out test of rule-choice
predictors was added as the bar a learned model must clear. Round two began with the
replication of the dose-off joint arm on two further fold seeds.

\pfstep{Three days on, 2026.10.15} Ten rounds are on file, every arm on both machines.
The readout is the cell, the composition is Bliss, the model sits at the rules' level
zero-shot on the private combinations (\hydrOneZerozsOnegrowthmean{} with one source,
\hydrOneZerozsThreegrowthmean{} with three) and the dose offset gives it a dose axis at
no public cost; the public bar is still ridge's. The bottom row ships with the model
beside the rules, which was the third open decision. What is not on file: a deviation
term beyond Bliss, a gene-cold split on the public screens, and any arm above ridge on
the public side.
