\section{Where these tables enter the model\secstatus{todo}}

Figure~\ref{fig:cgt-module} places the flux module inside the cell graph transformer. The
module is neither of the two instrument classes the architecture previously had: a Type I
instrument maps a representation to a representation and a Type II instrument maps a
representation to a measured output, while the flux module maps a representation to a
\emph{flux vector}, a latent physical state from which phenotypes are then read. It sits
between them.

Three of its inputs are parameter tables rather than learned weights, and those three are
what this document builds. The $k_{cat}$ table sets the upper bound of the box that the
flux is parameterized inside, so it fixes what fluxes are reachable at all. The formation
energies set the reaction potential whose sign the second-law penalty acts on, which is
what makes that penalty a physical statement rather than a regularizer. The $K_M$ table is
built and not yet enabled, for the reason Section~\ref{sec:limits} gives.

\subsection{Why these two tables, in two equations\secstatus{todo}}

Each table earns its place by removing a specific failure the stoichiometry alone cannot.

\paragraph{$k_{cat}$ is what lets a genotype change a flux.} In one line, with the
gene-protein-reaction fold abstracted into $c_j \in [0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
  |\nu_j| \;\le\; \underbrace{c_j\big(H_{\mathrm{pert}}\big)}_{\text{GPR availability}}
  \cdot \underbrace{k_{cat,j}\,\bar E}_{\text{capacity}} .
  \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}

$c_j$ is not a pooled function of every gene. Each GEM gene is gated on its own embedding,
$\gamma_g = \sigma(w^{\!\top} h_g)$, with a deletion pinning $\gamma_g = 0$; those gates are
folded to the reaction by a softmin over the subunits of a complex and a sum over isozymes.
A reaction with no GPR keeps $c_j = 1$, since an unannotated reaction is one nobody assigned
a gene to rather than one no gene catalyzes.

Without Equation~\eqref{eq:capacity} the only limits on $\nu_j$ are yeast-GEM's own bounds,
and those carry no capacity at all: \textbf{4{,}129 of 4{,}131 reactions sit at $\pm1000$ or
$0$}, which encodes direction and on-off and nothing else. A gene deletion would then have
no route to change the \emph{magnitude} of any flux. $k_{cat}$ is the unit conversion from
an amount of enzyme to a ceiling on rate, and this product is the only path by which a
genotype reaches the network's capacity.

\paragraph{$\Delta_r G$ enters as a term in the loss, not as a constraint.} There is no
solver and no binary variable, so the second law is imposed by making its violation
expensive. The trained objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{data}}
  \;+\; \lambda_{\mathrm{th}}\,
  \underbrace{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{J}|}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}}
    \frac{\big[\,\nu_j\,\Delta_r G_j + \epsilon\,\big]_{+}}
         {|\nu_j|\,|\Delta_r G_j| + \epsilon}}_{C_{\mathrm{th}}},
  \qquad
  \Delta_r G_j = \Delta_r G'^{\circ}_j + RT\!\!\sum_{i \neq \mathrm{H}^+}\!\! S_{ij}\ln c_i,
  \label{eq:secondlaw}
\end{equation}
where $[\,\cdot\,]_{+}$ is the hinge and $\mathcal{J}$ runs over reactions with a known
$\Delta_r G$, excluding exchange, biomass and water transport. The tabulated formation
energies supply $\Delta_r G'^{\circ}_j$, which is the one place this document's thermodynamic
table reaches the model.

\textbf{The normalization is load-bearing, not cosmetic.} The raw hinge
$[\nu_j \Delta_r G_j]_+$ carries units and, measured at initialization on a real batch, is
about \textbf{72 against a data loss of about 1}. An unnormalized term of that size does not
regularize the model, it replaces it. Dividing by $|\nu_j||\Delta_r G_j|$ caps each
reaction's contribution at 1 and turns $C_{\mathrm{th}}$ into the fraction of the available
driving force running uphill, which is also the quantity worth reporting.

Why it earns its place: because $\Delta_r G$ is a difference of potentials it sums to zero
around any cycle, so no cycle can run downhill at every step, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:secondlaw} therefore penalizes internal loops. Capacity does not do this.
Requiring growth at 90 percent of optimum narrows 2{,}316 reactions, 56.1 percent of the
network, and leaves \textbf{427 reactions still free to take any value in $\pm1000$}, all of
them internal loops. Only the thermodynamic term removes those, and it does so with no
binary variable, which is what a loopless flux balance program needs one for.

The heterologous cassette enters as a typed perturbation on the genome-scale model, which
is what puts a betaxanthin pathway and a gene deletion inside the same object rather than
in two parallel code paths.
