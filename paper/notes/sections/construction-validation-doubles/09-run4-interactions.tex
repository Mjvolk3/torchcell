\section{Digenic interaction versus Costanzo --- measured, and not yet usable}
\label{sec:run4-eps}

The design supports the comparison cleanly. Both constituent singles of every double sit on
the same plate, so

\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{ab} \;=\; f_{ab} \;-\; f_a f_b
  \label{eq:eps}
\end{equation}

is formed entirely within a plate --- no cross-plate normalization enters the interaction
term, and the three plates give three independent replicates of the same quantity, which is
what the across-plate bootstrap turns into an error bar.

Run against the published Costanzo $\varepsilon$ for the same pairs, the answer is
\textbf{$r=-0.245$ ($p=0.44$, $n=12$)}: no agreement, and the sign is wrong.

\subsection{Why this is an artifact and not a result}

\textbf{It should not be read as a failure to reproduce Costanzo's interactions.} Four
observations, all from \path{experiments/W019-echo-crispr-array/results/run4_interactions.csv},
identify it as a normalization problem upstream of Equation~\eqref{eq:eps}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
diagnostic & value \\
\midrule
singles scoring \emph{above} the on-plate WT & \textbf{8 of 12} (up to 1.333) \\
multiplicative expectation $f_a f_b > 1$ & \textbf{10 of 13} pairs (up to 1.712) \\
sign of $\varepsilon$ & \textbf{negative for all 13} \\
$\mathrm{corr}(\varepsilon,\; f_a f_b)$ & $\mathbf{-0.926}$ ($p=5\times10^{-6}$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A multiplicative null above 1.0 for two deletion mutants is not a meaningful expectation, and
an $\varepsilon$ that correlates at $-0.93$ with its own expectation term is tracking the
singles, not the doubles. Concretely, for the strongest published interaction in the panel:

That $-0.654$ is arithmetic from two inflated singles, not a measured epistasis.

\subsection{Where the inflation comes from}

The singles are on the same scale as run 3's --- just shifted. The eleven strains common to
both rounds correlate at \textbf{$r=0.685$}, but run 4 sits a near-uniform
\textbf{$1.26\times$} above run 3 (median ratio; dividing it out leaves a mean absolute
difference of 0.111). In run 3 every single scored \emph{below} WT (0.67--0.92); in run 4
most score above it.

This matters for $\varepsilon$ and not for the correlations because \textbf{$\varepsilon$ is
not scale-invariant}. Under a uniform inflation $f \to k f$,

\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon' \;=\; k f_{ab} - k^{2} f_a f_b,
\end{equation}

so the expectation term grows quadratically while the observed term grows linearly, driving
$\varepsilon$ negative in proportion to $f_a f_b$ --- exactly the $-0.926$ correlation
observed. A Pearson correlation, by contrast, is invariant to $k$, which is why the singles
still reproduce at $r=0.706$ while every interaction is corrupted.

\subsection{What is not yet known}

\textbf{Why the WT reference moved is untested.} At the colony level the WT median is
\emph{higher} than the mutant median on all three plates (ratio 0.90--0.94), so this is not
simply sick or contaminated WT wells. Run 3 named its wild type \texttt{BY4741} explicitly;
run 4's strain list gives it as \verb|'-|. Whether that is a different stock, a different
plating density, or something else has not been checked, and no correction should be applied
before it is --- rescaling to make the numbers look right would bury the question.

The cheapest next diagnostic is to re-score run 4 against the \emph{plate median} instead of
the WT wells. If the singles fall back onto run 3's scale, the problem is localized to the WT
wells specifically and $\varepsilon$ becomes computable; if they do not, it is something
broader in the round and belongs at the bench.
