\section{Terms\secstatus{todo}}\label{sec:terms}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]

\item[boot SE] The standard error of a strain's mean fitness: resample the units
  the mean was taken over, take the mean of each resample, and report the spread
  of those means. Both \emph{boot SE} columns of
  Table~\ref{tab:existing-singles} are this same statistic, and what differs is
  the unit resampled. Ours resamples the three replicate plates. Costanzo's
  resamples the seventeen replicate control screens standing behind a query-strain
  value, with the colonies of a screen averaged before the resampling, so their
  unit is a whole screen and there are seventeen of them against our three. Two
  consequences worth stating: their number is the tighter one for the same
  underlying spread, and a strain-by-strain comparison of the two is therefore not
  like for like. Costanzo publishes the column under the name standard deviation.
  It is the spread of bootstrapped means, which makes it a standard error, and it
  enters our loaders as one, typed \texttt{bootstrap\_se}.

\item[colony SD] The published uncertainty on a double
  (Table~\ref{tab:existing-doubles}), and a different kind of number from either
  \emph{boot SE}: a sample standard deviation over the four to eight replicate
  colonies within one screen, which the authors warn underestimates the true
  variance because adjacent colonies are not independent. Being a spread rather
  than an error on a mean, it compares to our \emph{plate SD}. Costanzo publishes
  no bootstrap standard error for a double.

\item[fitness] Colony growth relative to the wild type on the same plate, so 1
  is wild-type growth.

\item[plate SD] The standard deviation of a strain's fitness across the
  replicate plates it was measured on. It is larger than that strain's
  \emph{boot SE}, which is the error on the mean of those same plates.

\item[routes] How many different ways the bench can make a triple from strains
  that already exist. Every triple is built by crossing one existing double with
  the remaining single. \emph{routes} 1 means only one of the triple's three
  pairs is in the freezer, so there is a single way in, and a failed cross
  blocks that triple until the matching new double from
  Table~\ref{tab:new-doubles} is done. \emph{routes} 2 means two of its pairs
  exist, so a failed cross has an immediate second path.

\item[tier] How a double was classed when run 4 was designed.
  \texttt{validation} reproduces a measurement someone has already published,
  \texttt{coverage} was added so that later triples would have a parent to be
  built from, and \texttt{novel} had not been measured by anyone.

\end{description}
