\subsection{Flux balance analysis on four media\secstatus{todo}}

The published FBA comparator is cobrapy \texttt{single\_gene\_deletion} with glucose as the
carbon source, scored on all 1,121 labeled genes. The phenotype it is compared against was
called on rich medium, and the model here is scored on the 195 held-out genes, so the
comparison as published is neither on the same medium nor on the same genes.
Table~\ref{tab:fba-media} recomputes it on yeast-GEM 9.0.2 under the exchange bounds the
model ships with and under the three media the ontology defines, with the essentiality
score being the negative knockout-to-wild-type growth ratio, rounded to $10^{-3}$ so that
the roughly 900 deletions that grow at 0.99 to 1.00 of the wild type rank as the tie they
are (without the rounding two identical runs differed by 0.03 AUROC on the held-out genes).

The medium moves the number of lethal deletions by a factor of two and the AUROC by about
0.01. The published 0.69 on all labeled genes is reproduced to within 0.02.

\subsection{yeast-GEM's own benchmark, and accuracy against balanced accuracy\secstatus{todo}}

yeast-GEM ships its own essentiality benchmark and prints it in its README: on 1,107
genes, an accuracy of 0.902 with 933 true non-essential, 65 true essential, 94 false
non-essential and 15 false essential calls. Its release file
(\texttt{results/external/yeast-GEM-9.1.1/}, version 9.1.1 at commit \texttt{2d594ae},
2026-08-30) says how: the Kennedy synthetic complete medium is written onto the
exchanges (every exchange closed, 28 uptakes at $-0.5$, glucose at $-20$, fifteen
inorganic uptakes open), each gene is deleted through the gene-protein-reaction rules,
and a deletion is inviable when its growth ratio is below $10^{-6}$; the genes are the
SGD verified ORFs in the model, and the labels are the Stanford deletion project's
inviable list of 2011, scored against the model at that one rule, a protocol the
repository attributes to Heavner and Price (2015). The released split's labels are the
same truth: on the 1,104 genes both name, the two sources agree on 1,102.

Table~\ref{tab:confusion} puts every scorer in this document at that rule. Two things
are visible at once. The first is that accuracy is the wrong statistic for this
endpoint: 86 percent of the genes are nonessential, so a scorer that calls nothing
essential reads 0.856 on yeast-GEM's set and 0.841 on the 195 held-out genes, and
yeast-GEM's 0.902 is that floor plus four and a half points; its recall on the
essential class is 0.41 and its balanced accuracy 0.70. FBA on the previous release, 9.0.2, under the ontology's own synthetic complete
recipe reads 0.907 on the released labels with a recall of 0.46, the same rule on the
same truth with a different medium composition, so the 9.1.1 number is reproduced to
within half a point. The second is where the transformer sits at a threshold: at its loss's
decision boundary it reads 0.856, the majority floor, with a recall of 0.68 and a
balanced accuracy of 0.78, and at the threshold that calls 31 genes essential it reads
0.867 with precision and recall both at 0.58. The released Flux Cone Learning
predictions at $P(\text{viable}) < 0.5$ call eight genes essential on the 195, all eight
correctly, for a recall of 0.26. A ranking statistic is what separates the scorers, and
the balanced accuracies, 0.63 to 0.72 for the solvers and 0.78 for the transformer,
order them the way the AUROCs do.

\subsection{Flux Cone Learning on the same genes\secstatus{todo}}

Recomputed from the authors' released per-gene predictions: 0.7308 on the 223 held-out
genes (published 0.73) and 0.742 on the 195 genes the model scores. On those same 195 genes
the released FBA predictions score 0.640, so the published pair overstates FBA by about
0.06 relative to a head-to-head on one gene set. The paper prints a third number, 0.78
$\pm$ 0.065 in the legend of its Figure 3a, which is the mean over five cross-validation
folds drawn inside its 898 training genes; a model scored on the held-out genes is compared
with the 0.73 line, never with that one.

\subsection{The classical ladder on the same split\secstatus{todo}}

The fitness and expression lines each carry a ladder of models that need no graph and no
training beyond a solve, and essentiality gets the classification form of it: FBA's growth
ratio with no fitting, then four model classes ($L_2$ logistic regression, a random
forest, a radial-basis support vector classifier, cosine nearest neighbors) on each of
twenty-four gene encodings, every pair fit twice, with and without the four FBA growth
ratios appended. The encodings are the seventeen the fitness ladder carries (codon
frequency, the chromosome and pathway indicator, six nucleotide windows, the two FUDT
embeddings, the species language model, CaLM, ProtT5 and ESM2 with and without dubious
genes, and their concatenation, \texttt{mixed}) plus seven controls: a one-hot gene
identity, and standard normal vectors of dimension 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 6,607 (the one-hot's
width) and 8,192, drawn once at seed 0. A one-hot rung must score chance on held-out genes,
since every held-out column is zero in training, and a random vector must score chance at
every width; either scoring above chance would mean the split leaks. Every fitted rung is
fit on the split file's 629 training genes, its one hyperparameter chosen by five-fold
stratified cross-validation inside those genes, and scored once on the 158 validation and
195 held-out genes, on which nothing was chosen. The transformer cells in
Sections~\ref{sec:backbone} and~\ref{sec:module} train on the same 629 genes and score on
the same 195. Three further encodings come from the solve itself rather than from the
sequence, added after round 8 (\texttt{ess\_reference\_flux\_table.py}): the deletion's
own parsimonious FBA flux vector on YPD (4,131 reactions), that vector's difference from
the wild-type vector, and eight numbers about the gene's own reactions under the
wild-type solve and its flux variability at 90 percent of the optimum (reaction count,
reactions carrying flux, the largest and the summed absolute flux, the largest forced
flux, the smallest and largest variability range, and the deletion's growth ratio).
Figure~\ref{fig:ladder} shows all 216 fitted rungs; Table~\ref{tab:ladder} keeps, per
encoding, the model class whose cross-validation AUROC is highest.

The controls behave. The one-hot rung scores 0.506 and the six random widths score 0.45
to 0.59, chance within the 195-gene noise, and with FBA appended they score 0.56 to 0.69,
FBA's own number, so no rung reads anything but its features. Without FBA the protein and
codon language models are the only encodings clear of 0.70: ProtT5 0.732, CaLM 0.734, the
concatenation 0.768, and the chromosome-pathway indicator 0.775 [0.68, 0.86], each
level with Flux Cone Learning's 0.742 on the same genes. FBA lifts every one of them:
logistic regression on ProtT5 with the ratios scores 0.821 [0.74, 0.90] from 1,029
parameters, the support vector classifier on 64 codon frequencies 0.818 [0.73, 0.90], the
random forest on CaLM 0.806 [0.72, 0.89], and logistic regression on the 197-dimensional
chromosome-pathway indicator 0.830 [0.75, 0.90]. The rung with the highest
cross-validation AUROC, the random forest on CaLM with FBA at 0.850, scores 0.806 on the
held-out genes; the highest held-out number in the figure, 0.841 for cosine nearest
neighbors on codon frequency with FBA, is the maximum over 192 rungs and is not a
selected model. Neither an embedding nor the solver reaches 0.80 alone, and their sum
does on four encodings. FBA's ratio is a feature the ontology computes for every gene at
no training cost, so the transformer is measured against 0.80 to 0.83 from here on, not
against the published 0.73.

The solve carries more than its growth ratio. The eight reaction statistics under a
random forest score 0.797 [0.70, 0.88] with no sequence information, and 0.817
[0.73, 0.90] with the four ratios appended, at the highest cross-validation AUROC in the
ladder (0.847); the growth ratio alone, which is one of the eight, scores 0.645 on the
same genes. The full deletion flux vector scores 0.756 under its selected model and
0.771 under logistic regression, level with ProtT5 and with the pooled transformer, so a
gene's position in the wild-type flux solution and how much the solve moves when it is
deleted rank essential genes about as well as its protein sequence does, and the two
sources are different: no other encoding reaches 0.80 without the solve, and the eight
statistics come within 0.003 of it with nothing else.

\subsection{Graph-only baselines: message passing over the gene graph\secstatus{todo}}

The Gene\_Graph project (2021 to 2023) classified essential genes by two-layer graph
convolution and graph attention over YeastMine's physical, genetic and regulatory
interaction graphs with all-ones node inputs, and saved no number. The same design is
rerun here on the released split (\texttt{graph\_baselines.py}): transductive node
classification on one gene graph, where every gene's logit is a function of its
neighborhood and its own features and no genotype is perturbed, on the physical
interaction graph and on the union of the nine graphs the transformer reads, with all
ones, with log degree and the clustering coefficient, or with ProtT5 as inputs, beside
an MLP on ProtT5 with no graph (Table~\ref{tab:graph-baselines}).

\input{tables/graph_baselines}

Structure alone carries little and structure with sequence carries a lot. With all-ones
inputs the models read 0.50 to 0.61, chance to the ladder's random-vector level, and with
degree and clustering 0.58 to 0.70. With ProtT5 the MLP reads 0.754, the ladder's
logistic rung plus 0.02, and graph convolution over the union of the nine graphs reads
0.836, 0.829 and 0.836, a seed mean of 0.834 with a spread of 0.003 from 69,825
parameters: the edges add 0.08 to the protein embedding, the physical graph alone adds
0.015, and attention adds nothing over convolution on either graph. That cell is above
the pooled transformer (0.774) and level with the transformer with FBA's four ratios
(0.821) with no solve at all, and its three seeds select epochs 196 to 197 of 200 with
the validation curve still rising, so the budget is what bounds it. The transformer reads
the same nine graphs and the same embedding; what it adds over this cell, 0.87 with the
pathway indicator and the ratios, is the perturbed-graph reading and the two inputs, and
the graph-convolution cell with those two inputs is the untested comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:open}).
