\subsection{Published perturbation models\secstatus{todo}}

\Cref{tab:methods} places the three operators beside published designs. The published
rows repeat the reading recorded in \file{notes-tex/019-simb-multimodal}, section
``What the perturbation-response literature says''; they are descriptions of other
groups' models, and none was measured here. In that table $h_u$ is the other model's gene
state and $h^{\mathcal P}$ its perturbation embedding. The published models are
GEARS\external{Roohani 2023, doi:10.1038/s41587-023-01905-6},
State\external{Adduri 2025, doi:10.1101/2025.06.26.661135},
CPA\external{Lotfollahi 2023}, and the token-edit family, Geneformer and

\pfstep{The difference from GEARS and State is the readout} Both are additive at the
point of entry, as operator A is. Each gives every gene its own output parameters. CGT
reads every gene through one shared MLP, so gene identity enters only through $h_i$. A
per-gene output weight in the style of GEARS costs $6{,}127\times 91=557{,}557$ parameters
on the expression model.

\pfstep{Token editing avoids the one-key problem and forfeits the single encode} Each gene
attends to the edited gene among 6{,}607 tokens, so no key set of size one arises. The
cost is one encoder pass per strain.

\pfstep{The trigenic result and the single-deletion result are different regimes}
Hypothesis (untested): operator A serves experiments 025 and 030 because a trigenic
interaction is one scalar per strain at $M=3$, where the softmax chooses among keys and the
CLS summarizes; expression asks for about 6{,}000 gene-specific values at $M=1$, where the
same operator delivers one vector to all genes. The trigenic state of the art supports
the operator in its regime and is silent on the single-deletion, per-gene regime.

\subsection{Epistasis forms at two or more perturbations\secstatus{todo}}

The following is reasoning from the equations; none of it is measured.

\pfstep{Operator A averages} $\Delta h_i=\sum_t\beta_{i,t}v_t$ with weights summing to 1,
so a double mutant's shift is a convex combination of the two single shifts and never
their sum. Neither the additive nor the multiplicative null is the operator's default, and
the layers after it have to learn even the case of no interaction.

\pfstep{Operator B sums} $\Delta h_i=\sum_t\sigma(s_{i,t})v_t$, so the latent shift of a
double is the sum of the single shifts. With a linear readout that is the additive null;
with a readout that predicts $\log f$ it is the log-additive null. Interaction is then
what the model adds beyond the sum.

\pfstep{An explicit second-order term names the interaction} With $t<u$ ranging over pairs
of perturbations in the strain and $\phi$ a small learned map,
\begin{equation}
\Delta h_i=\underbrace{\sum_{t}\beta_{i,t}\,v_t}_{\text{single effects}}
+\underbrace{\sum_{t<u}\beta_{i,t}\,\beta_{i,u}\;\phi\big(v_t\odot v_u\big)}_{\text{pairwise interaction as seen by gene } i}.
\label{eq:second}
\end{equation}
The second sum is empty at $M=1$, so it costs nothing on single-deletion data and cannot
be learned from it.

Single deletions can teach the first-order map: how deleting gene $g(t)$ moves every other
gene $i$, the array of $\beta_{i,t}v_t$ over all pairs $(i,t)$. Sections
\ref{sec:operators} and \ref{sec:capacity} concern that object. The pairwise term needs
double and triple mutants: the Sameith doubles for expression, and the stores of
experiments 025 and 030 for fitness.
